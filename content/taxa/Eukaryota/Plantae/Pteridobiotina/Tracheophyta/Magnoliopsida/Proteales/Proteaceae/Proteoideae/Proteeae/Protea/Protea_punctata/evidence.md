## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
Populations on moister sites such as those in the Tulbagh and Riviersonderend mountains, produce larger, more robust leaves, rather similar to the foliage of P. lacticolor. However, when in bloom, P. punctata is unmistakable as the involucral bracts of each flower-head spread outwards almost at right angles to the bud position, leaving the floral parts apparently everted and the whole flower-head having a water lily-like form. The species' most reliable diagnostic character is that the pollen presenters are uniformly filiform with no trace of an apical swelling. Moreover, in the fruiting stage the filiform pollen presenters turn outwards and curl over.
<!-- /evo:text -->
