## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/0/text -->
An erect, much branched shrublet 0.5-0.7 m in height, to 0.5 m in diam., with numerous 10-15 mm diam. stems arising from a subterranean caudex, very occasionally with a short single main stem to 20 mm in diam., swollen at base to form a bulbous caudex. Stems highly branched, woody, 2-5 mm in diam., with short (20-50 mm) growth increments; glabrous, covered with dark verrucose bark; leaf scars prominent; bare of leaves except for ultimate and penultimate growth increments. Leaves erect, ascending, oblanceolate-elliptic, glaucous, glabrous, 40-60 mm long, 7-13 mm wide; apices obtuse, margins distinct. Inflorescence globose, 40-50 mm in diam., surrounded by several axillary shoots. Receptacle conic, acute, 10 mm wide, 10-12 mm in height. Involucral bracts 3-4 seriate, greenish cream to dirty pink, glabrous; inner series linear-oblong, 25-30 mm long, 3-7 mm wide, apices rounded, slightly concave; outer series broadly ovate, 10-15 mm long, 10 mm wide, margins brown. Perianth 30-35 mm long, slightly adaxially curved; tube region 10 mm long, glabrous externally but inner margins densely setose distally; claws sparsely setose with a ferruginous indumentum on inner and outer surface; limbs 10 mm long, apices sharply acuminate, outer surface sparsely setose with a tawny ferruginous indumentum. Anthers 4, subsessile, 5-6 mm long, filaments 1.5 mm long. Style 35-40 mm long, adaxially curved, tapering subterminally, white. Pollen presenter very narrowly linear, simple, scarcely differentiated from style, 10 mm long, pink. Ovary obconic, 5-6 mm long, covered with long, straight, tawny trichomes. Hypogynous scales lanceolate, acuminate, 1-5 mm long.

## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
Protea nubigena may be distinguished from all other pro-teas from the summer rainfall region of South Africa, on account of its dwarf, highly branched growth habit, the very small inflorescences and flowers (styles 35-40 mm long) and the small, oblanceolate, glabrous, glaucescent leaves 40-60 mm long, 7-14 mm wide.

## ecology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/ecology/claims/0/text -->
No other South African protea endures so rigorous a microclimate as P. nubigena does. Even in summer the cool, moist cliff faces it inhabits are in an almost perpetual shadow, while in winter they are probably frozen for weeks on end. Direct sunlight rarely falls on P. nubigena. During the summer, torrential deluges generated by afternoon thunderstorms pour a mean annual rainfall in excess of 1500 mm over the basalt cliffs and even if it is not raining, clouds may enshroud the kranzes day after day. Not surprisingly, P. nubigena is a very slow grower.
<!-- /evo:text -->
