## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/0/text -->
Erect, glabrous herb of up to 0.5 m high. Tuber(s): taproot tuber 0.1-0.25 x 0.1-0.2 m, spindle-shaped to cylindrically spindle-shaped; adventitious root tubers sub-spherical to cylindrically spindle-shaped. Aerial stems up to 0.5 m long, 5 mm diameter, erect or sub-erect, becoming violet with age, branching dichotomous or opposite. Leaves petiolate; petiole 2-10 mm long, violet; blade 50-60 x 20-30 mm, oblong-ovate, above dark green, beneath green, margin violet, veins translucent, secondary veins divaricate and 10-19 to a side, apex obtuse-mucronate, base cuneate to obtuse. Inflorescences terminal or axillary, violet, solitary monochasium or plumose from a few monochasia, each monochasium few-flowered, peduncles 10-20 mm long, pedicels 5-10 mm long; bracts 3 mm long, very narrowly ovate. Sepals 3 x 1 mm, narrowly triangular, brownish-violet, apex attenuate. Corolla tube 3-4 mm long, campanulate, outside pale greenish- violet, inside whitish-violet; lobes spreading, 8-10 x 5 mm, obovate to oblong-obovate, outside pale greenish-violet, inside whitish-violet, apex obtuse. Corona whitish-violet; coronal feet fleshy, apically lobed, free; lobe 1-2 mm long, angular-ovate, arched over style-head, apex bifurcate. Stamens arising from inner base of coronal feet; filaments 1-2 mm long, filiform, violet; anthers ±2 mm long, narrowly angular-ovate, lemon- yellow, full length slits, apex violet and acute. Ovaries 1-2 mm long, style 1-3 mm long, terete; style-head 2 x 3 mm, broadly rhomboid, pale violet; translators 1.2-2.3 mm long, spoon oblong-ovate. Gynostegium in corolla mouth. Follicles erect, normally solitary, 180-270 x 8-10 mm, very narrowly cylindrical obovoid, apex acuminate. Seeds 8-10 m long, obliquely narrowly oblong, pale brown, smooth, margin thickened; coma 40-50 mm long, whitish.

## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
R. palustris and R. hirsuta are very similar in their vegetative and flower structure. It is therefore difficult to distinguish between these two species without their distinctive follicles present. R. palustris has long erect spears in contrast to the short spreading ovoid follicles of R. hirsuta.
