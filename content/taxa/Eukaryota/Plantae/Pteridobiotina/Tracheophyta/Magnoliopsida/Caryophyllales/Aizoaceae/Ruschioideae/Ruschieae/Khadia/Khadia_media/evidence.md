## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/0/text -->
Compact dwarf succulent, arising from a main tap root, not tuberous, further branching usually occurring below soil surface. Leaf pairs symmetric, occasionally anisophyllous, triangular in transverse section, occasionally semi-terete in lower two-thirds, with pellucid dots concentrated along the margins, dark green, sometimes tinged reddish, tapering abruptly into a hyaline point; old leaf bases persisting. Flowers small, ca. 15 mm diameter, white or rarely light pink. Calyx: with 5 sepals, 2 long and 3 short with membranous margins. Androecium: petaloid staminodes present, filaments of three lengths, outside, 5-6 mm long, middle 3-3.5 mm long, inner, 2-2.7 mm long. Ovary with 5 or occasionally 6 locules, stigmata ca. 4 mm long, variable. Fruit with lower part distinctly concave (attenuating into pedicel), top rather flat, valve rims prominently recurved, placenta forming protrusions or placental 'closing body', covering membrane thin, without spongy tissue, closing ledges present. Seeds orange; testa cells more or less isodiametrical to elongate with smooth, convex periclinal wall.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
K. media differs from K. acutipetala by having capsules with 5 or 6 locules and white (or rarely light pink) petals, as opposed to the (6-)8-10(-11) locules and darker pink petals of the latter species. During our investigation, the 7-locular capsules reported for K. borealis by Chesselet et al. (1998) were found to be present in less than 6% of the capsules examined (n = 123), whereas no 8-locular capsules could be found. The occurrence of 7- and 8-locular capsules is therefor considered to be exceptions for this species. It may be worth pointing out that the holotype of K. borealis had a capsule with 8 locules. Ecologically, K. media's habitat on igneous rocks (granite-gneiss, gabbro) differs from the usual quartzite based habitat of all other known species. As K. nelsonii N.E.Br, (a synonym of K. beswickii) has been reported on 'greasy loam' soils (Chesselet et al. 1998) not normally associated with quartzites, there is still some doubt as to the habitat preference of this species.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## ecology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/ecology/claims/0/text -->
Khadia media occurs in specialised communities of succulents (Aloe L. spp.; Crassula L. spp.) and resurrection plants (e.g. Selaginella Drègei (Presl) Hieron; Xerophy-ta Juss. spp.), on shallow soil associated with large rock outcrops on the western (drier) side of the Woodbush range watershed. Geologically, these ridges are formed from the Basement complex igneous rocks, consisting mainly of granite-gneiss and gabbro, with diabase intrusions. This area represents a narrow zone of short grassland or open woodland transitional-between Afromontane vegetation to the east, and thornveld or mixed bushveld to the west. Most of the precipitation is received in the form of mist. Frost is frequently experienced in winter.
