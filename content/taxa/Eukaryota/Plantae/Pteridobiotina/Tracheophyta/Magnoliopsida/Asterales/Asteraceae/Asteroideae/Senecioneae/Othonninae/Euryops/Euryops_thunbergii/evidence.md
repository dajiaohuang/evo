## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/0/text -->
Closely leafy shrub to 1.2 m, densely cobwebby on young parts. Leaves spreading, filiform, terete or somewhat flattened, woolly on lower midline, 10-50 mm long. Flower heads radiate, on slender peduncles together forming loose umbels, yellow. Achenes white-puberulous, mucilaginous when wet.

## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
E. thunbergii has been confused with E. linifolius, which it much resembles. It differs in the more slender, filiform, almost terete leaves, which are provided with a narrow woolly strip on the adaxial side and have truncate tips. Furthermore, the involucres are hemispherical and mostly herbaceous and rather thin, and the achenes are puberulous. The colour of the young branches, and sometimes also of the leaves, is normally reddish brown. E. linifolius has more flattened, acute or acuminate leaves of a pure green colour, campanulate involucres with firm and usually distinctly nerved involucral bracts, and always glabrous achenes. A few intermediate collections are interpreted as hybrids. Some plants have the involucral bracts free from the base. In this respect they differ from one of the generally accepted generic and even subtribal characters. Previously two species with free involucral bracts have been separated as a genus of its own, viz. Lysichlamys Compt. In my opinion the discrete involucre is a derived condition within the genus. As shown elsewhere, the genus Lysichlamys must be sunk in Euryops. In the case of E. thunbergii I have found no other significant differences between plants with free involucral bracts and those with a connate involucre. The collections with free phyllaries are from Clanwilliam Div. (Lewis 2032, Barker 6579 in BOL, the same number in NBG has mostly connate involucral bracts), Worcester Div. (Barker 5928), and Malmesbury Div. (Maguire 89 p.p.; part of this collection is normal with connate phyllaries). The collection from the Giftberg (Vanrhynsdorp Div., Phillips 7424) is somewhat intermediate with some phyllaries free and others connate to various degree.
<!-- /evo:text -->
