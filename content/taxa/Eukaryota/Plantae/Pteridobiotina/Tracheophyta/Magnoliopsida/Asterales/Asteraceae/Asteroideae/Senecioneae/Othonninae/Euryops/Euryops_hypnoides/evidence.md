## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/0/text -->
A slender glabrous erect or ascending shrublet, probably c. 2-4 dm high, branching dichotomously often with unequal branches or suppression of one branch. Branches closely leafy; old branches clothed with dry leaf remains, finally denuded. Leaves very closely set, erecto-patent-spreading, 5-10 mm long, pinnatisect with 2-4 pairs of lobes; leaf-lobes mostly opposite, 2-5 mm long, erecto-patent-spreading, 0.1-0.2 mm wide near the base, filiform-subulate, evenly tapering to very fine setiform points; rachis basally c. 0.5 mm wide, flattened, midveined, tapering towards the apex; leaf-base somewhat dilated. Peduncles terminal, solitary or 2-3 together at the ramifications, sometimes seemingly lateral through the suppression of branches, erect or ascending, ± flexuous, 3-8 cm long, filiform, 0.2-0.3 mm thick, terete, somewhat thickened below the capitulum. Involucre campanulate, 4-6 mm in diam. Involucral bracts uniseriate, 8, ovate-lanceolate, c. 3 mm long, connate to the middle or slightly more, 3-lined, subacute-obtuse with minutely puberulous tips. Receptacle narrowly conical, c. 1 mm high, indistinctly alveolate. Ray-florets 5(-8), yellow. Tube cylindrical, 0.6-0.8 mm long. Lamina elliptic-oblong, 4-5 mm long, 1.8-2.2 mm wide, 4-veined. Style terete; style branches 0.2-0.3 mm long, truncate. Disc-florets c. 15-25. Corolla c. 2 mm long. Tube cylindrical, c. 0.5 mm long. Limb campanulate, c. 1.5 mm long; lobes deltoid-ovate, 0.4-0.6 mm long. Anthers c. 1 mm long incl. the narrowly ovate subacute appendage. Style terete; style branches c. 0.1 mm long with slightly convex to almost truncate tips. Pappus 0. Achenes narrowly oblong-oblong-obovate, 2.2-3 mm long, 1-1.3 mm wide, with a narrow apical annulus, light brown-honey-coloured, without distinct ribs, finely papillate with short whitish papillae.

## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
The specific epithet chosen alludes to the moss-like appearance of the plant. E. hypnoides is closely allied to E. polytrichoides but at once distinguished by its finely pinnatipartite leaves. Furthermore, the involucral bracts are more connate, and the rays are normally fewer. Like in E. polytrichoides there is a tendency towards monopodial branching. The peduncles, which in E. hypnoides usually occur two together, thus often appear in a seemingly lateral position.
<!-- /evo:text -->
