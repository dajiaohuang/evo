## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/0/text -->
Perennial herb, up to 1 m high. Leaves alternate; blade linear, narrowly elliptic to elliptic or narrowly obovate, up to 60 x 20 mm, apex acute or obtuse, mucronate, base cuneate, margins entire, silky silvery on both surfaces, concolorous; petiole 1-2 mm long. Heads discoid, few to many in terminal, corymbose panicles. Involucral bracts generally narrowly ovate, apex attenuate to a long-acuminate bristle tip or acute and bristle-tipped. Flowers: disc florets only, violet; Nov.-Apr. Fruit with cypsela turbinate to subcylindric, densely hairy. Pappus: outer of short, linear scales; inner of barbellate bristles.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
Hilliardiella aristata is the only species in the genus with a densely silvery-silky indumentum on both surfaces of the leaves, rarely slightly thinner on the upper surface, the blades mostly linear-oblanceolate to narrowly elliptic but rarely ovate or almost sub-orbicular in some specimens from Swaziland. The involucral bracts are distinctive, mostly narrowly lanceolate and long-attenuate-conduplicate or smoothly acute, with a conspicuously bristle-like apex. Both H. elaeagnoides and H. capensis have more or less discolorous leaves with the upper surface thinly silky and the lower surface densely silvery-silky, and the inner involucral bracts more or less abruptly contracted to an apiculate tip. Intermediates among all three species are known, however, and introgression seems likely where they co-occur.
<!-- /evo:text -->
