## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/0/text -->
Perennial herb, 20-200(-350) mm tall. Leaves filiform-linear, 3-lobed or pinnatipartite, sometimes entire, 10-110 mm long. Peduncles solitary. Involucral bracts connate to the middle or more. Achenes 3.5-5.0 mm long, papillate-puberulous, often only basally, sometimes glabrous. Flowers yellow.

## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
E. laxus has the same fire-resistant life-form as E. transvaalensis, producing short or elongated annual stems from a thick subterranean caudex. From the latter species E. laxus is distinguished by the longer and less coriaceous leaves with linear or filiform rachis and lobes, which are not rigid and subulate. Like many other fire-resistant species it is highly variable, especially in vegetative features. The leaves vary much in length, width, and degree of lobation. The name Gamolepis debilis has been applied to forms with small and pinnati-partite leaves with more or less filiform segments. Other forms have longer and broader, three-lobed leaves with linear, flat segments, and in some collections the leaves are quite entire. Between the extremes all kinds of transitions occur. There is also considerable variation in the floral parts, e.g., in size of capitula and florets. The pappus of E. laxus consists of very few short bristles, which sometimes may lack altogether. This feature explains the original treatment under Gamolepis, but the species takes a more natural position in sect. Chrysops.
<!-- /evo:text -->
