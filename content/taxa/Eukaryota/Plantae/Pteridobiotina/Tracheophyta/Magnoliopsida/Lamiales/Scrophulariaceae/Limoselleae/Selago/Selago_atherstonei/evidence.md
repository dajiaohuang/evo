## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
Among its close allies, the distinguishing features of S. atherstonei are its stems clad in simple acute patent hairs often underlain by minute glandular ones, short hairy fascicled leaves in which only the midrib is clearly visible, bracts scarcely longer than the calyx, calyx 2-3 mm long. In his key to the species of Selago, Rolfe (1901) distinguished S. muddiiirom S. atherstonei by 'heads solitary' versus 'heads not solitary'. The solitude of the heads depends on the age of the stem carrying the inflorescence: in young stems such as those sprouting anew from the caudex, heads are initially solitary, but subsequent branching eventually produces loosely panicled heads. Only S. procera has stems with patent hairs like those of S. atherstonei. It is easily distinguished by its much larger primary leaves with the 3-5 main veins clearly visible, its large profusely branched panicle, at least the lowermost bracts in each raceme far exceeding the calyx, and often longer calyx (3-4.4 mm). The area of S. atherstonei lies wholly within that of S. procera. Selago atherstonei is very closely allied to S. lydenburgensis with which it may hybridize.
<!-- /evo:text -->
