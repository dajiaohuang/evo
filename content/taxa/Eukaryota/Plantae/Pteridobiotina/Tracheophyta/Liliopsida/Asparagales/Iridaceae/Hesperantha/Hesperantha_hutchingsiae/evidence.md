## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/0/text -->
Perennial herb, geophyte, ± 0.3 m high; corm 8 x 8 mm, tunics not evident; stem simple, rigid, erect. Leaves 4, lower 3 basal, uppermost sheathing, unfolded, 10-22 x 1.5-2.0 mm, linear, acute, margins thickened; midrib prominent, veins smooth on both sides. Bracts green, tinged brownish, with red-purple. Inflorescence a spike; perianth 37 mm long, tube 21 x 0.5 mm, throat abruptly dilated; tepals subequal, 16 x 5.5 mm, elliptic, obtuse, rosy purple. Stamens exserted; filaments 5 mm long; anthers 7.5 mm long. Flowering time Mar.-?.

## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
One of several species of eastern southern Africa with long-tubed, pink to purple flowers, Hesperantha hutchingsiae appears to be allied to the Eastern Cape and KwaZulu-Natal species H. pulchra. It is distinguished from that species by the shorter, narrower leaves 1.5-2.0 mm wide, 2- or 3-flowered spikes, and rosy-purple flowers. Hesperantha pulchra has leaves 3.0-5.5 mm wide, and spikes with mostly 5 to 11 bright pink flowers. Hesperantha hutchingsiae also stands out in the brown cataphyll sheathing the base of the stem, and in the dark brown anthers and pollen, both unusual in the genus, although dark brown to purple anthers also occur in the pink-flowered H. grandiflora and H. stenosiphon, both of which have flowers with a substantially longer perianth tube, 40-55 mm long in H. grandiflora and 45-60 mm long in H. stenosiphon; and in H. mtamvunae, with much longer anthers 11-12 mm long.
<!-- /evo:text -->
