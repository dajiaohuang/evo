## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
With the smallest flowers in subg. Visciramosae, Moraea inconspicua is distinguished by its flowers with fully reflexed tepals, the outer mostly 12-18 mm long (rarely up to 25 mm), the limb at least twice as long as the claw. In addition the rhipidial spathes are short, usually less than 28 mm long and sometimes only 18 mm. Flower colour is variable, mostly yellow or partly to completely brown, but white-flowered morphs occur in the Biedouw Valley near Clanwilliam and on the Agulhas Plain near Elim, the latter with the smallest flowers recorded in the species. In Namaqualand flowers are sometimes ivory-coloured or pink, but also yellow or brown as elsewhere. The foliage leaves are usually straight and erect below but often trailing distally, and have channelled blades, but plants from Namaqualand have distinctive spirally coiled leaves with a plane surface and on this basis have been segregated as subsp. namaquensis. Populations from the northern Cederberg and Biedouw Valley have unusually large flowers for the species, the outer tepals up to 25 mm long, thus matching those of closely allied M. bituminosa. The narrow leaves, short style crests and almost fully reflexed tepal limbs conform to M. inconspicua. Superficially similar Moraea viscaria, is a white-flowered species with spreading tepals 15-23 mm long, a strong floral odour, and relatively well-developed style crests up to 6 mm long, half as long to ± as long as the style branches. Moraea saldanhensis may also be confused with M. inconspicua but has slate-blue flowers with the tepal claws as long as to slightly longer than the limbs, the latter usually reflexed to ± 45º.
<!-- /evo:text -->
