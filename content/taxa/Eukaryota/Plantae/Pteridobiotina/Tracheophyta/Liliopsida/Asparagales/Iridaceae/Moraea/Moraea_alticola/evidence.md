## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
A clump-forming species, Moraea alticola is distinguished by its habit and broad, more-or-less plane foliage leaf 14-15 mm wide, both features shared with M. spathulata. Despite its occurrence in an alpine area, at high elevations in the Drakensberg, it is easily the largest, most robust species in the genus. Although size alone makes M. alticola easy to recognise it is also distinctive in its uppermost cataphyll, which forms a well developed, pale, basket-like network well above the ground. Similar net-like cataphylls are found in some other species of subg. Grandiflorae but never as well developed. The pale, watery yellow flowers have rather narrow nectar guides on the outer tepals, unlike the large, yellow to orange nectar guides of most of the many forms of M. spathulata.
<!-- /evo:text -->
