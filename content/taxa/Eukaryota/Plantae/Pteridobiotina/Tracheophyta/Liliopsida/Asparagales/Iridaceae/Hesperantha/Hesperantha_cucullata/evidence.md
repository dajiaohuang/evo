## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
Distinguished by the relatively large, white flowers mostly with the outer tepals red outside and usually 12-20 x 6-10 mm, and the large asymmetric corms up to 14 mm diam. with imbricate tunics. The flowers are crepuscular, usually opening after 16:30 and then sweetly scented. Well-grown plants have at least four leaves, the lower three basal and with long, plane blades up to 10 mm wide. Populations from the Bokkeveld Mtns growing on sandstone pavement are often shorter in stature, with a slightly longer perianth tube, 10-12 mm vs. 6-10 mm, and shorter anthers, 4-5 mm vs. 6.0-8.5 mm, and perhaps merit taxonomic recognition. The style branches reach the tips of the anthers in these plants because the anthers are relatively short, whereas the style branches reach to between the base and middle of the anthers in other populations. Plants from the southern Roggeveld with spikes of fewer flowers (mostly two or three) and the outer tepals brown outside (red elsewhere), and growing in wet habitats are referred to Hesperantha palustris.
<!-- /evo:text -->
