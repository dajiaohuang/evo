## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
Recognised by the slender stem seldom reaching 1 m in height, often unbranched or at most with 1 or 2 short branches, unlike the strongly branched C. paniculata with which it may be confused. The spikes in both species are strongly flexuose, with the flowers borne in two opposed rows, facing away from one another. The flowers of C. pearsei are larger, some 60-70 mm long, with a perianth tube 40-45 mm long and a dorsal tepal ± 20 mm long, whereas typical C. paniculata has flowers with a perianth tube 33-40 mm long and a dorsal tepal 14-18 mm long. The two species occupy quite different habitats, C. paniculata being a plant of wet sites, growing in deep, rich soils at moderate elevations, unlike the high-altitude, well-drained basalt slopes and cliffs favoured by C. pearsei.
<!-- /evo:text -->
