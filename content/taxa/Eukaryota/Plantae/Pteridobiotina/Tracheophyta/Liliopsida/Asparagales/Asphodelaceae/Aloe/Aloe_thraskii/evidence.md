## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/0/text -->
Trees; stems simple, 1-2(-4) m tall. Leaves many, 1.0-1.6 x 0.13-0.22 m, deeply channelled, olive-green, upper surface without prickles, lower surface with few prickles in median line. Inflorescence with 4-8 racemes, 500-800 mm tall; racemes cylindric-conical, very dense; bracts ovate-acute, 7-9 x 4-6 mm, 5-7-nerved. Flowers greenish to orange in bud, lemon-yellow to pale orange at flowering, 18-29 mm long, ventricose; outer segments connate in basal third, inner segments free but adnate to outer in basal quarter; pedicels 1-2 mm long. Anthers exserted 4-20 mm. Ovary 5.0-6.0 x 2.0-3.5 mm, lemon to pale green; style exserted 4-20 mm. Fruit ±30 x 16 mm, reddish brown.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/2/text -->
A. thraskii is a stately and very distinctive species with its long, very deeply channelled, and much recurved leaves; and with the stem densely bearded with old dry leaf remains, it closely resembles A. recurvifolia from the Eastern Transvaal, but they could never be confused when in flower. The latter has a simple inflorescence, with long narrowly cylindric racemes, while A. thraskii is distinguished by having, on the average, 2-3 inflorescences each with 3-6 branches. The racemes are rather broadly cylindrical, up to 10 in. long, with the flowers first opening up the sunny (north) side, while the orange exserted stamens in reflected sunlight give the racemes a glowing golden effect. Sometimes one sees the inflorescences alive with bees attracted by the pollen. The flowers contain a copious supply of pale nectar.
<!-- /evo:text -->
