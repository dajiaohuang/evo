## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
Flowers of Babiana avicularis resemble those two other predominantly red-flowered species of the genus, B. hirsuta and B. ringens, except that they are almost half the size. Like B. ringens, the main axis is sterile and, as in B. ringens it serves as a perch for birds taking nectar from the flowers but their flowers differ in some important respects apart from their smaller size. Flowers of B. ringens subsp. ringens have a perianth tube 28-40 mm long, a dorsal tepal 25-45(-50) mm long, enlarged lower lateral tepals ± 20 mm long, and the upper tepals are recurved. In addition, the filaments are 37-70 mm long, the anthers 5.0-7.5 mm long, and the style divides opposite the upper half of the anthers or shortly beyond their tips. Subsp. australis has slightly smaller flowers with the dorsal tepal 18-30 mm long and filaments 24-34 mm long. In contrast, flowers of B. avicularis have a tube 16-21 mm long and distinctive in the lower cylindrical part of the tube 4 mm long and kinked below the apex. All three lower tepals are pale green and directed forward. In both species, as well as in B. hirsuta, the margins of the dorsal tepal curve inward and enclose the filaments and style with only the distal third or quarter expanded and slightly recurved. The filaments of B. avicularis are 25-32 mm long, the anthers 3.5-5.0 mm long and the style divides below the bases of the anthers. The leaves of B. ringens are lanceolate to sublinear but firm to rigid in texture with almost pungent tips, in contrast to the longer, linear, arching, softer leaves of B. avicularis The floral bracts of Babiana avicularis are notable in two respects: they are rust-coloured at the tips and prominently mucronate, and the shorter inner bracts are divided for about 3?4 of their length, with the two halves strongly divergent, whereas in both B. hirsuta and B. ringens, the inner bracts are divided in the upper third to half and do not notably diverge. The two coastal populations of B. avicularis are sympatric and co-blooming with B. hirsuta, but no hybrids have been recorded at either site.
