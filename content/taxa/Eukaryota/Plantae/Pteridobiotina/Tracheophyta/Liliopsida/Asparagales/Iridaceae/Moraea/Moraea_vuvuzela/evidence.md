## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
With the spreading, ± fringed stigmatic lobes of ser. Galaxia, Moraea vuvuzela stands out in its pale yellow flowers with brown or purplish markings at the top of the darker yellow floral cup. Most of the remaining members of the series have uniformly bright yellow flowers. The corm tunics of M. vuvuzela are ± woody with prominent, thickened longitudinal ribs only partially separated from one another by transverse fibres. The corm tunics in most other species are less woody, with the longitudinal ribs separated by transverse fibres, with the exception of M. angulata, which has unique, woody or corky tunics with wing-like longitudinal ribs.
<!-- /evo:text -->
