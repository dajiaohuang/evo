## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
Evidently allied to the two other southern African Drakensberg species that flower early in the season at which time the foliage leaves are dry or withered, with the new season’s leaves appearing on separate shoots later in the year, Hesperantha crocopsis and H. schelpeana. It is most like H. schelpeana in its fairly large flowers but differs in having a perianth tube 20-27 mm long and uniformly creamy white perianth, whereas H. schelpeana has whitish or pink flowers with the outer tepals flushed to uniformly darker pink to purple on the outside, and a perianth tube 5-10 mm long. Hesperantha crocopsis has smaller flowers, the tepals only 6-7 mm long but a perianth tube 9-14 mm long, and like H. schelpeana, the outer tepals are red to purple on the outside. Foliage leaves are lacking on flowering specimens, but plants from ridges west of Thaba Ntlenyana in Lesotho that appear to be H. altimontana have a distinctive, falcate leaf with a plane blade like no other known member of the genus.
<!-- /evo:text -->
