## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/0/text -->
Plants up to 350 mm high. Corm 8-11 mm diam.; tunics brown, ± woody, inner layers entire, outer splitting from base. Stem mostly 3 or 4(5) internodes long, with 1 or 2 branches at each node, branches slightly flexed above sheath of subtending leaf. Leaves 3 to 5, lowermost longest, inserted 10-20 mm above ground, linear, channelled, ± straight or loosely twisted, up to 450 mm long, upper leaves progressively shorter, none entirely sheathing. Rhipidial spathes: inner 35-40 mm long, ± truncate, apex brown, somewhat lacerated, outer 1/2-2/3 as long, with acute, brown apex. Flowers buff-yellow, lightly brown-veined, outer tepals darkly veined abaxially, limbs with deep yellow nectar guides at bases edged brown, spreading slightly below horizontal, inner tepals with reddish brown spot at bases of limbs, similarly spreading, outer tepals 30-32 mm long, limbs 20-22 x 10-12 mm, claws ± 10 mm long, inner tepals oblanceolate, ± 22 x 5-6 mm. Filaments 8-9 mm long, united in lower 5-6 mm, diverging distally; anthers dark purple, 3.5-5.0 mm long; pollen red. Ovary exserted, oblong-elliptic, 5-6 mm long; style branches ± 10 mm long, crests 10-14 mm long, erect, narrowly wedge-shaped. Capsules narrowly obovoid, 9-11 mm long. Seeds softly angular, ± 1 mm long, with rugulo-reticulate surface, light yellow-brown.

## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
One of few species of subg. Umbellatae with leaves basal and cauline rather than crowded at the first aerial node, Moraea orthrosantha has a flower typical of the genus, with well-developed style branches and crests and nectar guides only on the outer tepals. The flowers are dull yellow with conspicuous dark veins and closely resemble those of M. margaretae. As the specific epithet suggests, flowers open soon after sunrise and collapse about noon (Greek, orthros = morning, anthos = flower). The flowers of most species of subg. Umbellatae open in the mid- to late morning and wilt and collapse in the late afternoon, and only a few other examples of morning flowering phenology are known in Moraea, notably in M. vlokii and three Namaqualand species of subg. Homeria, M. flexicaulis, M. knersvlaktensis and M. schlechteri. Records for M. margaretae, with which M. orthrosantha is most easily confused, indicate flowers open midmorning and collapse toward sunset.
<!-- /evo:text -->
