## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/0/text -->
Plants 90-150 mm high, with short membranous sheathing cataphyll. Corm broadly ovoid, flattened on one side in lower 1/3, 8-10 mm diam.; tunics woody, dark brown, notched below into sections. Stem erect, simple. Leaves 3, lower 2 basal, lanceolate to falcate, up to 50 mm long, mostly 3-6 mm wide, firm, margins and main veins slightly thickened, appearing raised when dry, uppermost leaf subbasal, partly sheathing stem, free in upper half. Spike lax, 1- to 3-flowered, spaced 9-12 mm apart; bracts green with membranous upper margins, outer ± 10 mm long, inner slightly shorter. Flowers crepuscular, salver-shaped, white, reverse of outer tepals mottled grey-brown along midline, opening after 19:00, tepals then outspread, sweetly scented; perianth tube cylindric, ± 12 mm long, well exserted from bracts; tepals subequal, elliptic, ± 11 x 5 mm. Filaments erect, ± 2 mm long; anthers held horizontally, ± 5 mm long, white; pollen white. Style branches ± 4 mm long, ascending, reaching to ± middle of anthers in closed flower. Capsules ± ovoid-elliptic, obtuse, smooth, ± 11 mm long. Seeds unknown.

## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
Small and inconspicuous Hesperantha filiformis stands up to 150 mm high and has lax spikes of 1 to 3 small flowers with a particularly slender perianth tube about 12 mm long and tepals about 11 x 5 mm. Plants consistently have only three leaves, the lower two basal and the uppermost inserted in the lower third of the stem and sheathing for about half its length. The white flowers are closed during daylight hours so that only the dull grey-brown reverse of the outer tepals is then visible. The flowers open after dark between 19:00 and 19:30 and then have a mild, sweet odour. The ovoid corms are slightly asymmetric, being flattened on one side, with the woody, dark brown tunics typical of sect. Concentricae. Its relationships are uncertain but possibly lie with the Namaqualand species H. flexuosa, which also consistently has three leaves, the uppermost partly sheathing the stem, but the somewhat fleshy leaves of this species are linear and no more than 1.5 mm wide. The firm-textured leaves of H. filiformis are 3-5 mm wide and have thickened margins and main vein, very different from those of H. flexuosa. Capsules of H. filiformis are surprisingly long for so small a plant, 10-11 mm long, but seeds are so far unknown.
<!-- /evo:text -->
