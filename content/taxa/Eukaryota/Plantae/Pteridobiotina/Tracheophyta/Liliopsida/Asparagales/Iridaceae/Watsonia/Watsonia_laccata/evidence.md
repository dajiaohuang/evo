## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
A relatively short species with funnel-shaped, white or pale pink to mauve or rarely orange flowers with declinate stamens and short, pale yellow (rarely purple) anthers 4-5 mm long. The broad-based bracts, pale and dry above with netted venation, and the fusiform capsules are also distinctive. The flowers are mostly pink to mauve (rarely orange near Heidelberg) and broadly funnel-shaped with the upper part of the perianth tube flared and 7-10 mm long but up to 15 mm long in some populations. Populations in the upper Breede River Valley between Montagu and Worcester represent a distinct variant with consistently bright orange, narrowly funnel-shaped flowers with the upper part of the perianth tube scarcely or only weakly flared and 15-25 mm long, thus ± 1.0-1.5 times as long as the tepals, and often purple anthers. Some of the longer-tubed forms may also indicate introgression with that species. Watsonia aletroides has ± tubular flowers with the tepals less than half as long as the upper part of the perianth tube. Both species have similar bracts and unusual, fusiform capsules.
<!-- /evo:text -->
