## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
A large, polymorphic species in which four largely interfertile varieties are recognized, with some inter-mediates between the varieties. The flower colouring, which varies from deep old-rose to yellow, without any bluish tints, distinguishes this species from R. rosea; also the recurved flexuose peduncles and the 2n chromosome number of 22.

## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/2/text -->
Distinguished from Romulea rosea by the somatic chromosome number 2n = 22 (2n = 18 in R. rosea), the presence of some degree of yellow pigmentation in the tepals (distal to the cup), the flowers ranging from yellow through apricot-, terracotta-coloured and salmon-pink to rosy-pink, with a greenish yellow to orange-yellow cup, and the fruiting peduncles remaining widely spreading on drying. Flowering plants without careful colour notes are impossible to assign between the two species with any accuracy. Yellow-flowered forms resemble R. monticola from the Bokkeveld and Matsikamma-Gifberg Mtns, but the flowers are duller in hue with proportionally longer outer bracts more than half as long as the flowers, inner bracts with membranous margins distinctly narrower than the central green portion, and peduncles widely spreading in fruit. Romulea obscura is best known historically from the West Coast between Saldanha and the Cape Peninsula but is now less often encountered as a result of increasing urbanisation. The species is polymorphic for flower size, colour and markings.
<!-- /evo:text -->
