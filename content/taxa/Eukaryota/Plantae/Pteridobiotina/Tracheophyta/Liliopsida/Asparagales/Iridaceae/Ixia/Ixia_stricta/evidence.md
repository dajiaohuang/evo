## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
While not particularly distinctive in its radially symmetric flowers with short, erect stamens, Ixia stricta is identified in ser. Dichone by a combination of the rotate flowers and a basal fan of (3)4 or 5 sword-shaped leaves, the blades with a prominent main vein and thickened margins. The sweetly scented flowers have a filiform tube about half as long as the tepals and a particularly short style ± 1 mm long. Blooming late in the season, in November and December, the leaves are often dry or partly so, a feature unusual in the genus. Raimondo et al. (2009) suggest a conservation status of (LC), least concern, but (NT), near threatened, is suggested to be more appropriate.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/2/text -->
Typically flowering late in the year, in November and December, the foliage of Ixia stricta is often more-or-less dry at flowering. Not particularly distinctive, the pink flowers are radially symmetric and have short, erect stamens. It is identified in ser. Dichone by a combination of the rotate flowers and a basal fan of (3)4 or 5 sword-shaped leaves with a prominent main vein and thickened margins. The sweetly scented flowers have a filiform tube about half as long as the tepals and particularly short style branches ± 1.5 mm long.
<!-- /evo:text -->
