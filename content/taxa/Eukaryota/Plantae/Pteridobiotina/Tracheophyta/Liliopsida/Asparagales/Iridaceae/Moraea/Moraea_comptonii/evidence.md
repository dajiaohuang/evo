## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
Large-flowered Moraea comptonii is distinguished by the fiddle-shaped to spathulate tepals weakly divided into claw and limb, with the lower halves forming an open cup and upper halves spreading. The flowers are particularly strongly and sweetly scented and either entirely yellow, or salmon-pink to orange with a yellow cup, often with dark green markings in the lower third. Like its close relative M. elegans, plants have a solitary leaf, a partly to fully included ovary, and the inner rhipidial spathes become enlarged to enclose the developing capsules. Also like M. elegans, the filaments are fully united in a column tapering from a wide base but the anthers are usually shorter than the style branches in contrast to M. elegans, in which the anthers exceed the style branches. Another difference between the two is that the tepals of M. elegans are more-or-less ovate, widest in the lower third, and the tepal markings are confined to the outer tepals in the upper third, whereas tepals of M. comptonii are widest in the distal third and are either unmarked or have markings at the bases of the tepal limbs of both whorls.
<!-- /evo:text -->
