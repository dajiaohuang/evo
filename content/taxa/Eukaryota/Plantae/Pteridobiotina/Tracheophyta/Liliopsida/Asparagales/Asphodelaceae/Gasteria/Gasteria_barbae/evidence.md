## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/0/text -->
Plants acaulescent, decumbent, 80-120 mm tall, up to 80-240 mm diam., solitary or proliferating from base and cluster-forming (3-5 heads). Roots succulent, 3-4 mm diam. Leaves (8-12 per rosette) rosulate, linear-lanceolate, linear or deltoid-lanceolate to 120-170 mm long, 22-38 mm broad at base, slightly falcate to falcate and ascending spreading, sometimes becoming patent; adaxial surface flat to becoming canaliculate, plane towards apex, matt dark green to greyish-green (becoming reddish during the dry season), densely white-tuberculate in obscure transverse bands; abaxial surface somewhat convex with a distinct eccentric keel, densely tuberculate, tubercles arranged in obscure transverse bands; margin denticulate, the tubercles 1-1.5 mm high, apex acute or subacute, sometimes acuminate, mucronate. Juvenile leaves distichous, lorate, patent to ascending; epidermis white-tuberculate; apex obtuse, mucronate. Inflorescence racemose, the raceme about 270 mm long, bearing 18-26 secundly arranged flowers (up to 2-6 flowers open at the same time). Perianth oblong, 35-38 mm long, stipitate for 4 mm; segments fused for margins of the inner 3 segments), sub-cylindrical, curved in the upper third (and with a slight constriction). Lower and upper half inflated to about 5-6 mm in diameter, orange-pink, upper half white with green striations; apices erect becoming erectly spreading, obtuse; margins of inner segments free and channelled for about 12 mm, diverging gradually towards apex. Stamens 28 mm long; anthers about 2 x 1 mm long, included or shortly exserted. Ovary oblong, 7 x 2.5 mm, green; style 25 mm long, stigma included or shortly exserted, curved upwards, minute. Capsule 18-30 mm long, narrow obovate, triangular in cross-section, obtuse at apex, 7-8 mm diam. Seeds 3.5-4 x 1.5-2 mm, black. Flowering time: November (late spring).

## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
Differs from G. batesiana by its ascending spreading densely white-tuberculate leaves 120-170 x 22-38 mm, and denticulate margin with tubercles 1-1.5 mm. Gasteria barbae grows prolifically from the base, solitary or forming small clusters. It is at once distinguished from G. batesiana by its matt grey-green ascending, patent leaves and different flowers. The leaves of Gasteria barbae are very brittle and detached leaves are commonly found in habitat, most of which form roots and new plantlets, a vegetative propagation ‘back-up’ commonly associated with cliff-dwelling succulent plants. To the east and west of our new species, Gasteria acinacifolia, a much larger related species with smooth adult leaves and a spreading paniculate inflorescence, grows mostly associated with sandy dune vegetation. To the north, Gasteria polita, another Fynbos species, grows on steep hilly terrain bearing smooth leaves and shorter flowers.

## ecology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/ecology/claims/0/text -->
The rosettes of Gasteria barbae grow in crevices and on humus-rich ledges of mineral-poor acidic quartzitic sandstone rocks of the Table Mountain Series (Cape Supergroup) in dry Fynbos often in partial shade. Gasteria barbae has been recorded from a number of sites, always associated with steep coastal cliffs and north and north-west facing aspects. The vegetation consists of South Outeniqua Sandstone Fynbos. Adjacent and further north the habitat is fringed by dense Southern Coastal Forest. The cremnophilous habitat of Gasteria barbae is shared with associated succulent plants such as Aloe arborescens, Litanthus pusillus (Drimia uniflora), Crassula perforata, C. nudicaulis, C. rubricaulis, C. lanceolata, Curio crassulifolius (Senecio crassulaefolius) and Bulbine latifolia. Non-succulent associated plants include Erica speciosa and Polygala myrtifolia and Phylica sp.
<!-- /evo:text -->
