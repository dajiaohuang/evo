## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
A striking species in bloom, Ixia gloriosa has a bright magenta perianth with a small, glossy, almost black centre, and purple-black stamens and style branches. The leaves are particularly narrow, typically 2-3 mm wide and the corm tunics are composed of fine fibres. The species is also distinctive in having up to three short branches held close to the main axis, each usually bearing fewer blooms than the 4 to 6 on the main spike. Plants give the illusion of having a particularly long, lax spike with flowers scattered along its entire length but some of the open flowers are borne on the branches. The pale floral bracts, ± 6 mm long, have prominent purple veins, the outer with three short, acute teeth. When described by Lewis (1962), I. gloriosa included I. superba and the original description is an amalgam of the two species. The anthers, 5-7 mm long and slightly more than twice as long as the filaments, were originally described as connivent at the tips, but there is no evidence of this in available preserved material. The style divides at or just above the bases of the filaments and style branches are unusually long, reaching up to 5 mm and exceeding the shorter filaments. Among other differences, I. superba has a longer style, dividing above the bases of the anthers, but shorter style branches 2-3 mm long.
<!-- /evo:text -->
