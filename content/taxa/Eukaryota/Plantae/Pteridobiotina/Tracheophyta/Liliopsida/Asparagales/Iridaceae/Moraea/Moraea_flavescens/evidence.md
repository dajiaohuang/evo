## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
Relatively poorly documented, Moraea flavescens is rather variable as circumscribed at present. Plants in the south have smaller flowers than those in the north of its range and specimens from this area bear the manuscript name H. klawerensis L.Bolus. The species is characterised by its pale, watery yellow flowers with a wide tepal cup and emarginate outer tepals, and flat style branches shorter than the anthers. Populations from the southern Cederberg have the unusual diploid number of 2n = 9, and form complex heterozygotes at meiosis. Moraea flavescens is closely related to a second Cederberg-Gifberg endemic, M. demissa, which also has unusual chromosome cytology with diploid numbers of 2n = 10, 9 or 8. Moraea demissa has smaller flowers, also pale yellow, but the tepal claws are suberect and form a narrow cup around the lower half of the filament column. Flowers of M. flavescens open in the morning while those of M. demissa open after midday and last through the late afternoon.
<!-- /evo:text -->
