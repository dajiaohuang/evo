## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/0/text -->
Plants 3-15 cm tall, sometimes clumped. Corm somewhat depressed-ovoid, 7-19 mm diam., covered with hard, brown, fibrous tunics; fibres unbranched proximally, firmly attached to rim of basal disc, reticulate distally, often extended into slightly outspread tips up to 10(-25) mm long; roots emerging from proximal half of corm. Cataphylls absent at flowering. Leaves 3-11, sheathing at base up to 4 cm, spreading to recurved, linear, 10-115(-120) x 1-7 mm, tapering evenly upwards, canaliculate, shiny green, firm-textured, margin often with minute, widely spaced, recurved teeth. Inflorescences up to 4 in flower at a time, 1-flowered, clustered amongst leaves; scape hidden by leaf sheaths, up to 10 x 1 mm, laterally compressed, whitish; bract 1, linear, inserted at ovary base, ca. 10-24 x 0.5-1.2 mm, whitish, thin-textured. Flower sessile, partly subterranean, more or less rotate, white with yellow centre, backed with pink to maroon distally on outer tepals, sometimes scented; perigone tube arising from a slender, elongated, ovary beak, bowl-shaped, 3.0-5.5 x 3.0-3.5 mm; tepals 6, oblong-lanceolate, 10-18(-30) mm long, outer 3.5-10.0 mm wide, minutely mucronate, inner 3.0-8.0 mm wide. Stamens 6, suberect, subequal, yellow; filaments inserted near perigone tube base, 3.0-3.5 mm long, usually shorter than anthers; anthers linear, latrorse, (2.5-)3.0-5.0(-7.0) x 0.7 mm, basal lobes up to 0.5 mm long; pollen yellow. Ovary subterranean, narrowly ovoid, compressed ventrally, ca. 8 x 2.5 mm, 3-locular, distal beak 18-70 x 1 mm; style 2.5-4.0 mm long; stigma branches erect to spreading, linear-oblong, 2.5-5.0(-11.0) x 0.5-0.7 mm, ca. equalling or exceeding stamens, yellow, densely papillose. Capsule shortly exserted above ground, narrowly ovoid, ca. 9 x 3 mm, beaked, walls thin, disintegrating irregularly. Seeds somewhat globose, 0.93 x 0.82 mm; testa brownish black, densely covered with pliable, blunt, elongated trichomes.

## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
Pauridia alticola is distinguished by a solitary-flowered inflorescence held close to the ground within a tuft of leaves. Specialized characters contributing to this compact habit are the shortened scape and sessile flower in which the subterranean ovary tapers into a long beak that raises the perigone clear of the ground. The exposed rotate perigone has a short, yellow, bowl-shaped tube and six, outspread white tepals, those of the outer whorl attractively backed with pink. P. alticola is distinguished from the closely related P. verna by larger, heavily fibrous corms, often slightly larger flowers, the style which is shorter than the stigma branches, and the dense covering of elongated trichomes on the seeds. The partly subterranean flower accounts for this species having first been assigned to Forbesia Eckl. ex Nel, known more recently as Empodium. Subsequently the species was transferred to Saniella Burtt, a genus of just two species. In doing so, Burtt nevertheless conceded that the separation of Saniella from Spiloxene and Pauridia may not be justified. Confirmation of this has since come from the molecular based phylogenetic analysis of Kocyan et al. which resolved that Saniella is nested within an expanded Pauridia.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## ecology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/ecology/claims/0/text -->
On the Hantamsberg and Roggeveld the plants are restricted to seasonally inundated summit plateaus in doleritic soils, whereas in the Cederberg they occupy shale bands. Records indicate that the plants once occupied clay-loamy soils of the high-lying valleys in the Kouebokkeveld, but most of these habitats have been replaced by dams and orchards.
<!-- /evo:text -->
