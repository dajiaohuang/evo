## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
He most common and widespread of the three species of sect. Trigella, Cyanella orchidiformis is recognized by its lanceolate leaves, 10-25 mm wide, and racemes of pink to mauve flowers, mostly darker or patterned toward the centre, with the anthers partially or almost wholly greyish or purple, and large, ovoid-ellipsoid fruits, 14-25 mm long. The three species are essentially parapatric or allopatric, although both C. cygnea and C. orchidiformis have been collected near Steinkopf. An exceptionally large-flowered variant with tepals 20 x 6-7 mm has been collected on the Gifberg Pass, growing in sandstone soil after fire and may be polyploid.Cyanella orchidiformis is closely allied to C. cygnea, with which it shares the distinctive large fruits, patterened perianth and coloured anthers, but from which it is distinguished by its generally larger flowers, with tepals mostly 10-13 mm long vs 8-10 mm long, and its unexceptional stamens. The upper filaments in C. orchidiformis are arcuate or weakly geniculate, without a bulbous base and not evidently filiform in the distal half, and the lower anthers are relatively large, 5-6 mm long. The style is very variable in length, mostly 5-10 mm long but occasionally up to 15 mm long. In contrast, C. cygnea has mostly smaller flowers, with tepals 8-10 mm long, and very distinctive stamens, with the upper filaments geniculately sigmoid and sharply narrowed and filiform in the distal half, and with much smaller lower anthers, 2.5-3.0 mm long, and a short style 3-4 mm long. The range of C. orchidiformis is largely to the south and east of C. cygnea but both species have been collected near Steinkopf.The large capsules readily distinguish C. orchidiformis from C. ramosissima, which has subglobose-ovoid fruits 8-10 mm long, but non fruiting specimens are separated by the combination of broader, lanceolate leaves, 10-30 mm vs 8-10 mm wide, and mostly shorter styles, 5-10 mm long vs 10-15 mm long. Occasional forms with longer styles are identified by their foliage and by their partially greyish or purple anthers.
