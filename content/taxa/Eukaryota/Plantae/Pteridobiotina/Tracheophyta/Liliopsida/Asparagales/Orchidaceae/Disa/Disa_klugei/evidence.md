## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/0/text -->
Slender terrestrial, (200-)260(-320) mm tall. Leaves cauline, spreading, up to 90 mm long, upper surface texture matt, blade applanate apically, grading into floral bracts; bracts dry back from apex, longer than the ovaries. Inflorescence laxly 12-26-flowered. Flowers white, partially suffused pale to deep pink. Lateral sepals 8 mm long, spreading, lime- yellow, irregularly blotched vivid red-purple. Petals white, sparsely speckled and blotched red-purple. Lip 8 mm long, white, smudged raspberry red basally and randomly blotched vivid red-purple apically. Spur 9-11 mm long, decurved from ± middle, slightly dorsiventrally flattened at base becoming terete, apex rounded.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
The rather similar flower colours of D. klugei and D. vigilans may cause confusion, but they are easily separated. D. klugei is undoubtedly closely allied to D. patula var. transvaalensis, but various characters in combination separate them. D. klugei, flower colour: white & lime-yellow, blotched red-purple; leaves: spreading applanate, soft; spur 9-11 mm long, decurved; plant: ± 260 mm tall, rare, restricted distribution. D. patula var. transvaalensis, flower colour: shades of pink, dotted lilac; leaves: spreading conduplicate, firm; spur 9-12 mm long, straight; ± 410 mm tall, common, widespread.

## ecology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/ecology/claims/0/text -->
D. klugei is a newly described species and, although not inconspicuous in its rocky habitat of short, sour grassland and forbs, easily overlooked. Occurring in small, scattered colonies of four to 10 individuals on mountain slopes in the mistbelt with good drainage and reliable rainfall. Associated taxa include Rabdosiella calycina, Erica drakensbergensis, Watsonia wilmsii, and the grasses Diheteropogon filifolius, Rendlia altera and Alloteropsis semialata. No fragrance detected.
