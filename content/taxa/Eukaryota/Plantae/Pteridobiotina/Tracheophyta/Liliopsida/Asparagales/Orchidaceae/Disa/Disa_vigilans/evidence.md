## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/0/text -->
Erect terrestrial herb, 160-280 mm tall. Leaves 7-9, cauline, erect or slightly spreading, rigid, linear-lanceolate, 50-90 x 2-4 mm, with 3 main veins, margins thickened and translucent. Inflorescence lax, cylindrical, 40-75 mm long; bracts light green suffused pinkish with darker green veins, linear-lanceolate, acuminate, 11-29 x 2-3 mm, scarious at anthesis. Flowers white suffused with carmine- pink and green, blotched and speckled maroon; lateral sepals very pale green to white, blotched maroon, with green median veins, patent, oblanceolate, apiculate, 11-14 x 3.0-3.5 mm; dorsal sepal narrowly galeate, apiculate, margin weakly revolute, 10-14 x 5-7 mm, spur spring-green and blotched maroon, straight, terete, 12-16 x 0.75-1.0 mm, nectariferous; lateral petals translucent white with maroon markings along distal margin, erect, narrowly ovate, ±5 x 3 mm, tapered, apex curved within the galea, 1.5 mm broad, basal anticose lobe small, apex pustulate-erose; lip white, distally maroon blotched, narrowly oblanceolate, slightly decurved, 10-14 x 3 mm. Anther reflexed, 2 mm long, deep blue-maroon basally and spring-green at the apex. Stigma white with a pink margin, 1.5 x 1.0 mm; rostellum as broad as the stigma, flat, lateral lobes short, raised at 45°, central lobe vestigial. Ovary obliquely patent, narrowly oblong, 12-18 x 2-3 mm.

## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
This species was initially considered a geographic colour form of Disa montana, a rare high altitude species of the Eastern Cape which is allopatric from D. vigilans. D. montana differs in its larger stature, broader leaves (±6 mm vs 2-4 mm broad), longer ovaries (20-26 mm vs 12-18 mm) and much broader perianth segments, the dorsal sepals in particular. In D. montana the dorsal sepals are flattened and usually orbicular, 10-18 x 9- 16 mm, whereas those of D. vigilans are oblong, galeate, and 10-14 x 5-7 mm. The species also differ in the size and shape of the lip. In D. montana the lip is ovate-acuminate, oblong or rounded and 10-15 x 3-5 mm. In D. vigilans the lip is oblan- ceolate, broadest in the distal third, and 10-14 x 3 mm. In D. montana the lateral sepals are broader, 12-17 x 4.0-6.5 mm. In D. vigilans the lateral sepals and droop are obliquely patent and narrow, 11-14 x 3.0-3.5 mm. D. vigilans is sympatric with its close ally Disa amoena. Both are very narrow endemics limited to the Mt. Mauch-Mt. Anderson area, although the latter is locally fre quent. D. amoena is a larger species 300-600 mm tall with longer leaves and larger inflorescences. The flowers are deep pink in D. amoena and all the segments are larger (dorsal sepals 15-20 x 6-9 mm vs 10-14 x 4-7 mm; lateral sepals 13-20 x 6-10 mm vs 11-14 x 3.0-3.5 mm; lip 15- 18 x 3-4 mm vs 10-14 x 3 mm). The spur of D. amoena is also considerably longer (25-35 mm vs 12-16 mm).
