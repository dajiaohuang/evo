## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
Varying in the length of the stem, number of basal leaves, flower colour, and length of the style but generally recognised by the lavender-blue (rarely white) flowers with orange or golden-yellow cup extending onto the basal half of the tepals, mostly solitary basal leaf, and often somewhat submembranous inner bracts with brown-flecked membranous margins. The species is closely allied to Romulea leipoldtii, which is distinguished by its invariably two basal leaves, generally larger, white or cream-coloured flowers with tepals 18-35 x 5-8 mm, the outer tepals not marked on the reverse, and inner bracts that are green and leathery in the centre. Specimens with two basal leaves may also be confused with Romulea schlechteri from drier situations, which has the yellow pigment restricted to the cup itself and not extending onto the lower half of the tepals, and inner bracts without brown flecks on the membranous margins.
<!-- /evo:text -->
