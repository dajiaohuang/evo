## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
Broadly resembling the more common Moraea miniata, M. karooica may differ in leaf number, usually single (rarely two or three) vs. two or three in M. miniata, but more fundamentally in the flowers. Moraea karooica has ascending tepal claws that together form a shallow cup and a cylindric filament column, whereas in M. miniata the short tepal claws clasp the base of the bulbous filament column. Moraea karooica may be most closely allied to the rare M. fenestrata of the Tanqua Basin, which has a similar habit but consistently two or more leaves, and tepals conspicuously narrowed at the base, thus usually forming a windowed cup. Populations of M. karooica with more than one leaf are from the Koedoes Mtns and Verlatenkloof near Sutherland.
<!-- /evo:text -->
