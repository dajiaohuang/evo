## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
Recognised by the solitary foliage leaf sheathing the lower half of the stem, short lateral branches borne in the upper half of the stem, and pink or yellow, star-like flowers with short tepal claws and spreading tepal limbs. The filament column is swollen and bulbous just above the narrow base and tapers in the upper third to a narrow apex. The tepal claws, ± 2 mm long, are erect and clasp the bulbous part of the filament column. The flowers are virtually identical to those of the widespread M. miniata but the latter is very different vegetatively, having at least two and often three or four foliage leaves, not clasping the stem for any distance. The two species can occasionally be found growing nearby but no intermediates are known and experiments have shown they cannot be crossed. The distinctive vegetative form of M. bifida is shared with M. fragrans but the flowers are rather different, the latter with tepal claws diverging from the base to form a shallow cup. Hybrids have been found in the wild between these two species in the Nieuwoudtville area where the two are common though confined to different soils.
<!-- /evo:text -->
