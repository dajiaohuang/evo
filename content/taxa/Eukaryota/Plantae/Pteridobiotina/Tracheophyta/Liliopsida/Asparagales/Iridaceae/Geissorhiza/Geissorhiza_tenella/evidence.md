## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
Distinguished by the white or pale pink flowers with unusually narrow tepals 13-23 x 2.0-4.5 mm, Geissorhiza tenella has a perianth tube remarkably variable in length, mostly 30-40 mm long but as short as 20 mm and occasionally as long as 50 mm. The leaf blades are H-shaped in cross section with the margins raised into broad wings held at right angles to the blade. It is allied to the long-tubed Atlantic Coast species G. exscapa and to the Cape Peninsula endemic G. bonaspei, with which it shares the distinctive leaf with winged margins and H-shaped in cross section. Geissorhiza bonaspei has slightly larger, deep pink flowers with relatively broad tepals mostly 24-27 x 5-7 mm. Flower size is unusually variable in G. tenella. Plants from the east of its range, in the Caledon and Bredasdorp Districts as far east as Still Bay (as well as some from the Cape Flats), have a relatively short perianth tube, some 24-30 mm long and tepals 12-15 mm long. In contrast, most populations from the Cape Flats and along the Atlantic Coast usually have much larger flowers with a perianth tube 40-50 mm long and tepals up to 23 mm long. Such specimens approach G. exscapa in their flower size and this is probably related to their pollination biology.
<!-- /evo:text -->
