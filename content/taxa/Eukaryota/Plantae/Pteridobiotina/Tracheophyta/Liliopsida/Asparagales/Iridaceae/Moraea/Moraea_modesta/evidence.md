## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
Readily recognised by its small, predominantly white flowers with outer tepals up to 27 mm long, and dark purple style branches and crests, Moraea modesta is a low-growing species, seldom reaching more than 200 mm in height. The vestigial inner tepals are 5-6 mm long, either hair-like cusps or obscurely 3-lobed at the apex. It is one of several species that flower after the foliage leaf, produced the previous summer, is dry and dead. The leaf is sometimes retained at flowering, still attached to the base of the flowering stem but more often has decayed and is no longer evident. A new, emergent leaf is sometimes present at flowering, extending well above the stem later in the growing season. Populations from near Machadodorp in central Mpumalanga have more-or-less free filaments, unusual for the species, and require additional study. In other populations the filaments are united for at least half their length.
<!-- /evo:text -->
