## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/0/text -->
Plants 300-500 mm high. Corm globose, 12-16 mm diam. bearing long horizontal stolons ± 1.5 mm diam., up to 100 mm long, each bearing a large terminal cormlet ± 6 mm diam.; tunics fibrous, mostly oriented vertically, sometimes accumulating. Stem usually unbranched, robust plants sometimes with a node in upper 1/3 bearing a dry, attenuate scale, sheathing only at base, often recurved. Leaves mostly 4, lanceolate, somewhat fleshy and soft-textured, ± 1/2 as long as stem, 8-15 mm wide, uppermost sheathing lower 1/2 of stem, free distally, fleshy and soft-textured. Spike congested, 3- to 7-flowered; bracts brown above, green or becoming white to pale grey below, outer 9-12 mm long, with short acute central tooth and rounded lateral lobes, only central vein developed, inner 2-veined, shortly forked at apex, as long or slightly shorter than outer. Flowers salver-shaped, pale yellow with dark brown to black, shallowly concave centre, unscented; perianth tube 10-13 mm long; tepals ovate, subequal or inner slightly smaller, 21-30 x 8-15 mm. Filaments 3.5-5.0 mm long, united in lower 1/3 (rarely only at base), ± brown below, pale blue or yellow in upper 1/2; anthers ascending, 8-11 mm long, yellow; pollen yellow. Style dividing opposite base of anthers, branches 2.5-4.5 mm long.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
Recognised by the pale yellow perianth with a large, solid dark brown to black centre, the dark portion concave, partly united filaments and relatively broad, soft-textured leaves 8-15 mm wide. It shares with orange-flowered I. calendulacea corms that produce long, ribbon-like stolons, each bearing a single large cormlet. Closely related I. maculata and I. calendulacea also have filaments partly united and similar dry floral bracts rust-brown in the upper half. The differences between these three species are subtle and poorly preserved specimens lacking corms and their stolons can be difficult to distinguish. When seen alive they are unmistakeable as their flowers differ in colour and shape, as well as in leaf shape and texture. The stolons in I. maculata are short, branched and bear several small cormlets.
<!-- /evo:text -->
