## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/0/text -->
Stemless, cormous geophyte, up to 50 mm tall. Leaves lanceolate, horizontal, held at right angles to the sheaths, scarcely pleated, smooth, prominently 3- or 4-ribbed. Flowers 1-5 in a decumbent spike, bilabiate, lilac and yellow, perianth tube ± 25 mm long, about as long as the dorsal tepal, ovary smooth, bracts smooth, the inner forked apically.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
Easily recognised by the unusual leaves, borne on pseudopetioles sometimes up to 30 mm long, with the expanded, lanceolate to oblong blades prominently 3- or 4-ribbed, scarcely pleated, and held horizontally at right angles to the sheaths. The flowers typically have lilac outer and yellow inner tepals but may also be entirely yellow. The immediate relationships of B. horizontalis are uncertain, but it seems to be allied to the several dwarf, acaulescent species of Namaqualand, all of which have a smooth ovary and similar inner bracts forked at the apex.
<!-- /evo:text -->
