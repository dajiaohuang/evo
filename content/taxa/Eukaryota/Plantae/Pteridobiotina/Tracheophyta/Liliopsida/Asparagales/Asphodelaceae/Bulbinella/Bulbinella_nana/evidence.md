## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
This is the smallest of the Bulbinella species, forming dainty, delicate-looking plants. It has close affinities with B. gracilis, which occurs in a similar area, but is separated from that species by the more numerous and very fine filiform leaves compared with the more succulent ones of B. gracilis. It also has more prominent basal sheath fibres and distinct veining in the cataphylls which is not so obvious in B. gracilis. The plants from Ratelpoort were at first thought to be a dwarfed form of B. divaginata as the fusiform roots and numerous filiform leaves are closer to that species. A few seeds formed in cultivation were markedly similar to the distinctive seeds of B. divaginata. However, seeing collections of the three species in flower together, it was noticed that B. nana had the patent pedicels of B. gracilis after the flowers had faded.
<!-- /evo:text -->
