## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
Recognised by the dense spike of small flowers with overlapping bracts ± two internodes long and the basal fan of tough, fibrous leaves with the margins and midrib plus one or more pairs of secondary veins thickened and hyaline. The flowers vary greatly in colour but are most frequently pink to salmon or dull purplish with darker nectar guides highlighted with yellow. Plants with orange to coral-pink flowers occur locally in Mpumalanga. The species is often confused with Gladiolus densiflorus, which occurs mainly to the east and at lower elevations, but their distribution overlaps along the Mpumalanga Escarpment, where G. crassifolius typically flowers slightly later. The flowers in the two species are very similar, although the perianth in G. densiflorus is sometimes finely speckled, but the two are most reliably separated by their foliage: the leaves in G. densiflorus are characterised by numerous, fine, closely spaced tertiary veins uninterrupted by evident secondary veins between the margins and main vein. Some plants may, however, be difficult to place. Gladiolus serpenticola from near Barberton can also be confused with G. crassifolius but is typically a taller plant with erect stems 750-1500 mm high, less fibrous leaves, and mostly shorter bracts 12-16 mm long.
<!-- /evo:text -->
