## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/0/text -->
Perennial herb, geophyte, 0.4-0.8 m high; corm with concentric tunics; stem simple, erect, straight. Leaves 4, lowermost basal, uppermost inserted on stem, reduced to sheathing bracts. Bracts up to 36 mm long. Inflorescence a 5-11-flowered spike, straight or slightly flexuose; flowers bright pink, 28-52 mm long; perianth tube straight, dilated in throat, 14-25 mm long; tepals subequal, elliptic, obtuse or subacute. Stamens exserted; filaments 4-6 mm long; anthers 7-11 mm long. Flowering time Nov.-Apr. Capsule oblong.

## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
A late-flowering species, Hesperantha pulchra is recognised by the bright pink flowers with a perianth tube of intermediate length, mostly 15-20 mm long (14-28 mm), and relatively short filaments, 3-6 mm long. Plants from KwaZulu-Natal, north of the Tugela River, with a tube 15 mm long (Gerstner 6713, PRE) and ± 12 mm (Fakude 3, NH) fit uncomfortably in this otherwise Eastern Cape species. Plants from Mt Thomas, near Stutterheim in Eastern Cape (McMaster s.n., NBG), collected in later flower on 11 February, with ripe capsules at the base of the spike, resemble H. pulchra except for the early flowering. These plants have narrowly elliptic capsules 25 mm long that contain large seeds, up to 4 mm long with the wings at either end ± 1 mm long and the seed body ± 2 mm long. Capsules of H. pulchra are described by Hilliard & Burtt (1986) as 10-17 mm long with seeds ± 1.25 mm diam., with weakly or strongly developed wings. We need to know more about both the Zululand and Mt Thomas plants. Additional collections, especially in fruit, will be helpful in understanding the range of variation in this species.
<!-- /evo:text -->
