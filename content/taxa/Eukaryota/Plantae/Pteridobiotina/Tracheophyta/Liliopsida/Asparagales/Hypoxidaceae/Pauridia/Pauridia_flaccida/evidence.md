## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
Pauridia flaccida can be recognized by a combination of characters. The often many-layered corm tunics have somewhat hard fibres, mostly with outspread, pointed tips. The two bracts, each clasping the base of a pedicel, are characteristically broad and thin-textured, displaying a prominent midrib and a few, lateral veins. The style is short (1-2 mm long) and supports large, often convoluted stigma branches, (2.5-)4.0-5.5(-6.5) mm long, which are characteristically broad-based (1.5-2.5 mm wide) with outspread basal lobes protruding between the filaments. In several regional Floras the circumscription of P. flaccida included collections from as far east as Humansdorp, Eastern Cape. After careful study, plants from the Langeberg, east of Swellendam, through the Outeniqua and Tsitsikamma Mountains to the Humansdorp area are now placed in P. maryae, which is distinguished from P. flaccida sensu stricto by the outer sheathing leaf bases forming a smooth, brown and papery to leathery neck and the narrowly oblong (2.0-3.5 mm) stigma branches lacking spreading basal lobes.
<!-- /evo:text -->
