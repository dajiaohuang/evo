## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
Recognised by the solitary foliage leaf inserted on the stem well above ground, main stem and branches flexed above the sheaths of foliage or sheathing leaves, and a pink or yellow flower with the tepal claws diverging to form a relatively wide cup that includes the filaments. The filaments are united in a narrow column that is velvety in the lower half, and the diverging anthers slightly exceed the style branches, the crests of which are vestigial. It is apparently related to the more widespread M. minor of the Western Cape Atlantic Coast and Olifants River Valley and the two are easily confused, especially when dried. However, in M. minor the tepal claws form a narrow cup including both filaments and anthers and the short style branches have larger, clearly visible crests. Both species are diploid but M. minor is autogamous and M. vallisbelli is self-incompatible.
<!-- /evo:text -->
