## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
Recognised by the branched stem bearing two to four foliage leaves, and moderate-sized, blue to blue-mauve, violet or rarely white flowers with style branches as narrow and as long as or slightly longer than the anthers and lacking style crests. The ascending tepal claws diverge slightly to form a relatively narrow cup that includes the filaments and the lower part of the anthers or all of the anthers, and the tepal limbs spread more-or-less horizontally. The filaments are united in a slender, smooth column usually 7-9 mm long. A notable variant of the species from near Albertinia and Herbertsdale in southern Western Cape has slightly smaller, white or pale blue flowers with the filaments only 5-6 mm long, and both filaments and anthers are included in the floral cup whereas the anthers are usually fully excluded from the cup in typical M. polyanthos. The species is closely allied to M. bipartita, which has conventional Moraea-type flowers with broad style branches with prominent crests but the two are identical in vegetative features. Hybrids between these two species have been recorded where they co-occur.
<!-- /evo:text -->
