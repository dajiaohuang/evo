## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/0/text -->
Plants 350-480 mm high, evidently lacking basal sheathing cataphylls at flowering. Corm broadly ovoid, flattened in lower 1/3 on one side, 12-14 mm diam.; tunics woody ± black, notched below into sections. Stem suberect, usually 2- or 3-branched from near base or simple, branches laxly arching outward. Leaves 4 or 5, mostly basal, ± linear, mostly 6-8 mm wide, soft and ± succulent, uppermost sometimes inserted shortly above ground, partly sheathing stem. Spike lax, 1- to 3-flowered, spaced 25-35 mm apart; bracts green, soft to ± membranous, 12-15 mm long, inner narrower, with membranous margins. Flowers crepuscular, rotate, white, middle 1/2-2/3 of outer tepals mottled grey-brown outside, opening after 19:00, tepals then outspread, evidently unscented [or faintly scented]; perianth tube ± cylindric, ± 8 mm long, reaching to ± top of bracts; tepals subequal, elliptic, 16 x 4 mm. Filaments erect, ± 6 mm long; anthers held horizontally, 6-7 mm long, white; pollen white. Style branches ± 5 mm long, ascending, reaching to lower 1/3 anthers in closed flower. Capsules ± ovoid-elliptic, obtuse, smooth, 12-14 x 6-7 mm. Seeds ± 2 mm long, irregularly angled by pressure, with light brown, loose testa.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
Distinctive in the marshy habitat, unique for the genus, and also stands out in its tall stature, up to 480 mm high, the long, linear, soft-textured leaves, and the unusually lax spikes of only one to three flowers. The flowers are white with the outer tepals coloured grey-brown in the middle section and the tepals are narrowly elliptic, ± 16 x 4 mm. Flowers open after dark, 19:00 and appear unscented or at best weakly scented. The capsules are unusually large, up to 14 mm long. Hesperantha palustris is perhaps most closely related to the widespread H. cucullata, a shorter species with firm to almost leathery leaves and the outer tepals of the white flowers usually bright, glossy red outside, with the dark pigment usually covering the entire outer surface. Flowers of H. cucullata open well before sunset, mostly 16:00-16:30, and then produce a strong, sweet odour. The tepals are relatively broader than in H. palustris, usually 12-16 mm long and at least a third to nearly half as wide.
<!-- /evo:text -->
