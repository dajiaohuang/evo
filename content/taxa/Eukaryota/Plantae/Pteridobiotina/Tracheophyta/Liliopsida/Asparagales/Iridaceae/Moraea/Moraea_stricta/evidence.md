## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
Recognised by the combination of a dry (or absent) foliage leaf at flowering, a fairly short stem up to 250 mm high, and small, pale blue-mauve to lilac flowers. Only the outer tepals bear nectar guides and the style branches are relatively well developed and bear short, erect crests ± 3 mm long. The foliage leaf, also distinctive, is emergent at or soon after flowering, and has a terete, prominently ribbed blade with a thickened apex. Ultimately the leaf may be up to 600 mm long or longer, then trailing through low, grassy vegetation. At the end of the growing season the leaf turns dry and brown and the flowering stem to which it is attached begins to elongate in preparation for flowering the following spring. Flowering at the end of the dry season, the rhipidial spathes and sheathing leaves are, like the foliage leaf, usually dry and brown. Moraea stricta is one of relatively few polyploid species.
<!-- /evo:text -->
