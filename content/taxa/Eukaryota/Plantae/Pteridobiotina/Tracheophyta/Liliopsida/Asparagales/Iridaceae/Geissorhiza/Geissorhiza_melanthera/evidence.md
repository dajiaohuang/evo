## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/0/text -->
Plants 140-185 mm high. Corm ± ovoid, 5-8 mm diam.; tunics concentric, woody, light brown, fragmenting vertically into elliptic segments, not accumulating. Stem suberect, flexed outward above sheaths of upper 2 leaves and at base of spike, suberect, simple or 1- or 2-branched from cauline leaf axils. Leaves usually (2)3, lowermost basal, suberect, 140-180 x ± 1.3 mm, blades ± square in outline, margins raised into wings extended at 90° to blade surface, sparsely ciliolate on edges, sticky outside with sand adhering, main vein hardly thickened, plane and paler green between margins and main vein, upper leaves smaller than basal. Spike inclined to ± horizontal, flexuose, with flowers borne on upper side, main spike 6- to 12-flowered; outer bract (12-)16-22 mm long, inner slightly shorter to ± 1/2 as long as inner, 2-keeled and forked apically. Flowers zygomorphic, nodding, unscented, pale beige-yellow, red at tepal bases and in tube, outside of tepals sometimes flushed red, anthers black; perianth tube ± cylindric, 10-14 mm long, constricted in lower 8-9 mm, wider distally; tepals narrowly obovoid-oblong, ± 24-26 x 7-8 mm, ultimately spreading at right angles to tube, inner slightly wider than outer. Filaments unequal, unilateral, declinate, ± horizontally, ± 5 mm long, red; anthers unequal, laterals 5-6 mm long, central 6.5-7.0 mm long, forked at base, ± black, remaining straight and rigid after dehiscence with white connective exposed; pollen dark purple. Style arching below filaments, dividing opposite upper 1/3 of anthers, ± 2.7 mm long.

## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
Geissorhiza melanthera is a particularly striking member of sect. Engysiphon. Its moderate-sized flowers recall smaller-flowered G. helmei (also a narrow endemic of the Piketberg) but G. melanthera has larger, beige-yellow flowers with perianth tube 10-14 mm long and spreading tepals 24-26 mm long. The flowers are zygomorphic with declinate stamens and style typical of the section but the anthers are black and slightly elliptic with the central anther slightly longer than the laterals. It has the smallest corms in sect. Engysiphon, only 5-8 mm in diameter. The leaves are H-shaped in cross section, the margins bearing wings as wide as the leaf axis and held at right angles to the blade whereas the midrib is only slightly raised. This leaf type is characteristic of G. exscapa, which has larger flowers, and also pink-flowered G. bonaspei and white-flowered G. tenella. It is to these species that G. melanthera is immediately allied, all of which have a somewhat to significantly longer perianth tube at least 20 mm long and 40-80 mm in G. exscapa. The anthers of G. melanthera are its most striking feature, unusual for the genus not only in their black colour but in retaining their elliptical shape after releasing their pollen, with a portion of the connective exposed and showing white between the thecae. In other species of the section the anthers lose their shape, shrinking and becoming deformed after releasing pollen.
<!-- /evo:text -->
