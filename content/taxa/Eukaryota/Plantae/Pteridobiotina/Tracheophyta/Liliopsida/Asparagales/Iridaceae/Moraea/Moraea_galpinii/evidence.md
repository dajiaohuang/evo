## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
The rare M. galpinii is distinguished by the single, slender foliage leaf, usually barely emergent at flowering time but ultimately exceeding the stem. The blade appears at first to be terete but has a narrow adaxial groove separating the two closely appressed leaf halves. The flowers, fairly typical of subg. Grandiflorae, are bright yellow and have outer tepals 40-50 mm long with spreading limbs and erect inner tepals ± 35 mm long. Particularly distinctive is the collar of hard, blackish vertical fibres enclosing the base of the stem and extending a short distance above ground. This feature is shared with M. robusta, which is distinguished by paler, larger flowers with erect to spreading inner and outer tepal limbs. Young plants not yet of flowering age have rather soft, unbroken ± membranous cataphylls.
<!-- /evo:text -->
