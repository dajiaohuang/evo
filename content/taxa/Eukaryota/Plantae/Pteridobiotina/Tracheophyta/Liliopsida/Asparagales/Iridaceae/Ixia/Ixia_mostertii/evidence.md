## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
Recognised by the hard, coarse corm tunics, the fibres extending upward as a collar around the base of the plant, Ixia mostertii also has relatively large flowers, somewhat crowded apically and with a prominent dark central eye. The flowers are either pink, mauve-pink or white with the central mark dark blue or iridescent green. Notably, the style is long, dividing opposite or above the bases of the anthers. It has been suggested to be allied to I. vinacea on account of the coarse collar of fibres around the base and flowers with a dark centre, but I. vinacea has linear leaves up to 3 mm wide and exceeding the spike, very different from the shorter, broader leaves of I. mostertii. It also differs in the relatively few-flowered spike and short style dividing opposite or slightly above the base of the filaments. The prominent anthers, up to 9 mm long (before anthesis), remain straight and suberect as the flower ages, another important feature of I. mostertii and useful in distinguishing plants collected without corms from I. polystachya, the anthers of which spread widely, often becoming somewhat twisted and almost horizontal after anthesis.
<!-- /evo:text -->
