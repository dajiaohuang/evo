## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/0/text -->
Plants 200-400 mm high, usually in small colonies, usually with 1 or more cormlets in leaf or cataphyll axils, and sometimes with collar of fibres around base. Corm mostly 10-15 mm diam.; tunics of light (rarely dark brown), relatively fine, firm or soft fibres; often producing two new corms to replace parent corm. Stem rarely velvety to pilose or glabrous, simple or 1(2)-branched, sheathing leaves or becoming dry from tips, attenuate, 55-70 mm long. Foliage leaf solitary, shallowly channelled, usually exceeding stem and up to twice as long, 3.5-5.5 mm wide, often trailing distally. Rhipidial spathes with dry, attenuate tips, inner 55-80 mm long, outer ± 1/2 as long. Flowers long-lived, pale blue to pale violet or light purple, outer tepal limbs with small, triangular yellow (rarely ± white) nectar guides at bases edged dark blue, short-velvety (rarely glabrous), spreading at 45-60º, scented of vanilla; outer tepals 30-40 x 15-20 mm, claws 12-18 mm long, velvety, limbs ± orbicular, widest above middle, 15-20(-23) mm long, usually ± as wide, inner tepals hair-like, erect, 1.5-3.5 mm long. Filaments ± free or united < 0.2 mm, 4.0-5.5 mm long; anthers 8-11 mm long, usually not reaching stigma lobes; pollen orange-red. Ovary ellipsoid, slightly narrowed below apex, (10-)12-15 mm long; style vestigial, branches ± 15 mm long, crests linear, 8-12 mm long. Capsules 16-19 mm long, narrowly ovoid-oblong with thickened rim. Seeds angular, relatively large, 2-3 mm long, angles forming wing-like ridges.

## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
The most striking of the species of the Moraea tripetala complex, M. grandis has relatively large corms up to 15 mm diam., with tunics usually of fine, soft (rarely firm) fibres, and the largest flowers in the alliance. The outer tepals are 30-40 mm long with ± orbicular limbs 18-23 x 15-20 mm, thus almost as wide as long, and have unusually small nectar guides, usually yellow (sometimes ± white) on a white background, minutely spotted with dark blue, and usually edged with darker blue or violet. The limbs are also widest in the upper third and usually have an abrupt, comma-like tip. The flowers have a pleasant, vanilla-like odour. Like its relative, M. amabilis, it typically grows in colonies resulting from vegetative reproduction by axillary cormlets and sometimes by the replacement of the parent corm by two daughter corms. It also resembles M. amabilis in having virtually free filaments (united for < 0.2 mm), but the anthers, 8-11 mm long, are at least twice as long as the filaments and sometimes 2.5 times as long, easily the largest in the M. tripetala complex. The pollen is consistently bright orange-red, not unusual for the complex, but contrasting with the white pollen in most western Karoo populations of M. amabilis, which has smaller anthers 4.5-7.0 mm long and ± as long as or up to 1.5 times as long as the filaments. Moraea grandis sometimes grows in close proximity to M. amabilis, and the two can be seen flowering together at sites in the Calvinia District and occasionally elsewhere. Difficulty in distinguishing the two in the immediate vicinity of Nieuwoudtville is probably the result of hybridisation and introgression between the two species.
