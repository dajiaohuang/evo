## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/0/text -->
Plants acaulescent, ascending to decumbent, 125 mm tall, up to 380 mm diam., solitary or proliferating from base and cluster-forming (3-5 heads). Roots succulent, up to 2.5 mm diam. Leaves rosulate, patent spreading (at first ascending), firm, lorate-lanceolate, 165-230 mm long, 60-65 mm broad at base, slightly falcate; adaxial surface canaliculate, plane towards apex, faintly white-spotted; abaxial surface somewhat convex with a distinct eccentric keel, faintly spotted, spots arranged in obscure transverse bands; both surfaces dull green with smooth, slightly minutely asperulous epidermis; margin minutely serrulate to almost entire, apex obtuse to subacute, mucronate. Juvenile leaves distichous, lorate, patent to ascending; epidermis smooth, minutely asperulous, densely white-spotted, spots arranged in transverse bands; apex obtuse, mucronate. Inflorescence ascending to flat-topped panicle (racemose in juvenile plants). Flowers, secundly arranged and up to 6 flowers open at the same time, up to 1150 mm long, bearing up to six ascending to horizontally spreading side branches up to 750 mm long. Scape 250-330 mm long 14 mm broad at base, flattened; floral bracts 6-7 x 1.52 mm, piliferous (peduncular bracts present, slightly larger); pedicels 4 mm long, pink. Perianth 27-29 mm long, stipitate for 3-4 mm; segments fused for their greater length (accept the perianth apices and margins of the inner 3 segments), subcylindrical, curved in the middle, the lower portion of perianth slightly decurved; upper half inflated to about 7 mm in diameter, uniform dull pink, the upper half with faint green striations; apices erect becoming erectly spreading, obtuse; margins of inner segments free and channelled at base for 10-12 mm, diverging gradually towards apex. Stamens 23-25 mm long; anthers 3 x 1.5 mm long, included. Ovary 6.5 mm long, 3 mm diam., green; style 13 mm long, stigma included, curved upwards, minute. Capsule 16-25 x 7-10 mm long, narrow obovate, triangular in cross-section, obtuse at apex. Seeds oblong, 5 x 3 mm, black.

## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/1/text -->
Gasteria loedolffiae is at once distinguished by its dark pink flowers 27-29 mm long, its matt dull-green leaf surface of spreading leaves and its juvenile leaves of which the surface is minutely asperulous. Superficially it resembles G. croucheri, the latter with much different and larger flowers. Gasteria loedolffiae belongs to section longiflorae Haw. series longifoliae Haw., which now includes five species, G. acinacifolia (J.Jacq.) Haw., G. batesiana, G. croucheri (Hook.f.) Baker, G. tukhelensis and the new species. All these species have large flowers (33-45 mm long) that are gasteriform over half of the perianth length. G. batesiana, a smaller species occurring to the north, has shorter, distinctly tuberculate, recurved leaves, smaller, curved flowers and smaller fruiting capsules. G. tukhelensis is confined to the Thukela River in KwaZulu-Natal. Gasteria croucheri occurs widespread to the south with a dull to slightly glaucous surface. In size it resembles G. loedolffiae with ascending, spreading to pendent leaves 200-360 mm long. In G. Croucheri the inflorescence is a flat-topped panicle flowering from November to February, whereas in G. loedolffiae the side branches are ascending. G. croucheri is found from Durban southwards to the Msikaba River in Eastern Cape (Transkei region). Gasteria loedolffiae grows solitary or forms clusters by offshoots of up to 5 plants.

## ecology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/ecology/claims/0/text -->
Eastern Valley Bushveld (Sub-Escarpment Savanna Bioregion). Gasteria loedolffiae appears to be confined to Beaufort shale cliffs (Adelaide subgroup) of both the Mzimvubu and Msikaba River to the north. It has only been observed on sheer cliff faces (altitude 200-500 m). The rosettes grow in crevices often on humus-rich ledges of shale rocks in full sun or the shade of thicket consisting of Euphorbia triangularis, E. tirucallii, Ficus ingens, Portulacaria afra, Aloe rupestris, Aloe arborescens, Commiphora harveyi, Bulbine natalensis, Caputia medley-woodii, Cotyledon orbiculata, Crassula expansa subsp. fragilis, C. orbicularis, C. cordata, Gerrardanthus macrorrhizus, Albuca longibracteata, Mervilla plumbea, C. perfoliata var. perfoliata, Delosperma sp., Plectranthus montanus, P. spicatus and Huernia hystrix.
