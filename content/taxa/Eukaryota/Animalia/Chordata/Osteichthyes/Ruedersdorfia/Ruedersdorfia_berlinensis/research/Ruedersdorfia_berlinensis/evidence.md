# Ruedersdorfia berlinensis

## claims / statement

<!-- evo:text /records/claims/0/statement -->
Ruedersdorfia berlinensis is a named stem teleosteomorph placed as Teleosteomorpha incertae sedis, based on holotype MB.f.19910 and paratypes MB.f.14888 and MB.f.19911.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## claims / confidenceRationale

<!-- evo:text /records/claims/0/confidenceRationale -->
The primary description provides a species diagnosis, type material and explicitly states that its position among stem teleosts is unknown and is treated as Teleosteomorpha incertae sedis. Confidence retains that qualified placement.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## claims / statement

<!-- evo:text /records/claims/1/statement -->
The Ruedersdorfia berlinensis type and referred skull roofs come from the Rüdersdorf opencast mine in the Central European (Germanic) Basin, Germany; this occurrence does not establish global distribution.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## claims / confidenceRationale

<!-- evo:text /records/claims/1/confidenceRationale -->
The primary study reports the named mine and basin for all type material. Confidence is medium because the locality and three skull roofs cannot establish absence, endemism or a global range.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## claims / statement

<!-- evo:text /records/claims/2/statement -->
Ruedersdorfia berlinensis is associated with the study's marine restricted-basin fossil setting, but the skull-roof-only sample does not establish diet, locomotion or a trophic guild.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## claims / confidenceRationale

<!-- evo:text /records/claims/2/confidenceRationale -->
The source documents the depositional setting but preserves no body outline or feeding apparatus sufficient for a species-specific ecological reconstruction. Medium confidence marks the explicit limitation.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## claims / statement

<!-- evo:text /records/claims/3/statement -->
Ruedersdorfia berlinensis has a fused skull roof with a nearly equal orbital and postorbital region, a short nasal region and middle pitline, and characteristic ganoine ornamentation.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## claims / confidenceRationale

<!-- evo:text /records/claims/3/confidenceRationale -->
The diagnosis, Table 1 and Figure 11 directly document the proportions, pitline and ornamentation of the named skull-roof sample. Confidence is medium because body anatomy is not preserved and the canals are obscured in the holotype.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## claims / statement

<!-- evo:text /records/claims/4/statement -->
The displayed approximately 244 Ma point bounds the lower Anisian Rüdersdorf sample of Ruedersdorfia berlinensis and is not a global teleosteomorph or teleost first appearance.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## claims / confidenceRationale

<!-- evo:text /records/claims/4/confidenceRationale -->
The primary paper places the type and referred skull roofs in the lower Middle Triassic, lower Anisian middle Muschelkalk at about 244 Ma. Confidence is medium because this is one locality sample, not a global range endpoint.
<!-- /evo:text -->
