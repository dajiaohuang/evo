## Six reported high-Andean localities
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5704321-4gwqh
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The description reported four localities outside and two inside Peru’s Pui Pui Protected Forest, at 3350–3890 m, with an estimated extent of occurrence of 101.3 km². The elevation interval and area are study-record summaries, without establishing physiological limits, continuous occupancy or a complete contemporary distribution.

## Rock and moss shelters across forest patches and puna
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5704321-4gwqh
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
Specimens were found beneath rocks or in moss during morning and afternoon searches, in Polylepis forest patches, mountain slopes and puna near Laguna Sinchon. These sheltered daytime encounters do not establish that exposed surface activity occurs throughout the day or that the species breeds beneath the same shelters.

## A single male with ectoparasitic mites
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5704321-4gwqh
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
One male, specimen MUSM 31203, carried five trombiculid mites near the insertion of its right upper arm. This individual observation documents ectoparasitism, without estimating infection prevalence, population health effects or whether the mites were specific to this frog species.

## A dated author proposal
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5704321-4gwqh
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
The 2017 description proposed Near Threatened status while discussing the small recorded range, possible additional localities and threats to Andean amphibians. This is the authors’ dated proposal rather than a verified current IUCN assessment, and general regional threats are not rewritten as measured impacts on every known population.
