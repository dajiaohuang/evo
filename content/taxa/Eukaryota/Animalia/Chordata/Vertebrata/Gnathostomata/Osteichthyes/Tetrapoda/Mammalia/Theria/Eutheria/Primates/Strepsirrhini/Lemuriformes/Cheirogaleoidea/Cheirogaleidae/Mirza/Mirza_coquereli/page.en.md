## socialEcology

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
A genetic and behavioral study of one population found that females sharing mitochondrial haplotypes were spatially clustered. Some females dispersed, but the study found no evidence of successful immigrant female settlement. Males ranged widely in search of receptive females and paternity was spread among males, a pattern the authors described as scramble-competition polygyny. These results describe the sampled population, not every population of the species.

## canopySurvey

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
Nocturnal line-transect surveys at Zombitse-Vohibasia National Park ran from December 2021 to June 2022. The full effort covered 342 km; filtered analyses retained 258 km and 1,500 observations, including 123 Mirza coquereli detections. First-detection height averaged 5.23 ± 0.19 m, similar to Cheirogaleus medius in that survey. Transects sampled accessible park areas and were not designed to cover every habitat type.

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
The genetic and social findings are from one population, while canopy measurements are from selected transects in one park. Survey detections are not unique-animal counts, and these studies do not estimate current species-wide abundance or distribution. The Chinese display name is editorial. No dossier or external expert review was added. This page is not externally expert-reviewed.
