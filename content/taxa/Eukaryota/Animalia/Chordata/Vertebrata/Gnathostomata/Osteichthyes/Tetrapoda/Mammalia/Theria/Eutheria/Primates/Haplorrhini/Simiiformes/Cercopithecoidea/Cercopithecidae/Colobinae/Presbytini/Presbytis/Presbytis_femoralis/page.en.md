## ecology

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
In a secondary, human-modified forest in Johor, researchers observed one group for 41 days and recorded 186.3 hours. Feeding records were split roughly between leaves and fruit; young leaves dominated leaf feeding. A substantial share of fruit came from cultivated plants, showing that this group used crops in a village forest landscape. This is one site and one group, not a species-wide or seasonal diet.

## life_history

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
During 22 months of observation in Singapore’s Central Catchment Nature Reserve from 2008 to 2010, researchers recorded at least six births. The observed newborns were white with a cross-shaped dark pattern on the back. The authors treated these records as direct evidence that the Singapore group was reproducing, while describing the data as preliminary. They do not establish a species-wide breeding season or infant survival rate.

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
Diet evidence comes from one group in one secondary forest in Johor; reproductive records are local observations from Singapore’s Central Catchment. Neither study provides a species-wide range or status assessment. The Chinese common name is an editorial translation; this page has not received external expert review. This entry is not externally expert-reviewed.
