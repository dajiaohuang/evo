# Xericeps curvirostris


## An additional lower jaw referred in 2023

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
The 2023 account retains FSAC-KK 10700 as a partial mandibular symphysis and newly refers partial mandible FSAC-KK 5203. Its diagnosis retains the upcurved profile, continuous ventral midline sulcus and paired posterior occlusal ridges. The newly referred material is not a new holotype or an individual associated with a complete skull and wings. That referral alone establishes neither a species size range nor an age series or population count.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Sources differ on the horizon of the same type

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
The 2020 Xericeps account places the Aferdou N’Chaft type in the Douira Formation. The 2023 account instead lists the Ifezouane Formation and retains the age wording ?Albian–Cenomanian. Both concern FSAC-KK 10700, so a changed stratigraphic interpretation does not count as another discovery. The dated difference and question mark remain visible without changing the fixed database collection row or its broad 100.5–93.9 Ma bracket, or adjudicating a precise horizon.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Finer relationships and an unknown upper jaw remain open

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The 2023 authors question finer placements based on an upturned mandible or broadly distributed foramina and cautiously retain Xericeps as an indeterminate non-tapejarid azhdarchoid. They discuss several alternatives for jaw morphotype C, FSAC-KK 5200, 5201 and 5202: an Apatorhamphus lower jaw, a Xericeps upper jaw, or another clade. Those alternatives cannot become a confirmed Xericeps upper jaw. This page also does not adjudicate the possible synonymy with Alanqa raised in 2020 or inspect all matrices behind the relationship hypotheses.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## The type is part of a lower jaw

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
Holotype FSAC-KK 10700 is a partial dentary symphysis held in the Aïn Chock geological/palaeontological collection of Université Hassan II, Casablanca. The 2018 description records discovery by local workers at Aferdou N’Chaft and purchase at the mine site in January 2017; the 2020 study places its provenance in the Douira Formation. The lower-jaw material lacks an associated skull or complete wing bones, so it does not directly supply whole-body proportions.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Preserved and reconstructed jaw lengths

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The anterior tip is missing, and the posterior break lies near the expected division into mandibular rami. The 2018 manuscript reports 173 mm preserved length; extending the margins to an assumed pointed tip yields about 200 mm reconstructed length. That is neither a 200 mm preserved fragment nor the length of the missing portion. The specimen is largely uncrushed but has slight fractures and lateral displacement. Removing sandstone reveals its three-dimensional surface without providing a complete jaw or associated body skeleton.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## An upcurved jaw and continuous ventral groove

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
In lateral view, the slightly concave occlusal margin and convex ventral margin produce a gentle upward curve. A longitudinal ventral midline groove extends along the preserved fragment and is emphasized in the original diagnosis. Two posterior occlusal ridges bound a broader central groove; elongate foramina occur on lateral and occlusal surfaces. Comparisons with other toothless pterosaurs support an azhdarchoid attribution. The grooves and foramina are not preserved food, a complete beak sheath or direct feeding observations.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Bone surface supports a limited maturity inference

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
The 2018 authors interpret a surface smooth to the naked eye but slightly fibrous under magnification as suggesting skeletal maturity or near-maturity at death. The origin of the posterior ridges also remains uncertain: they discuss splenials or mandibular outgrowths, without a visible suture resolving the alternatives. Surface texture and local fusion in one jaw fragment do not establish exact age, sex, adult wingspan or a species growth series.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Separate size categories from wingspan estimates

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
The original diagnosis calls the animal medium to large, using roughly 3–8 m to explain a broad pterosaur size category and favoring its lower end. This is not a wingspan range sampled across individuals of this species. The 2020 comparison estimates at least about 3–4 m for the type from more complete related material. Without complete wings or independent remeasurement, the category’s 8 m upper bound cannot become a confirmed species maximum.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## The relationship to Alanqa remains unresolved

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/text/en -->
The 2018 authors support a non-tapejarid azhdarchoid attribution while leaving finer relationships unresolved. They already discuss the possibility that the Alanqa type is a rostrum and that the two names represent upper and lower jaws of one or closely related species. The 2020 study retains possible synonymy and requires more complete material to confirm or reject it. Similar morphology and occurrence in the same deposits establish neither one individual nor one species; fixed PBDB names and parent chains are retained without merging records.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Compare jaws without turning depositional setting into diet

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/text/en -->
Compare the straight margins and type reinterpretation of [Alanqa saharica](../../../Azhdarchidae/Alanqa/Alanqa_saharica/page.en.md) with the upcurve and ventral groove of Xericeps. The 2018 paper relates continental deposition and differing jaws to possible terrestrial feeding, while explicitly stating that fragmentary jaws cannot readily establish diet and that ecological roles need more complete remains. Read collection names and broad ages separately in the retained database records; the modern desert and name etymology do not establish the ancient habitat.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Which fossil records this name represents

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/10/text/en -->
Xericeps curvirostris is accepted in the fixed PBDB pterosaur working roster, with database parent Xericeps. This guide uses 1 eligible species-level occurrence record from 1 collection and 1 reference identifier. Occurrences, collections and references are different units, not specimen or individual counts.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Age bounds of these records

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/12/text/en -->
Selected occurrences have an outer age envelope of 100.5–93.9 Ma, where Ma denotes millions of years ago. It combines the oldest early bound and youngest late bound among selected records, rather than a continuous record at every time or measured species origin, extinction or individual lifespan. Each collection retains its own age bracket. Broad intervals are not narrowed to precise specimen ages, and original dating interpretations are not revised here.
