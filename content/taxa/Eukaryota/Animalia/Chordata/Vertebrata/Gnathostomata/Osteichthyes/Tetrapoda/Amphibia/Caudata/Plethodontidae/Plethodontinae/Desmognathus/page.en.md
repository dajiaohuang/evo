## Larval stream records require local identification and sampling context
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
The 2025 Georgia study compared folkertsi and amphileucus larvae in one stream over three sampling days. Visible parasite cysts provided a locally PCR-supported identification proxy, not a universal character for all dusky salamanders. Folkertsi occurred on both sides of a tributary, whereas amphileucus was found only downstream in that survey. Higher-flow encounters for amphileucus describe occupied riffles, with shared riffles counted in both species groups and empty riffles excluded from comparison. Seven upstream folkertsi females attended nests; amphileucus nest-site choice and larval drift remained hypotheses. This example illustrates evidence units, rather than prescribing a single flow requirement, larval duration or nesting strategy for the genus.

## Adult escape comparisons leave northern species identity unresolved
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
The 2025 locomotion study compared 2010 adult samples of aureatus, amphileucus and marmoratus with an unresolved northern semi-aquatic group.
