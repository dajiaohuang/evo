## distribution_habitat

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
A production-forest study in Kuningan District, West Java, surveyed 19 forest patches covering about 52.57 km². Local residents helped identify survey locations, so patches were not randomly sampled. The reported langur densities ranged from about 37 to 54 animals per km², with a mean of 44.71. These estimates describe the surveyed production-forest patches, not all of Java or protected areas.

## ecology

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
The study compared food-tree richness and stump density across patches. Langur density was positively associated with the number of food-tree species and negatively associated with stump density; the model explained less than half of population variation. The authors treated production forests as a conditional habitat case and called for broader sampling and improved survey design. These associations do not prove a single causal mechanism.

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
Density estimates are limited to 19 production-forest patches in Kuningan. Sites were identified with local guidance rather than random sampling, and the model explained limited variation.
