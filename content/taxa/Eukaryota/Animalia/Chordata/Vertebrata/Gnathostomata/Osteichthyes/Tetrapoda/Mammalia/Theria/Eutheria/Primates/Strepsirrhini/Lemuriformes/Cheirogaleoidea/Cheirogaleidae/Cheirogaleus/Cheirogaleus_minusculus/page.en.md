# Cheirogaleus minusculus


## Why a broad revision left this name unresolved

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
The 2014 dwarf-lemur revision discusses Cheirogaleus minusculus while explicitly remaining unable to assess its status. Follow the sampling gap, type comparison and empty table entries here. Its pinned COL26.8 accepted identity remains; genetic or ecological results from other dwarf lemurs do not transfer to this species.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Ninety-one samples did not fill the Ambositra gap

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
The sampling methods report 91 Cheirogaleus samples from 31 Malagasy localities during 1999–2008, but no comparable field samples from the Ambositra area were obtained, preventing assessment of minusculus. This is a boundary of that study, not evidence of local absence, 91 individuals of this species or a current population survey.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Resemblance to sibreei is a type-based comparison

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The authors place minusculus among other potential species of the sibreei group, citing a dorsal stripe, relatively restricted eye rings, grey muzzle and dark, sparsely haired ears as similarities. The type is smaller, with a higher, rounder braincase, a non-convex facial skeleton, broader palate and reduced upper third molars; the tail tip appears white. These source comparisons of type morphology do not establish a genetic sister relationship, a stable field diagnosis or proportions shared by all adults.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Tables retain the name without supplying body-size samples

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
Table 3 retains minusculus with a Data Deficient footnote; n.i. in comparison columns means not included or not explicitly mentioned, rather than support for its own genetic clade. Table 4 pools adult male and female information and excludes juveniles, but weight, head-crown length, body length, tail length and examined-specimen entries for this species are dashes. Means and sample counts from other species do not fill them. The existing museum juvenile-type record remains distinct from gaps in this adult table.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## One locality and historical status do not define a range

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The 2014 account reports only the type locality, Ambositra, and Table 4 lists 1,678 m for that row. The methods explicitly lack comparable field samples there, so this is not a new captured animal’s GPS record, a continuous elevation range or proof of current occupancy. The account’s historical Data Deficient status does not replace a current conservation assessment; the authors call for field and laboratory investigation to confirm taxonomic status.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Boundaries of the type and field evidence

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
The name Lesser Iron-gray Dwarf Lemur was based on a museum holotype from Ambositra; the Natural History Museum catalogue records the specimen as a juvenile. A multilocus taxonomic study explicitly found the available evidence insufficient to assess the species' status. This page preserves the catalogue identity without adding behaviour, ecology, or a complete range unsupported by field observations.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Relative weights of food categories

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/text/en -->
The archive’s nonzero food weights are fruit 50%; other plant material (the source category also includes fungi) 40%; invertebrates 10%. Code ABC pools high, reasonable and partial confidence; some estimates can borrow from an ecologically similar congener. ABC cannot be rewritten as direct observations of this species. These percentages standardize verbal descriptions rather than measure diet composition; zero does not prove an item is never eaten. Categories such as Nect, Seed and PlantO are broader than their everyday names and cannot imply exclusively nectar, seeds or plants.

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
Field ecology is underdocumented; one holotype cannot represent population variation or current distribution. Catalogue identity and biological evidence maturity are reported separately. This page has not received external expert review.
This is a reading guide to historical same-name, same-family foraging records in EltonTraits 1.0, not a complete species monograph. Matching names and classification do not establish equivalence between historical species concepts and COL26.8. Food and avian stratum percentages are coarse relative weights standardized by the authors from literature descriptions, not direct measurements of stomach contents, intake, energy or observation time. Avian A/B diet codes retain source and proportional uncertainty. Mammalian ABC diet or activity codes can also refer to a congener judged ecologically similar by the compilers; this page does not claim direct observations of this species for every field. Genus/family typical values, non-species-level avian strata and split records needing individual review do not fill definite conclusions. Underlying books and papers have not been individually reread, and historical citations do not constitute external expert review. Individual, seasonal and local variation, current range, population and conservation still require other sources. The added 2014 revision guide covers named sampling, table and type-comparison evidence without obtaining new field samples of this species or reading the complete article, supplementary material or original 2000 description. The first-round source gap remains open and reader-facing delivery is unfinished.
