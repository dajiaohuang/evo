## fieldStudy

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
McKey and Waterman studied one group for 11 months in coastal rainforest at Douala-Edea, Cameroon. They compared seasonal ranging with food supply, diet and weather. They proposed that, where nutrient-rich, readily digestible leaves were scarce, clumped seeds of high nutritive value could support a feeding strategy requiring less ranging.

## fieldStudy

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
A 1995–1996 survey estimated black-colobus density in primary forest at Lopé and combined it with then-available logged-forest data to estimate about 55,900 animals across the reserve. The reported density difference between forest types was not significant; the authors linked this result to low extraction of 1–2 trees per hectare.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## classification

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The Lopé reserve total is a historical estimate assembled from 1990s data, not a current count or evidence that heavier logging would have the same result. The Douala-Edea study followed one group; the two sites are not combined into a species-wide diet or ranging rule.
