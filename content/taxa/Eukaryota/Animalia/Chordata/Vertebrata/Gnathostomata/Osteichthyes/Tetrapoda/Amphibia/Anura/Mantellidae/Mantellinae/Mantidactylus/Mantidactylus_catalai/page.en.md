## A pointed snout and flattened head distinguish the examined material
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10563080-3xwd8
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The revision reports male snout–vent length of 41–45 mm and female length of 51–62 mm. The broad, flattened, fish-like head has a pointed snout projecting 1.75–3.45 mm. Toes are webbed, with 1–1.5 phalanges of toe IV free; a superior lip stripe and obvious inguinal streak are absent in the diagnosis. These are a combination of characters from examined material, not independent identification by size alone. The sex-specific ranges are not total body lengths or measurements of every population.

## Water-edge holes and rocks were used by day and night
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10563080-3xwd8
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
Observations in rainforest and bamboo habitat extended from 09:00 to 00:15. Frogs occupied holes and rock interstices near stagnant water or rivers, with fast streams rarely reported. Rocks and ground were used mainly during the day; no leaf or branch perches were observed, although specimen UADBA 4521 was collected on a root. The account describes semi-aquatic and burrowing behaviour, without establishing obligatory underground nesting or absence from vegetation throughout the species range. Observation times are encounter records, not an experimentally measured daily activity budget.

## The revision places confirmed records in southeastern rainforest
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10563080-3xwd8
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The distribution account restricts catalai to southeastern Madagascan rainforest at low and middle elevations. It provides no numerical elevation bounds in this paragraph, so low and middle are retained as qualitative source categories. This locality summary is not a mapped area of occupancy, a complete survey of the southeast, or evidence that suitable but unsampled forest is occupied. Records assigned to other Ochthomantis species are not pooled into this distribution.

## Resurrection includes a specific historical sequence reassignment
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10563080-3xwd8
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
Previously treated as a synonym of femoralis, catalai was resurrected after examination of types, morphology and mitochondrial 16S groupings; the resurrection account reports 33 examined specimens. The molecular group includes AY324821, voucher FGMV 2002.173, formerly labelled cf. mocquardi and candidate species 47, alongside material associated with Ambatolahy, Ranomafana. This is a particular voucher reassignment, not permission to relabel all mocquardi records. The revision’s greater-than-3% pairwise 16S distances support its integrative argument; they are not a universal species threshold or whole-genome proof of reproductive isolation.
<!-- /evo:text -->
