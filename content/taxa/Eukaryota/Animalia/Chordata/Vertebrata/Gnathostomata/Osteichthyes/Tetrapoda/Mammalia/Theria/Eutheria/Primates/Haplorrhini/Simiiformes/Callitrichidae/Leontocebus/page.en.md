# Leontocebus

## Distribution records

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
Mourthé et al. (2022) Supplementary Table S1 lists four Leontocebus taxa: L. fuscicollis and L. weddelli each overlap Inambari; L. fuscus overlaps Jaú and Napo; and L. nigricollis overlaps Napo. The table contains five species–interfluve overlap entries.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Classification

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
COL26.8 lists ten accepted species in Leontocebus: *L. fuscicollis*, *L. fuscus*, *L. lagonotus*, *L. leucogenys*, *L. nigricollis*, *L. nigrifrons*, *L. tripartitus*, *L. weddelli*, *L. illigeri* and *L. cruzlimai*. Matauschek et al. (2011) examined species limits in the nigricollis group; Sampaio et al. (2015) used Leontocebus at genus rank, whereas Garbino and Martins-Junior (2018) treated it as a Saguinus subgenus using combined phenotypic and genetic evidence. This guide preserves the pinned COL26.8 rank while making the differing combinations visible.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Choose a reading path by research question

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
Begin with the existing pages for *L. fuscicollis*, *L. fuscus*, *L. nigricollis* and *L. weddelli*: they compare range-map intersections across major Amazonian interfluves, not field densities. Then follow the site-based studies: the long-term single-troop account of *L. illigeri* at Río Pacaya; gap observations of *L. leucogenys* at Panguana; and the primary-versus-secondary-forest foraging study of *L. nigrifrons* at Quebrada Blanco. Next compare the local Ecuadorian surveys of *L. tripartitus* and *L. lagonotus*, then read the separate observer-habituation study of *L. lagonotus*. Finish with the neotype and transect records for *L. cruzlimai* in Brazil’s Purus basin. Sites and methods are not combined into a single genus-wide ecological rule.

## Scope of the evidence

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
This is a matrix comparison of taxa in one Brazilian Amazon range-map study, not a complete genus checklist, natural-history review or observation of individuals crossing rivers. Main-text Table 2 has internal differences in species totals and percentages; this page reports only the range-map intersections in Supplementary Table S1. This guide is. The reading path connects all ten accepted species in pinned COL26.8; local studies and taxonomic schemes remain separate and do not form a complete genus natural-history or species-delimitation review.
