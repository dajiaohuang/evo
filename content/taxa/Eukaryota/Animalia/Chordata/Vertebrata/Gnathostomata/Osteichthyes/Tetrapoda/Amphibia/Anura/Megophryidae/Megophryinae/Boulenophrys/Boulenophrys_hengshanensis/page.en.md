## Re-examined types separate the old mixed name
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12466624-lx9jq
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The 2025 revision identifies the historical holotype as male, contrary to the original female label. It associates nine newly collected specimens with hengshanensis by morphology of the unsequenced historical types, while fifteen specimens cluster with the 2021 material formerly sequenced as hengshanensis and are assigned to newly described gutu. Type morphology and a historical sequence label therefore have different evidence roles. This is a dated reassessment, not an independently sequenced holotype or a certification of every earlier record.

## Nine male measurements and one historical female
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12466624-lx9jq
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The revised diagnosis reports male snout–vent length 34.4–38.0 mm for nine males and 48.4 mm for one female. It combines weak vomerine ridges without teeth, rudimentary toe webbing, narrow toe fringes, heels not meeting under the stated posture, a narrow supratympanic fold and broad dark ventrolateral stripes. These are study-scoped characters; the one-female measurement is not a female population range. Historical formalin-preserved types remain separate from the newly collected ethanol material.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Live male pattern is not the preserved type colour
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12466624-lx9jq
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The live account is based on nine males and describes brown backs, sometimes with small orange patches; the dorsal pattern may be indistinct or absent. Broad dark abdominal side stripes can merge with central dark patches, and pale lower-abdominal dots have diffuse boundaries. Breeding males have spiny pads on the inner two fingers and an internal subgular vocal sac. These variable male observations do not fix female colours or establish all reproductive behaviour.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Observed calls and a proposed seasonal barrier
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12466624-lx9jq
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The ecological account reports stream-side moist forest and scrub on Mt. Hengshan around 700–1100 m, with calls from October to April. Discussion instead gives late September to early April. It reports elevational overlap with gutu but different calling seasons, suggesting a possible prezygotic barrier. The differing month statements remain separate; hearing calls is not observation of all mating or spawning, and seasonal separation was not directly tested as reproductive isolation.
<!-- /evo:text -->
