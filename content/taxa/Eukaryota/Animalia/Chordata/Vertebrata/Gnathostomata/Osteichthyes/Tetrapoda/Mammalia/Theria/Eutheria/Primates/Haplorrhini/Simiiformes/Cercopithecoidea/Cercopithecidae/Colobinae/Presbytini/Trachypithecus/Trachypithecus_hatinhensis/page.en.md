## feeding_ecology

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
A 2026 study used plant DNA barcoding on wild Hatinh langur samples from Tuyen Phu Forest, Quang Tri Province, Vietnam. It collected 116 fecal samples. Monthly samples came from the same social group of about 15–20 animals, but individual sample identity could not be tracked across months. The analysis identified 129 plant genera in 60 families; Phanera, Ficus, Sideroxylon, Ipomoea and Thunbergia together made up 59.5% of the detected diet composition. The study lacked concurrent vegetation and phenology surveys, so it cannot separate food availability, habitat differences and preference, or extend this group’s diet to the whole species.

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
Diet evidence comes from non-invasive samples from one social group in Tuyen Phu Forest, Vietnam. Barcode detections are not equivalent to food intake or nutritional contribution, and the study did not measure plant availability concurrently.
