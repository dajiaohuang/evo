## Captive guarding with an explicit identification uncertainty
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc11802484-6vq8c
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/text/en -->
The frogs maintained as Plethodontohyla mihanika laid eggs in water-filled captive cavities, and males stayed with the eggs and larvae through metamorphosis. The authors explicitly warned that separating live mihanika from notosticta by forward-bent hind-leg reach was difficult; founder identification was morphological rather than genetic. These are observations attributed to the maintained label, not certified evidence that every individual belonged to mihanika or an equivalent record for notosticta. Guarding was observed, while additional care functions and their effects on survival were not tested.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Yolk-supported larvae and captive development timing
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/10/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc11802484-6vq8c
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/10/text/en -->
The study described the maintained mihanika tadpoles as endotrophic and did not supply external larval food. They developed in the adult enclosures, with the minimum reported egg-laying-to-metamorphosis interval twenty days. This interval is not a hatching-only time, a mean development duration or a measured thermal optimum. It is conditional on captive care and the morphology-based species assignment. Rapid development was discussed as potentially useful in drying-prone habitats, but the experiment did not quantify dehydration tolerance or establish the development schedule of genetically confirmed wild mihanika populations.
