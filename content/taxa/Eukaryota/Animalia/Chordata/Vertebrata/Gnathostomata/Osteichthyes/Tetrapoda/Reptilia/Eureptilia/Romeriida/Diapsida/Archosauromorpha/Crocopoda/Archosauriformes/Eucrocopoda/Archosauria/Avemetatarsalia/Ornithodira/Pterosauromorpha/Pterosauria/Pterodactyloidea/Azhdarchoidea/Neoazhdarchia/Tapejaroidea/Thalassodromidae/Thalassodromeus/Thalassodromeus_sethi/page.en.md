# Thalassodromeus sethi


## The 1.42 m length includes the crest, not the body

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
The 2002 holotype DGM 1476-R is a nearly complete skull and mandible from Romualdo deposits in Brazil’s Araripe Basin, held at Museu de Ciências da Terra, Rio de Janeiro; MN 6678-V is its cast. The reported total skull length of 1,420 mm includes the crest, while squamosal-to-premaxillary-tip length is about 798 mm. Mandibular length is 635 mm preserved and 710 mm estimated. These use different endpoints and distinguish preservation from reconstruction; none is body length. Parts of the ventral skull and mandibular tip are missing, and this is not a complete animal skeleton.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## A large but lightly built bony crest

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
The original sagittal crest runs from the rostral tip to beyond the occiput and involves premaxillae, frontals, parietals and a supraoccipital contribution to its posteroventral edge, with internal trabecular support. The 2018 redescription likewise describes a huge but lightly built crest: only its left external side is exposed, while matrix covers the right. Bone margins of the posterior V-shaped ending remain enclosed in matrix, leading the authors to distinguish that natural outline from other V-shaped collection breakages. Not every broken edge represents original crest form.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Vascular impressions inform a hypothesis, not measured cooling

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The 2002 paper interprets branching surface channels as vascular impressions and proposes heat dissipation alongside display, recognition and aerodynamic roles. The 2018 discussion uses living toucan bills as functional comparisons and continues to consider multiple roles, while calling for more specimens to assess possible sex-linked variation and physical experiments to quantify aerodynamic effects. Bone surfaces do not directly preserve blood flow, temperature, color or courtship. One crest establishes neither cooling efficiency nor sex.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Further preparation reveals structures, not a new holotype

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The 2018 study redescribes the same holotype DGM 1476-R after further preparation exposed regions including the palate and occiput. Fused bones and the absence of open sutures support an adult interpretation, while also limiting identification of individual bones. The paper reports approximately half of the palatal surface preserved and includes small ovoid postpalatine fenestrae and an enlarged plate-like lateral ectopterygoid region in the revised diagnosis. Preparation expands observation without supplying an exact age at death, another complete individual or every missing region.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## The 2002 skimming interpretation and later tests

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The 2002 paper compares toothless blade-like rostral ends and jaw configuration with living Rynchops skimmers, proposing capture with the lower jaw immersed during flight. It also estimates a wingspan of about 4.2–4.5 m by comparison with other material, rather than measuring wings from the holotype. The 2007 study tests skimming drag and energy demands, and the 2018 revision still treats feeding as unresolved. The genus name meaning “sea runner” and the original analogy therefore do not establish observed hunting behavior.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Flume trials measure model-jaw drag

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The 2007 authors towed suspended bill and jaw models above a water flume and estimated drag from strain gauges. Pterosaur models included a jaw fragment then attributed to Thalassodromeus. Trials first used the same 4 cm immersion depth as the bird bill, followed by hydrodynamic adjustment of proportional immersion and flight-cost estimates using regressions for wingspan, mass and wing area. Fossil jaw geometry, keratinous covering and mass still affect interpretation. This page records the method without rebuilding models, repeating flume trials or recalculating all equations.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Models challenge habitual skimming without identifying another diet

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
Under their jaw-shape, immersion and flight-power assumptions, the 2007 authors find that Thalassodromeus cannot meet the energetic demands of sustained skimming. Their anatomical comparison also finds no equivalent suite of highly specialized Rynchops structures. The 2018 discussion considers alternatives such as terrestrial predation but still calls for biomechanical support. Evidence against one feeding mode does not establish another or identify prey species, hunting frequency or a complete diet.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Keep older model material separate from later jaw revisions

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
The 2007 model paper records its jaw fragment as DGM 1476-M, then referred to T. sethi, and applies its dimensions to the holotype model rather than directly measuring every holotype jaw dimension. In 2018, examination of cast MN 4703-V of NMSG SAO 251093 leads the authors to interpret that previously referred jaw, later named Banguela oberlii, as a separate species, Thalassodromeus oberlii. Both papers’ specimen numbers are retained; this page has not independently established whether the different identifiers refer to one physical object. Older referrals and borrowed dimensions are not direct holotype measurements. Read model conclusions with their material, geometry and later revisions; all matrices and subsequent taxonomy remain incomplete.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Which fossil records this name represents

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/text/en -->
Thalassodromeus sethi is accepted in the fixed PBDB pterosaur working roster, with database parent Thalassodromeus. This guide uses 1 eligible species-level occurrence record from 1 collection and 1 reference identifier. Occurrences, collections and references are different units, not specimen or individual counts.

## Age bounds of these records

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/10/text/en -->
Selected occurrences have an outer age envelope of 119.57–113.2 Ma, where Ma denotes millions of years ago. It combines the oldest early bound and youngest late bound among selected records, rather than a continuous record at every time or measured species origin, extinction or individual lifespan. Each collection retains its own age bracket. Broad intervals are not narrowed to precise specimen ages, and original dating interpretations are not revised here.
<!-- /evo:text -->


## Read preservation setting separately from living habitat

These describe database collection settings and assemblages. A depositional setting does not determine this species’ diet or habitat, and an assemblage label does not describe every fossil individually. Unfilled source fields remain unfilled.
