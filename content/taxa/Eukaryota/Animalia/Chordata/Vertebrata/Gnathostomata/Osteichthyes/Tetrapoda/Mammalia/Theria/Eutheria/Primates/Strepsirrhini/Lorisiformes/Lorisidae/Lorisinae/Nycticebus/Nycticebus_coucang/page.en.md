## ecology

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
At Cikananga Wildlife Center in West Java, Indonesia, researchers presented prey in 75 trials to 22 rescued adult greater slow lorises that had been captured from the wild. They recorded brachial-gland licking five times, four during grooming. In the sole non-grooming observation, a male approached a caterpillar, withdrew to a perch and licked the gland without trying to catch it. This captive rescue-center study does not show how wild lorises use venom during prey capture.
