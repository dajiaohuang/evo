## Thigh patches, variable folds and preservation
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7080247-scggv
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
Pictus was described with basal toe webbing and fringes, a centrally smooth and laterally granular belly, and pale yellow thigh patches on a brown background. In alcohol those patches became whitish. The variation account recorded both present and absent dorsolateral folds, a female without dorsal tubercles, and some smooth bellies; its age labels and voucher examples should not be expanded into a universal developmental rule. These are specimen-based states, not an invariant colour-and-fold identification key. Live colour and preserved appearance remain separate observations.

## Northern Mato Grosso and Jacareacanga records
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7080247-scggv
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The 2020 account listed pictus from Cotriguaçu, Novo Mundo, Tabaporã, Paranaíta and Sinop in Mato Grosso, and Jacareacanga in Pará, Brazil. Its habitat paragraph described edges of preserved forest near pastures. These records do not establish continuous occupation between municipalities or demonstrate that all life stages prefer edge habitat. They are not a current range-wide occupancy survey, population estimate or proof that pasture conversion is harmless. No species-specific nesting site or complete seasonal schedule is inferred from the short locality paragraph.

## Four to five pulsed notes with an overlapping diagnostic range
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7080247-scggv
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
Pictus calls were reported with four to five pulsed notes and four recorded Jacareacanga males, archived as MAP-V 313–316. The four-note condition overlaps giorgii, while both four and five overlap moa. Note counts must therefore be read with other acoustic and morphological evidence rather than as unique identifiers. Differences from compared species are attributed to the study and its recordings; they do not demonstrate mate rejection or a field-tested recognition threshold. The diagnosis’s short duration values are not reassigned as full-call durations here.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Sympatry with pluvian does not directly test gene flow
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7080247-scggv
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The study recorded pictus and pluvian together in some northern Mato Grosso localities, yet recovered distant mitochondrial placement and substantial marker differences. Its phylogeny used concatenated 16S and COI mitochondrial sequences, with no sampled haplotype sharing among the four newly described species. These observations contribute to integrative delimitation but do not establish the absence of nuclear introgression or experimentally prove infertility of crosses. The discussion’s secondary-contact explanation was a possibility, not a directly reconstructed movement history for pictus.
