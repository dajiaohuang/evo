The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## Pond specimens in a two-year wet-season survey
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10348306-68ltj
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
The Tena River microbasin study in Napo, Ecuador, recorded 38 Boana punctata specimens in ponds during its visual encounter surveys. Three ponds and three river transects were each visited six times, in January–March 2020 and February–April 2021. Water was collected in the morning before night surveys by three researchers for 30 minutes per site. The 38 specimens describe this sampling effort, rather than population density, a complete regional inventory or a species-wide abundance estimate.

## Repeated visual records and molecular detections differ
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10348306-68ltj
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
Boana punctata was repeatedly observed at pond ChP2. The paper reports fitted detection probabilities of 0.49 for visual surveys and 0.58 for eDNA. These estimates came from presence–absence matrices in a single-species, single-season occupancy model, although visits span two calendar years. They are conditional on this design and identification workflow; they are not universal assay sensitivity or probabilities that every sampled water body contains a living frog. The deposited matrices and model assumptions were not independently reanalysed.
