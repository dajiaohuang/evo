## ecology

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
Heymann and Aquino reviewed records from the Peruvian Amazon and found that most known sites were in non-flooded terra firme forest or mixed habitats, so the Ucayali bald uacari should not be summarized as a flooded-forest specialist. They also note that their material combines published work, unpublished reports and personal observations. The highest encounter rate came from the relatively high-elevation, less disturbed Sierras de Contamana, so habitat quality cannot be separated from lower human disturbance as an explanation. The article uses the older name C. calvus ucayalii.

## communication

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
León and Bowler classified 1,155 vocalizations from one wild population using acoustic and behavioral analyses. They described 12 distinguishable call types, including two not previously reported, High-chick and Shriek; Hic was the most common call. They also observed calls paired with tail wagging, suggesting that visual and acoustic signals can work together. The findings provide a baseline for acoustic monitoring at a local population, not a complete communication inventory for the species.
