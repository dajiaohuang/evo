# Eosimias (Shanghuang and Yuanqu tarsal sample)

## atlas-profile / overview

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/overview -->
Isolated calcanei and tali from Shanghuang and Yuanqu were attributed to Eosimias and compared in a character matrix testing anthropoid affinity; they are not dentally associated skeletons, a direct ancestor, or a complete record of Eosimias anatomy.

## An ankle assembled from scattered finds
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/0/text/en -->
Gebo and colleagues describe isolated calcanei and tali from fissure fillings at Shanghuang and from the Yuanqu Basin in China. The fossils are small pieces of the ankle rather than a foot preserved with teeth or a skull. Their attribution to Eosimias relies on comparisons with the taxon’s known anatomy, so the locality and specimen records should be kept visible when reading the conclusions.

## Testing early anthropoid anatomy
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/1/text/en -->
The authors scored ankle characters in a comparative matrix to ask whether the fossils fit an early anthropoid pattern. The bones show a mixture of features rather than a simple checklist, and the resulting placement depends on which characters and fossil comparisons enter the analysis. The paper therefore offers a test of anthropoid affinity, not an uncontested label read directly from one bone.

## An ankle is not the whole animal
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/2/text/en -->
The studied tarsals are not dentally associated with the Eosimias fossils that define the genus, and they do not reveal a complete locomotor repertoire, diet or habitual habitat. Attribution and evolutionary position are separate inference steps. This small postcranial sample adds a useful anatomical test while leaving much of the animal’s body and ecology unknown.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## atlas-profile / readerLimitations

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerLimitations/en -->
This account is limited to isolated ankle bones and their comparative attribution. It is not a complete anatomical or ecological description of Eosimias, and the fossils do not directly establish a species-wide locomotor pattern or direct ancestry.
