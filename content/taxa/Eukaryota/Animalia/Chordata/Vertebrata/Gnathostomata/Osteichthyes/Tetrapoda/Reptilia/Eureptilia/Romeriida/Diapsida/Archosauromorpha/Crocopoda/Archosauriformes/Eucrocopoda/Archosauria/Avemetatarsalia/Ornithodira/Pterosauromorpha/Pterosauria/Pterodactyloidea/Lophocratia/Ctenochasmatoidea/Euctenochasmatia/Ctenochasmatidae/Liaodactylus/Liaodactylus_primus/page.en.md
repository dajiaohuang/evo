# Liaodactylus primus


## Filtering clues preserved in a skull

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
The 2017 original description establishes Liaodactylus primus from holotype PMOL-AP00031: a nearly complete skull, articulated mandibles and atlas-axis complex from the Tiaojishan Formation about 500 m west of Daxishan village, Jianchang, Liaoning, China. Its skull is 133 mm long, with a slightly concave dorsal profile in lateral view and a rostrum about 49.1% of skull length. This specimen supplies no complete wing or body skeleton. Its skull measurement is not converted into a measured wingspan, body mass or species-wide adult size range.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## A comb of teeth shortening toward the rear

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
The authors record 152 marginal teeth across both sides of the upper and lower jaws. Anterior teeth are long, needle-like and inclined laterally; they shorten posteriorly until the last teeth are peg-like. Close spacing produces a comb-like dentition. The upper row extends beneath the anterior part of the nasoantorbital opening rather than being confined to the tip. This arrangement explains the filtering interpretation more specifically than a high tooth count alone. The 152 teeth are the specimen count in the description, not population abundance or a constant count established for every individual.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## How proportions and palate features aid recognition

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The diagnostic combination also includes a nasoantorbital opening about 31% of skull length, a mandibular symphysis about 30.5% of jaw length, and a lateral pterygoid process subdividing the subtemporal opening. Confluence of the nostril and antorbital opening links it to the broader Monofenestrata in the authors’ framework; that shared feature alone does not identify this species. Diagnosis compares a suite of preserved structures. The specimen and palate were not independently examined or redrawn here, and these few proportions are not a field-identification formula established across age, deformation or material differences.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## A filtering interpretation is not an observed diet

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
Dense, slender, laterally inclined teeth lead the authors to interpret Liaodactylus as a filter feeder and compare it with Ctenochasma, Gnathosaurus and Pterodaustro. Possible crustaceans, small molluscs, insect larvae or plankton are discussed for those comparative taxa, but no identifiable gut contents are reported for the Liaodactylus holotype. Its teeth support an interpretation of filtering small food items without transferring a complete comparative food list, a particular water-intake mechanism or modern flamingo behavior to this species.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## The authors’ interpretation at about 160 million years

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The paper summarizes Tiaojishan volcanic-rock dates as 161.8 ± 0.4 to 159.5 ± 0.6 Ma and uses them to interpret this approximately 160 Ma pterosaur. These are stratigraphic dates cited by the authors, not a fresh dating of the specimen here. The PBDB 161.5–154.8 Ma record below retains its separate precision. Their 2017 claim of the earliest known ctenochasmatid filtering record is a dated interpretation, not a continuously updated earliest-record ruling. Their phylogenetic analysis places Liaodactylus as sister to Ctenochasma, without establishing the latter’s direct descent from this animal or species.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## The sample limits of morphospace comparison

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The authors add the new taxon to an existing phylogenetic dataset of 113 species and 224 characters, yielding three most-parsimonious trees. A separate feeding-morphology comparison uses 89 species and 34 characters, assigns inferred feeding adaptations to ten categories in advance, then compares morphospace using multidimensional scaling (MDS) and permutational multivariate analysis of variance (PERMANOVA). This examines agreement between morphology and prior dietary interpretations rather than observing 89 species feed or sampling 89 Liaodactylus individuals. Supplementary coding, all dietary source assignments and statistical operations were not independently reviewed or rerun here; evolutionary patterns retain their sample and method limits.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Evidence limits

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
This batch is a guide to PBDB occurrence records dated 2026-10-02, not a complete species monograph. Entries belong to the fixed 276-item pterosaur working roster, retain the current same name and a complete parent chain to the recorded Pterosauria storage anchor, and have usable species-level occurrences without uncertain-identification qualifiers. Database accepted names can connect older combinations; that database correspondence does not decide specimen identifications or all taxonomic disputes and is not merged with same-name COL concepts. Collection sites, stratigraphic intervals, depositional settings and collection-level preservation modes have distinct meanings. They do not directly establish complete range, origin or extinction time, fixed species ecology, biological abundance or the condition of every specimen. Original descriptions, revisions, morphology, diet and behavior remain incompletely assembled;
New paragraphs use all main scientific sections of Zhou et al. (2017): abstract, introduction, methods, systematic description and discussion, with captions 1–3. Figure pixels, supplements/matrices and specimens were not examined, and trees/statistical analyses were not rerun. Skull measurements, stratigraphic dates, comb-dentition filtering interpretations, dietary categories and observed feeding remain separate; “earliest” is bounded to the authors’ 2017 judgment. Group comparisons also use the 2023 Balaenognathus selections actually read in P5-139 without transferring its pumping model to this species.
