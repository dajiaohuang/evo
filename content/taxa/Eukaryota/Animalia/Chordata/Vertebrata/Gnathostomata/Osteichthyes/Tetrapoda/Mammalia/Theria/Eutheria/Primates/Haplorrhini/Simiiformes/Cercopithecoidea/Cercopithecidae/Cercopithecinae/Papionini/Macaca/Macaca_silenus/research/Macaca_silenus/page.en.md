# Macaca silenus

## atlas-profile / overview

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/overview -->
COL26.8 accepted species usage 3WWP6 identifies Macaca silenus (Linnaeus, 1758).
<!-- /evo:text -->

## atlas-profile / ecology / diet

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/ecology/diet -->
At Puthuthottam, the studied troop consumed plant parts from 19 plant species and other non-plant foods; plant feeding was greater in the forest interior than at the forest edge, while invertebrate feeding was greater in the open patch than in the interior. This is a single-troop dry-season result.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## atlas-profile / ecology / habitat

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/ecology/habitat -->
During over 480 follow-hours in one habituated troop, active foraging was higher in the open forest patch and forest edge, and lower in the human settlement than in the forest interior; the activity budget differed between interior and settlement. One troop was observed from February to May 2016.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Sampling across the Western Ghats
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/0/text/en -->
The COL26.8 checklist accepts Macaca silenus. A 2010–2012 genetic study collected wild fecal samples at 15 sites in Karnataka, Kerala and Tamil Nadu; it also analyzed separately identified captive blood samples. These records document sampled places, not the complete present-day range.

## Activity in a forest-fragment matrix
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/1/text/en -->
From February to May 2016, researchers logged more than 480 follow-hours for one habituated group at Puthuthottam, Tamil Nadu, across forest interior, edge, an open patch and a human settlement. Active foraging was higher in the open patch and at the edge, and lower in the settlement than in the interior. This is one group in one dry-season study.

## Foods recorded in the same troop
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/2/text/en -->
The Puthuthottam troop ate parts from 19 plant species as well as non-plant foods. In the study’s habitat comparisons, invertebrate feeding was more frequent in the open patch than in forest interior, while plant feeding was greater in the interior than at the edge. ## A four-group seed-dispersal preprint
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/3/text/en -->
A 2024 bioRxiv version-one preprint reports 375.9 hours of focal observations across four groups in a 92-ha Puduthottam fragment. Subadults consumed a greater variety of native and non-native fruits than adult males or females, and dispersed fewer Ficus seeds than adult females. The sampling year was not reported; this result has not been certified by peer review.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Mitochondrial lineages around the Palghat Gap
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/4/text/en -->
The Western Ghats study distinguished sampled lineages north and south of the Palghat Gap. A fossil-calibrated model using 893 mitochondrial bases estimated a mean most-recent common ancestor at 2.11 million years. That model is not a nuclear-genome species tree or a direct fossil date for the origin of M. silenus.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Genetic diversity in forest fragments
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/5/text/en -->
A separate comparison of HVR-I mitochondrial samples from eight Anamalai forest fragments found lower diversity than in nearby continuous-forest samples. This comparison describes those samples and markers; it does not directly measure current population viability or predict extinction risk.

## atlas-profile / readerLimitations

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerLimitations/en -->
This bilingual reader draft combines selected genetic and local ecological studies, not a complete species review. The local troop evidence is season-bounded, and the seed-dispersal study is an unreviewed preprint. No current range-wide ecology or formal conservation assessment is established here; the dossier remains incomplete and not independently reviewed.
