## A rainy-season skin-microbiota study
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12795882-fzwq
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
This study sampled salamanders on two consecutive nights per site in October 2019 in secondary cloud-forest fragments with previous agricultural or livestock use. A. cafetalera was the most widely represented species across the sampled sites, allowing a separate within-species analysis. That sampling coverage is study-specific and does not establish a complete geographic range or the same exposure history for every population.

## Community differences associated with previous land use
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12795882-fzwq
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
Across the sampled A. cafetalera, skin bacterial communities differed between the two former land-use categories using both unweighted and weighted UniFrac comparisons. Overall alpha-diversity tests were reported as showing no category difference. These are observational microbial associations, not a controlled demonstration that land use caused altered disease resistance. Some stratified alpha-diversity wording and the figure caption do not agree clearly with the printed statistics, so a categorical conclusion for those subsets is withheld.

## Four positive Bd samples with no species-specific denominator here
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12795882-fzwq
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
The paper reported four Bd-positive samples from A. cafetalera. Its total of five positives among 111 swabbed salamanders pooled six species, with the other positive from Parvimolge townsendi. The pooled denominator is not used to calculate an A. cafetalera infection prevalence, and a positive sample is not by itself evidence of clinical disease or mortality.

## Wild-population skin bacterial sampling in a comparative study
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10571319-fzwq
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
Aquiloeurycea cafetalera was one of six Mexican plethodontid species contributing 111 newly generated skin-microbiome samples to the 2023 comparative study. The methods identify these six species as sampled from wild populations; 111 is their pooled total, not a count for this species. The study used 16S rRNA amplicons to characterize bacterial communities. Its aquatic or terrestrial habitat labels refer to where and when an animal was sampled, rather than a complete species life-history classification. The methods subsequently refer to “five species” despite listing six plethodontids plus the axolotl; this inconsistent scope is retained and the rinse and swab protocol is not independently certified for every listed species. The pooled diversity and habitat associations are not species-specific infection resistance, a natural-history census or a demonstrated probiotic treatment.
