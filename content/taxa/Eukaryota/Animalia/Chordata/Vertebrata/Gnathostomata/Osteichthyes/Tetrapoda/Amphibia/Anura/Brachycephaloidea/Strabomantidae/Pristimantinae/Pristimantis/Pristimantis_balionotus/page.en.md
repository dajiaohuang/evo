## One-note, two-note and longer calls stay separate
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7482940-6vwgz
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
Three males were recorded at Madrigal in2018 and Abra de Zamora in2019. Calls are usually one or two whistle-like notes, occasionally up to eight. Table2’s0.069±0.005s duration summarizes31measurements; the text separately reports double-note calls averaging0.355±0.110s. Mean rate is10.0±1.610calls/min across three males, and dominant frequency2431.7±59.371Hz across69measurements. Different note structures and measurement units are not pooled into a universal call duration.

## Two subpáramo localities and terrestrial bromeliads
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7482940-6vwgz
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/text/en -->
The source reports Abra de Zamora and Madrigal about9km south, encounters2770–2960m in subpáramo, and most Abra specimens outside the national park. All encountered animals were in or on terrestrial bromeliads. Calling was recorded infrequently in several months, on highly humid rain/fog nights. This association is not an experimentally demonstrated humidity threshold or a complete annual phenology census.
