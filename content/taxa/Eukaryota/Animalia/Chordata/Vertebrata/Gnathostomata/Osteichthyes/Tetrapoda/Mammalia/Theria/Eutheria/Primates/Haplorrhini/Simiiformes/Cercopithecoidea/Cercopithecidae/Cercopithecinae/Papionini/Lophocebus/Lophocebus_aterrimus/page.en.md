## lakeTumba

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
A two-year study in secondary forest on the west bank of Lake Tumba, then Zaire, reported multi-male, multi-female groups of 14–19 animals. The observed mangabeys were arboreal, fed mostly on fruit and nuts, and often associated with other arboreal monkeys. These are records from one forest site in the 1980s, not current abundance or a complete account of the species.

## surveyMethod

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
A later Salonga National Park study evaluated line-transect cutting disturbance across 378 km and five primate species. For L. aterrimus, the authors found that observed behavior took at least ten days to return to an unaltered state after transect cutting. This is a survey-method result from that site; it cautions against treating short-lag counts as undisturbed detections, rather than describing a fixed species trait.
