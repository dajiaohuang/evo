## overview

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
COL26.8 accepts Simias concolor (4XF7W); the Mammal Diversity Database gives “Pig-tailed Langur” as its preferred English common name. Hadi and colleagues describe the species as endemic to the Mentawai Islands. The Chinese display name 豚尾叶猴 is editorial.

## socialOrganization

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
A 2009 study described three free-ranging groups on Siberut. Their mean group size was 8.7 ± 1.1 individuals, with an adult male:female ratio averaging 1:3. The authors considered polygyny the most likely mating system, but three groups are too small a sample to treat these values as species-wide constants.

## habitatComparison

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
In mixed primary forest in northern Siberut, GPS and scan/focal observations covered three Simias groups and two sympatric Presbytis potenziani groups. Simias used lower canopy levels (<20 m) more often and fed predominantly on leaves; the reported Pianka dietary-overlap index between the sampled species was 0.32. These are local interspecific comparisons, not a range-wide habitat or diet profile.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## breeding

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
At the undisturbed Pungut site on Siberut, researchers recorded 11 births in eight groups over two years. Births occurred in seven months in that sample; the authors cautioned that sample sizes were very small. The result challenges an earlier report of births only in June and July at that site, but does not by itself establish timing across the species’ range.
