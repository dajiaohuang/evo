# Aymberedactylus cearensis


## A nearly complete mandible anchors the known anatomy

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
The 2016 description designates MN 7596-V, housed at Museu Nacional/UFRJ, as the holotype. It is a three-dimensionally preserved mandible about 270 mm long, lacking the left retroarticular process and part of the dentary crest. No associated upper jaw, postcranial skeleton or complete wing is known. The 2025 review lists no referred specimens, so this single mandible cannot supply a complete body or an age series.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## A toothless, relatively broad Y-shaped mandible

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
The dentaries fuse anteriorly into a symphysis about half the total mandibular length. The rami expand abruptly behind it, producing a pronounced Y shape. The authors report a width-to-length ratio of about 0.34 and an angle near 30° between the rami, under their stated measurement conventions. Three-dimensional preservation does not remove all distortion: dorsoventral compression may have reduced the original downward curvature of the tip.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## An elongated retroarticular process and a rugose splenial fossa

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The original diagnosis identifies two autapomorphies: a retroarticular process about 7% of mandibular length and a shallow splenial fossa with distinctive fine rugosity. A deep symphyseal shelf, short shallow dentary fossa and continuously concave dorsal symphyseal surface complete the combination. Damage to the dentary fossa’s ventral margins prevents assessment of its entire original length.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## A shallow crest and horny covering have inferential limits

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
Much of the dentary crest is damaged. Preserved anterior and posterior ventral edges and the widths of broken bases suggest a shallow crest rather than a directly measured complete height. Small posterodorsal neurovascular foramina suggest a possible horny sheath. Neither a complete beak covering nor a crest on the skull roof is preserved for this species, leaving color, full outline and soft-tissue architecture unknown.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Sutures support a subadult; wingspan comes from comparison

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
Visible dentary–surangular and splenial–angular sutures, together with incomplete ossification interpreted at the joint surface, led the 2016 authors to regard the holotype as a late subadult. Postcranial maturity criteria are unavailable. The estimate of roughly 2 m wingspan is scaled from other taxa’s mandibles, rather than measured from preserved wings or established as the species’ adult maximum.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Jaw-opening control and weak biting are functional hypotheses

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The authors infer that the thin symphysis, spread rami and small muscle-attachment fossae would limit resistance to shaking and torsion or handling large struggling prey. The long retroarticular process suggests a relatively developed jaw-depressor muscle and better control of opening. These are bone-based comparative interpretations rather than preserved muscles, measured bite force or an observed meal. Toothlessness alone does not establish obligate fruit eating.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## The original placement depends on matrix changes

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
Adding the species to the original matrix left it in an unresolved relationship with Tapejarinae and Thalassodrominae. After modifying symphyseal proportions and adding jaw width, the strict consensus of five most parsimonious trees placed it as an early-diverging tapejarine. The authors also corrected serrated-tooth miscoding in toothless taxa. This distinction is retained without independently checking the supplementary matrix or rerunning analyses; inconsistent proportion-character numbering in the text is not adjudicated here.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Compare later classification with Caupedactylus

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
The 2025 review retains the species as valid, distinguishes it from Tupandactylus by retroarticular length and the symphysis’s dorsal step, and summarizes a sister relationship with [Caupedactylus ybaka](../../Caupedactylus/Caupedactylus_ybaka/page.en.md). Its non-tapejarid tapejariform scheme differs from the 2016 paper and the fixed directory’s Tapejarinae placement. The review rejects subadult status as a sufficient explanation for the missing step. Later cited matrices were not independently read in full here.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Quarry-area provenance without precise coordinates

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/text/en -->
The original study reports donation of a specimen found near Nova Olinda’s quarries, without new fieldwork or precise coordinates. It assigns the material to the Crato Formation; the 2025 review notes matrix type and coloration typical of the Nova Olinda Member while keeping the exact locality unknown. The database’s 119.57–113.2 Ma bracket remains a record boundary. Depositional waters, living habitat and geographic origin are distinct questions.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Which fossil records this name represents

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/text/en -->
Aymberedactylus cearensis is accepted in the fixed PBDB pterosaur working roster, with database parent Aymberedactylus. This guide uses 1 eligible species-level occurrence record from 1 collection and 1 reference identifier. Occurrences, collections and references are different units, not specimen or individual counts.

## Age bounds of these records

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/11/text/en -->
Selected occurrences have an outer age envelope of 119.57–113.2 Ma, where Ma denotes millions of years ago. It combines the oldest early bound and youngest late bound among selected records, rather than a continuous record at every time or measured species origin, extinction or individual lifespan. Each collection retains its own age bracket. Broad intervals are not narrowed to precise specimen ages, and original dating interpretations are not revised here.

## Evidence limits

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
All five scientific main sections and textual figure captions of the 2016 description, plus the complete genus account in the 2025 review, were read. Images, physical specimens, supplementary matrices and later primary relationship studies were not independently examined. Damage, compression, wingspan scaling, subadult interpretation, function and biogeographic hypotheses retain their scopes. Complete development, body anatomy and diet remain incomplete. A full monograph, external expert review and reader-facing delivery are pending.
