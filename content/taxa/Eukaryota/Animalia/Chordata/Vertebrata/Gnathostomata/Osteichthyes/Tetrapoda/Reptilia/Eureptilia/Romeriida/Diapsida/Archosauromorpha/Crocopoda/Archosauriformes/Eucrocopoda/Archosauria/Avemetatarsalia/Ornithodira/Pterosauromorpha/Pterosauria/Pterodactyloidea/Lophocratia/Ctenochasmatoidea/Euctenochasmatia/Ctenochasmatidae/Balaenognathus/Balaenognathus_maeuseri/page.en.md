# Balaenognathus maeuseri


## A near-complete skeleton recovered from spoil

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
The original description of Balaenognathus maeuseri is based on one near-complete, largely articulated specimen, NKMB P2011-633, reported in the Bamberg Natural History Museum. Fragments were found in September 2011 in spoil at the Wattendorf quarry in Bavaria, Germany, and the remaining pieces of the same slab were sought; it had broken into 17 pieces, with some small fragments recovered using a UV lamp at night. Its completeness does not mean it was excavated intact in place. Part of the left wing metacarpal joint region and ilium is missing, and slab gaps and minor restoration accompany the fossil.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## A spatulate snout and slender hooked teeth

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
Its most striking features are oral: the jaw tips expand into a triangular spatula with an edentulous front edge and slender teeth along the sides. The authors estimate about 130 teeth per upper side and 110 per lower side, totaling at least 480; poor preservation near the tip prevents an error-free exact count. Crown tips carry nubbins or hooklets. Opposing tooth rows interdigitate into a fine grid, while their expanded tips limit further closure; different upper and lower jaw curvature also maintains an anterior opening. This is a dental filter rather than baleen: the whale-jaw name refers to a functional analogy.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Let water pass while retaining small food

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The 2023 authors strongly favor filter feeding from this dentition: water would enter through the toothless snout opening, pass through the posteriorly narrowing spatula, then exit laterally through the interlocking dental grid, retaining small food items. A longitudinal palatal keel is interpreted as dividing and guiding the flow. Plankton is a proposed food source, not identified stomach contents from this specimen. Tooth geometry supports filtration without directly measuring meal intake, a prey-species list or a particle-size range.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Two models remain for generating water flow

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The authors consider passive suspension feeding in shallow flowing water and active buccal/gular pumping. The first uses an existing current; the second proposes coordinated jaw, tongue and hyoid movements to change oral/gular volume. A long retroarticular process offers a possible muscle lever, but these soft-tissue movements are not directly preserved. They reject aerial skimming because the broad snout and long front teeth would generate substantial drag, a functional argument from morphology. Two shallow-water filtering modes may have varied with conditions; neither is an observed exclusive behavior, and comparisons with dabbling ducks do not establish identical motions.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Skeleton, wing-membrane traces and estimated span

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
Ordinary light, UV and X-rays helped the authors distinguish overlapping bones, teeth and small soft-tissue traces. Most bones are flattened and fractured; some teeth detached during preparation and were glued back, so completeness is not absence of deformation. A small patch by the left wing phalanges is interpreted as wing membrane from its position and parallel fibres, rather than a preserved complete wing surface. Summed wing-element lengths yield an estimated span of about 1.17 m for this specimen, a skeletal reconstruction rather than a live measurement or species-wide adult range.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Lithological placement after an ex-situ discovery

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
Because the specimen was discovered ex situ, the authors assign it by distinctive restricted lithology to bed 12 of the Wattendorf Torleite Formation, a laminated-limestone unit about 150 mm thick. Their geological section cites same-horizon Aulacostephanus eudoxus for an early part of the late Kimmeridgian, while the type-horizon account also gives a broader Upper Kimmeridgian–Tithonian wording, approximately 157–145 Ma. The existing PBDB 152.21–149.2 Ma bracket is a separate database formulation. These are retained separately without constructing a more precise individual age. Depositional setting does not directly locate feeding behavior or establish a complete habitat range.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Placement within the family and the scale of comparison

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
The original description places Balaenognathus in Ctenochasmatidae from skeletal characters and phylogenetic analysis, while acknowledging weak support and unresolved relationships within the family. Comparisons with Ctenochasma, Gnathosaurus and Pterodaustro frame questions about fine teeth and filtration, but this species’ hooklets, toothless front edge and incomplete jaw closure require their own explanation. This presents the 2023 analysis without rerunning its supplementary matrix, turning neighboring branches into direct ancestors or revising the separately retained PBDB navigation path.
<!-- /evo:text -->
