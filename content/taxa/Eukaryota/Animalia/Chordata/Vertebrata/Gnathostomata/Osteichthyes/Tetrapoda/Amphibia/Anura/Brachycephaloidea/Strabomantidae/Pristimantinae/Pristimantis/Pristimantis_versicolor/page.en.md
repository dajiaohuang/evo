Four Abra de Zamora males recorded in2016/2018 produced low squeak/chirp-like calls, usually one pulsed note and sometimes up to five. Table2 reports109duration measurements averaging0.084±0.006s, four-male rate30.3±3.138calls/min and139dominant-frequency measurements averaging1753.6±80.323Hz. The text separately reports double-note0.403s and five-note1.211s means,6–14pulses/note and mean pulse rate114.58/s. Suggested social triggers and the link between quiet sound and absent sac/slits are hypotheses, not controlled causal tests.

## Night perching and year-round calling
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/11/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7482940-4mjr9
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/11/text/en -->
The species was among the most frequently encountered frogs in higher Abra de Zamora. Animals were found at night on vegetation30cm–1.5m high or in bromeliads, and calling males were encountered year round, especially on rainy nights. Relative frequency is not a density estimate; occurrence in bromeliads does not demonstrate specialization identical to colodactylus or matildae.
