The article’s charius species row describes a pulsatile call with duration 92.6 ms and dominant frequency 2.4 kHz. Its listed pulse rate is 226.6 pulses per second. Seventeen relatively short and closely packed pulses are listed; calls are not grouped. These are source summary values, not a complete range or a universal constant. Type numbers label the study’s call ordering or frequency of delivery, rather than independently demonstrated functions such as alarm or aggression. Temperature, social context and within- and among-individual variation were not fully accounted for in these summaries. Call similarity or difference alone does not establish reproductive isolation or population decline.
