# Natator depressus

## The accepted catalogue record

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
Natator depressus (Garman, 1880), COL 45TDR, is the sole strict accepted species under Natator in COL26.8. This establishes its catalogue name and placement; the reader path then turns to sampled adult skull morphology without substituting a phylogenetic result for taxonomy.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Three adult skulls and internal anatomy

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
Chatterji, Hutchinson and Jones (2021) describe three adult N. depressus skulls and use X-ray micro-CT to examine internal braincase structures. The abstract reports that their Bayesian analysis of morphological characters supports a sister-group relationship with Chelonia mydas. Keep this as a result from the study's sampled morphology, rather than a claim about every skull or an amendment to COL26.8.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Different stages in the mass fields

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/text/en -->
Historical summary values are adult body mass 74400 g; mass at birth or hatching 39.3 g. Adult mass can combine males, females and adults of unspecified sex; birth/hatching mass describes a different life stage. These are not a longitudinal series of the same individuals or a species-wide range of individual masses.

## Evidence limits

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
Three adult skulls do not establish variation across the species' age classes, populations or full range. The cited study concerns skull morphology and phylogenetic relationships; it does not supply population abundance, current distribution or conservation status. The page is source-linked, and does not reproduce figures or tables.
This batch uses historical same-name records from the 2015 life-history archive. Values are reported compilation medians or averages of the two middle values, except that maximum longevity uses the largest record found. They are not direct measurements of one individual, individual variation ranges, mean wild lifespans or current population surveys. The compilation can share and inherit data across historical species and subspecies. Matching name and family and the presence of same-name sparse rows support the association here; they do not establish equivalence to COL26.8 concepts or remove all inherited components from the reported summaries. Wild or captive conditions are not uniformly confirmed here, and underlying publications have not been individually reviewed. Diet, behavior, current range, conservation and missing fields require other sources;

## Distinguish the measured length

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
Recorded dimensions are reported maximum straight carapace length (SCL) 960 mm. SCL measures the shell in a straight line, excluding head, neck and tail. Compiled maxima are reported material, not theoretical growth limits or typical individual size. Sex-specific values may come from different localities or studies and alone cannot quantify population sexual dimorphism.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## How to read reproductive records

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
the reproductive-mode field uses the original label oviparous; sex determination is recorded as TSD; consolidated mean offspring or eggs per litter/clutch 52.8. Clutch/litter summaries can use midpoints of reported means or, when means are unavailable, extrema; they are not necessarily arithmetic averages of observed clutches. Fractions do not describe non-integer young in a litter. The lowest/highest fields are defined as endpoints of reported means rather than guaranteed smallest/largest individual clutches. Reproductive and sex-determination labels do not establish fetal nutrition or uniformity across all populations.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Limits of these trait data

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
This account paraphrases the same-name ReptTraits v1-2 entry, retaining its 2023 taxonomic framework and sources for individual traits. One row per species compresses variation in sex, age, locality and populations; matching name and family do not ensure equivalent later concepts. Reported maximum length is not an absolute species limit, and reproductive means are not fixed counts in every clutch. Many mass entries are estimated from length equations and are not presented here as measured mass. Juvenile-length headers and definitions differ and are also omitted from reader conclusions. Conservation, venom, current distribution and population trends are not updated here; unrecorded fields remain gaps. Dataset citation labels preserve the source chain and do not imply that this project independently reread every original work.
