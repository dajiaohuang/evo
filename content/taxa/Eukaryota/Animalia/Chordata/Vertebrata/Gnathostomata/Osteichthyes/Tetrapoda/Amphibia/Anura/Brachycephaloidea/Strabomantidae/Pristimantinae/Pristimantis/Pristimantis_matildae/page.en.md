## Accepted name and placement

COL26.8 accepts Pristimantis matildae Székely, Eguiguren, Ordóñez-Delgado, Armijos-Ojeda & Székely, 2020 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Strabomantidae (family) → Pristimantis (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## Size, tympanum and digit proportions
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7482940-scfq7
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
Female SVL is20.8–23.3mm (22.5±1.12SD,n4), male19.9–21.2mm (20.4±0.68,n3); the male holotype is20.2mm and0.50g. The diagnosis combines no tympanic membrane/annulus or supratympanic fold, finely tuberculate dorsal skin, slightly expanded truncate discs and lateral digit fringes. Compared with colodactylus, relative hand and foot lengths are longer in the reported samples, with inner-tarsal folds and no single large heel tubercle. These proportional contrasts do not directly measure locomotor performance.

## Bromeliad use and an unknown advertisement call
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7482940-scfq7
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
Individuals were found in or next to terrestrial and arboreal bromeliads by day and night, and retreated into plant-held water when threatened. The authors call the species common in that microhabitat. No calling males were heard; males lack vocal sac/slits and the advertisement call is unknown. These observations do not demonstrate complete life-cycle confinement to bromeliads, inability to vocalize or a quantified abundance relative to all habitats.

## Genetic comparisons and a dated protection proposal
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7482940-scfq7
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
The colodactylus-subgroup placement is accompanied by reported16S distances5.4–5.8% to colodactylus and6.6–7.5% to muranunka. These are source-reported comparisons, not remeasured supplementary matrices. The authors propose Near Threatened despite two localities and an estimated area below50km², citing relative frequency and national-park protection. This2020 recommendation is not a verified present assessment or evidence that small range alone predicts risk quantitatively.
