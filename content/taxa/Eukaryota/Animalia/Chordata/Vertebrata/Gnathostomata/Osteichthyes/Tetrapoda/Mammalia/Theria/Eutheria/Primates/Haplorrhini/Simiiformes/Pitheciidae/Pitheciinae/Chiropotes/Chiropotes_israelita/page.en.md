## classification

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
This page follows fixed COL26.8 accepted species ID 5Y6KL for *Chiropotes israelita* (Spix, 1823). The current MDD *C. chiropotes* page includes israelita in its name and taxonomy notes and says their relationship is not fully resolved; its range summary applies to that *C. chiropotes* entry and is not an independent range boundary for this fixed COL identity. Carneiro et al. (2023) treat *C. israelita* as a junior synonym and use *C. chiropotes* and *C. sagulatus* for forms west and east of the Branco River, respectively; support for this pair was weaker in nuclear-only analyses. The page preserves the checklist ID and reports competing treatments without merging name rows.

## taxonomicEvidence

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
Bonvicino et al. (2003) analysed 25 specimens from several Brazilian Amazon localities. They reported distinguishing pelage and described karyotypic and molecular differences; on that basis they referred to the Rio Negro material as C. israelita and treated it as allopatric with the other forms they compared. This is a taxonomic argument based on historical samples, not a current range-wide survey.

## rangeModel

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
Boubli and de Lima (2009) used a maximum-entropy algorithm to model the distributions and fundamental niches of Chiropotes israelita and Cacajao in northwestern Amazonia. The mapped suitable areas and boundaries are predictions; the authors noted that further field surveys were needed to clarify regional distributions. The model is not point-level confirmation, a current population count, or a trend estimate.

## regional_survey

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
Boubli et al. (2015) reported 20 diurnal primate species from surveys in the Rio Negro–Rio Branco region, identified morphologically; they listed *C. israelita* in the assemblage between the two rivers. Their comparative phylogenetic analysis discussed relationships among populations separated by major rivers and used 94 cytochrome-*b* samples. The abstract states that sampling covered 19 of the region’s 20 species, so not every lineage relationship should be described as directly measured in that survey. This adds a geographically bounded field-survey context for the older name *C. israelita*, but is not a population estimate or a study of behavior or diet.

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
Fixed COL26.8 and the current MDD *C. chiropotes* page differ in the treatment of israelita, and a 2023 study proposes another correspondence between names and river-bank populations. This page separates the 2003 specimen analysis, 2009 predictive model and 2015 regional survey. The regional material shows that the older label *C. israelita* was used for survey records between the two rivers; it does not resolve type-based nomenclature, current species limits or the complete range of the fixed COL identity. It also provides no current population estimate, trend, behavior or diet data. This is a source-bounded introduction to taxonomy and regional records, not a population or conservation assessment; model boundaries need further field surveys. The display names are editorial, and the page has not received external expert review.
