## Daytime activity following heavy rain
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc8417023-pygfc
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
On Gorgona Island, Colombia, frogs were observed by day in litter, low vegetation and low forest-floor objects, well away from streams. During the reported visit, individuals were observed only after about six hours of heavy rain on 26 May 2016. This encounter history does not establish obligatory rain dependence or absence during every dry period.

## One male’s playback response

## Hand glands in the examined adult-male evidence

In its preserved-specimen comparison, the study lists Leucostethus siapida in the adult-male specialized mucous gland table. Types I are scored present; types II, III, IV are scored absent. These scores concern specialized glands of the hand, not the absence of ordinary mucous or serous glands, and are not prevalence estimates. A zero is a scored condition in the examined material, not proof of absence in all populations, ages or reproductive states. ## Gland types describe structure rather than chemical identity

primary-pmc8819055-pygfc

Type I glands are chiefly alveolar, with a small lumen, a single layer of secretory cells and an incomplete myoepithelial covering; they lie near the epidermis. ## Preserved material, finger numbering and staining
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc8819055-pygfc
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
No new specimens were collected for this study. Its dendrobatoid material comprised 77 specimens representing 36 source species labels; four additional specimens represented three hylodid species, and two Melanophryniscus outgroups were taken from literature. Exact preservation histories were unknown, although the authors understood the specimens to have been formalin-fixed and stored in ethanol. They usually removed one hand, decalcified it in EDTA and made 4.5 micrometre resin sections. Multiple stains tested tissue structure, neutral and acidic mucopolysaccharides, proteins and lipids. Fingers are numbered II–V under a tetrapod homology convention, so finger IV is the third of the four remaining digits. These methods describe the comparative study; they do not supply a species-specific sample size in the inspected main-text table. Appendix S1 and Tables S1–S2 remain unread, and figures were inspected only as captions.

## A proposed reproductive role remains untested
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc8819055-pygfc
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
The authors suggest that protein-containing specialized hand glands could mediate chemical courtship signals during contact with a female's head. They did not characterize the secretions, identify a pheromone, test female responses or establish whether delivery is olfactory or transdermal. Hand location and stain reactions therefore cannot certify that Leucostethus siapida uses a particular chemical mating mechanism, causes ovulation or oviposition, or secretes an adhesive. A lack of these gland types likewise does not prove a species lacks every form of chemical communication. Behavioral observations cited in the discussion come from earlier studies and populations, not new mating experiments in this histological comparison.

## Swelling detected despite earlier absence reports
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc8819055-pygfc
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/text/en -->
The examined Leucostethus siapida material showed swelling although earlier accounts had reported it absent. The study found external swelling on dorsal and axial surfaces, not on the palmar surfaces, and stresses that weak swelling may require comparison with other digits or females. This revises an observation for the studied material; it does not independently verify every earlier specimen or establish identical expression across the species.
