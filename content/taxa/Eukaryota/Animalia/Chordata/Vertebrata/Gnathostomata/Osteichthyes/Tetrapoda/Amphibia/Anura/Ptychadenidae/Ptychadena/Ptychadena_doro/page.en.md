## Accepted name and placement

COL26.8 accepts Ptychadena doro Goutte, Reyes-Velasco, Freilich, Kassie & Boissinot, 2021 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Ptychadenidae (family) → Ptychadena (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## A geographically sampled mitochondrial and nuclear dataset
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10434514-smlb6
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The 2023 Ethiopian Highlands study lists 12 mitochondrial samples and 7 nuclear-SNP samples for Ptychadena doro in Table 1, with mitochondrial nucleotide diversity π=0.18%. Samples across the twelve-species study were collected in 2011–2019; these are analysed specimens, not a population census. All assembled mitochondrial genomes had the usual two rRNAs, thirteen protein-coding genes and twenty-two tRNAs, but no species had a completely resolved control region because repeats could not be confidently assembled from short reads. The phylogenetic alignment used selected genes (13,937 bp across 303 samples), rather than an alignment of complete control regions.

## A study-defined highland clade and regional distribution
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10434514-smlb6
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The paper places Ptychadena doro in its P. neumanni clade and reports it from the western side of the Great Rift Valley. These distributions combine the authors' geographic sampling and earlier range work; they are not independently updated complete range polygons. The twelve-species highland clade is a subset of Ptychadena. Its mitochondrial-clock analysis estimated a common ancestor around 11.7 million years ago, with a reported interval of 8.827–15.494 million years. The east-of-rift ancestral reconstruction had a model probability of 69%; neither that estimate nor inferred crossings is a directly observed migration event or a nuclear-genome divergence date for this species.
<!-- /evo:text -->
