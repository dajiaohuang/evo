## Read new-slab anatomy separately from gliding models

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->

Continue to the [M. gui model study](Microraptor_gui/page.en.md) to compare fossil anatomy with reconstructed aerodynamic evidence. ## How feathered limbs enter gliding research

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
Feathers on Microraptor’s arms, legs and tail make several body regions potential lifting surfaces. A 2013 wind-tunnel study organizes them into reconstructed models to compare posture, stability and trajectories. This provides a route from anatomy to function at genus level: the model draws on the M. gui holotype and another genus-level specimen without assigning identical measured flight to three species.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Read model construction before stability and trajectories

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The [M. gui wind-tunnel study guide](Microraptor_gui/page.en.md) separates fossil morphology, modern-feather reconstruction, airflow settings, calculation assumptions and gliding interpretation. Stable-point ratios of about 4.7:1 and 2.8:1 are specific model results, not maximum performance assigned to real individuals. Flat-plate comparisons concern surface requirements without negating fossil feathers.
