## ecology

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
A study in southern Bahia, Brazil, focused on one golden-headed lion tamarin group at Colônia and also used movement records from two groups previously studied at Una Biological Reserve to model Pourouma seed dispersal. Across 26 complete observation days during fruiting season, the Colônia group averaged about 1.87 km per day; observed Pourouma seeds averaged about 111 m from their parent trees. The model suggests these short daily paths may contribute little to long-distance forest regeneration while supporting local seed deposition. Deposition outputs were sensitive to fruit-maturity and feeding-distance thresholds, so results are limited to one site, focal group, tree genus and model setup.
