## feeding

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
Martins and colleagues studied one crested capuchin group with access to forest and cultivated areas at Reserva Natural Vale in Espírito Santo, Brazil. The monkeys ate cultivated jackfruit and oil-palm fruit, but forest-fruit feeding records were more numerous in all but one month; the group used 56 forest-fruit species and six exotic cultivated species. Cultivated plots had a higher proportion of fruiting trees through the study, so this group does not represent groups without access to cultivated foods.

## ecology

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
The group’s estimated home range was 120.5 ha across the year: 102 ha in the wet season and 111.5 ha in the dry season. The authors noted that protection from hunting and both natural and cultivated fruit sources may have contributed to the relatively small range; group composition also changed during the study. This result from one group is not a species-wide home-range standard.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
Source-linked local group study; not externally expert-reviewed. Findings concern one group in a protected site where cultivated fruit was available, and should not be generalized to all groups or the full species range.
