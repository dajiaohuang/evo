## ecology

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
At Mt Calebasses, Mauritius, one group of 19–23 long-tailed macaques was observed from December 2019 to December 2020. Mean monthly home range was 10.6 ± 3.0 hectares during the high-fruit period and 17.8 ± 5.6 hectares outside it; monthly range was negatively correlated with fruit availability (r = −0.65, adjusted p = 0.037). This is a one-year record from one introduced group, not a species mean from the native range.

## ecology

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
In plant-feeding scans of the same Mauritius group, invasive plants accounted for 98% of records and 97% of fruit-feeding scans. Recorded fruit feeding was concentrated in invasive Litsea (40.2%), Psidium cattleyanum (39.3%) and Flacourtia indica (9.5%). The authors discuss possible seed-dispersal effects, but the study did not measure germination, seedling recruitment or realized plant-invasion rates.

## morphology

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
A micro-CT and geometric-morphometric study compared 13 long-tailed macaque mandibular second molars. Molars with accessory cusps were larger and had shorter relative cusp heights, while peripheral-cusp presence was associated with more centrally positioned primary cusps. The pattern broadly fit the patterning-cascade model, but the model did not explain every accessory-cusp form in the sample, which came from one primate-centre collection.

## evolution

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
A whole-genome comparison of rhesus and long-tailed macaque samples reported 184 candidate genes consistent with reinforcement-related patterns and a signal of Y-chromosome introgression from rhesus into long-tailed macaque populations. The authors described the findings as consistent with a model of reinforcement, not proof of it; the study is not a complete species phylogeny.

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
The main ecological evidence comes from one introduced group in Mauritius and does not represent typical diet or home range across the native range. Dental findings use 13 specimens, and genomic conclusions depend on allopatric and parapatric samples and their models; none is a complete species synthesis. Source-linked and not externally expert-reviewed.
