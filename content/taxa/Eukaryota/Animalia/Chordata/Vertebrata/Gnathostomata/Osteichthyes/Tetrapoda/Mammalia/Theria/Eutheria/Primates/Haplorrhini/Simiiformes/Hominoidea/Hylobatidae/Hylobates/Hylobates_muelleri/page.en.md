## behaviour

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
Mitani played male songs from different positions at similar distances from the study subjects. Responses varied with location: songs from the center of a range led mated males to guide silent group approaches, while songs from the periphery sometimes prompted group approaches or duets; playback from a neighbor’s range rarely produced duetting. The results support a role for song in intergroup spacing in that experiment, rather than a universal rule for every context.
