## Mostly infertile captive egg masses and one fertile plastic-plant clutch
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc11802484-nxldc
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
More than half of the maintained ambakoana egg masses were found on the terrarium floor under leaf litter rather than on the supplied plants. All but one mass were infertile; the single fertile clutch was laid in a plastic artificial bromeliad’s leaf axil. A few infertile masses occurred during June–September. These observations do not demonstrate successful year-round reproduction, a natural preference for plastic plants or a survival rate for the whole species. The authors suggested that the offered enclosure structures may not have provided adequate laying sites, rather than experimentally identifying the cause of infertility.

## Morphological similarity limits the breeding-label certainty
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc11802484-nxldc
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The paper explicitly noted superficial colour similarity among ambakoana, rianasoa and methueni. Its founders had been identified only by morphology, without genetic verification. Consequently, the captive laying-site and fertility observations remain attached to the authors’ maintained ambakoana identification, not a certified reconciliation of all three contemporary concepts. The study discussed Pandanus-associated natural history and offered similar-shaped plants in captivity, but the single fertile artificial-plant clutch cannot be transferred into independently observed reproduction of rianasoa or methueni.
