## sampling window

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
Estrada-Grossmann and colleagues compared regurgitated food boluses from one wild Guanay cormorant colony on Grande Pescadores Island, Peru: a pre-spill collection in November 2018 and collections from August to November 2022, seven to eleven months after the January oil spill. The birds were not individually identified or tracked, so the comparison describes colony samples, not changes in the same birds.

## prey composition

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
The study identified five fish species across its samples and reported Peruvian anchoveta (Engraulis ringens) as the predominant prey before and after the spill. Anchoveta made up 99.736% and 99.168% of the numerical prey proportions in the two periods, respectively; the corresponding occurrence proportions were 98.165% and 99.881%. The paper's post-spill sample denominator is inconsistent: Methods and Results state 998 boluses, while Table 1 gives N = 837. The figures here follow the table as printed and are not recalculated.

## interpretation limits

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
This is a two-period, one-colony diet comparison, with no samples collected immediately after the spill. The design cannot establish that the spill caused a dietary change or the absence of one, and the study did not measure cumulative contaminant exposure in prey. Its results do not establish a species-wide diet or current population condition.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
All biological claims are limited to regurgitated boluses from one wild colony at Grande Pescadores Island and two collection periods. The paper reports conflicting post-spill bolus totals (998 in Methods and Results; 837 in Table 1); values are retained as printed. The observational comparison does not show an oil-spill cause, immediate effects, or contaminant exposure, and cannot establish a species-wide diet. The Chinese common name is an editorial translation. This page has not received external expert review.
