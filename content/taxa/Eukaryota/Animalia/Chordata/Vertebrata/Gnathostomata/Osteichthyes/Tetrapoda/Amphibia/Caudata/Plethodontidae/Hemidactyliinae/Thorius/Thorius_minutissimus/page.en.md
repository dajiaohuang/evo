## A small female sample and posterior-vent standard length
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5119241-56kt3
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
The redescription retained female holotype AMNH 52673, collected at Santo Tomás Tecpan, Oaxaca, on 3 March 1946, and examined two additional females IBH 23011–12 from near Santo Tomás Teipan. Female mean standard length was 23.0 mm, range 22.3–23.6 mm; the diagnosis said the only known adult male was 19 mm. Standard length follows the paper’s snout-to-posterior-vent definition. The female summary reported no maxillary teeth, mean seven vomerine teeth with range 5–9, and mean SL/tail ratio 1.03. These sparse sex-specific observations do not establish a population-level dimorphism test or universal body-size limits.

## Two skeleton preparations and specimen-level tooth differences
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5119241-56kt3
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/text/en -->
Osteology used CT-scanned female IBH 23011 and cleared-and-stained female IBH 23012. Skulls were weakly ossified apart from developed otic capsules. The external summary reported one premaxillary tooth per female, whereas skeletal examination found none in IBH 23012 and one mature plus two unerupted teeth in IBH 23011; these different observations remain explicit. The reported phalangeal formulae were 1–2–3–2 in the hand and 1–2–3–2–1 in the foot, and the described left and right ankles differed in fusion pattern. This limited material does not establish invariant adult skeletal architecture or developmental timing.

## Two evening road-bank captures near the type locality
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/10/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5119241-56kt3
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/10/text/en -->
The 2016 account knew the species only near its type locality. Two specimens at 2458 m were taken from a road bank in an oak-forest patch near Santo Tomás Teipan, in the early evening between 19:00 and 20:00: one partly emerged from a hole and the other was obtained by digging. No salamander was found in adjacent forest during the day; one individual was later reported in an April 2009 assessment. These observations do not prove obligate road-bank dwelling, exclusive nocturnality or absence in the forest. Nearby Bolitoglossa zapoteca records were not reported as confirmed sympatric captures.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## A name historically applied to several other species
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/11/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5119241-56kt3
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/11/text/en -->
The authors explained that minutissimus had been misapplied to populations representing several species. In particular, animals called T. minutissimus in cited 1982–1985 cranial and limb studies belonged instead to T. longicaudus. Damaged historical specimens were assigned partly from resemblance and the single known regional Thorius, an evidence boundary that remains distinct from genetic confirmation. The account cited a Critically Endangered assessment as of 2016 and requested further locality surveys. Historical results under the misapplied name are not automatically transferred to this reader, and the dated threat category is not a verified current assessment.
