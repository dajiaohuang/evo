# Nothoprocta perdicaria


## The material behind this genome

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
Sackton and colleagues (2019) added a genome for Nothoprocta perdicaria. NCBI master record PTEW00000000 belongs to PRJNA433110 and records sex as male; voucher ROM:ORN:Ctin4; tissue blood. These fields identify assembly material, not species-wide or wild-population statistics. Missing tissue or locality fields are not inferred here. The main and companion phylogenetic papers use the same genome set, not two independent population samples.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Flighted tinamous are nested within palaeognaths

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
This species is one of four tinamous in the 2019 analyses. Sequence analyses recover tinamous as sister to little bush moa, nested among lineages traditionally called ratites. Flightless palaeognaths therefore do not form a natural group excluding tinamous. Their flighted condition informs comparisons of flight-loss history; the genome tree does not measure this species’ flight distance, frequency or field behaviour.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## The name and this data account

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
Nothoprocta perdicaria is accepted in COL26.8 within Tinamidae. This account summarizes AVONET v7 data under its HBW–BirdLife 2020 name. The source records 10 measured individuals, 8 with a complete morphological set; usable sample sizes may differ among traits.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Measured beak dimensions

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
Recorded mean beak measurements are 13.5 mm from the anterior nostril edge to the tip, 5.9 mm in width and 5.5 mm in depth at that edge. These describe separate dimensions at standardized landmarks, not field-identification cutoffs.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Wing, tarsus and mass

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
Mean unflattened wing length from the carpal joint to the longest primary is 161.5 mm; mean tarsus length is 34.7 mm. Compiled mean mass is 458 g, with source category Dunning. Mass comes from separate species-average information; this does not mean every morphological specimen was weighed.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Habitat, feeding and lifestyle

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The dataset assigns habitat Shrubland, trophic niche Omnivore and lifestyle Terrestrial. These are dominant categories for cross-species comparisons; they do not imply exclusive use of one habitat or food type.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Movement and geographical summary

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
The migration field classifies this record as sedentary. Historical breeding and resident maps span latitude endpoints -41.77° to -28.41°. This excludes a complete account of non-breeding movements and is not a current-position or global occupancy estimate. Geography and morphological measurements come from different compilation steps.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Evidence limits

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
This is a guide to historical AVONET v7 data. Morphological values are species averages from the listed samples, not identification diagnoses or ranges of individual variation. Mass uses separately cited literature or weighing sources; morphological sample counts must not be assigned to mass. Habitat, diet and migration are coarse categories; geography concerns historical breeding and resident ranges only. One-to-one historical crosswalks and a matching catalogue name provide the association here, not proof of equivalent species concepts after later splits or lumps. Reproduction, current populations and conservation status require other sources.
New genome text uses Sackton et al. (2019), published main text, four figure captions and figure 1 pixels; the read sampling, marker extraction, species-tree methods, selected results and discussion passages of Cloutier et al. (2019); and ten NCBI WGS master records. Companion papers share material rather than independent population replicates. Other figure pixels, supplements, raw sequences and analysis code were not independently examined or rerun; the companion paper was not reviewed paragraph by paragraph in full. Candidate regulatory acceleration, open chromatin and a single-region chicken reporter assay are distinct evidence levels, not functional validation across all species. Complete biology and external expert review remain unfinished.
