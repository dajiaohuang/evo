## ecology

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
A 2013 thesis study at the Allpahuayo Research Center near Iquitos, Peru, used scan and instantaneous sampling. It reported a local home range of about 87.3 ha, greatest use of the middle canopy, 28 food plants and two palm species used as sleeping trees. These observations describe one study area, not the full range.

## conservation

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
Rowland et al. (2024) used line transects and distance sampling in the adjacent Villa Belén and Jaldar Indigenous territories in Peru; the paper calls the taxon S. cassiquiarensis macrodon. Estimated squirrel-monkey density was 192 versus 107 individuals/km², with no detected group-size difference; estimated primate richness was 7 versus 11 species. These results are local to the two territories and do not by themselves establish a causal logging effect or range-wide density.

## interpretation

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
This page follows the COL26.8 accepted species ID 4TZJX, Saimiri macrodon. Some field papers retain the trinomial S. cassiquiarensis macrodon. The source combination is preserved, and similar epithets are not treated as proof that checklists use identical species concepts.

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
The ecological evidence comes from one thesis study area near Iquitos, Peru, and distance sampling in two Indigenous territories; it does not represent the full range. The COL26.8 accepted name and the trinomial used by some papers are both retained. The exact MDD join supplies only the English label; the Chinese name is editorial. The page has not received external expert review. This page is not externally expert-reviewed.
