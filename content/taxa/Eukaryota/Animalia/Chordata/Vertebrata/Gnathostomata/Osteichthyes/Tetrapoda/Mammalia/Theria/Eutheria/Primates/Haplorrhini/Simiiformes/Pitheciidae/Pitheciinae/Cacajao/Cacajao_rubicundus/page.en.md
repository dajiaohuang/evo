## taxonomy

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
COL26.8 and the 2025 ICMBio assessment recognize Cacajao rubicundus at species rank. The older IUCN assessment and the 2022 molecular paper used the subspecies combination C. calvus rubicundus. The ICMBio assessment cites the later five-species treatment of bald uakaris; these changing ranks and names should not be mistaken for separate populations.

## molecular sampling

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
The 2022 systematic study included eight cytochrome-b sequences and six ddRAD samples labelled C. calvus rubicundus under the paper's subspecies taxonomy. Those genetic sample counts are not a population estimate or census.

## reported distribution

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The 2025 ICMBio assessment reports three geographically separated occurrence areas in Amazonas, Brazil: Jacurapá, Auati-Paraná and Jutaí-Solimões. It describes records along the Jacurapá channel and says local interviews corroborated occurrence in the várzea between that channel and the Solimões. It also notes contact with C. calvus in Auati-Paraná. These are assessment-reported records and do not establish a continuous or complete range. The assessment infers likely várzea association partly by comparison with other bald uakaris and says the species has not been the subject of ecological studies.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## conservation assessment

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The ICMBio SALVE sheet was published in 2025, but its Least Concern category is dated 27 September 2019. It reports no significant threats identified in that assessment and cites a 2.31% habitat-loss calculation for 1990–2020 across the known geographic distribution using MapBiomas 6.0. The sheet says total population size, mature-individual count, group size and population trend are unknown, and calls for ecological and population studies. The category date and reported model result are not a new 2025 population survey.
