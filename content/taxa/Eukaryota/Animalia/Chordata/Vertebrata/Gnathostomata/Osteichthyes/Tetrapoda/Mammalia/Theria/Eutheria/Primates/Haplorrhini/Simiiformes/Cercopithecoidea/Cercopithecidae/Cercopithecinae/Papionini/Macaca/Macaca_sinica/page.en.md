## ecology

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
A 2019 fecal survey in Sri Lanka covered 14 sites and examined 180 samples assigned to three geographic subspecies. Within each subspecies, 20 samples came from each of urban, suburban, and wild habitats. This design compares fecal detections at the sampled places and times; it is not a census of the species across its range.

## ecology

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
In the sampled dry-zone subspecies M. s. sinica, parasite-positive samples were 90% (18/20) in urban sites, 70% (14/20) in suburban sites, and 45% (9/20) in wild sites. The authors reported a significant urban–wild contrast (χ²=9.231, p=.002). This cross-sectional comparison does not show that urbanization caused infection, and a fecal detection is not a clinical diagnosis. Although the article title refers to zoonotic parasites, this survey did not test transmission between macaques and people.

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
This source-linked guide covers a local parasite study, not a comprehensive species account; it is not externally expert-reviewed. Results are limited to the subspecies and sites sampled in 2019. Cross-sectional fecal screening does not establish causation, clinical disease, or transmission to people, and cannot represent the full range, all seasons, or unsampled populations.
