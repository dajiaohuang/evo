# Phoenicopterus chilensis


## Eight juveniles sampled over eight force-plate sessions

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
Chang and Ting (2017) recorded ground reaction forces and reference video from eight juvenile Phoenicopterus chilensis at Zoo Atlanta over eight sessions. Sample masses were 0.25–1.40 kg and leg lengths 0.17–0.40 m, describing juveniles rather than adult species ranges. Force-plate sampling at 200 Hz and video toe positions supported estimates of centre of pressure beneath the foot. Eight sessions and eight birds are distinct counts, not sixteen individuals.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Smaller pressure-centre excursions when quiet with eyes closed

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
During quiet one-legged standing with eyes closed, the juveniles’ centre of pressure lay near the tarsometatarsophalangeal joint and moved less than during activity. Main-text 95% excursion radii were 3.2 ± 0.5 mm when quiescent and 5.1 ± 0.4 mm when active. These describe movement of the resultant force location beneath the foot, not displacement of body centre of mass or fixed species thresholds. The figure’s representative individual values are separate from these summaries.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## A state transition in one twenty-minute recording

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
In one highlighted twenty-minute continuous trial, a bird initially kept its eyes open, then became quiescent with eyes closed and possibly fell asleep, with a more localized centre of pressure. Possible sleep was inferred from behaviour, not sleep staging by brain recordings. This example does not establish wild continuous sleep duration or resting posture across all ages, surfaces and weather.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Balance trajectories cannot replace muscle recordings

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
Localized loading and small excursions are compatible with balance requiring little muscle activity, but the authors explicitly cannot exclude high tonic muscle coactivation. There were no direct muscle-activity or metabolic measurements. Juvenile force-plate results must also remain distinct from adult P. ruber cadaver angles. Passive mechanisms have experimental support as candidates, while actual muscular effort in live birds needs direct measurement.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Measured beak dimensions

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
Recorded mean beak measurements are 79.1 mm from the anterior nostril edge to the tip, 24.4 mm in width and 35 mm in depth at that edge. These describe separate dimensions at standardized landmarks, not field-identification cutoffs.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Wing, tarsus and mass

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
Mean unflattened wing length from the carpal joint to the longest primary is 401.8 mm; mean tarsus length is 245 mm. Compiled mean mass is 2277 g, with source category Dunning. Mass comes from separate species-average information; this does not mean every morphological specimen was weighed.
