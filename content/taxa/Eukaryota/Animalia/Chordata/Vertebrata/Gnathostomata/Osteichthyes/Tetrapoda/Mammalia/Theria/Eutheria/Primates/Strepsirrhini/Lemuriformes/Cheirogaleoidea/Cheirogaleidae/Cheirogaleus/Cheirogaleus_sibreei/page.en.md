# Cheirogaleus sibreei

## How an older village name links to the type locality

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
The 2014 account lists sibreei type BM 97.9.1.160 as a skin and skull from Ankeramadinika. A 1904 missionary account links the disused village name to the vicinity of Ambatolaona, compared with Forsyth Major’s description of a day’s journey east of Antananarivo. This is a historical locality reconstruction, not new type GPS coordinates or a current occupancy survey.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## The 2014 support interpretation retains historical differences

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
The revision treats sibreei as a monophyletic species supported by earlier genetic studies while calling for more fieldwork. The 2009 and 2010 material below retains its own analyses and assignment limits; a 2014 summary does not erase unresolved interpretations in earlier work. Pinned acceptance, authors’ species-limit interpretations and support from local samples are separate levels.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## A dark dorsal stripe and cranial comparisons retain group scope

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The sibreei-group description includes medium-grey head and upperparts, a deep-brown dorsal stripe and dark tail tip, with variable eye rings and dark but not black, sparsely haired ears. Cranial comparisons describe a short, low, slightly convex facial skeleton, elevated nasals and large auditory bullae, alongside high tooth cusps and a triangular third molar. Resemblance to the minusculus type does not merge them into one genetic group or identify a wild animal from a dorsal stripe alone.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## One shared-tree-hole observation is not a general grouping rule

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The account reports four animals identified as crossleyi and one as sibreei occupying one tree hole at Anjozorobe, attributed to Louis’s personal observation rather than a systematic survey of repeated frequency. Its 2014 range summary from Anjozorobe through Tsinjoarivo to Ranomafana does not replace a current map. Compare the local study in [crossleyi](../Cheirogaleus_crossleyi/page.en.md) and missing-sample account in [minusculus](../Cheirogaleus_minusculus/page.en.md), then return to the [genus route](../page.en.md).
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Ecological evidence

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
A 2026 study tracked 11 Sibree's dwarf lemurs in the Andasivodihazo fragment at Tsinjoarivo and compared them with 13 Crossley's dwarf lemurs at the same site. During pre- and post-hibernation observations, Sibree's dwarf lemurs travelled farther at night and used the fragment's western area more often. Their mean home range was larger, but the full-sample difference was not statistically significant; the authors caution that the sample was small.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Classification

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The taxonomic history and genetic evidence for Sibree's dwarf lemur are not fully congruent. A 2009 multilocus study placed its mitochondrial data within the C. medius lineage but could not clearly resolve its position morphologically; a 2010 Tsinjoarivo study used mitochondrial and nuclear markers to support a distinct genetic clade for local C. sibreei-like material.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Evidence limits

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
The ecological comparison covers 24 individuals at one high-altitude Tsinjoarivo fragment, and earlier taxonomy work reported unresolved morphological placement. Local movement and home-range differences should not be generalized to the species. This page has not received external expert review.
