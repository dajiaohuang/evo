## research

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
Field evidence for Wolf's monkey is sparse in the material used here. A Lomako Forest census study included it in a six-monkey comparison of habitat use and mixed-species associations; the authors described its group sizes as similar to those reported from other sites. A later ground-camera survey in Lomami recorded one independent C. wolfi event among a broader survey of arboreal monkeys. One camera event is only a detection, not an abundance measure or behavioural profile. These studies support a local research note, while species-specific diet, demography and range limits remain poorly resolved here.

## Lomako habitat

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
McGraw (1994) reported a preliminary multispecies census from Lomako Forest, then in Zaire, comparing mixed primary, secondary, monodominant primary and swamp forest. Wolf's monkeys occurred in all four forest types, though to varying extents; the author described their group sizes as similar to reports from other sites. The abstract supplies no precise species-wide group-size mean, so this page retains only the local comparison.

## Lomami camera record

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
Fournier et al. (2023) studied the cryptic Lesula monkey in the Lomami landscape using ground cameras to document its activity and behaviour. In a comparison that also detected arboreal monkeys, Wolf's monkey appeared in only one independent event, classified as a terrestrial event. This low-frequency record shows that the survey detected the species; it does not describe its usual activity stratum, abundance or behaviour.
