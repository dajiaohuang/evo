## study

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
In Colombia's Selva de Florencia National Natural Park, researchers randomly sampled 31 plots of 1 km² and used repeated transects to estimate the proportion of plots used. The estimate for C. versicolor was 0.36 (95% probability interval 0.17–0.49), with detection probability 0.08. During seven months of sampling, the team logged three vocal detections and five group sightings across eight plots.

## ecology

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The model gave human presence the greatest explanatory weight for C. versicolor, with direction suggesting lower use where human activity was present. However, the paper reported no significant covariate effects in the parsimonious models for its three primates, so this direction is not an established cause. A separate fragmented-forest study included this species among 185 fecal samples from three primates; 72.9% was the pooled parasite-positive share, not a species-specific rate.
