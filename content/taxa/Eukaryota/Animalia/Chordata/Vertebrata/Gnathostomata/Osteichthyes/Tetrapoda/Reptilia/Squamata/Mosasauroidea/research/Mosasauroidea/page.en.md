# Mosasauroidea

## atlas-profile / overview

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/overview -->
Mosasauroidea is retained as a broader branch-defined mosasauroid route, distinct from node-defined Mosasauridae. Dallasaurus preserves a plesiopedal limb condition in the middle Turonian sample, while the nomenclatural analysis separates clade definitions from any single basal topology.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## atlas-profile / ecology / diet

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/ecology/diet -->
Sample-bounded: tooth-enamel isotopes and associated body-size data support resource-partitioning interpretations among five type-Maastrichtian mosasaur taxa, not one diet for all Mosasauroidea.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## atlas-profile / ecology / habitat

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/ecology/habitat -->
The cited samples are marine; habitat interpretation is limited to the documented Texas and Maastrichtian study contexts.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## atlas-profile / ecology / locomotion

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/ecology/locomotion -->
Dallasaurus is described with plesiopedal limbs, while the cited family analysis distinguishes plesiopedal and hydropedal grades among sampled mosasauroids.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## atlas-profile / ecology / bodySize

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/ecology/bodySize -->
Body-size comparisons are available for the five named taxa in the type-Maastrichtian isotope sample; they are not a global size distribution.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## atlas-profile / ecology / guild

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/ecology/guild -->
The five-taxon Maastrichtian sample supports a local resource-partitioning interpretation, not a universal mosasauroid guild assignment.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## atlas-profile / traits

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/traits/0 -->
Mosasauroidea and Mosasauridae have distinct branch- and node-based definitions in the cited nomenclatural analysis

<!-- /evo:text -->

## atlas-profile / traits

<!-- /evo:text -->

## atlas-profile / evidenceSummary

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/evidenceSummary -->
Madzia and Cau define the clade-boundary distinction, Bell and Polcyn document the Dallasaurus skeletons and limb grades, and Schulp et al. provide a five-taxon isotope and body-size sample. All ecological and geographic statements remain study- and specimen-bounded.
<!-- /evo:text -->
