COL26.8 accepts Centrolene elisae Franco-Mena, Vega-Yánez, Reyes-Puig & Guayasamin, 2024 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Centrolenidae (family) → Centrolene (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## Six males and one female in the diagnostic material
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc11348905-ml5l3
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The diagnosis reports male snout–vent length 22.9–25.3 mm for six males and 27.2 mm for one female. A green, usually finely white-spotted dorsum, partly translucent belly, reticulated grey-white iris with a brown horizontal stripe, and male humeral spines and vocal slits form part of the combination. Inner fingers lack webbing, while outer fingers and toes have webbing; ulnar and outer tarsal folds are low. The sole known female was larger than the measured males, but that is not a population-wide estimate of sexual size dimorphism. One male was darker and more spiculate than others. Colour terminology varies between the diagnosis and specimen accounts, so identification cannot rest on one hue alone.

## Nocturnal records along narrow forest streams
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc11348905-ml5l3
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
At Las Caucheras, six frogs were found on shrub and fern leaves near a stream and river, 20–250 cm above ground, with five calling. Other records include a male two metres above stream level at Río Pucayacu and a calling individual 230 cm above ground at Chamanapamba. These different reference surfaces are retained. Streams were reported at 100–150 cm width in primary and secondary forest. Only three individuals were found during the cited three-year Yanayacu inventory, leading the authors to suggest rarity; encounter numbers without a detection model are not population density. Occurrence in a paddock or secondary forest does not establish disturbance tolerance or breeding success there.

## Three clutches on the upper leaf surface
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc11348905-ml5l3
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
At Chamanapamba, the researchers found three clutches with embryos at Gosner stage 22, attached to the upper side of a leaf approximately 170 cm above a small stream. The clutches contained twenty-eight, twenty-one and forty-seven embryos. An adult male was observed near the eggs. These are three observed embryo counts, not a measured mean laid-clutch size, lifetime fecundity or proof that all clutches are deposited at that height. A male nearby does not by itself demonstrate parentage, continuous guarding or experimentally confirmed benefits of attendance. Hatching success and development duration were not established by this observation.

## A two-note field call recorded at ten degrees Celsius
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc11348905-ml5l3
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The field description used one recording from male ZSFQ 5369 at Yanayacu on 11 March 2023, at 23:19 and 10 °C. Calls contained two pulsed notes, averaged 0.203 s and had mean dominant frequency 3.7 kHz. The first note averaged 0.102 s and 12.3 pulses, while the second averaged 0.045 s with two or three pulses. Inter-call intervals were 13.7–15.0 s and call rate 4.1 per minute. Notes, calls and pulses are distinct units; repeated measurements from one recording do not become independent frogs. This temperature- and individual-specific account is not a complete acoustic range or a universal identification threshold.

## Laboratory interaction calls are a separate sample
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc11348905-ml5l3
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
The authors also accessed a laboratory recording from another male, QCAZ 26032, during male interaction. Recording quality did not permit precise note and pulse measurements, although frequency, total duration and spacing could be measured. Calls still contained two notes, but intervals were much shorter, 0.343–0.844 s, and call rate was 81.4–89.6 per minute. Reported call duration ranged 0.198–1.284 s and dominant frequency 3.5–4.1 kHz. These values remain separate from the field male and cannot be pooled into an ordinary wild calling rate. Social context may explain differences, but this account does not isolate a causal mechanism through a controlled experiment.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Four recorded cloud-forest localities and a dated proposal
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc11348905-ml5l3
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
The distribution account named Las Caucheras, Yanayacu, Chamanapamba and Río Pucayaku at Nelson Palacios Reserve, on the eastern Ecuadorian Andes, at 2,100–2,586 m. The authors estimated extent of occurrence below 5,000 square kilometres and proposed Endangered, citing deforestation and fragmentation associated with agriculture and cattle farming. Some records were in private reserves, but that does not establish secure habitat or a stable population. The 2024 proposal is not independent confirmation of a current IUCN listing, and extent of occurrence is neither occupied habitat nor frog density. Four known localities do not prove that no other population exists.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## A supported lineage within an unresolved polytomy
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc11348905-ml5l3
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/text/en -->
Elisae was recovered as monophyletic, with posterior probability 1 and bootstrap 84. Its broader clade contained an unresolved polytomy with venezuelense and potential unnamed Colombian and northern Ecuadorian lineages. The analyses used mitochondrial 12S and 16S, not nuclear genomic data. An unresolved node cannot be rewritten as a unique sister relationship, and cf. venezuelense is not a certified assignment of every Colombian specimen to that accepted species. Support for the sampled elisae lineage does not prove absence of gene flow or resolve all species boundaries in the complex.
<!-- /evo:text -->
