The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## Historical Huangyan identifications require a concept boundary
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc13463510-7dbk8
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/text/en -->
The 2026 account placed the 2010 Huangyan records previously called wenxianensis in the chresonymy of the newly described sheni. Their Huangyan breeding chronology and embryonic observations therefore are not automatically contemporary wenxianensis biology. Its direct comparison attributed male villiform cloacal papillae, indistinct middorsal nodules, toe order III > IV > II > V > I, fingertips reaching the snout, an obtusely rounded snout and connected vomerine-series tips to wenxianensis. These are source-scoped comparative characters, not a reconciliation of every older record or a complete current range.

## Seven external and five osteological records delimit size evidence
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7369190-7dbk8
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/text/en -->
Table 1 lists seven external-head and five osteological records for Tylototriton wenxianensis. The study mainly used males; the two measurement datasets are not additive animal counts. Its cranial centroid size was consistently below asperrimus in the reported datasets, and its dorsal external-head size was below Pleurodeles. Cranial landmark size is not body mass or snout–vent length, and this sample does not establish female size or a complete species-wide size range.

## A mixed field-and-literature inventory in northern Sichuan
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/12/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12189923-7dbk8
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/12/text/en -->
The 2025 paper includes source-labelled Tylototriton wenxianensis in its Tangjiahe National Nature Reserve inventory in northern Sichuan, China. The total list contains 25 species, with 20 from field surveys and five from literature. This page does not assign every table row to a newly captured 2024 animal without the supplementary species-by-species evidence. The field design searched adults on rain-free nights from May to August 2024 using 39 transects, 200–500 m long and 5 m wide, with five trained observers. It is not a year-round census, a juvenile demographic study or an independently checked voucher identification.

## Regional gradients and survey gains do not certify recovery
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/14/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12189923-7dbk8
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/14/text/en -->
The reserve description spans 1,150–3,830 m, the sampling Methods report 800–2,600 m, and statistical Methods describe observed amphibians at 800–2,500 m. These scopes are retained rather than assigned as this species’ measured range. Richness peaks in two regional bands but its linear elevation association is nonsignificant (reported R² 0.114, p 0.412). Historical recorded-species increase is also nonsignificant (Mann–Kendall p 0.148). Neither result establishes unchanged populations, increased abundance or a causal benefit of management. Discussion and Conclusions use stronger fragmentation, conservation-success and large-population language than these tests alone support; raw records and historical sampling comparability have not been independently reanalysed.
