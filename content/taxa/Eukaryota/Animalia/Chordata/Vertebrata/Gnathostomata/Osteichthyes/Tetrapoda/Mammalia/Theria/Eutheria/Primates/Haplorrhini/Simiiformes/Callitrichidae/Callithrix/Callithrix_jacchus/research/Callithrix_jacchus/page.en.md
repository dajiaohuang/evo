# Callithrix jacchus

## atlas-profile / overview

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/overview -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/evidenceSummary -->

These sources do not establish wild life history, species-wide ecology, complete distribution, a nuclear-genome divergence date or formal conservation status.

## A coat pattern described from adult samples
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/0/text/en -->
A Brazilian study sampled 30 adult C. jacchus among 209 adult Callithrix specimens collected from mixed wild and captive sources during 2015–2019. It described grey-tipped crown hairs, a grey chin, white ear tufts and a prominent forehead star, with dark or grey dorsal bands over orange underfur. In the sampled adult comparison, mean values for 13 measured traits did not differ significantly from C. penicillata. These are comparative sample descriptions, not a stand-alone field diagnosis; hybrids may show overlapping pelage.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Native and introduced records in Brazil
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/1/text/en -->
The study’s occurrence-only compilation treated coastal and inland records in northeastern Brazil as native, and records in São Paulo, Paraná, Rio de Janeiro and Minas Gerais as introductions. Observed locations, modeled habitat suitability and a complete range are different kinds of evidence. The compiled records do not set exhaustive range limits or prove absence elsewhere.

## A captive study of fathers and infant outcomes
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/2/text/en -->
At one Wisconsin research colony, 24 experienced fathers and 62 offspring were included in a study of responses to infant distress calls. Offspring of fathers classified as responsive had higher survival beyond one month (p = 0.002); the paper also reported group weight differences at weeks 28 and 30. This is an association from one captive-colony study, not a wild survival estimate or proof that the response caused the outcome.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## What Brazilian rabies surveillance can show
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/3/text/en -->
Brazilian Ministry of Health passive surveillance recorded 67 rabies outbreaks affecting common marmosets, with 91 cases across 41 municipalities from January 2008 through October 2020. The study reported spillover of a Desmodus rotundus antigenic variant to C. jacchus in northern and southern reporting areas. These records describe reported outbreaks and an identified pathogen interaction; they do not measure disease prevalence throughout the species’ range.

## A conservation question involving introduced relatives
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/4/text/en -->
In a sample of 18 free-living marmosets with putative hybrid appearance from four southeastern Brazilian localities, five carried a C. jacchus maternal COX2 haplotype. The study discusses hybridization between native threatened C. aurita and introduced congeners, including the common marmoset, as a conservation concern. The sample does not estimate hybridization frequency in C. jacchus, measure population effects or establish the common marmoset’s conservation status.

## A mitochondrial lineage estimate, not a species origin date
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/5/text/en -->
A study combining 49 newly sequenced Callithrix mitogenomes with published sequences estimated that sampled C. jacchus and the Caatinga lineage of C. penicillata were sister lineages that diverged about 0.51 million years ago. The estimate depends on the sampled mitochondrial lineages and model. It is not a nuclear-genome species tree or a definitive date for the origin of C. jacchus.
<!-- /evo:text -->
