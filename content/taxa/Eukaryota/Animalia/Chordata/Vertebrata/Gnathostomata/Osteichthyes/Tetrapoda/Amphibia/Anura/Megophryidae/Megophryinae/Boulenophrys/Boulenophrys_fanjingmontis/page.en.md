## Fanjing and Youyang records with a provisional Xiushan assignment
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10083232-v5lvn
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
Male holotype TRU trxy01 came from Heiwanhe, Fanjing Mountain, Guizhou, at 900 m. The monograph examined Heiwanhe material at about 860 m and recognized Fanjing and Youyang, Chongqing, at 800–950 m. It suggested that historical B. spinata records from Xiushan might belong here on biogeographic grounds, explicitly requiring additional vouchers with molecular data. That suggestion remains distinct from the recognized locality set.

## Eight examined adults and partial toe webbing
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10083232-v5lvn
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The diagnosis reported four adult males at 58.2–63.6 mm snout–vent length and four females at 62.8–72.2 mm. Characters included weak vomerine ridges without teeth, one-third toe webbing with wide lateral fringes, and sparse tiny black nuptial spines on the dorsal bases of fingers I and II in breeding males, with a subgular vocal sac. These are sample measurements and qualitative anatomical descriptions, not population-wide size bounds or measured swimming performance.

## A previously overlooked subspecies elevated in the monograph
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10083232-v5lvn
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The remarks traced conflicting treatments of the large Fanjing horned toads as B. spinata and as the subspecies Megophrys binlingensis fanjingmontis. Some checklists listed both names for what the monograph interpreted as the same regional frog. Citing earlier molecular delimitation and its detailed morphology, the authors elevated fanjingmontis to a full species distinct from B. spinata and B. binlingensis. The history and elevation decision are attributed to the paper rather than independently certified as a complete review of all regional populations.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## A character cited in the gutu comparison

The 2025 gutu description lists Boulenophrys fanjingmontis as larger in body size than the gutu sample. This is an attributed comparator statement within that paper’s taxonomic scope, not a new measurement series for this species. The gutu adult sample spans 34.4–44.7 mm in seven males and 36.2–52.8 mm in eight females; those numbers belong to gutu, not to this comparator.
