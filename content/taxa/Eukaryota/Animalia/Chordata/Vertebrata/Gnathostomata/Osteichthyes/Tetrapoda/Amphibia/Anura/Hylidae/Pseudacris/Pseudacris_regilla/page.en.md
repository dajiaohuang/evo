## Repeat annotations are computational observations
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/21/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12822217-4nh9v
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/21/text/en -->
The authors built species-specific repeat libraries with RepeatModeler2, classified unknown elements with RFSB and supplemented the libraries with vertebrate repeats before applying RepeatMasker. Its hits are termed insertions in the paper, but a hit is not independently demonstrated to be a complete, expressed or mobile element. Superfamily labels can aggregate distinct families. Element counts, total occupied length and genome proportions are separate quantities; overlapping hierarchy categories cannot safely be added as independent fractions. No new repeat annotation or expression experiment for Pseudacris regilla was performed here.

## Across-species correlations do not prove this species mechanism
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/22/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12822217-4nh9v
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/22/text/en -->
The study compared61 assemblies using phylogenetically adjusted models, an earlier pruned tree, contig-N50 covariates and multiple-comparison correction. Recently active Ty3/DIRS1-associated sequences correlate with genome size across that sample, but recent activity is a divergence-based proxy, not direct observation of current transposition in this species. Length and open-reading-frame similarity filters likewise do not demonstrate active protein expression. The authors' suggested developmental constraints are comparative hypotheses; they do not establish a causal TE effect on this species' larval duration or clutch size.

## primaryStudyScopes / sectionEvidence / evidenceSummary

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/primaryStudyScopes/0/sectionEvidence/0/evidenceSummary/en -->
The 2023 review includes Pseudacris regilla among amphibian hosts with records of adult intestinal cestodes. Its named parasite associations are Distoichometra bufonis. These describe compiled host–parasite records, not parasites measured in every member of the species. The scope is adult worms in wild continental North American herpetofauna; larval cestode records are excluded. An intestinal association alone does not establish disease severity, host mortality, zoonotic transmission or the species’ role in completing every parasite life cycle. Prevalence, intensity, examined-host denominators and experimental susceptibility are not supplied by this host checklist.
