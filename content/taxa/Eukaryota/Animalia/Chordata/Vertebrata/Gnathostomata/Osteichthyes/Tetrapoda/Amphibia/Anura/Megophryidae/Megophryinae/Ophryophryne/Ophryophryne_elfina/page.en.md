## Montane forest encounters by day and night
<!-- /evo:text -->

primary-pmc5527341-74qyf

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The description found specimens at night after heavy rain along rocky cascade streams, waterfalls or intermittent brooks, and by day beneath logs or in litter. ## Calling windows differ among mountains
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5527341-74qyf
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
At Hon Ba, calling males were recorded during December, March and October survey windows. At Bidoup, Hon Giao and Chu Yang Sin, reproductive activity and male calling were recorded from 10 February into mid-July. Calling males used fern leaves or stream banks; females sheltered beneath logs or in litter. The geographically separate records do not establish a single universal annual breeding calendar.

## Larvae and ovarian eggs retain separate samples
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5527341-74qyf
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
Tadpoles were found from April to July in streams with calling males, hiding under flat stones or dead leaves by day and appearing in shallow sandy areas at night. Metamorphosed individuals were observed at Chu Yang Sin in May. Fifteen ovarian eggs measured in one gravid female were approximately 2.2–2.8 mm across; that egg sample is not a laid-clutch count or a population-wide egg-size range.
