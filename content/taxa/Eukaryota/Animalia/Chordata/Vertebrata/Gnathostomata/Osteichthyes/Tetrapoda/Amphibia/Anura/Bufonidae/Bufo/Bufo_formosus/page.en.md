## Four egg-string cohorts define this laboratory sample
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc13457435-np5s
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
In April 2021, four approximately 30 cm egg-string portions, described as containing 100–150 fertilized eggs, were collected from separate puddles at the University of Tokyo Botanical Gardens in Tochigi. Each string was treated as a sibling cohort without a reported genetic parentage test. Larvae were transferred at Gosner stage 22, before swimming/feeding interactions, to dechlorinated tap or pond water and group or solitary rearing. Group containers held twenty larvae and solitary containers one. After two weeks at 18 °C with a 12:12 dark/light cycle and food provision, stages 30–35 were tested. These are rearing conditions, not natural thermal limits, complete female clutch size, larval growth rate or independent population sampling.

## Ninety subjects each faced unfamiliar equal-sized stimulus groups
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc13457435-np5s
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
A total of ninety subject tadpoles were tested once each against unfamiliar sibling and nonsibling stimulus groups, ten larvae per side. Subjects and stimuli had been raised in separate containers. Each trial involved five-minute acclimation and two thirty-minute observation periods with left/right placement reversed, yielding sixty minutes of recorded behaviour. Tracking at one-second intervals measured time on sibling side, neutral zone and nonsibling side. Analysis used individual time proportions rather than treating consecutive seconds as independent animals. Equal-sized groups do not test preference for a larger aggregation, and repeated seconds do not enlarge the ninety-subject sample.

## No kin-biased side preference was detected in these tests
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc13457435-np5s
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
Paired comparisons found no significant sibling-versus-nonsibling time difference in any rearing treatment: reported p-values were 0.680 for tap/group, 0.899 for tap/solitary, 0.966 for pond/group and 0.619 for pond/solitary. This is a lack of detected kin-biased association under the chosen equal-group, unfamiliar-stimulus design, not proof that every formosus larva lacks kin recognition. The discussion proposes insufficient differentiation in odour cues and possible later learned recognition, but neither genetic odour differences nor adult learning was experimentally measured. Nonsignificance does not establish exact equality or universal absence of an effect.

## Isolation affected neutral-zone time in the fitted analysis
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc13457435-np5s
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
Solitary-reared larvae spent a greater proportion of time in the neutral zone than group-reared larvae; the reported social-experience term was t=3.155, p=0.002. Models for sibling-side and nonsibling-side proportions did not show significant social-treatment effects. The analysis included clutch as a fixed factor rather than independent observation seconds. More neutral-zone time suggests a changed response to conspecific groups, but general locomotor activity was not measured independently. The study therefore cannot separate reduced social responsiveness from broader movement changes or certify an isolated neural mechanism. Neutral-zone time is not a measured stress-hormone concentration or survival outcome.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Two water treatments do not test every natural water condition
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc13457435-np5s
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
Larvae were reared in dechlorinated tap water or pond water from Ichijiro Pond and tested in the corresponding water type with similarly reared stimuli. The fitted analyses did not detect a water-type effect on association zones, including neutral-zone p=0.767, or a significant interaction with social treatment. Rearing water and test water were matched rather than independently crossed, so developmental versus immediate cue effects were not separately isolated. No measured microbial, odour or chemical mechanism was demonstrated. These comparisons do not establish identical responses in every pond, pollutant concentration or natural seasonal water regime.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Predator defence and neural explanations remain hypotheses
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc13457435-np5s
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
The discussion relates aggregation to possible predator avoidance and proposes that group size could outweigh kinship, drawing on other toads and earlier literature. Both stimulus groups here had ten larvae, so the experiment did not manipulate group-size alternatives or measure predation. Comparisons with fish and mice suggest possible neural effects of isolation, but no brain circuitry, neural development or hormone measurement was conducted on these tadpoles. The source’s black-colour/warning interpretation is also background reasoning, not a chemical toxicity or predator-choice experiment in this cohort. Proposed functions remain separate from measured zone occupancy.

## Larval results do not establish persistence after metamorphosis
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc13457435-np5s
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/text/en -->
The author calls for nonsocial control conditions and follow-up beyond the larval stage. No later adult social response, mate choice, inbreeding avoidance, field fitness or persistence after metamorphosis was measured here. The experiment concerns early rearing followed by stage-30–35 choice behaviour in one local collection, not the full formosus life history or every population. Developed neural or olfactory recognition after metamorphosis is proposed as a possibility, without being added as an established adult trait. The study supplies no new species distribution boundary or current conservation assessment.
