## ecology

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
At Ngel Nyaki Forest Reserve, three semi-habituated groups were observed across forest edge and grassland. The study collected 164 fecal samples and reported seed deposits from both habitats; many deposits contained intact seeds. Six transects totaling 4.69 km were walked 18 times from December 2009 to September 2010; the authors estimated more than 2,000 scans and 378 observation hours for troop time-use observations.

## evidence-boundary

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
The paper's seed samples came from one Nigerian montane reserve, and collection was concentrated in the dry season because rain washed deposits away. Population-level seed-dispersal rates also use local density and defecation rates measured from three captive monkeys, so they are estimates for the study landscape rather than a species-wide rate.

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
A local seed-dispersal study introduction; not externally expert-reviewed. Field deposits, three groups and captive defecation measurements do not establish a species-wide dispersal rate.
