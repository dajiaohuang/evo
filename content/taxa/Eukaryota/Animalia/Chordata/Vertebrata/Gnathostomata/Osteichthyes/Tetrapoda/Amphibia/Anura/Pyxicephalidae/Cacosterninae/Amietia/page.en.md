## Collective elicitation and scope of correspondence
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
Thirteen native isiZulu-speaking volunteers from five Zululand areas participated in two2016 workshop groups. Photograph-assisted discussions yielded six broad folk categories; later species naming combined those categories, published names and guidelines with assistance from Bongani Mkhize. Additional broad terms in the paper came from earlier publication or borrowing. Folk categories sometimes cross several scientific genera or families, so one name does not necessarily identify one scientific genus or species. These selected Amietia entries do not establish community-wide name frequencies, independent recognition accuracy, current local occupancy, or ancestral genus-level habitat/call traits. The study's linguistic and naming evidence is kept separate from measured biological evidence.

## Species scores preserve different conservation inputs
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/text/en -->

DBS reflects represented Rwanda ecozones and national endemism; TBS uses previous national red-list proposals, and SBS is a categorical habitat-disturbance sensitivity input. SI sums those three inputs for a species. It is not a genus score, extinction probability or an experimentally measured tolerance. A site's RABI sums all detected species SI values without dividing by richness. Doubled category weights encode the authors' prioritization choices rather than measured doubling of biological risk. Threat scores are historical study inputs; the combined VU/DD score cannot by itself distinguish Vulnerable from Data Deficient.
