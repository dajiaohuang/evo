# Liaodactylus


## Begin comparisons with an early comb dentition

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
The 2017 Liaodactylus description uses a skull, mandibles and first two cervical vertebrae; 152 closely spaced teeth shorten toward the rear and support the authors’ filtering interpretation. The 2023 Balaenognathus case instead has a near-complete skeleton, at least 480 teeth, crown hooklets and a spatulate toothless front edge, with specific water-flow models. Both frame a question about dental filtration, while specimen completeness, tooth geometry and inferred mechanisms retain different limits. Pumping proposed for the latter is not known behavior in the former.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Read teeth before dates and evolutionary models

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
Start with the skull, posteriorly shortening tooth row and 2017 morphospace analysis in [Liaodactylus primus](Liaodactylus_primus/page.en.md), then the hooklets, snout opening and shallow-water models in [Balaenognathus maeuseri](../Balaenognathus/Balaenognathus_maeuseri/page.en.md). Finish with PBDB occurrences below and the phylogenetic relationships in each paper. A dated earliest-record claim, a sister group and database navigation answer different questions without establishing a complete family or genus natural history.

## Evidence limits

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
This route introduces occurrence records for species authored in this batch, not a complete descendant catalogue or a common ecological summary. Record counts are not comparable measurements of specimens, individuals, populations or sampling intensity; outer age bounds are not actual origin and extinction times. PBDB classification paths organize the material while remaining distinct from COL and other phylogenetic schemes.
New paragraphs use all main scientific sections of Zhou et al. (2017): abstract, introduction, methods, systematic description and discussion, with captions 1–3. Figure pixels, supplements/matrices and specimens were not examined, and trees/statistical analyses were not rerun. Skull measurements, stratigraphic dates, comb-dentition filtering interpretations, dietary categories and observed feeding remain separate; “earliest” is bounded to the authors’ 2017 judgment. Group comparisons also use the 2023 Balaenognathus selections actually read in P5-139 without transferring its pumping model to this species.
