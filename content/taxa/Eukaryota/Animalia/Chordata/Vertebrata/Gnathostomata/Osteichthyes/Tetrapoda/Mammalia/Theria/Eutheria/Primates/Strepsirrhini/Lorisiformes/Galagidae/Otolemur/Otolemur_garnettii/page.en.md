## socialOrganization

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
Mark-recapture and radio-tracking at two coastal-forest sites found that adult females of different ages used extensively overlapping ranges, while females of similar ages overlapped little. Adult male ranges were larger and overlapped female ranges; resident males usually overlapped little with one another, and male turnover was frequent in both populations. Adults rarely slept together. These observations are from the Kenyan study populations and should not be generalized to all habitats.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
The field evidence is from two Kenyan coastal-forest populations and older studies published under Galago names. It does not establish current range-wide social organization or diet. Taxonomy follows COL26.8; the Chinese display name is editorial. No dossier or external expert review was added. This page is not externally expert-reviewed.
