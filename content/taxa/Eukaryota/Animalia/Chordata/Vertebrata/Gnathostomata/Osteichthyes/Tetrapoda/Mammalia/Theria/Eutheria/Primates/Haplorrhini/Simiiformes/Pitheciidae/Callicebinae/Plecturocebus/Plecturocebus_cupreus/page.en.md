# Plecturocebus cupreus


## One adult female paralectotype

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
The 2025 Naturalis catalogue lists RMNH.MAM.39086 as a paralectotype of Callithrix cuprea Spix, 1823: an adult female mounted skin with the skull in situ, corresponding to Jentink’s 1892 entry 52 a. A paralectotype is another original-series member retained after a lectotype has been selected, not the museum’s unique holotype. One adult female does not establish species-wide pelage variation, size range, sex ratio or abundance.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Collection, arrival and accession are different dates

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
Brackets around [Tabatinga] and [early January 1820] mark locality/period additions inferred by the catalogue authors from itinerary evidence, not exact original-label dates. They discuss Spix reaching Tabatinga on 9 January 1820 and collecting for at least four days, plus a female and male in an October 1824 receipt list; only the female remains at Naturalis according to the catalogue. Arrival is not this female’s certain collection date, and two receipt entries are not two specimens currently present. Original Solimões wording and the later restriction “opposite Tabatinga” remain separate, without creating newly measured distribution coordinates.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Separate a cross-museum type series from ecology

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The catalogue cites Hill’s 1960 selection of female ZSM 10 in Munich as lectotype and reports three other paralectotypes at ZSM plus another at MfN. These external-museum statements come through references, without fresh collection-by-collection confirmation here, and multiple accession numbers do not measure independent population samples. A type series anchors a name; the existing interfluve map-overlap section answers a different geographic question. Together they do not establish direct animal observations throughout every region.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Distribution records

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
Mourthé et al. (2022) overlaid IUCN range maps on ten major Brazilian Amazon interfluves. Supplementary Table S1 lists Plecturocebus cupreus (Spix, 1823) in 1 area: Inambari. Here, overlap is a region-level map result; it does not confirm animals at every site and is not a population count or complete range assessment.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Evidence limits

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
The abstract and methods describe 80 species, while main-text Table 2 occupancy classes sum to 81 and report the 46 single-interfluve species as 57%, unlike the 58% stated in the text. Names and counts here come from Supplementary Table S1; they are range-map overlaps, not point-level field confirmations, population counts or complete range assessments. Chinese common names are editorial translations, and these guides are not externally expert-reviewed.
New paragraphs use the 2025 Naturalis catalogue’s front matter, selected introduction/methods/nomenclatural discussion (pp. 3–4, 6–8) and titi accounts (pp. 49–50); page layouts 49–50 were viewed, without specimens, original labels, specimen photos or live collection portals. All 164 pages, nineteenth-century originals and other museums’ complete series were not reviewed, nor was the separate 2025 northwestern-titi revision freshly read in full. The catalogue uses its own taxonomic reference; publication-year and type-locality interpretations remain separate from pinned COL data. Rejected historical type attributions do not fill field-concept correspondence. No complete monograph or external expert review is claimed, and discolor name/field-group correspondence remains unresolved.
