## seasonalFeeding

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
A wild group followed in southeastern Madagascar during July–November 1994 and July 1995–February 1996 mainly ate new bamboo growth. Liana bamboos were used more in the cool dry season, while Cephalostachyum perrieri was eaten more in the warm wet season. The group spent more time feeding and traveling in the dry season and rested more in the wet season; the authors suggested food availability or reproductive timing as possible influences, not settled causes.

## localDietVariation

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
At two Ranomafana sites, a comparison of three small groups found that each group emphasized plant foods most abundant within its home range. Groups at the more disturbed Tala site were larger and occurred at higher density than those at Vato, but the authors discussed food abundance, resource quality, disturbance and topography as possible contributors. These observations describe a small site comparison, not a general response to disturbance across Madagascar.

## bambooChemistry

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
A semi-quantitative test of 102 food, urine and fecal samples from two pre-dry seasons detected cyanide in bamboo foods and urine from H. griseus and two sympatric bamboo lemurs. The authors interpreted the urine results as evidence that dietary cyanide is absorbed and at least partly excreted by the kidneys. The study does not establish the full detoxification mechanism.
<!-- /evo:text -->
