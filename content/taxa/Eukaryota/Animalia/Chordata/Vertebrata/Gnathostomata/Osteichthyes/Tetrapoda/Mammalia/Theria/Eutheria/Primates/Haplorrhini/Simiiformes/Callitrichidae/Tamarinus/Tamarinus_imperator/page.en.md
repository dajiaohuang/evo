# Tamarinus imperator

## Overview

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
COL26.8 lists *Tamarinus imperator* separately from *T. subgrisescens*. Gregorin et al. (2023) compared pelage, cranial morphology and mitochondrial DNA and found several congruent differences. Sampling of the nominal imperator lineage was small, however, and the possible contact area remains inadequately sampled.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Classification

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
In the comparative study, the authors directly examined 60 specimens and reviewed photographs of six more; the cytochrome-b comparison included only three individuals representing the two lineages. They reported pelage and morphological differences and genetic divergence, while noting that samples were insufficient to establish a hybrid zone or contact. This page presents the taxonomic evidence and does not assign broad field observations published as *S. imperator* directly to this species.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Research observations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
Bicca-Marques (2006) studied one group published under the older name *Saguinus imperator imperator* in a 100-ha forest reserve in Rio Branco, Acre, Brazil. During 54 days from October to November 1997, four artificial banana-feeding stations, 58–256 m apart, were placed within the group’s range; it visited the stations 207 times. After leaving a station, the group chose the nearest station more often than expected by chance and avoided the farthest. The experiment tested routes between artificial food patches, not natural diet or universal species behavior. A later amended IUCN assessment raised the former nominal subspecies and maps that name to *T. imperator*; this page uses that taxonomic bridge and does not extend the result to *T. subgrisescens*.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Evidence limits

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
The field evidence here is limited to one group’s artificial-feeding experiment in Rio Branco. It supports a local study guide, not a natural-diet account, range-wide behavior, or complete ecological review.
