# Berthasaura leopoldinae


## A nearly complete holotype still comprises disarticulated bones

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
The 2021 description designates MN 7821-V as the holotype of Berthasaura leopoldinae. It is a nearly complete but disarticulated skeleton, preserving part of the skull, lower jaw, vertebral column and limbs, housed at Brazil’s Museu Nacional. Comparison with more complete ceratosaurs yields an estimated length no greater than one metre for this individual, not a measured maximum adult size for the species. Associated material supports body comparisons while missing elements and tentative identifications remain relevant to reconstruction.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Toothless holotype jaws and a possible beak

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
The holotype premaxilla, maxilla and dentary preserve no teeth. The premaxilla has a thin buccal rim and medial lamellae; the dentary is short and downturned rostrally. External pits and grooves lead the authors to suggest a rhamphotheca. Micro-CT examines the dentary interior, where the authors describe alveolar vestiges as absent and/or modified into trabecular cavities. Preserved bones, scan interpretation and an inferred horny covering are distinct evidence; a reconstruction does not make a complete beak a preserved object.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## A later dentary suggests tooth loss during development

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
A 2025 abstract reports alveolar vestiges in another dentary, supporting developmental tooth loss. This limits extrapolation from the toothless holotype to lifelong toothlessness. The full study remains unread.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Maturity combines fusion and ossification signals

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
Preserved cervical and dorsal vertebrae retain unfused centra and neural arches, and frontal and parietal remain separate, while long-bone articular ends are well ossified. The 2021 authors therefore interpret the individual as a young subadult rather than an early juvenile or adult. Comparison draws on Limusaurus, without establishing identical developmental sequences or an exact age in years. Later histology needs separate reading; this skeletal assessment does not establish a complete growth history.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Preserved vertebrae do not supply a complete serial count

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The axial material includes part of the neck, eleven trunk vertebrae, five unfused sacrals and sixteen vertebrae from different tail regions. The neck is incomplete, and the tail number counts preserved material rather than the animal’s full caudal series. Some cervical vertebrae have pneumatic openings, while trunk morphology varies along the series. These bones inform diagnosis and relationship comparisons without independently determining respiratory efficiency, running speed or gait.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Diet requires evidence beyond toothlessness

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The 2021 paper relates the gracile skull, downturned dentary, simplified occlusal surface and possible cutting beak to herbivorous or omnivorous possibilities, explicitly retaining morphological inference limits. No gastroliths are associated with the holotype, and isotopic evidence is unavailable in that study. Toothlessness and a possible rhamphotheca are insufficient to establish herbivory. This page therefore does not assign demonstrated obligate herbivory or infer everyday foods from desert deposition.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Relationships retain low support and polytomies

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
The 2021 analysis places Berthasaura near early branches of Noasauridae. With full sampling, the consensus is poorly resolved and most nodes have low support; basal polytomies remain after unstable taxa are removed. It is not recovered as a close relative of Vespersaurus from the same site. Shared classification and locality do not establish a closest-sister relationship. This page retains the fixed database parent and the original analysis date and scope, without rerunning the matrix or deciding every later classification scheme.
<!-- /evo:text -->
