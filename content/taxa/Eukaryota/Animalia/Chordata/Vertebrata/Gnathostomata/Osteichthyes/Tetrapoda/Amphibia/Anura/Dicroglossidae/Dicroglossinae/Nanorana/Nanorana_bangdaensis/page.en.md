## Comparison combines tympanum, nuptial pads and body features
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10649098-v4klm
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The account contrasts bangdaensis with laojunshanensis by an absent tympanum, male nuptial pad on finger I rather than I and II, head width approximately equal to head length, a smoother back and smaller flank spots. It also distinguishes bangdaensis from parkeri by the finger distribution of nuptial pads. These comparative characters draw on earlier descriptions; they are not a new population-wide trait-frequency estimate or a measured reproductive function.

## Low marker distances coexist with a published lineage distinction
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10649098-v4klm
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
Table 4 gives bangdaensis–parkeri distances of 1.0% in 16S and 3.4% in COI. The authors connect an eastern lineage with bangdaensis topotypes and a western lineage with parkeri topotypes, citing earlier genomic work on restricted gene flow. The new paper does not itself repeat a whole-genome contact-zone analysis. Low marker distance alone neither merges the species nor certifies every historical parkeri record as bangdaensis.
