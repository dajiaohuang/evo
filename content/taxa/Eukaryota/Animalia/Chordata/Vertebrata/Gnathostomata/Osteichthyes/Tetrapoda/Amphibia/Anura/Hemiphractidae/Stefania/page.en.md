## Cryptic summit diversity changes how old names are read
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The paper describes imawari and upuigmae, formerly included under satelles, using preserved morphology and microCT comparisons linked to earlier molecular lineages. Satelles is restricted to Aprada-tepui in the source account. A still-unnamed sp.4 had instead been confused with ginesi. Colour polymorphism and conserved osteology limit identification from phenotype alone; new lathropae scans show no unambiguous full-skeleton distinction among the closest species. These findings do not certify all historical records or resolve every unexamined summit population.

## Summit observations do not supply complete brooding histories
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
The imawari series contains five adult males, three adult females and one immature female; upuigmae is described from one adult female. A short rainy-season imawari visit found no females carrying eggs or juveniles, though one had a glutinous back patch. The upuigmae female also carried none when collected. These nondetections do not establish absence of back-brooding, developmental duration or a complete breeding calendar. A disturbed daytime upuigmae encounter is not a demonstrated diurnal lifestyle.
