## Accepted name and placement

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
COL26.8 accepts Eleutherodactylus humboldti Devitt, Tseng, Taylor-Adair, Koganti, Timugura & Cannatella, 2023 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Eleutherodactylidae (family) → Eleutherodactylus (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## A male holotype and three male paratypes
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10007972-nnm5t
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
Adult male holotype MZFZ 4505 was collected at Rancho El Pinal, Temascaltepec, State of México, on 27 June 2022 at 2428 m. Two male paratypes came from the same locality; a third, MZFZ 4506, came from Hotel Las Piñas Avándaro at 1982 m on the same date. The holotype measured 24.638 mm snout–vent length, head width 9.599 mm and tibia length 10.154 mm, with vocal slits present. These are individual and type-series records, not a population size interval, female description or statistical dimorphism result.

## Live orange surfaces fade in preservative
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10007972-nnm5t
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The live account described a brownish-tan dorsum, a paler unmarked snout, irregular dark flank blotches and a black-and-white inguinal gland pattern. Hidden posterior thigh and upper-arm surfaces were suffused with orange. In preservative the dorsum became grey and the orange faded to pale yellowish grey; dorsal tubercles could become difficult to see. The authors compared longer, more slender fingers with E. maurus but explicitly considered their sample sizes too small for robust population comparisons. Colour and comparative anatomy are kept within that sample scope, not interpreted as toxicity or measured locomotor performance.

## Calls from three males compared with four maurus males
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10007972-nnm5t
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The methods recorded three E. humboldti males and four E. maurus males, filtering background noise and selecting stabilized calling portions for analysis. Both calls were classified as pulsatile, meaning poorly defined energy bursts without intermittent silence. In the sampled comparison, humboldti had longer calls, intercall intervals and rise times and lower dominant frequency. These are relative acoustic results with male sample sizes retained; the paragraph provides no numerical frequency interval, and repeated calls are not treated as additional independent frogs.

## Thunderstorm encounters in a disturbed pine–oak clearing
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10007972-nnm5t
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The description knew three localities in the Valle de Bravo volcanic field. Its type series was collected during a thunderstorm between 21:00 and midnight at ambient air temperatures of 9–11 °C in a disturbed pine–oak clearing. Frogs called from vegetation below 1.5 m, often within dense non-native grass. These encounter conditions do not establish a critical thermal limit, obligatory storm dependence, a complete breeding season or successful reproduction in every disturbed clearing.

## A mitochondrial sister relationship within the sampled analysis
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10007972-nnm5t
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
The authors reported approximately 3% 16S divergence from E. maurus and ultrafast-bootstrap support of 96 for the pair’s sister relationship in their tree. They combined genetic, morphological and advertisement-call differences in describing humboldti. These are the 2023 study’s evidence and support statistic, not a universal 3% species threshold, an independent nuclear-genome analysis or certification of every population across the range.
