## fieldStudy

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
From February to August 2005, the researchers observed the animals for 22 days and 144 contact hours across the two parks, recording 903 scans and 1,537 feeding scores from 63 individuals. Black-shanked doucs ate mostly leaves year-round but also used fruit and flowers from many plant species. Plant diversity in the diet increased during the wet season, when fruit made up nearly twice its dry-season proportion of feeding scores.

## interpretation

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The paper recorded foods from 152 plant species but found no small set of heavily preferred species. Sampling covered two parks and a limited observation period; the authors found no evidence of foraging to maximize protein or energy. The study therefore documents diet breadth and seasonal differences at those sites, not a range-wide diet or fixed preference.
