## Overlapping measurements in a geographically bounded comparison
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7830202-q37w5
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The account knew notialis from the Jiri Mountain foothills to Gwangyang, Masan and Haman, and on Namhae, Changseong and Geoje islands. It reported sympatry with geojeensis, unisacculus and perplicatus in different areas. Size overlapped strongly with geojeensis, and the wider seven-clade analysis found no universally nonoverlapping measurement. Sex was omitted from analyses because not all specimens could be sexed. Geographic range and selected sample thresholds therefore do not form a universal morphology-only identification key or a test of sexual dimorphism.

## Natural history pooled across three species
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7830202-q37w5
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The authors explicitly pooled natural history for H. notialis, H. geojeensis and H. perplicatus because separate breeding ecology was poorly known. They described still-water reproduction in roadside or field ditches and fallow rice paddies, earliest breeding in late January, a late-February to early-March peak and some late-March spawning. Females generally deposited two curved sacs, sometimes looped or nearly straight, and attachment varied with the site. Larvae developed paired balancers and metamorphosed before the following winter; larval overwintering was not known. These pooled observations are not independently measured schedules, clutch sizes or developmental durations for each species.

## An estimated range threshold and a dated Endangered proposal
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7830202-q37w5
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The authors estimated extent of occurrence below 5000 km², explicitly using a threshold because fine-scale landscape use and presence data were unavailable. They described mainland and three disconnected islands and proposed Endangered under B1ab(i,ii,iii,iv,v). Population sizes and precise dynamics were unknown. This threshold is not a measured occupied polygon, and the 2021 proposal is not an independently verified current Red List assessment or a quantified extinction probability.

## A vouchered Hadong specimen underlies this sequence
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12673973-q37w5
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
The specimen was collected on 19 March 2021 at Bukcheon-myeon, Hadong-gun, Gyeongsangnam-do, Republic of Korea, and deposited as cgrb16302, field ID mms11038, at the Conservation Genome Resource Bank for Korean Wildlife. Species identification was reconfirmed with cytochrome-b sequences. Tail tissue supplied DNA for paired-end Illumina sequencing, GetOrganelle assembly and MITOS annotation with manual curation. A single voucher and collection locality do not estimate population diversity or delimit the whole distribution. Laboratory library sizes and sequencing yield are not animal body measurements.

## The reported 16,408-base-pair assembly is a mitochondrial resource
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12673973-q37w5
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
The source reports a complete 16,408-base-pair mitochondrial assembly under PX412262, with thirteen protein-coding genes, twenty-two tRNAs, two rRNAs and a noncoding control region. ND6 and eight tRNAs lie on the light strand; the remaining genes and control region are reported on the heavy strand. Base composition is A 33.6%, T 32.0%, C 20.7% and G 13.6%; the rounded values need not sum to exactly 100%. These are source-reported annotations and rounded composition, not whole-genome size, experimentally established gene function or an independently downloaded and validated sequence.

## The sampled mitochondrial tree placed notialis near unisacculus
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12673973-q37w5
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/text/en -->
The maximum-likelihood analysis used eleven mitogenomes: ten Hynobius species and Onychodactylus fischeri as outgroup, with 1,000 bootstrap replicates. The reported tree placed notialis closest to unisacculus, in a clade also containing quelpaertensis, within a broader sampled Korean Hynobius group. It was not a comparison of every Hynobius species, nor did it integrate an independent nuclear genome. The Discussion interprets Korean grouping as compatible with limited gene flow after diversification, but this tree did not directly measure gene flow. Maternal inheritance, introgression and substitution saturation remain acknowledged mitochondrial limitations.

## Restricted-range and threat statements are dated background
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12673973-q37w5
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/text/en -->
The Introduction cites the 2021 description for occurrence along the southern Korean Peninsula and a narrow extent of occurrence, and cites IUCN 2023 for a Vulnerable category. These are attributed historical statements, not a newly mapped range or independently checked current assessment. The single-sequence study proposes value for future evolutionary and conservation research without measuring habitat loss, population trend or intervention success.
