## Sex-specific measurements retain variable-specific sample counts
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12694819-5wpyd
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
In the 2025 comparative table, male snout–vent length for Brachycephalus mirissimus is 9.9–11.7 (N = 10) mm and female length is 10.0–12.9 (N = 3) mm, with N retained separately for each sex. Dorsal skin is smooth (n = 14) and lateral skin densely rough (n = 14). N refers to animals observed for each variable, so the size, skin and colour denominators cannot be pooled into extra specimens. These examined ranges are not whole-species maxima, a statistical proof of population sexual dimorphism or a growth series. Smooth skin in this study can include small, inconspicuous warts; external digit absence does not alone establish skeletal absence.

## Live and preserved colours are separate evidence

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
Orange with a white dorsal midline, becoming cream while retaining a pale stripe after preservation. These are the authors’ comparative observations rather than a standardized colour-chart measurement. Colour and specimen counts vary by trait; similar colours recur among species, so a single hue is not a complete identification rule. The table’s N counts animals with one or more recorded and analysed calls, not a universal count of notes or complete calls.
