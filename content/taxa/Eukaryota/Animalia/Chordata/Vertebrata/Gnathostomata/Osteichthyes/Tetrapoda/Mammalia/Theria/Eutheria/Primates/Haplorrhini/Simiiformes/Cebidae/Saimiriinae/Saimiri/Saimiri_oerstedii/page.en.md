## habitat

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
Costa Rican field studies reported a preference for secondary forest; one troop’s seasonal ranges were about 79–110 ha, with approximately 176 ha used across 11 months. The work also described age-, sex- and context-related differences in branch substrates and feeding postures. These are local troop observations, not fixed species-wide parameters.

## behavior

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
A field vocalization study continuously sampled 21 marked adult females, analyzing 18.6 hours of focal samples and 3,080 sonagrams. Vocalization rate increased as the nearest adult female was farther away; rapid calling was also associated with troop movement and females at the front. The sample covers one study context and limited observation time.

## distribution

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
Ceballos et al. combined repeated field surveys in southern and central Costa Rica with forest-cover layers from 1992 and 2012. Their mapping estimated a historical Costa Rican extent of occurrence of about 5,500 km² and a current extent near 2,250 km², a reduction of about 60%. These are the study’s map-and-record estimates, not a population count or a full survey of Panama.

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
Habitat, home-range and vocal results are limited to local Costa Rican field studies. The range-change estimate depends on that study’s historical records, accessible surveys and forest-patch thresholds. Some sources spell the epithet S. oerstedi; this page follows COL26.8 ID 4TZJZ. The Chinese name is editorial, and the page has not received external expert review. This page is not externally expert-reviewed.
