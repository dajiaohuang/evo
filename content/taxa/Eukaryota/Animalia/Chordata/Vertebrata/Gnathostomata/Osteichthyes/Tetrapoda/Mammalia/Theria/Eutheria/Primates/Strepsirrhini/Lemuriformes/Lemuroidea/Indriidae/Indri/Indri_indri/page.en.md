## lifeHistory

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
A longitudinal study at Maromizaha Forest, Madagascar, followed eight wild infants from eight family groups. Maternal carrying was frequent in the youngest sampled class and declined as locomotion increased; play and grooming with non-maternal group members were first recorded at 128 days. Singing appeared only in the 16–20-month class in this sample, which cannot represent species-wide developmental variation.

## ecology

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
Leaves were the most consumed food in all four sampled infant age classes. Fruit made up 15.14% of feeding time in the 0–3-month class and 27.09% in the 4–7-month class. Across the study, the authors recorded foods from 18 plant families and found that mother–offspring co-feeding declined with infant age. These are feeding observations from young indris at one forest, not a complete species diet.
