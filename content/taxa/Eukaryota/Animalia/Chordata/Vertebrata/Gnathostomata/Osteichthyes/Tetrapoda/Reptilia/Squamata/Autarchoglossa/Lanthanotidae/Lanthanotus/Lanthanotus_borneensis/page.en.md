# Lanthanotus borneensis

## Catalogue placement

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
Lanthanotus borneensis Steindachner, 1878 is an accepted species in pinned COL26.8, under Lanthanotus.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Skull samples and models

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
McCurry et al. (2015) built skull models from CT geometry of Lanthanotus borneensis: YPM 6057. Table 1 records dorsal cranial lengths of YPM 6057, 1.8 cm. These are sample measurements, not species size ranges or dimensions of volume-standardized models.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Response under standardized loading

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The sampled skull belongs to the shorter, more robust part of morphospace. Both the cranium and mandible fall among the lower-strain models under bite loading. This supports a discussion of shape and response, not a measurement of feeding performance in every individual.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Evidence limits

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
The finite element models standardize volume and bite loading and use homogeneous isotropic bone properties with shared muscle-attachment assumptions to compare shape. Model strain is not measured live bite force. Single-skull samples, age differences and model restraints limit generalization; they do not establish whole-species rankings, conservation status or complete life histories.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Distinguish the measured length

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
Recorded dimensions are reported maximum snout–vent length (SVL) 220 mm; female maximum snout–vent length (SVL) 166.9 mm. SVL runs from snout to vent and differs from total length including the tail. Compiled maxima are reported material, not theoretical growth limits or typical individual size. Sex-specific values may come from different localities or studies and alone cannot quantify population sexual dimorphism.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Where and how it is active

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
In the entry, microhabitat is Semi-Aquatic; diet is Carnivorous; active timing is Nocturnal. These categories summarize activity and resource use rather than an exhaustive habitat list or fixed schedule. Positions such as arboreal and terrestrial can be combined. A carnivorous category does not specify prey, and populations, seasons and life stages may differ.

## How to read reproductive records

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
the reproductive-mode field uses the original label oviparous; consolidated mean offspring or eggs per litter/clutch 5.3; lowest reported clutch/litter mean 2; highest reported clutch/litter mean 6. Clutch/litter summaries can use midpoints of reported means or, when means are unavailable, extrema; they are not necessarily arithmetic averages of observed clutches. Fractions do not describe non-integer young in a litter. The lowest/highest fields are defined as endpoints of reported means rather than guaranteed smallest/largest individual clutches. Reproductive and sex-determination labels do not establish fetal nutrition or uniformity across all populations.

## Limits of these trait data

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
This account paraphrases the same-name ReptTraits v1-2 entry, retaining its 2023 taxonomic framework and sources for individual traits. One row per species compresses variation in sex, age, locality and populations; matching name and family do not ensure equivalent later concepts. Reported maximum length is not an absolute species limit, and reproductive means are not fixed counts in every clutch. Many mass entries are estimated from length equations and are not presented here as measured mass. Juvenile-length headers and definitions differ and are also omitted from reader conclusions. Conservation, venom, current distribution and population trends are not updated here; unrecorded fields remain gaps. Dataset citation labels preserve the source chain and do not imply that this project independently reread every original work.
