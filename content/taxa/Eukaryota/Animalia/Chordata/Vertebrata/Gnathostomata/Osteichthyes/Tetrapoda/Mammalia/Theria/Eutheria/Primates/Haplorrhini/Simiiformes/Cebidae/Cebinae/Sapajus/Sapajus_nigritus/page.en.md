## ecology

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
A study of fragmentation in Brazil's Atlantic Forest used playback surveys around Alfenas and recorded black capuchins in 9 of 45 fragments during 2010–2013. Model comparisons identified patch area as the leading explanation for local occurrence; functional isolation, visibility and surrounding land use appeared as secondary variables in some candidates that also included area. The authors tested models in 20 fragments near Poço Fundo, but the abstract lists area and area-plus-visibility models for this species, while the Figure 6 caption lists area and area-plus-matrix models. This guide retains the finding that area mattered without choosing between those conflicting descriptions. The sites and models do not establish abundance or general habitat requirements across the species' range.
