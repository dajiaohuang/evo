## A local cloud-forest occurrence and its elevation
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc8050035-3pld9
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
The 2021 Santa Cruz Tepetotutla checklist reports Incilius spiculatus at 600–1760 m above sea level during its 2018–2019 survey. Table 1 compares this with an earlier reported elevation of 800–1689 (Mendelson 1997) and lists photographic catalogue number IBH-RF 607. The locality is in the Chinantla region of Oaxaca, Mexico. This is a reported local occurrence, not a surveyed global range, independent verification of the archived photograph, or evidence of occupancy at every intermediate elevation. Historical elevations are retained as attributed comparisons rather than merged automatically with the new observation.

## Interpret the record within the survey design
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc8050035-3pld9
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/text/en -->
Four observers surveyed pristine and managed cloud-forest sites on eleven five-day trips in 2018–2019, in wet and dry seasons. Three daily windows were 09:00–13:00, 15:00–19:00 and 21:00–01:00; the paper reports 2,640 effective field hours for the whole herpetofaunal inventory. Animals were photographed and released, and GPS locations had a reported 5 m error range. The checklist's Incilius spiculatus row establishes presence in that sampling context. Total effort and the nineteen amphibian species are not species-specific detection probabilities, population estimates or trends. The accumulation curve had not reached an asymptote, so a missing record cannot establish local absence.
