## Read the study

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
Does a broad snout necessarily mean a stronger bite? This guide follows the accepted COL26.8 record PBBS and reads the study at its stated sample and geographic scale. The bite-force experiment compared the broad-snouted caiman with the long-snouted Australian freshwater crocodile: despite sharply different snout shapes, similarly sized animals produced comparable bite forces at the rear molariform tooth position. Across 23 living species, body mass explained bite force better than snout width, and tooth-pressure values did not form a simple, uniform group for broad-snouted forms.

Continue with Crocodylus johnstoni (COL C6Q4M) to compare the broad- and slender-snouted examples from the same experiment. The Crocodylia group guide provides the wider reading route; the neighboring page retains its own sources and limits.
