## feedingBiomechanics

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
The bite-force experiment compared the broad-snouted caiman with the long-snouted Australian freshwater crocodile: despite sharply different snout shapes, similarly sized animals produced comparable bite forces at the rear molariform tooth position. Across 23 living species, body mass explained bite force better than snout width, and tooth-pressure values did not form a simple, uniform group for broad-snouted forms.
