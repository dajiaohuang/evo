## distribution

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
Freire Filho and Palmeirim combined existing records with their own surveys to model the potential distribution of the Caatinga howler across northeastern Brazil. The source data included 20 direct observations and 112 reports from community interviews collected during 2004–2010; the authors also surveyed two poorly documented regions from August 2016 to May 2017. Their initial database contained 184 occurrences before spatial filtering. The model highlighted priority areas in mangroves, humid enclaves, the Caatinga and the Piauí–Maranhão border, but these are model outputs based on historical and unevenly sampled records, not confirmed occupancy or a current census.

## habitat

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
In a 2024 master’s thesis, Carvalho used adapted boat transects along the Mapari River in the mangroves of Humberto de Campos, Maranhão, from August 2023 to January 2024. The thesis reported a relative-abundance estimate of 0.61 per 10 km for Alouatta ululata and modelled habitat use against vegetation cover, distance to agriculture and distance to settlements. Its model suggested a potentially positive association with vegetation cover, no significant effect of proximity to agriculture, and records farther from settlements. This is a local, six-month study and should be read separately from the broader, older distribution model.

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
Source-linked introduction; not externally expert-reviewed. The 2020 potential-distribution model uses historical records and uneven survey coverage; the 2024 master’s thesis covers one municipality and six months of mangrove transects. Neither is a current range-wide census or a complete species dossier.
