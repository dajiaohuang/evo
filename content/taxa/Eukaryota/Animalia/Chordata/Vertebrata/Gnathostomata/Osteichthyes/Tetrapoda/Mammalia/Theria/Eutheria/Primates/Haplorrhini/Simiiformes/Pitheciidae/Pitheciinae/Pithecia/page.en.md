## overview

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
Taxonomy in Pithecia has been repeatedly revised. Marsh's 2014 treatment brought together museum skins, skulls, photographs and historical names in a broad genus revision. Serrano-Villavicencio and colleagues revisited the P. irrorata group in 2019 using 54 skins from nine scientific collections across its range; they placed P. rylandsi, P. pissinattii and P. mittermeieri within P. irrorata while retaining P. vanzolinii. A 2024 addendum then questioned the geographic basis for applying P. inusta to populations along the Ucayali and upper Juruá. Pinned COL26.8 still retains several distinct accepted names, so readers should separate catalogue identity, the evidence used by each study, and later name treatments.

Suggested path: begin with the 2014 revision for names, types and specimens; compare the Napo and Equatorial saki Curaray survey and its local records on opposite riverbanks; then read the Isabel's saki type series and Miller's saki Colombian records. Finish with the Vanzolini's, Rylands' and Pissinatti's bald-faced saki pages to compare the 2019 pelage argument with COL/MDD name differences, and close with the 2024 P. inusta addendum. This path covers this batch and selected related pages, not the full genus or a final classification.

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
This is a genus-level guide to the matrix in Supplementary Table S1 of Mourthé et al. (2022), not a complete natural-history or range review. Range-map overlap is not point-level field confirmation; main-text Table 2 has inconsistent 80/81 totals and 57%/58% reporting. This guide is not externally expert-reviewed. This guide selectively covers part of the taxonomic history and local studies; it is not a species-by-species literature review of the genus. Sources differ in accepted names; this account preserves those differences and does not infer a final species count, present-day range or conservation status. Chinese names are editorial, and this batch has not received external expert review.
