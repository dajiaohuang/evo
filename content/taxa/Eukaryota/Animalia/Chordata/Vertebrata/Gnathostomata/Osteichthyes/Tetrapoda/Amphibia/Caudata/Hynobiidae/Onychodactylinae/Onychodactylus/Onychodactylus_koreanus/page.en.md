## Two inferred population breeding windows at one cave
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12875514-49r6f
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The 2026 study combined twenty-nine fortnightly surveys from April 2024 to June 2025 in Hwanseongul Cave with water eDNA analysis. Breeding adults, including females with visible eggs and females without visible eggs interpreted as post-oviposition, emerged mainly in late spring and early winter. Together with egg observations and DNA signals, the authors inferred population breeding windows in April–June and November–December. This is a site- and period-specific synthesis; it does not prove that every koreanus population follows identical months, that all eDNA peaks mark spawning, or that the same female breeds twice annually. Females were classified from appearance rather than continuously observed through each laying event.

## Winter clutches and a differing July visit date
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12875514-49r6f
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
On 23 December 2024 researchers found three clutches, one unfertilized; the other two contained fifteen and fourteen eggs split between paired sacs. The eggs hatched on 2 May 2025. The results report 110 clutches on 28 July 2025, whereas the methods date the extra egg-check visit to 29 July; that date conflict remains unresolved. Ten examined summer clutches had mean ± SD 16.6 ± 1.1 eggs, range 15–18, rather than a count applicable to every recorded clutch. Neither two winter clutches nor the summer subsample defines a species-wide clutch-size limit, female annual output or survival of every embryo.

## Individual seasonal participation and isolation remain open questions
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12875514-49r6f
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
The authors distinguished two population breeding seasons from the untested possibility that one female reproduces in both. They considered repeated annual spawning by the same female unlikely on energetic and egg-development grounds, but did not mark and follow females to test that prediction. Proposed nutritional and temperature windows, or genetic differentiation between seasonal breeders, were explanations for future work. The reported seasonal pattern therefore does not establish two reproductively isolated lineages, sympatric speciation, a measured recovery interval or an individual female’s lifelong schedule.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Water DNA signals are conditional indicators rather than animal counts
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12875514-49r6f
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
The cave-outlet sampling site had more eDNA detections than the upstream sampling point. Only upstream DNA quantity correlated significantly with observed breeding adults; it did not significantly correlate with larval counts. Larvae remained present during some summer visits without positive DNA detection. Later winter peaks occurred without observed adults, and decomposition of sixteen dead larvae was discussed as a possible contribution. Site hydrology, DNA shedding and degradation can therefore affect the signal. Copies per microlitre, sample-positive proportions and PCR replicate counts are not interchangeable population densities, and nondetection is not demonstrated absence.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Size classes and yolk observations do not fix larval ages
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12875514-49r6f
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/text/en -->
Researchers used approximately five centimetres total length to divide larval size classes because many animals could not be captured. Methods describe these as less-than-one-year and greater-than-two-year groups, while results summarize one-year and two-year larvae; those labels are not independently verified birth dates. Results report yolk-bearing larvae from 13 November to 10 January, but discussion extends the observation to 20 January, an unresolved end-date difference. COI sequencing identified sampled larvae of several sizes and adults as koreanus, supporting identity rather than age or cohort assignment. The observed roughly 130-day winter egg interval is not a universal larval metamorphic duration.
<!-- /evo:text -->
