Male holotype NWIPB 770650 came from Gelin, Medog, Xizang, at 1530 m. The monograph recognized records from several Medog localities at 1530–2003 m. Its remarks attributed some specimens previously assigned to this species to misidentifications reallocated to J. yeae using morphological and molecular evidence. ## Cited measurements rather than a new examined series
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10083232-q32xk
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The diagnosis was explicitly based on previous descriptions, reporting seven adult males at 33.6–36.6 mm snout–vent length and five females at 35.8–42.8 mm. It described vomerine ridges and teeth, rudimentary basal toe webbing with narrow fringes, and a grey pad with dense nuptial spines on the dorsal base of finger I in breeding males, with a subgular vocal sac. These are the monograph’s synthesis of earlier material, not a newly measured twelve-specimen series or measurements of only the three original types.

## Bari and Renqingbeng population interpretation
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10083232-q32xk
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
The remarks discussed a population previously labelled M. cf. pachyproctus from Bari and Renqingbeng Temple, about 20 km from Gelin. Earlier proposed differences concerned the postcloacal protrusion, inner metatarsal tubercle and osteology. The monograph regarded the external characters as individually variable, noting possible preservation changes, and cautioned that the osteological comparison relied on one individual. It therefore treated that population as J. pachyproctus. This is an attributed conspecificity interpretation; the account does not independently test reproductive compatibility or demonstrate a preservation effect in every specimen.
<!-- /evo:text -->
