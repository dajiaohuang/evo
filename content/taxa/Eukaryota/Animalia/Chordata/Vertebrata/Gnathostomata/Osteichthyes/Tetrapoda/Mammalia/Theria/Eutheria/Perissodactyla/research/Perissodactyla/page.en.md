# Perissodactyla

## atlas-profile / overview

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/overview -->
An early Eocene radiation whose initially similar branches diverged into horses, tapirs, rhinoceroses and many extinct groups; the profile treats that history as a branching record, not a ladder.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## atlas-profile / ecology / diet

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/ecology/diet -->
Terrestrial herbivory across sampled lineages
<!-- /evo:text -->

## atlas-profile / ecology / habitat

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/ecology/habitat -->
Lineage-specific terrestrial habitats; no single order-wide habitat
<!-- /evo:text -->

## atlas-profile / ecology / locomotion

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/ecology/locomotion -->
Terrestrial, with strongly divergent limb specializations
<!-- /evo:text -->

## atlas-profile / evidenceSummary

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/evidenceSummary -->
Tissier and Smith constrain the earliest sampled radiation and dispersal; Prothero synthesizes the broader fossil branching pattern. Neither source supplies an exact origin date or one ecology for the whole order.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Body, life and relationships
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/0/text/en -->
Horses, tapirs and rhinoceroses look strikingly different today, while early perissodactyl fossils are often distinguished by subtle dental and cranial features. Teeth, skulls and limbs changed along different branches. Living and extinct groups together record that diversification.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/1/title/en -->
## Living differences and early dental and cranial features
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/1/text/en -->
Horses, tapirs and rhinoceroses look strikingly different today. Distinguishing early perissodactyl branches often relies on subtle dental and cranial features. Comparing them connects familiar living groups with the fossil record.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/2/title/en -->
## Teeth, skulls and limbs along different branches
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/2/text/en -->
Perissodactyla also contains many extinct groups. Teeth, skulls and limbs changed along different lineages. A branching account is more faithful to that history than a row of fossils labelled as successive ancestors.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/3/title/en -->
## Material for Eocene diversification and dispersal
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/3/text/en -->
Studies constrain diversification and dispersal among sampled early Eocene branches. They do not supply an exact origin instant or one way of life for the whole order. Ask which lineage, places and preserved structures support each conclusion.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## atlas-profile / readerLimitations

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerLimitations/en -->
Anatomical observations, interpretations of behaviour and modelled branching times answer different questions. The selected material does not identify a direct ancestor or establish a complete distribution.
<!-- /evo:text -->
