## Accepted name and placement
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
COL26.8 accepts Ptychadena robeensis Goutte, Reyes-Velasco, Freilich, Kassie & Boissinot, 2021 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Ptychadenidae (family) → Ptychadena (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## A geographically sampled mitochondrial and nuclear dataset
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10434514-smllj
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The 2023 Ethiopian Highlands study lists 61 mitochondrial samples and 10 nuclear-SNP samples for Ptychadena robeensis in Table 1, with mitochondrial nucleotide diversity π=0.03%. Samples across the twelve-species study were collected in 2011–2019; these are analysed specimens, not a population census. All assembled mitochondrial genomes had the usual two rRNAs, thirteen protein-coding genes and twenty-two tRNAs, but no species had a completely resolved control region because repeats could not be confidently assembled from short reads. The phylogenetic alignment used selected genes (13,937 bp across 303 samples), rather than an alignment of complete control regions.

## A study-defined highland clade and regional distribution
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10434514-smllj
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The paper places Ptychadena robeensis in its P. erlangeri clade and reports it from the eastern side of the Great Rift Valley. These distributions combine the authors' geographic sampling and earlier range work; they are not independently updated complete range polygons. The twelve-species highland clade is a subset of Ptychadena. Its mitochondrial-clock analysis estimated a common ancestor around 11.7 million years ago, with a reported interval of 8.827–15.494 million years. The east-of-rift ancestral reconstruction had a model probability of 69%; neither that estimate nor inferred crossings is a directly observed migration event or a nuclear-genome divergence date for this species.

## A reference assembly in a comparative genome study

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12822217-smllj
<!-- /evo:text -->

The study published online in December2025 and in the 2026 volume lists Ptychadena robeensis assembly GCA_038048845.1, with an assembled genome-size summary of 1.59 Gbp and contig N50 12.4 Kbp. These describe this listed assembly and its continuity, rather than a direct measurement of DNA per cell, a universally fixed species genome size or a demonstrated ploidy. The source selected NCBI reference assemblies on 29October2024. Original sequence data, assembly quality and specimen identity have not been independently reassessed here.

## Repeat annotations are computational observations
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12822217-smllj
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/text/en -->
The authors built species-specific repeat libraries with RepeatModeler2, classified unknown elements with RFSB and supplemented the libraries with vertebrate repeats before applying RepeatMasker. Its hits are termed insertions in the paper, but a hit is not independently demonstrated to be a complete, expressed or mobile element. Superfamily labels can aggregate distinct families. Element counts, total occupied length and genome proportions are separate quantities; overlapping hierarchy categories cannot safely be added as independent fractions. No new repeat annotation or expression experiment for Ptychadena robeensis was performed here.

## Across-species correlations do not prove this species mechanism
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/10/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12822217-smllj
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/10/text/en -->
The study compared61 assemblies using phylogenetically adjusted models, an earlier pruned tree, contig-N50 covariates and multiple-comparison correction. Recently active Ty3/DIRS1-associated sequences correlate with genome size across that sample, but recent activity is a divergence-based proxy, not direct observation of current transposition in this species. Length and open-reading-frame similarity filters likewise do not demonstrate active protein expression. The authors' suggested developmental constraints are comparative hypotheses; they do not establish a causal TE effect on this species' larval duration or clutch size.
