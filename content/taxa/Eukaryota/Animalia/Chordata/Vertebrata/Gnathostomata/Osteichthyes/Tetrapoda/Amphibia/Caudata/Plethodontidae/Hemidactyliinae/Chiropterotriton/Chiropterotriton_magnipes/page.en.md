## A cave-associated species and limits of accessible searches
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7518159-5y6ls
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/text/en -->
The 2020 study described magnipes as an obligate cave-dwelling Neotropical salamander, with large fully webbed feet, protruding eyes and mostly unpigmented skin. Known historical caves were in Querétaro and San Luis Potosí; the study also surveyed Hidalgo sites. Searchers scanned accessible walls, ceilings and floors, but inaccessible passages and deeper areas remained unsearched. The attributed cave specialization is not proof that every cave in the wider karst is occupied or empty, and large webbed feet do not establish measured adhesion or swimming performance.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Positive skin swabs with repeated individuals possible
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7518159-5y6ls
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/text/en -->
Live swabs were collected in 2017–2018 without marking animals, so minimum distinct-individual counts were inferred from age and sex. Six magnipes encounters at El Coni represented at least five individuals and nine at La Trinidad at least six. The discussion reported Bd-positive swabs 2/6 and 2/9 respectively. Samples are not automatically unique animals. The results’ aggregate site denominators, 24 and nine, do not sum to the stated total 29 swabs and remain unresolved. No dead animals or skin lesions were observed; fourteen tested salamanders were Bsal-negative, which does not prove regional absence or resistance.

## One modern pathogen genotype and uncertain historical causation
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/10/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7518159-5y6ls
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/10/text/en -->
One successfully genotyped contemporary Bd-positive sample belonged to the Global Panzootic Lineage. Historical positives could not be genotyped, so the older fungus is not certified as the same lineage. Museum detections began in 1969 after earlier negatives, but the authors could not exclude historical false negatives or jar cross-contamination. They found timing consistent with a role in decline, while lacking susceptibility trials or direct evidence connecting infection and mortality. Detection, lineage assignment, chronology and causal interpretation therefore remain separate.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Sparse encounters do not certify extinction at old sites
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/11/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7518159-5y6ls
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/11/text/en -->
The study found extant magnipes at two surveyed sites and none at historical sites, but explicitly noted that some caves were visited only once and detection was imperfect even where animals remained present. Museum collections were not precise historical abundance estimates because not every seen animal was collected. Habitat degradation and historical collecting were discussed as possible additional contributors. Low contemporary counts are not standardized population density, and old-site nondetection is not definitive extinction. The account’s survey-site totals also differ between the methods and results and are not silently harmonized.

## Monitoring proposed for apparently healthy infected populations
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/12/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7518159-5y6ls
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/12/text/en -->
The authors described the two known extant populations as Bd-positive but apparently healthy and relatively constant at low encounter abundance since discovery. They called for repeated host and pathogen monitoring to resolve whether infection had reached an enzootic state or still affected survival, and protection of cave habitat from human impacts. These were 2020 interpretations and recommendations, not verified current population stability, measured survival probabilities or proof that monitoring was subsequently implemented.

## Selected Mexican survey localities, 2017–2018
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/13/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12135470-5y6ls
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Skin swabs describe a filtered bacterial community
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/14/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12135470-5y6ls
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/14/text/en -->
For Chiropterotriton magnipes and the other sampled hosts, the study changed gloves between animals, rinsed each with 100 ml sterile water and swabbed the whole body for thirty seconds. A separate ventral-body/tail/leg swab was used for Bd testing. The V4 16S bacterial assay retained forward reads, filtered very rare variants and rarefied to 17,536 reads per sample. It is not a complete skin metagenome, cultured viable-cell census or fungal community survey. Eight environmental swabs represented cave walls; each cave supplied only one wall sample, with no matched forest or karst environmental sampling. Negative bacterial controls did not amplify and were not sequenced, which is not independent proof of absence of contamination.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Bd detection is not a test of resistance or disease survival
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/15/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12135470-5y6ls
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/15/text/en -->
The Results report 3/13 (23.1%) Bd-positive Chiropterotriton magnipes samples. This is a small, dated sampled proportion, not regional prevalence, an experimental infection challenge or proof of resistance. No obvious disease signs or dead/dying animals were observed during the sampling visits; absence of observed signs does not establish later survival or population safety. Bd was quantified as ITS copies with synthetic DNA standards, not directly counted zoospores. ITS copy number differs among strains, so copy values cannot be equated to a cited fatal-zoospore threshold. Aggregate Results 31/86 differs from Methods microbiome comparison groups 30 positive/57 negative; skin-sequencing and diagnostic denominators remain unresolved without the individual supplement.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Antifungal capacity is inferred rather than demonstrated in this host
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/16/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12135470-5y6ls
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/16/text/en -->
The study matches bacterial marker sequences to a reference database of previously tested Bd-inhibitory isolates and predicts functions with PICRUSt2. It does not culture this Chiropterotriton magnipes sample’s bacteria to demonstrate inhibition, measure antibiotic production or test a probiotic treatment. Cave and forest samples did not differ significantly in abundance/diversity of potentially inhibitory bacteria; this does not establish equivalent protection. Pooled microbial co-occurrence edges are statistical associations, not observed cooperation or competition, and the networks were not estimated for each host species. The authors warn that reference-genome predictions may miss real functions or infer absent ones in poorly represented cave communities.
