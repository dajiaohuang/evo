## overview

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
This page follows accepted COL26.8 species ID 7TL9S. MDD v2.5 uses the display name Southern Patas Monkey and treats E. baumstarki as a species. The 2009 Tanzania review predates that species-level treatment and uses E. patas baumstarki; the 2021 review discusses the later species concept and adds Kenyan records.

## distributionSurvey

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
The 2009 review assembled 28 Tanzanian localities: 14 records before 1996 and 14 post-1995. It combined museum and literature records, more than 120 interviews, and a western Serengeti reconnaissance survey. Its estimate of roughly 33% reduction in mapped range since 1995 and 150–900 animals was the authors’ 2009-era reconstruction, not a current census.

## distributionUpdate

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The 2021 paper reports the first evidence for E. baumstarki in Kenya and combines fieldwork with museum, literature and solicited locality records. Its synthesis places post-2009 extent of occurrence near 2,150 km² and estimates 100–200 individuals, while noting losses from Kenya around 2015 and the Kilimanjaro Region around 2011. These are dated results of that review, not present-day confirmation.

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
The Tanzanian paper uses the pre-split subspecies combination and combines several kinds of locality evidence; its estimates are historical. The 2021 review’s estimates are not a live census. MDD v2.5 lists the authority year as 1906, while COL26.8 lists 1905; the pinned COL identity is retained. The Chinese common name is editorial; this page has not been externally expert-reviewed. This page is not externally expert-reviewed.
