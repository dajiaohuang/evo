## Accepted name and placement
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
COL26.8 accepts Occidozyga lingnanica Lyu & Wang, 2022 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Dicroglossidae (family) → Occidozyga (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## Local sex-specific body size and a fully webbed voucher
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc13109738-qt68s
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The Kim Bang lingnanica sample included 33 males with SVL 19.0–27.8 mm (mean ± SD 23.65 ± 2.77), and fifteen females at 26.6–32.5 mm (28.97 ± 1.54). The sex ranges overlap and remain local summaries. The described male voucher had no vomerine teeth, unwebbed fingers, fully webbed toes with expanded round tips, a nuptial pad and tuberculate dorsal skin. A 557-bp mitochondrial sequence from HUS 2026 A.03 was 98.91% similar to a lingnanica sequence from Zhuhai, Guangdong. These are identification data, not a measured swimming advantage, a universal diagnostic sequence cutoff or a reconstruction of connectivity between the sampled regions.

## Beetles, aquatic prey and a sample-denominator boundary
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc13109738-qt68s
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
Forty-eight lingnanica yielded 280 stomach items, averaging 5.83 ± 4.42 SD per stomach, range 1–19. The sample contained eight insect orders as well as decapods, isopods, gastropods, spiders and Gobiiformes fish. Coleoptera had the highest reported IRI, 47.2%, and standardized niche breadth was 0.33, higher than the two Microhyla in this sample. Stomach-volume results instead used n = 47; the account does not explain the one-sample difference, so it is not silently harmonized. These recovered prey and modelled volumes do not establish daily intake, prey preference independent of availability, fish specialization or annual trophic breadth.

## Encounters at the stream edge rather than a measured preference
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc13109738-qt68s
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
The local survey reported lingnanica on the ground (36), in leaf litter (9), and muddy areas (3), approximately 0–1 m from the water edge. The authors often saw feeding searches along streams and linked that setting to aquatic prey in stomachs. The secondary forest contained small hardwoods, lianas and shrubs; frogs were hand-collected at night during April and July 2025. These detections are not an availability-corrected preference, a population-density estimate, proof of obligate stream breeding or a complete habitat description across the species range. The feeding-site interpretation remains local to this observational sample.

## Diet differences and an unconfirmed body-size explanation
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc13109738-qt68s
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/text/en -->
Pianka overlap was 49% with heymonsi and 47% with mukhlesuri, with significant abundance and volume ANOSIM differences from both. The discussion suggested that lingnanica’s larger body size might account for larger prey, but the reported SVL–stomach-content-volume correlation for lingnanica was not significant. A higher sample mean does not establish larger stomach contents in every individual: the reported sample means were 40.8 mm³ for lingnanica, 32.42 for heymonsi and 22.86 for mukhlesuri, with substantial variation. These results do not establish a causal size mechanism, prey-item size equivalence with total stomach volume or competitive release.

## Two measured adults are not a population size range
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc13139858-qt68s
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/text/en -->
The Central Vietnamese account describes male IB A.6489 at 22.31 mm snout–vent length and female IB A.6490 at 25.93 mm. These are individual measurements, not replicated sex-specific ranges. The stocky body has a head slightly longer than wide, laterally directed eyes with diamond-shaped pupils, indistinct tympanic rim and no vomerine teeth. Finger tips are round without discs or webbing; toe tips expand into discs and toes are described as two-thirds webbed. A large elongated inner metatarsal tubercle is present, the outer absent. Rough tuberculate dorsal skin and a smooth underside join the source morphology. Neither the female’s larger measurement nor one external feature establishes a universal sex or identity rule.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Live and preserved colour remain different observations
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/10/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc13139858-qt68s
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/10/text/en -->
The live account describes a grey-brown dorsum with irregular black mottling, barred limbs and a dark supratympanic fold. The pupil has a yellowish border and iris is brown; the throat is cream with grey mottling, chest and belly creamy white with speckles, and ventral limbs grey-pink. A light-yellow, slightly translucent nuptial pad is also described. Preserved dorsal colour fades towards grey and ventral speckles fade. That preservation change is not a natural seasonal colour shift, a female pattern or proof of population-wide colour constancy. The two vouchers do not supply a colour-variation census.

## A mitochondrial comparison supports the authors’ voucher attribution
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/11/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc13139858-qt68s
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/11/text/en -->
The study uses mitochondrial 16S sequences, with an 800-character alignment in the phylogenetic results. Specimens KP.2023.8 and KKK.2023.80 differ by 0.21–2.78% from the original lingnanica population, and the authors identify them as lingnanica in conjunction with morphology. This reported comparison is not a universal species threshold or nuclear-genomic proof of identity. Other within-species distances and interspecific summaries in the same paragraph have their own taxon scopes and are not substituted for this pair of vouchers. The named record attribution remains the source’s integrative conclusion, not independent certification of every historical occurrence.

## Central Vietnamese vouchers and northern predictions have separate scope
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/12/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc13139858-qt68s
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/12/text/en -->
Male IB A.6489, field KP.2023.8, was collected at Kon Plong Commune, Quang Ngai, in April 2023; its label prints elevation “1.180 m,” retained as ambiguous punctuation rather than silently converted into a certified altitude. Female IB A.6490, field KKK.2023.80, came from Kon Ka Kinh National Park, Gia Lai, in May 2023 at 926 m. The paper presents these as a new Vietnam country record and discusses a range from southern China and Hainan to Central Vietnam. Similar northern Vietnamese habitats are predicted future records, not confirmed localities or continuous occupancy between countries. Later current-record claims require their own source verification.

## Puddles and rice-field wetlands do not establish niche separation
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/13/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc13139858-qt68s
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/13/text/en -->
One specimen occurred in a small puddle in Kon Ka Kinh and the other in a shrub- and grass-rich wetland near rice paddies at Kon Plong; males called at the water surface. The discussion reports syntopy with the newly described nishikawai and calls for research on possible niche segregation. Shared sites do not prove competition, identical ecological needs or measured resource partitioning. Water-surface calling does not provide call duration, pulse rate, egg placement, clutch size or parental-care evidence. Both natural-water and agricultural-neighbourhood records retain their individual habitat context rather than becoming an experimental disturbance-tolerance claim.
