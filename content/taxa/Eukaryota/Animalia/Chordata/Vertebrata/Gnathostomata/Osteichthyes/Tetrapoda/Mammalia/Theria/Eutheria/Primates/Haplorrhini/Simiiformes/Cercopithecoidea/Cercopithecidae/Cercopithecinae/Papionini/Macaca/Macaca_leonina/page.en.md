## ecology

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
A study at Sakaerat Biosphere Reserve in Thailand followed one wild-feeding troop of about 143 northern pig-tailed macaques. After a four-month pilot, habituation data were collected during two field periods in 2016 and 2017, spanning 13 months. The researchers defined five stages—early, minimal, partial, advanced, and full—using encounter frequency, contact duration, responses to the observer, and progress in data collection. The paper gives two different timings: its Results section says the troop reached the full stage after almost 10 months, while the abstract and conclusion describe the process as close to 13 months.

Scan samples began only at the partial stage. Observations during the partial and advanced stages overrepresented adult males and subadults; observed age-sex composition did not match the troop demographic count until the full stage. Recorded movement also declined and social behavior increased as habituation progressed. This single-troop comparison shows how observer presence and habituation stage affected which individuals and behaviors entered the dataset.

The work concerns one observer, one troop, and one degraded forest fragment. The roughly 10-to-13-month timing is not a species-wide constant, and the observed group composition and activity patterns are not range-wide descriptions. The authors also discuss conservation risks when habituated primates lose fear of people, so habituation is both a research method and a conservation tradeoff.

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
This source-linked guide summarizes a single-troop study and is not externally expert-reviewed. It concerns one observer, one troop, and one fragmented forest site in Thailand; it does not establish range-wide group composition, activity budgets, or a fixed habituation duration for northern pig-tailed macaques. The paper provides neither a current population census nor a formal conservation assessment.
