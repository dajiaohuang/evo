# Trimeresurus erythrochloris

## A new species described from two adult types

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
Described in 2025, this pitviper takes its name from red bands on a green back. The type section lists male holotype RIM-00078 and female paratype RIM-00079, collected on 7 October 2024 near Tham Khao Siwa Cave, Sa Kaeo, Thailand, at about 250 m. The authors proposed the English common name Red-barred Green Pitviper.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Measurements and colour belong to the sampled individuals

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
Table 2 gives male snout–vent and tail lengths of 425 and 110 mm; female values are 545 and 105 mm. These two individuals cannot establish species-wide size limits or general sexual size differences. The male has a white stripe below the eye, absent in the female. Both have golden irises and grass-green backs; reddish-brown bands are less distinct in the female.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## What the mitochondrial tree supports

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
Three mitochondrial fragments total 2,296 aligned bases. The inferred sister relationship with T. venustus has moderate support (ML ultrafast bootstrap 94; Bayesian posterior probability 0.87). This maternal genealogy supplies neither a nuclear-genome test nor a divergence date.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Rainy-night observations around limestone and forest

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
Survey observations span lowland mixed semi-deciduous monsoon tropical forest at about 200–300 m. The collected snakes were on low bushes after rain between 20:00 and 01:00; uncollected sightings included limestone rocks, saplings and fallen branches, with some individuals up to 400 m from rock outcrops. Downward-facing posture suggested ambush, without identifying consumed prey.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Recorded locality and unconfirmed range

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
Confirmed records in the description are from Khlong Hat limestone hills. Occurrence farther along the karst chain, including Cambodia, is a prediction requiring confirmation. Full distribution, population size and trends need further surveys. The authors’ tentative Data Deficient suggestion does not establish a current formal IUCN assessment.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## An unresolved paratype-number inconsistency

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The variation paragraph calls the female RIM-00078; the type section, Table 2 and Figure 4 give RIM-00079. This account follows the concordant type section, table and figure while retaining the conflicting paragraph; no museum check or erratum reconciliation is asserted.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Evidence limits

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
This account uses the 2025 description and its 2024 survey. Two adult types and additional uncollected sightings leave diet, reproduction, venom, lifespan and current population trends unresolved.
<!-- /evo:text -->
