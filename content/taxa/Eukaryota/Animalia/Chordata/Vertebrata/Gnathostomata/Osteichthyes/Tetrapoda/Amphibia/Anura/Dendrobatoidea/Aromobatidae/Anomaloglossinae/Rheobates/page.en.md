## Assay and sampling differences constrain comparison
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
This Rheobates subset contains 4 GC-MS animals and 0 UHPLC-HESI-MS/MS animals. GC-MS uses full skins from the2014 collection series and relative integrated signals; LC-MS uses separately processed dorsal/ventral skins from the2022 Panama or2024 Texas series and reports qualitative detection rather than alkaloid quantities. The methods differ in sensitivity, retention-time resolution and structural annotation, so their compound counts and signal outputs are not directly comparable. Most alkaloids lack standards for absolute measurement; GC-MS profiles were not mass-corrected and some co-eluting compounds could not be separated. Table1 reports individual ranges and medians, while text also gives pooled species totals; neither can be substituted for the other. Supplementary individual records and raw chromatograms have not been independently inspected here.

## Chemical detection does not establish a sequestration mechanism
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
The authors propose that dietary toxin exposure, resistance and delayed elimination may precede specialised transport/storage mechanisms of chemical sequestration. The sampled Rheobates records help compare chemical occurrence but do not measure elimination rates, trace each molecule to prey or reconstruct ancestral transport pathways. A skin detection alone cannot distinguish temporary passive retention from derived sequestration, and a non-detection cannot exclude every defence chemical. The proposed ancestral state and sequence remain hypotheses requiring tissue/time-series experiments and wider lineage sampling; they are not certified biological properties of every species in this genus.
