# Callithrix jacchus

## Ecological evidence

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
In a captive study at the UZH Primate Station in Switzerland, six adult common marmosets (three males and three females) took part in eight researcher-assigned mixed-sex dyads. Before pairing, repeated short visual exposures between unfamiliar animals were associated with a model-estimated 32.3% decrease in direct distance per exposure; after pairing, mate status had no clear association with distance (P=0.37). The study also analyzed 436 sociosexual behaviors in 91 recordings: scan counts were higher before pairing, while after pairing counts were lower in pair-mate than in less familiar extra-pair dyads. The small sample and assigned partners make this a captive short-term assay, not evidence about wild social structure or free mate choice.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Evidence limits

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
Source-linked captive pair-formation study guide, not externally expert-reviewed. It covers six adults, eight researcher-assigned dyads, and one station; it cannot establish wild mate choice, long-term pair maintenance, or species-wide social behavior.

## Gestation and first reproduction

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
Compiled averages are active gestation 4.9 months; female age at first reproduction 16.33 months. Gestation covers active fetal development and excludes delayed implantation. Age at first reproduction is the female age at first breeding when conception is possible. If a source gives first-birth age, the original method subtracts gestation. It is not interchangeable with sexual-maturity or first-birth age. These are summaries across sources, not reproductive timetables for every individual.

## From newborn offspring to weaning

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
Compiled averages are newborn mass 27.12 g; age at milk independence 5.42 months; mass at milk independence 97 g. Newborn mass concerns one offspring; the original definition excludes records explicitly measured more than 12 hours after birth. Weaning means independence from milk; age is measured from birth and mass refers to that transition. All wild, captive and locality conditions are not distinguished here; these values do not supply a growth curve or parental-care account.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Litter size and frequency

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
Compiled averages are offspring born per litter 2.24 offspring; litters per female per year 2 litters/year. Litter size counts born offspring, excluding estimates based only on embryo counts or reproductive scars. Fractions describe averages rather than literal non-integer litters. Litter frequency counts births per female over 12 months. The compiler excluded captive studies designed to maximize production where possible; this does not establish that every record is from the wild. The product of two averages is not annual reproductive success for an individual female.
