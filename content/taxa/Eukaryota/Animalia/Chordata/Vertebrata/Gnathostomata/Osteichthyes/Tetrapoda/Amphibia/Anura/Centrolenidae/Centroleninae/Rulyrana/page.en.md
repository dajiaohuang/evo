## Field temperature routes preserve locality and measurement type
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The selected Rulyrana routes retain 1 species and 3 reported adult records from nocturnal sampling in Tolima, Colombia, during September–December 2018. Species pages separate each site/elevation and forest/open row, with body, substrate and air temperatures in degrees Celsius as mean ± standard deviation and observed range. Body temperature means dorsal skin at the encounter; nearby air is measured twenty centimetres above the frog. These are observed temperatures, not critical limits, preferred temperatures or a complete physiological niche for the genus. The study does not establish all-season exposure or independently marked unique individuals.

## Internal-temperature assumptions and canopy definitions remain bounded
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
A nine-animal/four-species repeated skin–cloacal comparison is used to assume that dorsal skin represents internal temperature; nonsignificant differences do not independently certify equivalence in every sampled species and environment. Six hourly ground loggers cover three elevations and two broad canopy categories, but are not positioned at every frog’s substrate. Forest canopy above sixty percent and open canopy up to forty percent exclude intermediate canopy conditions from those definitions. The pooled analyses adjust for body size without a phylogenetic model, so species composition and habitat choice limit claims about evolutionary causation.

## Associations and unresolved probabilities do not demonstrate tolerance
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
The source finds stronger pooled body–substrate than body–air associations and proposes conductive heat exchange, but does not measure heat flux, water loss or species-specific temperature choice. Open/forest mean differences are significant at low/middle elevations and nonsignificant at the highest; habitat variance is also nonsignificant. Table 2 prints p < 0.000 and a low-open body–air p > 0.789 despite prose saying significance in all cases; those entries are not silently corrected. Discussion also calls selected same-elevation species temperatures similar although their printed means differ. Forest specialization and lower tolerance are proposed future tests rather than established warming vulnerability or conservation outcomes.
