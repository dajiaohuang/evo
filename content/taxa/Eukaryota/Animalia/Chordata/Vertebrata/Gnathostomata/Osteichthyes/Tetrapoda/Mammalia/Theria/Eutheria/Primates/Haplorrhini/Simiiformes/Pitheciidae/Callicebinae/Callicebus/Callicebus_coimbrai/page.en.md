## overview

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
At a 14.4-ha forest fragment at Fazenda Trapsa in southern Sergipe, Souza-Alves followed one group of four: one adult male, one adult female, one subadult and one infant. The annual study recorded 36 plant species used for feeding. The group’s estimated home range was 11.7 ha, with roughly the same 9-ha area used in both seasons.

## fieldStudy

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
Fruit made up 54.8% of the group’s feeding records across most of the study, and young leaves 15.2%. Fruit use declined in the dry season but remained substantial; the group also used burned forest with new leaves more in that season. This describes seasonal foraging by one group, not a species-wide diet.

## distribution

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
A separate regional survey visited 54 localities in 2008–2010 and recorded titis at 12. Animals were seen directly at only five sites; other detections relied on calls and the locally known taxonomic context. The paper gave a conservative minimum of 25 groups, or about 80 animals, for that survey area and identification method.

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
Diet and home-range data come from one four-animal group in one small fragment. The regional minimum comes from a separate locality survey, and some species assignments relied on calls and the taxa then known locally. These results are not species-wide averages or complete range limits. Not externally expert-reviewed. This page is not externally expert-reviewed.
