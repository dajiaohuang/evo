## Terrestrial eggs with larvae remaining in jelly
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc11802484-pd22w
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
Frogs maintained as Gephyromantis mitsinjo deposited most captive egg masses on land beneath dried leaves or other refugia, including flowerpot pieces and half-cut plastic pipes. The observed nonfeeding tadpoles hatched but remained within egg jelly on land, rather than entering a free-swimming aquatic phase. Staff moved discovered eggs onto gravel and moist sphagnum in a cup for continued development. This observed larval phase should not be erased by calling the event simply direct development without qualification. Artificial refugia use and transferred clutches do not establish all natural oviposition sites or undisturbed parental attendance.

## Small relative clutch size and a twenty-two-day minimum
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc11802484-pd22w
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
Mitsinjo had the smallest mean egg-mass size among the eleven maintained species according to the study’s comparison. The minimum reported interval to completed development was twenty-two days, without an aquatic larval phase. The narrative does not supply an exact mean clutch count here, so no number is inferred from the comparison or figures. These are retained captive observations; most recorded clutches across the study were culled to limit facility populations. The relative clutch ranking and minimum time do not estimate wild fecundity, survival, generation length or a population-wide development limit.

## Related-species conservation suggestions are analogies
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc11802484-pd22w
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The authors used mitsinjo’s terrestrial, jelly-associated larval development to suggest possible vulnerability of related Gephyromantis to drying and altered rainfall. They did not directly observe the same mode in every named relative or experimentally measure climatic survival thresholds. Founder identification for the study was morphological only. The captive record can inform hypotheses and planning, while contemporary concept checks, wild breeding observations and species-specific demographic evidence remain necessary before treating it as a certified fecundity estimate or projecting outcomes for another species.
