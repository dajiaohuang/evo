# Lepidochelys olivacea

## Accepted catalogue record

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
COL26.8 accepts Lepidochelys olivacea (Eschscholtz, 1829) as one of two species under Lepidochelys. The release establishes its name and placement; it does not provide an extant-status field. This account covers the specific specimen and methods described in one study.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Specimen and imaging path

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
Yoshida, Motokawa and Endo (2022) identify NSM-H6187 as the L. olivacea head sample in their micro-CT work. The reconstruction shows cranial arteries and orbital glands. The methods note that the left eyeball and surrounding tissue were excluded from this specimen's reconstruction for dissection.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Reading the comparative claim

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The paper's Figure 1 shows a L. olivacea skull and orbital-gland reconstruction; Figure 2 describes a distinct mandibular artery. Its quantitative comparison concerns foramen measurements across multiple turtle families and uses specimen-level measurements. Those results do not describe every L. olivacea individual.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/text/en -->

These are not a longitudinal series of the same individuals or a species-wide range of individual masses.

It does not establish population-level anatomy, species-wide physiological variation, current range, abundance or conservation status. The content is paraphrased; no figure or table is reproduced.

## Distinguish the measured length

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
Recorded dimensions are reported maximum straight carapace length (SCL) 610 mm. SCL measures the shell in a straight line, excluding head, neck and tail. Compiled maxima are reported material, not theoretical growth limits or typical individual size. Sex-specific values may come from different localities or studies and alone cannot quantify population sexual dimorphism.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Where and how it is active

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
In the entry, microhabitat is Marine; diet is Carnivorous; active timing is Diurnal; foraging mode is ACT. These categories summarize activity and resource use rather than an exhaustive habitat list or fixed schedule. Positions such as arboreal and terrestrial can be combined. A carnivorous category does not specify prey, and populations, seasons and life stages may differ.

## How to read reproductive records

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
the reproductive-mode field uses the original label oviparous; sex determination is recorded as TSD; consolidated mean offspring or eggs per litter/clutch 110.5; lowest reported clutch/litter mean 30; highest reported clutch/litter mean 168. Clutch/litter summaries can use midpoints of reported means or, when means are unavailable, extrema; they are not necessarily arithmetic averages of observed clutches. Fractions do not describe non-integer young in a litter. The lowest/highest fields are defined as endpoints of reported means rather than guaranteed smallest/largest individual clutches. Reproductive and sex-determination labels do not establish fetal nutrition or uniformity across all populations.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Limits of these trait data

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
This account paraphrases the same-name ReptTraits v1-2 entry, retaining its 2023 taxonomic framework and sources for individual traits. One row per species compresses variation in sex, age, locality and populations; matching name and family do not ensure equivalent later concepts. Reported maximum length is not an absolute species limit, and reproductive means are not fixed counts in every clutch. Many mass entries are estimated from length equations and are not presented here as measured mass. Juvenile-length headers and definitions differ and are also omitted from reader conclusions. Conservation, venom, current distribution and population trends are not updated here; unrecorded fields remain gaps. Dataset citation labels preserve the source chain and do not imply that this project independently reread every original work.
