## Reticulata was removed from triangulum synonymy
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5332023-6cl95
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The authors examined the historical reticulata type from the Napo region and associated its small rounded dorsal marks with clade C. They removed Hyla reticulata from triangulum synonymy and used the combination Dendropsophus reticulatus. Historical clade C samples labelled triangulum consequently require reconciliation rather than being treated as evidence for both accepted species. The remarks suggested that Nacebe specimens previously called triangulum resembled reticulatus, based on their published description. This attributed inference does not replace a new specimen examination or certify every older label under the current concept.

## Broad colour variation in a relatively large measured series
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5332023-6cl95
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The diagnosis includes 185 males at 20–29.6 mm snout–vent length, mean 23.8 mm, and sixty-six females at 28–39.7 mm, mean 33.1 mm. Dorsal colour varied from brown to reddish brown, white or live bright yellow, with or without round dark marks; some individuals were reticulated. An elongated pale band covers the shank, and webbing and ventral surfaces are described as red to salmon pink in life. Photographic accounts give salmon-pink limb and thigh surfaces at night and red by day, with a coppery-bronze iris. Larger sample size does not make every colour pattern universal or establish the physiological mechanism of change.

## Introductory notes and secondary notes must remain distinct
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5332023-6cl95
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The advertisement account describes a Type I trill averaging 0.10 s and twelve to seventeen pulses, followed by two to four Type II notes averaging 0.039 s and five to six pulses. A multiple-pulse introductory note occurs in most calls. Whole advertisement calls average 0.35 s and dominant frequency about 2,992 Hz. The aggressive account describes three to five Type II notes, often with an introduction, average whole duration 0.59 s and dominant frequency about 2,832 Hz. A stray seconds unit accompanies an advertisement pulse-count value in that paragraph, so that number is not adopted as a duration. Note durations and total calls cannot be pooled, and absence of an introduction in some calls does not contradict the qualifier most.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Forest and clearing records with an elevation-report discrepancy
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5332023-6cl95
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The distribution account includes Brazil, Colombia, Ecuador, Peru and Bolivia. It states an elevation range of 40–1,037 m but calls Tabatinga at 63 m the lowest locality, an internal discrepancy retained here. Records occurred in terra-firme forest, flooded forest and swamps, generally by streams, lakes or temporary ponds in clearings. Nocturnal perches were 20–100 cm above ground. Occurrence in secondary forest and artificial open areas led the authors to suggest some disturbance tolerance; it was not an experimental tolerance threshold or evidence of equal survival in every habitat. The distribution is a dated set of records rather than a present occupancy audit.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## A historical range polygon is not occupied habitat
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5332023-6cl95
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
The study estimated a minimum-convex distribution polygon of 390,082 square kilometres for this account. It reported 11,093 square kilometres (2.8%) degraded by human activities. Natural-vegetation estimates used the Amazon Deforestation 2010 layer. A polygon includes intervening space and is not equivalent to occupied area, population size or measured percentage population loss. The conservation paragraph appears under reticulatus but names triangulum in its concluding Least Concern sentence; that conflicting species attribution is retained, not silently rewritten as a reticulatus assessment. Protection and broad range alone do not demonstrate a stable population or current site security.
