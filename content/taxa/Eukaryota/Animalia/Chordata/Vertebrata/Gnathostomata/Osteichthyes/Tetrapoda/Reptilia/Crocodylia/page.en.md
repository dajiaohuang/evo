## overview

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
A 2012 comparison measured 83 sexually mature individuals from the 23 living crocodilian species recognized at the time. At the prominent molariform tooth near the back of the upper jaw, taxon-representative bite forces ranged from 900 N in Cuvier's dwarf caiman to 8,983 N in the saltwater crocodile. Body mass was the main cross-species predictor; the phylogenetically corrected model had R² = 0.87. Rostral proportion explained only a small part of the remaining size-standardized variation, so a broad snout is not a direct proxy for greater bite force; the gharial was the low-relative-force exception. The study's samples and taxonomy are historical: COL26.8 counts 27 accepted living crocodilian species, and this guide does not reassign records from older, broader taxon concepts to later-separated species.
