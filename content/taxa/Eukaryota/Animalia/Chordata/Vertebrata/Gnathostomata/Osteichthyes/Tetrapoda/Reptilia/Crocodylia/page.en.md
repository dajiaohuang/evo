## Catalogue scope and bite-force evidence

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
Crocodylia is the accepted order recorded as COL 329 in COL26.8 (published 20 August 2026), under Reptilia. Its three family branches contain 27 accepted species. This fixed catalogue list has no extant flag; it defines the reading index here, while biological claims come from the individual studies.

A 2012 comparison measured 83 sexually mature individuals from the 23 living crocodilian species recognized at the time. At the prominent molariform tooth near the back of the upper jaw, taxon-representative bite forces ranged from 900 N in Cuvier's dwarf caiman to 8,983 N in the saltwater crocodile. Body mass was the main cross-species predictor; the phylogenetically corrected model had R² = 0.87. Rostral proportion explained only a small part of the remaining size-standardized variation, so a broad snout is not a direct proxy for greater bite force; the gharial was the low-relative-force exception. The study's samples and taxonomy are historical: the pinned COL26.8 Crocodylia branch contains 27 accepted species, and this guide does not reassign records from older, broader taxon concepts to later-separated species.

## Evidence limits

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
Source-linked group introduction, not externally expert-reviewed. Measurements reflect a limited adult institutional sample and the taxonomy used in 2012; the pinned COL26.8 branch has 27 accepted species, and later splits are not inferred from older pooled samples.

## Reading routes

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/topic -->
overview
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
Begin with the adult bite-force comparison here, then read Caiman latirostris, Crocodylus johnstoni and Gavialis gangeticus together to compare force, pressure and snout form. Crocodylus porosus adds a second study about tracked movements; keep that study separate from its biting measurements.

For local field evidence, follow Alligator mississippiensis → Crocodylus niloticus → Mecistops cataphractus → Crocodylus intermedius. Read each page's sample, dates, geography and method before comparing its conclusion. For specimen and lineage evidence, follow Crocodylus suchus → Crocodylus novaeguineae → Crocodylus halli → Mecistops leptorhynchus → Osteolaemus tetraspis → Osteolaemus osborni; Crocodylus acutus provides a separate population-genetic route. Each page retains its own study citations and limitations.

Use the three family lists below to find the remaining accepted-species guides. They are a catalogue reading index, not a sequence of ancestors or a ranking of evidence maturity. A 2012 paper's 23-species framework does not assign its older pooled samples to all 27 COL26.8 records. PBDB fossil concepts and occurrence records remain a separate source list even when a scientific name matches one in this index.
