## Unwebbed toes and male nuptial spines
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10083232-lx9m5
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
The diagnosis described neither vomerine ridges nor vomerine teeth, a relatively rough dorsum with interrupted V-shaped or X-shaped ridges, and toes without webbing or lateral fringes. Breeding adult males had nuptial spines on the dorsal bases of fingers I and II and a subgular vocal sac. These sampled diagnostic traits do not supply a breeding season, mating-system description or measured functional explanation for the spines.

## Conspecificity interpretation with acoustic disagreement
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10083232-lx9m5
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
The remarks traced the Longnan and Renhua populations labelled M. sp8 and M. sp9 from a possible cryptic-species interpretation to a conspecific treatment. The authors cited small genetic divergences and found no distinguishing morphology from the Wuyi type series, despite the separated distributions. They also retained a reported acoustic difference between M. sp9 and B. ombrophila. Limited data or geographic isolation were suggested explanations for that disagreement; neither explanation was established as a cause, and the acoustic conflict remains part of the evidence.
