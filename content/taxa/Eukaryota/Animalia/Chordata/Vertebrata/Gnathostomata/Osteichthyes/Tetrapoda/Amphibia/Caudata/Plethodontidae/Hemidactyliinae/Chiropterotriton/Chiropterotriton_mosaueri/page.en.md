## One positive result among three cave-study swabs
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7518159-5y6m5
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
The 2020 cave study reported Bd-positive results for one of three mosaueri swabs from caves with extant magnipes. Its sampling protocol did not mark animals, so swab counts are not assumed to be three independently identified individuals. No dead amphibians or skin lesions were observed at survey sites. A positive molecular assay documents sampled fungal DNA, not a demonstrated lethal infection, resistance, population-wide prevalence or a complete current range of the species.

## Selected Mexican survey localities, 2017–2018
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12135470-5y6m5

Figure 1 associates source-labelled Chiropterotriton mosaueri with Cueva del Coni. Researchers searched 26 localities in five Mexican states from March 2017 to November 2018; salamanders were encountered at sixteen. Identification used external size, tail proportions, feet and color pattern. These sites are observed study links, not the species’ complete distribution or proof of present occupancy. ## Skin swabs describe a filtered bacterial community
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12135470-5y6m5
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/text/en -->
For Chiropterotriton mosaueri and the other sampled hosts, the study changed gloves between animals, rinsed each with 100 ml sterile water and swabbed the whole body for thirty seconds. A separate ventral-body/tail/leg swab was used for Bd testing. The V4 16S bacterial assay retained forward reads, filtered very rare variants and rarefied to 17,536 reads per sample. It is not a complete skin metagenome, cultured viable-cell census or fungal community survey. Eight environmental swabs represented cave walls; each cave supplied only one wall sample, with no matched forest or karst environmental sampling. Negative bacterial controls did not amplify and were not sequenced, which is not independent proof of absence of contamination.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Bd detection is not a test of resistance or disease survival
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/10/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12135470-5y6m5
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/10/text/en -->
The Results state that Bd was detected in every sampled species except Chiropterotriton priscus, which includes source-labelled Chiropterotriton mosaueri; they do not give an unambiguous species-specific prevalence fraction here. No denominator is invented. The diagnostic measure is ITS DNA copies, not direct zoospore counts or viable pathogen burden. No obvious disease signs or dead/dying animals were seen during visits, but this does not establish resistance, later survival or population safety. Aggregate Results31/86 differs from Methods microbiome comparison groups30 positive/57 negative. The I. niger statements additionally alternate between one positive of five and one of eight; these unresolved counts cannot be silently reconciled.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Antifungal capacity is inferred rather than demonstrated in this host
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/11/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12135470-5y6m5
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/11/text/en -->
The study matches bacterial marker sequences to a reference database of previously tested Bd-inhibitory isolates and predicts functions with PICRUSt2. It does not culture this Chiropterotriton mosaueri sample’s bacteria to demonstrate inhibition, measure antibiotic production or test a probiotic treatment. Cave and forest samples did not differ significantly in abundance/diversity of potentially inhibitory bacteria; this does not establish equivalent protection. Pooled microbial co-occurrence edges are statistical associations, not observed cooperation or competition, and the networks were not estimated for each host species. The authors warn that reference-genome predictions may miss real functions or infer absent ones in poorly represented cave communities.
