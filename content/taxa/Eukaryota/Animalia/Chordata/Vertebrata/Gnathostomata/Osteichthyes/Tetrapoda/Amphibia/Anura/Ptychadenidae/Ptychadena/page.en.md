## A highland clade sampled with two genomic compartments
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The study analyses 294 focal mitochondrial specimens across twelve Ethiopian Highlands species and a 135-individual nuclear-SNP subset. Its three named clades are cooperi, erlangeri and neumanni. These names describe relationships within the selected highland lineage, rather than divisions covering the entire genus. All mitochondrial control regions remained incompletely assembled. Nuclear comparisons partly agree with mitochondrial population structure, including three versus four clusters in cooperi and no matching nuclear geography for the two neumanni mitochondrial groups. Species pages retain their different sample sizes and marker-specific findings.

## Cross-rift history is a reconstruction with uncertainty
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
The mitochondrial clock estimates the highland clade ancestor near 11.7 million years ago, with a reported interval of 8.827–15.494 million years; the selected ancestral-area model favours the eastern side with probability 69%. Dispersal estimates differ among lineages, and the neumanni clade has alternative east- and west-origin scenarios. These are fossil-calibrated mitochondrial and biogeographic model results, not directly observed migrations or dated nuclear species splits. The authors acknowledge that gene divergence can predate population or species divergence. Geological and climatic mechanisms remain interpretations of the reconstructed pattern.
