## Read the study

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
What does finding two dwarf-crocodile lineages at one shared site establish? This guide follows the accepted COL26.8 record 8SBC9 and reads the study at its stated sample and geographic scale. A survey site in a forest swamp in northwestern Congo recorded the Congo dwarf crocodile (Osteolaemus osborni) and the dwarf crocodile (O. tetraspis) in syntopy. The study compared a small number of individuals using more than 400 nuclear loci, about 650,000 base pairs, and found divergence comparable to that between South American dwarf-caiman lineages. The shared-site sample was small; the authors called for larger samples to examine questions such as hybridization. This does not establish range-wide co-occurrence or gene flow.

Continue with Osteolaemus tetraspis (COL 6TBCB) to compare the shared-site genomic sample with cave ecology and geographic-lineage evidence. The Crocodylia group guide provides the wider reading route; the neighboring page retains its own sources and limits.
