## Read the study

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
What does finding two dwarf-crocodile lineages at one shared site establish? This guide follows the accepted COL26.8 record 8SBC9 and reads the study at its stated sample and geographic scale. A survey site in a forest swamp in northwestern Congo recorded the Congo dwarf crocodile (Osteolaemus osborni) and the dwarf crocodile (O. tetraspis) in syntopy. The study compared a small number of individuals using more than 400 nuclear loci, about 650,000 base pairs, and found divergence comparable to that between South American dwarf-caiman lineages. The shared-site sample was small; the authors called for larger samples to examine questions such as hybridization. This does not establish range-wide co-occurrence or gene flow.

Continue with Osteolaemus tetraspis (COL 6TBCB) to compare the shared-site genomic sample with cave ecology and geographic-lineage evidence. The Crocodylia group guide provides the wider reading route; the neighboring page retains its own sources and limits.

## How to read reproductive records

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The reproductive-mode field uses the original label oviparous; consolidated litters/clutches per year 1. Clutch/litter summaries can use midpoints of reported means or, when means are unavailable, extrema; they are not necessarily arithmetic averages of observed clutches. Fractions do not describe non-integer young in a litter. The lowest/highest fields are defined as endpoints of reported means rather than guaranteed smallest/largest individual clutches. Reproductive and sex-determination labels do not establish fetal nutrition or uniformity across all populations.

## Limits of these trait data

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
This supplemental account uses limited trait material and does not count as complete morphology or ecology. This account paraphrases the same-name ReptTraits v1-2 entry, retaining its 2023 taxonomic framework and sources for individual traits. One row per species compresses variation in sex, age, locality and populations; matching name and family do not ensure equivalent later concepts. Reported maximum length is not an absolute species limit, and reproductive means are not fixed counts in every clutch. Many mass entries are estimated from length equations and are not presented here as measured mass. Juvenile-length headers and definitions differ and are also omitted from reader conclusions. Conservation, venom, current distribution and population trends are not updated here; unrecorded fields remain gaps. Dataset citation labels preserve the source chain and do not imply that this project independently reread every original work.
