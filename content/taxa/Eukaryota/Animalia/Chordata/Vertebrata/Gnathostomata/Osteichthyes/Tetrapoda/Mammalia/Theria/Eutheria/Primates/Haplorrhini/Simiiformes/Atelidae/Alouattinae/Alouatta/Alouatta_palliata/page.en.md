## lifeHistory

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
At Los Tuxtlas in Mexico, researchers visited ten howler-monkey groups monthly from 2000 to 2011, making 454 group visits. The monitored population remained relatively stable overall while total individuals increased. The authors inferred that most conceptions leading to births occurred from April to June, with weaning peaking in April–May. These seasonal patterns come from the studied Mexican subspecies population, not a fixed species-wide cycle.

## ecology

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
Among night-time sleep records for two adult howler groups on Ometepe Island, Nicaragua, 54 of 55 mantled-howler records used a sit-in posture (98.2%); 76.4% were on horizontal substrates and 83.6% on large branches. Observations came from particular groups and nights in 2003–2004 and 2006, so they do not estimate species-wide proportions.

## ecology

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
In a comparative study of food investigation, mantled howlers sniffed shared food plants about three times as often as white-faced capuchins (Cebus imitator), but less often than Geoffroy's spider monkeys. The comparison uses animals and foods observed at study sites; records from those sites do not establish a species-wide sensory mechanism or everyday rate.

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
This introduction combines specific samples from Mexico, Nicaragua and other Central American study sites; it is not a range-wide survey. Reproductive seasonality, sleep posture and food-investigation results each have their own site, subspecies, time and sampling limits, and no species-wide value is estimated here. Source-linked and not externally expert-reviewed.
