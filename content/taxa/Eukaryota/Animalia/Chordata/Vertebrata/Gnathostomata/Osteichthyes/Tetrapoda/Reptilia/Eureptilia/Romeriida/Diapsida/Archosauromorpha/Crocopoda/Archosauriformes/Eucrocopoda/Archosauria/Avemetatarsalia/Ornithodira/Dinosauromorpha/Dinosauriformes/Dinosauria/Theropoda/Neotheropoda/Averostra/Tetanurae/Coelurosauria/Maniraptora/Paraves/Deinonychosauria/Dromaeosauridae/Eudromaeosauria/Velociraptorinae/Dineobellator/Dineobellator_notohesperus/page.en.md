# Dineobellator notohesperus


## An incomplete skeleton anchors the species

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
Named in 2020, Dineobellator notohesperus is a dromaeosaurid theropod from New Mexico. Holotype SMP VP-2430 represents one disarticulated but associated animal with at least 20 identifiable skeletal elements, including skull fragments, limbs and tail vertebrae. First recovered in 2008, further material was collected at the same site in later years. The complete body outline in Figure 2 is a reconstruction, not a complete fossil skeleton.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## The horizon and two age interpretations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
The description places the holotype in sandstone a few metres above the base of the Naashoibito Member, Ojo Alamo Formation, at Bisti/De-na-zin. It cites detrital-sanidine argon dating for a maximum depositional age of 66.5 ± 0.2 Ma, alongside a biostratigraphic interpretation of about 70–68 Ma. A maximum depositional age constrains how old the sediment can be; it does not directly date this animal’s death. The alternatives are not combined into one precise age.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## The ulna preserves evidence of feather attachment

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
At least six bumps on the right ulna are interpreted as quill knobs, attachment sites for feathers. The authors estimate about 12–14 secondary feathers on the forearm. These are bone features, not preserved feather impressions from this animal. Figure 1C uses feathers from the modern owl Megascops kennicottii to illustrate attachment. It does not establish Dineobellator’s colors, complete plumage or flight ability.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Forelimb and tail anatomy inform functional hypotheses

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The shape of the humeral deltopectoral crest and enlarged flexor tubercle of the second manual claw underpin the authors’ proposal of stronger forearm flexion and grasping. Joint anatomy near the tail base is interpreted as allowing greater mobility. These comparative functional hypotheses use bones and muscle-attachment sites. The study did not measure living grip force, running speed or turning performance, or identify prey species.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Injury traces and a proposed encounter

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
A gouge and depression on the inner face of a manual claw are interpreted as possible trauma near death; the authors speculate about an encounter with another Dineobellator or a predatory theropod. A deformed, remodeled rib may indicate a healed fracture. They explicitly call for more evidence to confirm or reject the pathology interpretations. The mark does not identify an attacker or establish a conspecific fight or cause of death.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Two matrices resolve relationships differently

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The first analysis used 38 operational taxonomic units and 180 characters, yielding 32 most-parsimonious trees and placing Dineobellator in Velociraptorinae. A second theropod matrix used 157 units and 860 characters but left it in a broad dromaeosaurid polytomy. Family assignment and resolution within the family have different evidential strength. The 2020 placement and intercontinental-dispersal interpretation remain results of this study, rather than a claimed consensus of later research.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Evidence limits

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
This page combines fixed PBDB occurrence records with the 2020 description of one holotype. Database intervals, maximum depositional age and biostratigraphic interpretations are retained separately. Morphology supports the authors’ functional, feather and phylogenetic hypotheses, without directly establishing behavior, prey, colors or population variation. All subsequent revisions and taxonomic disputes are not assembled. This is not a complete monograph, direct specimen inspection or external expert review.
