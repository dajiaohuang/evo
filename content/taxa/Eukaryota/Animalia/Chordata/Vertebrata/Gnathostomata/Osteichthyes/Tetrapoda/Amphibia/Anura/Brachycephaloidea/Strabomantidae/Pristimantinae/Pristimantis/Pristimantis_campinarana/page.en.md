## A small frog diagnosed from several characters together
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10252896-scd47
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The description measured sixteen males at 17.3–20.1 mm snout–vent length and six females at 23.2–26.5 mm. These type-series ranges describe the measured sample rather than absolute species limits. The diagnosis combines finely roughened dorsal skin, a weakly areolate belly, eyelid tubercles, no heel tubercle or tarsal fold, and a visible tympanum whose membrane is poorly differentiated and annulus only partly visible. Fingers lack lateral fringes, whereas toes III–V have fringes and toes IV–V have basal webbing. Males have vocal slits and a small subgular vocal sac but lack nuptial pads. Identification depends on this combination and comparison with similar species; size or a single colour alone is insufficient.

## Live colour and preserved patterns are different observations
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10252896-scd47
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
Live frogs ranged from ochre and orange to brown, with dorsal stripes present in some individuals and absent in others. The iris was dichromatic, with pale bronze portions and a mahogany-red median stripe; the lower iris varied among specimens. In the preserved series, irregular dark marks, sharply outlined marks and dark stripes were reported at 68%, 9% and 23%, respectively. Those frequencies concern the studied series, not regional prevalence. Groin and ventral skin appeared darker by day with expanded melanophores and much paler, sometimes almost white, at night. The authors also suggested activity-related variation in skin texture. Neither observation was a controlled experiment demonstrating camouflage function or its physiological mechanism.

## Records from two white-sand forest localities
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10252896-scd47
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The study recorded campinarana at two localities in Iranduba, Amazonas, Brazil, in primary or slightly modified white-sand forest. All observed individuals occurred in campinarana, with canopy below twenty metres; none was found in campina, the more open vegetation with canopy below ten metres and exposed white-sand patches. Locally abundant was a qualitative field description, not a measured density. The authors proposed endemism to the Negro–Solimões interfluve and restriction to white-sand ecosystems because surveys elsewhere had not detected it. This is a geographic hypothesis from available records and non-detections, rather than a complete occupancy survey or a certified boundary for the species.

## Rainy-season calling and amplexus, with eggs still unobserved
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10252896-scd47
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
Frogs were mainly crepuscular and nocturnal. Calling was observed during the November–February rainy season, usually starting around 18:00, intense until 20:00 and declining by about 22:00, although rainy nights could sustain calling throughout the night. Males generally called around one metre above ground and rarely above three metres; aggregations sometimes contained up to ten callers. Dry-season shelter among terrestrial Guzmania bromeliad leaves was not evidence that eggs were laid there. Three axillary amplexus observations were reported. Four females contained about fourteen to seventeen large oocytes, but no egg clutch was found in situ. Oocyte counts therefore cannot be presented as observed laid-clutch sizes, and the article does not establish a natural egg substrate or nest survival rate.

## Advertisement calls sampled from nine males
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10252896-scd47
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
The acoustic description used twenty-six calls from nine males, so calls are repeated observations rather than twenty-six independent frogs. Calls contained five to ten notes, most often six to eight, and lasted 550–1,061 ms, with mean 694 ms and standard deviation 115 ms. Dominant frequency ranged from 3,295 to 3,919 Hz, with mean 3,587 Hz and standard deviation 204 Hz. The table explicitly defines the reported dispersion as standard deviation. Analyses followed a call-centred approach; inter-call intervals were not measured because microclimate could affect them, and minimum/maximum spectral frequencies were defined relative to the peak. These measurements characterize the sampled advertisement calls, not all vocal behaviours, an invariant species-wide frequency or a demonstrated mate-recognition threshold.

## Sequence relationships and a tentative west-to-east history
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10252896-scd47
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
The phylogenetic work combined mitochondrial 16S and COI with the nuclear marker RAG1; it was not a mitochondrial-only analysis. Campinarana grouped in a strongly supported clade containing matidiktyo and ockendeni, sister to delius plus librarius. Within the sampled campinarana population, mean 16S p-distance was 0.2%, with maximum 0.4%. Mean interspecific distances of 6.1–11.2% were reported across the examined group, not as a universal delimitation threshold or a separate campinarana estimate for every pair. The discussion proposed west-to-east dispersal and changing river barriers, while considering alternative river-crossing explanations. These hypotheses are not a dated reconstruction of a uniquely demonstrated dispersal route, and the sequence tree does not itself certify absence of gene flow.

## Data Deficient was a proposal based on incomplete knowledge
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10252896-scd47
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/text/en -->
The authors recommended Data Deficient because the full geographic range and population trends were insufficiently known. That recommendation in a 2023 description is not independent verification of a current IUCN assessment. They emphasized the distinctive white-sand ecosystems in which the species was found and the need to conserve them. Two known localities and qualitative local abundance do not establish a stable total population, secure future habitat or a quantified extinction risk. The proposed habitat restriction remains subject to further surveys, and neither unobserved sites nor potentially suitable forest should automatically be added as confirmed occurrences.
<!-- /evo:text -->
