## A geographically sampled haplotype boundary
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc8388460-6mql9
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The 2021 study found H. setoi cytochrome-b haplotypes from western Hyogo through Tottori to easternmost Shimane, but did not detect them around northeastern Shimane. The authors questioned some earlier setoi assignments there as potentially combining two species. Those are marker results and a taxonomic interpretation, not proof that every unsampled site lacked setoi or that each historical voucher has been formally reassigned. The study’s separate lineage from H. kunibiki remains bounded by its sampled animals and marker.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Tail stripes do not alone distinguish setoi from kunibiki
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc8388460-6mql9
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
Both H. setoi and H. kunibiki had distinct yellow stripes on dorsal and ventral tail edges in the study. The authors found no significant difference in the previously proposed mean SVL or forelimb/SVL and hindlimb/SVL ratios, while some other sex-specific measurements differed. They warned that definitive identification could be difficult without DNA. Shared stripes therefore are not a standalone diagnostic test, and nonsignificance is not proof of identical morphology or absence of differences in every population.
