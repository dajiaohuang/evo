## New types and older comparison traits have separate evidence roles
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
The 2023 study describes laojunshanensis from six July 2019 types at Mt. Laojun: five adult males and one adult female. It separately measured eight pleskei and eight ventripunctata, while using earlier descriptions for many other comparisons. Size-corrected morphology and four-locus phylogeny support the new lineage, but cited fold, vocal-sac and male nuptial-spine characters are not new population samples for every species. Marsh/pond occurrence and a July–August breeding-season inference belong to laojunshanensis alone; no tadpoles or direct spawning were documented. The linked pages keep these evidence units separate.

## Four subgenus lineages are a source-bound framework
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
The combined analysis used three mitochondrial markers and nuclear RAG-1 with older reference sequences, some missing loci. The authors recognize four major lineages and propose Minipaa for minica, alongside Nanorana, Paa and Chaparana allocations. Internal relationships among Minipaa, Paa and Nanorana were unresolved. Low 16S distances coexist with named species and morphological differences; a single three-percent threshold would not reproduce this framework. Marker distances, tree branches and cited older genomic work are not interchangeable with divergence dates, population gene-flow estimates or independent certification of a taxonomic rank.

## Uncertain vouchers and conflicting assignments stay unresolved
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/text/en -->
The source’s nominal arunachalensis sequence falls near Ingerana, conditional on correct voucher identification. Its Discussion describes type-locality vouchers, while Table 1 lists ZSIS-M40 with a Cona, Tibet locality; that disagreement prevents uncritical transfer of sequence or morphology claims to the fixed Odorrana arunachalensis page. The paper also questions a Bhutan photograph-only identification. For xuelinensis, Discussion suggests Paa but Table 7 marks Chaparana. Annandalii, feae, gammii and mokokchungensis lacked sequence placement in the study. These dated uncertainties do not certify current absence of data, a new synonymy or a complete genus concept reconciliation.

## A regional quadranus study separates reproductive endpoints
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/11/text/en -->
The quadranus study samples 266 frogs at thirteen mountain sites, then uses different subsets for testes, sperm and ovarian output. Testis size, sperm dimensions, sperm counts and ovarian ova estimates are distinct endpoints. Females are larger in the sampled medians, but age/body-size regression is cross-sectional, not a longitudinal growth experiment. These observations cannot establish the same sexual dimorphism, reproductive investment or mating strategy throughout Nanorana.

## Observed richness does not independently demonstrate conservation causality

Recommendations for habitat restoration and disease monitoring are not completed conservation outcomes.

## primaryStudyScopes / sectionEvidence / evidenceSummary

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/primaryStudyScopes/0/sectionEvidence/0/evidenceSummary/en -->
The 2023 study describes laojunshanensis from six July 2019 types at Mt. Laojun: five adult males and one adult female. It separately measured eight pleskei and eight ventripunctata, while using earlier descriptions for many other comparisons. Size-corrected morphology and four-locus phylogeny support the new lineage, but cited fold, vocal-sac and male nuptial-spine characters are not new population samples for every species. Marsh/pond occurrence and a July–August breeding-season inference belong to laojunshanensis alone; no tadpoles or direct spawning were documented. The linked pages keep these evidence units separate.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## primaryStudyScopes / sectionEvidence / evidenceSummary

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/primaryStudyScopes/0/sectionEvidence/1/evidenceSummary/en -->
The combined analysis used three mitochondrial markers and nuclear RAG-1 with older reference sequences, some missing loci. The authors recognize four major lineages and propose Minipaa for minica, alongside Nanorana, Paa and Chaparana allocations. Internal relationships among Minipaa, Paa and Nanorana were unresolved. Low 16S distances coexist with named species and morphological differences; a single three-percent threshold would not reproduce this framework. Marker distances, tree branches and cited older genomic work are not interchangeable with divergence dates, population gene-flow estimates or independent certification of a taxonomic rank.

## primaryStudyScopes / sectionEvidence / evidenceSummary

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/primaryStudyScopes/1/sectionEvidence/0/evidenceSummary/en -->
The quadranus study samples 266 frogs at thirteen mountain sites, then uses different subsets for testes, sperm and ovarian output. Testis size, sperm dimensions, sperm counts and ovarian ova estimates are distinct endpoints. Females are larger in the sampled medians, but age/body-size regression is cross-sectional, not a longitudinal growth experiment. These observations cannot establish the same sexual dimorphism, reproductive investment or mating strategy throughout Nanorana.

## primaryStudyScopes / sectionEvidence / evidenceSummary
