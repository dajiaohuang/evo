## overview

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
The Mountain Dwarf Galago is associated with eastern African montane forests, but small galagos are identified largely through calls, and differences among mountain blocks affect taxonomic assignment. This page pairs a low-elevation record from Mkungwe Forest Reserve with a climate-suitability model built from occurrence records; the projections are not observed population changes. The Mufindi galago remains cf. orinus in its source, and the Taita population remains unresolved.

## fieldStudy

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
A Mufindi forest survey covered four nocturnal survey sites with 76 hours 30 minutes of night walks and 15 trapping nights. The report provisionally identified one small galago as *Galagoides cf. orinus* and recorded 64 calls across five call types. Its appearance and size resembled records from some Eastern Arc forests, but the authors noted differences from the Uluguru type-locality and Taita recordings.

## local field record

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The Uluguru Mountains Biodiversity Conservation Project report compiles 2000 survey records of *Galagoides orinus* at 550 m in Mkungwe Forest Reserve, about 50 m from *G. zanzibaricus*. The report called this the lowest known elevation record at the time. This locality record is not an abundance estimate or a complete range.

## taxonomy_and_evidence

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
A review of the Udzungwa Mountains noted that the montane galago there may differ vocally from other named populations and that its taxonomic status needs further study. Research on Taita Hills dwarf galagos likewise leaves their identity unresolved; this page does not merge those records into *P. orinus*.

## habitat projections

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
Miller (2025) used Maxent and climate variables at roughly 5 km resolution, then projected suitability under three climate models. Relative to the modelled current baseline, suitable habitat area is projected to decline by about 42% under SSP245 and 56% under SSP585 in 2041–2060; by 2061–2080 the projected declines are about 56% and 69%. Udzungwa National Park and Ukwiva Forest Reserve retain the largest modelled suitable and optimal areas across the assessed futures. These are model outputs, not observed habitat loss or population forecasts.

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
The Mufindi galago remains identified only as cf. orinus, and call differences and identities among mountain blocks are unresolved. The climate model used a non-systematically assembled set of heterogeneous occurrences at roughly 5 km resolution; the study also notes possible overprediction in low-elevation reserves. Future scenarios are not observed habitat loss or population forecasts. This page does not claim a complete range, links among mountain populations or an abundance estimate, and has not received external expert review. This page is not externally expert-reviewed.
