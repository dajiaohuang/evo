# Aotus jorgehernandezi


## A comparison with another 50-chromosome animal

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
Menezes, Bonvicino and Seuánez (2010) karyotyped A. trivirgatus female TR1 from the Barcelos area of Amazonas, Brazil. Their discussion compared its 2n = 50 with the unknown-provenance A. jorgehernandezi female, but none of their 18 sequenced Aotus specimens was A. jorgehernandezi. This comparison adds no specimen, molecular identification or wild locality for that taxon and does not adjudicate later synonymy.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Trace the museum number and photograph

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
The 2007 naming account designates female ICN 14023 as the type, but its authors could not locate it in the Instituto de Ciencias Naturales collection at Universidad Nacional de Colombia. External description relies on a photograph in Torres et al. (1998), and the karyotype also comes from earlier work. Reprinting an image in 2007 does not add an individual. Quindío identifies where the captive animal was found, not a confirmed wild collection locality.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Chromosomal differences and inferred reproductive isolation

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
Defler and Bueno argued that structural differences from A. brumbacki would impede meiotic pairing and predicted sterile hybrids. This is the 2007 authors’ inference supporting separation; the naming account does not report a breeding experiment between these taxa. A shared 2n = 50 alone settles neither separation nor merger. Historical karyotype classifications and the later synonym opinion remain distinct.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Name availability differs from species acceptance

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The publisher abstract of Montilla et al. (2025) regards A. jorgehernandezi as nomenclaturally available while proposing it as a junior synonym of A. brumbacki. Availability does not entail acceptance as a separate species. This pass read the abstract and publication metadata, not the subscription article’s analyses, figures or full evidence. The pinned COL26.8 record remains, and the synonym proposal supplies no new wild provenance or ecological account.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## A captive type and a synonym proposal

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
Defler and Bueno named this taxon in 2007 from a distinctive chromosome form reported for a female night monkey found in captivity in Quindío, Colombia. The authors could not locate the holotype specimen, and its exact wild origin was uncertain; Parque de los Nevados was only a reported possibility. In 2025, Montilla and colleagues argued from a comparison of Colombian Aotus that the name should be treated as a junior synonym of A. brumbacki. COL26.8 and the local MDD v2.5 crosswalk retain separate records, so this page preserves the catalogued ID and labels the synonymy as a published taxonomic opinion.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Matching karyotypes still lack wild provenance

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
A 2023 annotated mammal list for Quindío likewise says that the taxon is known from its holotype alone: a captive animal of putative Los Nevados origin whose museum specimen could not be located. The authors also discuss a second captive Aotus at the Barquisimeto zoo in Venezuela with the same reported 2n = 50 karyotype but unknown provenance. A matching chromosome number is not a confirmed wild locality or proof that the animal belongs to the same species. The review calls for integrated karyotype, molecular, morphometric or morphological work to test taxonomic affinities and distribution.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Chromosomes and appearance of one type specimen

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
Defler and Bueno’s 2007 account described the Quindío female’s karyomorph as 2n = 50, with nine pairs of metacentric, three pairs of submetacentric, and 12 pairs of acrocentric chromosomes. They distinguished it from *A. brumbacki*, which also has 2n = 50 but was reported with a different chromosome composition. Their specimen description notes two white patches above the eyes separated by a broad black frontal stripe, paired pale subocular bands divided by thin black malar stripes, and dense white fur along the inner arms, chest, and belly. These are observations and diagnosis from one captive type specimen; they do not establish a wild population’s appearance, distribution, or variation.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Evidence limits

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
The sources support a taxonomic-history guide, not a current ecological species account: both karyotyped individuals discussed in the regional review were captive and of unknown wild provenance, and no population or habitat study tied to this catalogue concept is available here. The 2025 synonym proposal conflicts with the pinned separate COL26.8 ID and local MDD crosswalk; no resolution is imposed. This page remains source-insufficient for a species-level ecology guide and has not received external expert review.
