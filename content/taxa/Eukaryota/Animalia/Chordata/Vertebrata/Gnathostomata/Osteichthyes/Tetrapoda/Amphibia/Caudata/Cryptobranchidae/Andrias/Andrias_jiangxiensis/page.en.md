Its recorded classification places it along Caudata (order) → Cryptobranchidae (family) → Andrias (genus). ## A previously named U2 lineage serves as a reference
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10831114-lqgkx
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The paper includes deposited jiangxiensis cytb sequences and notes that U2, once associated with farms, had subsequently been described from wild populations. Its present Japanese specimens instead fell in B,D andU1; the study does not newly find a Japanese jiangxiensis or establish its complete native range. Table1 reference localities and the cited wild-population description are different evidence scopes, not all new captures in this paper.

## Reference-marker role in a Japanese ancient-DNA comparison
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12149308-lqgkx
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The 2025 Ehime study uses previously published Andrias jiangxiensis sequence accession entries MH051481 in its short cytb comparison. Table1 records CGS291 as farm-bred with Jiangxi in parentheses; this is not a verified wild locality. These table entries are reference material, not newly excavated remains of this species or a complete population sample. The fossil assignment to japonicus does not date the origin or expansion of Andrias jiangxiensis, and a93 bp marker does not certify modern nuclear ancestry, hybridization history or all species boundaries. Original sequence records and specimen identities have not been independently rechecked.
