## Twelve types and a separately measured male series
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc9422979-q37wk
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
Male holotype KUHE 62785 was collected on Mt. Yatsuzura, Uwajima, Ehime, on 24 April 2021 at 1000 m. Eleven paratypes comprised ten males and one female, with six further referred specimens including five juveniles. The holotype measured SVL 82.7 mm, tail 66.3 mm, head length 18.6 mm and head width 13.6 mm. The diagnosis gave male adult SVL 73.6–87.5 mm; the variation comparison used twelve males and a single female at 84.6 mm. Type, juvenile and measurement denominators remain distinct. The female tail-dimension list repeats BTAH and MTAH with differing values, so the ambiguous assignments are not silently repaired.

## Adult plain colours and spotted young
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc9422979-q37wk
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
Adults were described as uniformly dark reddish-brown dorsally, sometimes darker and less reddish, with a lighter underside and dark brown iris. Young animals had white dorsal dots. Preserved adult dorsums faded towards grey-brown; larval gold tail-fin dots faded to white. Age and preservation states are separate: white juvenile dots do not contradict the attributed plain-adult description, and preserved colours do not recover live appearance. The account does not demonstrate a colour-signalling, toxicity or camouflage function.

## Crescent sacs and separate clutch and sac samples
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc9422979-q37wk
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The description reported mean clutch size 23.5, range 16–36, for 21 observations. Fifteen egg sacs supplied mean length 176.8 mm and width 17.0 mm, with a distinct free-end whiptail extension averaging 35.2 mm. Eggs were cream-coloured at both poles, with stated oval diameter 4.9–5.9 mm, arranged in one or two rows per sac. The printed uncertainty terms are not labelled as standard deviations without confirming their definition. Clutch observations are not assumed to be fifteen sacs or twenty-one distinct mothers, and these measurements do not provide incubation time, fertilization success or survival.

## Stage-specific larvae and one overwintering encounter
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc9422979-q37wk
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
Using the Iwasawa–Yamashita staging system, three stage-63 larvae averaged SVL 21.7 mm; one stage-64 larva at metamorphosis onset in late July measured 20.6 mm. Larvae had developed external gills, a high tail fin and no finger or toe claws. Across three night surveys dated in 2019 and 2022, only one overwintered larva, total length 71 mm, was found in an open stream. These small samples do not establish universal metamorphic size, a fixed larval duration or absence of overwintering. Total length is not SVL, and this staging system is not silently substituted with an anuran Gosner stage.

## Southwestern Shikoku streams and an inferred late-April season
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc9422979-q37wk
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
The account knew the species from the Onigajo and Sasayama Mountains in Ehime and Kochi, southwestern Shikoku. Animals occurred around mountain streams with partly exposed bedrock. Eggs, females with eggs and males were observed in water on 29 April 2019; twelve adult males and egg sacs were beneath submerged stones on 24 April 2021. From these observations the authors presumed late-April breeding. Nonbreeding adults and juveniles were under nearby stones, rotten wood and debris. The dated inference is not proof that reproduction is confined to those dates, and lack of observed sympatric hynobiids does not prove their regional absence.

## The Nanyo lineage and a different unresolved hybrid boundary
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc9422979-q37wk
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
The study separated the Nanyo group as H. oni using nuclear SNP structure and morphology. The SNP analysis detected 716 loci and examined clustering at different K values; Tsurugi and Central groups formed a clinal transition and were treated together for morphology, while Nanyo was distinct at K = 3. The authors said Tsurugi–Central species status remained unresolved pending hybrid-zone research. Clustering supports their sampled lineage interpretation, not complete reproductive isolation at every locality, and an unresolved boundary elsewhere is not promoted to additional named species.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Threat concerns and preliminary explanations of small adult size
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc9422979-q37wk
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/text/en -->
The authors called for protection against habitat degradation and private collecting in the small known mountain range. Their discussion hypothesized that shallow or unstable streams and fewer overwintering larvae could contribute to smaller metamorphosing and adult size. Those were preliminary field interpretations and analogies with other species, not a causal growth experiment. They also requested reassessment of H. hirosei after the split; previously cited local categories for hirosei sensu lato are not automatically assigned to oni. The recommendation is dated 2022 and supplies no verified current Red List status.
<!-- /evo:text -->
