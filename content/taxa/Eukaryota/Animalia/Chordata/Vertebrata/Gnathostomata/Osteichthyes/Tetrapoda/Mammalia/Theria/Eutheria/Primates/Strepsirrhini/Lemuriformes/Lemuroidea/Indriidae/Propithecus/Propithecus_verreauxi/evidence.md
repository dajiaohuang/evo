## lifeHistory / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/lifeHistory/claims/0/text -->
At Kirindy Forest/CNFEREF, the study measured development of 12 wild infants from two birth cohorts during the first six postnatal months, but follow-up differed by cohort: the 2017 cohort was monitored through February, while the 2018 cohort's observations ended in December before a full six-month window. Infants whose mothers had higher prenatal fecal glucocorticoid metabolite concentrations showed faster growth and more independent foraging and play. These are observational associations from one local population; they do not establish a causal effect or a species-wide developmental rate.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## ecology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/ecology/claims/0/text -->
At Kirindy Mitea National Park, the authors reported M/LWS opsin genotype variation consistent with polymorphic trichromacy in one wild sifaka population. Individuals in groups containing trichromats had higher body-mass indices and more fruit-feeding time, with longer fruit-feeding bouts, than individuals in dichromat-only groups. These are study-specific associations; genotype is a proxy for color-vision phenotype, not a retinal anatomical measurement, and a proposed dry-season fruit-detection advantage is the authors' hypothesis.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## ecology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/ecology/claims/1/text -->
In the 2017–2018 Kirindy study, maternal fecal glucocorticoid metabolite concentrations were higher during periods of low rainfall and during late pregnancy than in the early-to-mid prenatal period. The authors used rainfall as a local proxy for food availability; this observational result is limited to the study population and does not define the species' climate niche or demonstrate causation.
