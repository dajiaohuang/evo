# Cheirogaleus crossleyi

## How the historical name links to Crossleyi B

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
The 2014 revision proposes Crossleyi B as Grandidier’s crossleyi. This group includes BMNH 1948.160 collected about 30 miles northeast of Lac Alaotra; the authors separately report locating Grandidier’s type at Harvard’s Museum of Comparative Zoology, from the Forest of Antsianaka near the lake. A museum specimen within a genetic group and the name-bearing type have different roles; their joint mention does not establish newly obtained DNA from the type.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Keep two type identities separate

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
The account lists crossleyi holotype MCZ 44952 as an adult female skin and skull, and the type of the historical name melanotis, BM 70.5.5.24, as an adult male skin and skull from Vohima. Distinct individuals bearing different names do not combine into one individual’s traits or a population series demonstrating sexual differences.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Dark masks and furred ears form a group-level combination

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The crossleyi-group description emphasizes broad, somewhat angular dark eye rings joining the black muzzle, ears furred inside and out, reddish-grey upperparts and more sharply bounded pale underparts, with a low, straight facial skeleton. The authors note characteristic yellow facial fur in a Zahamena individual; this does not establish identical colour in every animal or exclude every candidate lineage. Dental and cranial comparisons retain their source group context.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Compare plateau material with local ecological studies

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The revision reports central-plateau forest records from Zahamena in the north to Tsinjoarivo in the south, rather than a continuous range newly confirmed here. Compare type-locality reconstruction and local observations in [sibreei](../Cheirogaleus_sibreei/page.en.md), returning to the [genus evidence route](../page.en.md) for species groups and candidates. The 2026 Tsinjoarivo study below retains its own individuals, site and years; it is not the life history of these historical types.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Ecological evidence

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
A 2026 study tracked 24 collared dwarf lemurs in the Andasivodihazo forest fragment at Tsinjoarivo: 13 Crossley's and 11 Sibree's dwarf lemurs. Observations before and after hibernation in 2011–2014 found smaller home ranges, shorter nightly movements, and more use of forest edges and degraded habitat by Crossley's dwarf lemurs at this site; researchers also recorded a more diverse diet.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Different stages in the mass fields

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
Historical summary values are adult body mass 420.11 g; mass at birth or hatching 18.06 g. Adult mass can combine males, females and adults of unspecified sex; birth/hatching mass describes a different life stage. These are not a longitudinal series of the same individuals or a species-wide range of individual masses.

## Reproduction and developmental timing

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->

The litter/clutch field combines offspring or egg counts, and a fractional summary does not mean a single event produced a fractional individual. Timing fields refer to different events and cannot be exchanged for one uniform reproductive cycle.
