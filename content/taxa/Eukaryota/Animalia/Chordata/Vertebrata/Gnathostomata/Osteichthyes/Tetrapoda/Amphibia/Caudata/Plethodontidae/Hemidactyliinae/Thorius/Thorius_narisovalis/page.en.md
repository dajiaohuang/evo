## Sexed adult sizes and a tooth-count boundary conflict
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5119241-56kt7
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/text/en -->
Female holotype FMNH 100089, formerly EHT-HMS 17859, was collected on Cerro San Felipe in August 1938. The 2016 variation sample comprised ten males with mean SL 25.2 mm, range 22.2–28.4 mm, and ten females with mean 27.8 mm, range 26.3–29.9 mm. SL means snout to posterior vent angle. Mean SL/tail ratios were 0.85 in five males and 0.82 in eight females, smaller tail-sample denominators. Maxillary teeth were absent. The diagnosis said fewer than seven vomerine teeth, while variation ranges reached seven in both sexes; the conflict is retained rather than treating the diagnostic boundary as universal.

## Variable stripes and an often unspotted belly
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5119241-56kt7
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/text/en -->
July 1976 live notes described brick-red or tan-brown dorsal stripes, occasional melanistic animals and dark venters lacking conspicuous white flecks in most specimens. Separate October 1997 notes described an entirely dark gravid female with black iris and no white spots. Preserved specimens could have white spots on the throat and lower flanks while the belly remained unspotted. The regions and observation dates matter: an unspotted belly does not mean every body surface lacked spots, and a single gravid animal does not establish egg number or a breeding season.

## Variable skeletal formulae and an inconsistent vertebral summary
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/10/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5119241-56kt7
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/10/text/en -->
The osteological account described variable skull-bone contacts and presence or absence of premaxillary teeth, while maxillary teeth were absent in all examined specimens. The modal foot phalangeal formula was 1–2–3–3–1 at 71%, with several alternatives, so it is not invariant. The caudal-vertebra summary printed a female mean of 36.8 with range 31–36 in five females, an internally inconsistent combination because the mean exceeds the stated maximum. It is preserved as an unresolved source error and not used as a definitive female vertebral count or silently repaired.

## Oaxaca forest records and dated low encounter counts
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/11/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5119241-56kt7
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/11/text/en -->
The 2016 account reported central and western Oaxaca mountain ranges including Cerro San Felipe, Cuajimoloyas, west of Zaachila and southeast of Tlaxiaco. The distribution paragraph gave 2780–3185 m, while the material list also contained Zaachila specimens at 2590 m; these differing entries remain separate. Animals were often encountered among exposed cover in disturbed pine-oak forest. Cerro San Felipe counts were one in October 1997 and, on five later visits from 2008–2015, one, sixteen, one, one and two. These dated observations document sparse encounters without standardized density or a present-day population census; they do not prove dependence on disturbance.

The paper cited Critically Endangered status in 2016, associating it with declines exceeding 80% over 30–40 years, severely fragmented distribution, an extent of occurrence thought probably below 100 km² and continuing forest deterioration. ## One sequenced individual and a separate DNA-content estimate
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/13/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc11562844-56kt7
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/13/text/en -->
The study extracted liver DNA from one Thorius narisovalis specimen as part of a sixteen-species, three-genus comparison, targeting ten gigabases of short-read data per species. Repeat analysis randomly sampled merged reads at roughly 0.1-fold coverage. Table 1 gives a genome C-value estimate of 22.9 picograms, based on blood-smear densitometry and species means from earlier genome-size work. Picograms of DNA are not gigabases, body mass or a complete genome assembly; the sequenced individual and the species-mean DNA-content estimate have different evidence roles. The sample does not determine variation among sexes, populations or ages.

## Detected repeat abundance is a method-dependent lower estimate
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/14/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc11562844-56kt7
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/14/text/en -->
Table 1 reports 73.3% detectable repeat abundance for Thorius narisovalis, including uncategorized repeats. Table 2 reports LTRs at 15.95%, and Table 3 separates ERV at 10.706% from Ty3/Gypsy at 3.811%. These are percentages of sampled bases identified by the study's repeat library, not percentages of animals, newly inserted elements or measured gene expression. Mutation can degrade repeats beyond recognition; the authors regard short-read masking as likely underestimating true repetitive content. Rounded table categories need not sum exactly, and uncategorized repeats should not be silently assigned to a known TE superfamily.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Repeat divergence suggests relative expansion histories
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/15/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc11562844-56kt7
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/15/text/en -->
The study groups lunaris, narisovalis and spilogaster as the three smaller-genome Thorius. Their ERV peaks were below Kimura distance four, with low-distance reads in several superfamilies interpreted as recent or ongoing expansion. Gypsy activity was comparatively weak. These repeat-distance profiles do not measure an insertion rate, date an evolutionary event in years or demonstrate the deletion/silencing mechanism; one sequenced specimen per species limits population generalization.
