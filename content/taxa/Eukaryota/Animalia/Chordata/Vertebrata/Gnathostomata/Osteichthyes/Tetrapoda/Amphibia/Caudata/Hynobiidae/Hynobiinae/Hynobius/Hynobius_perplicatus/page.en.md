## A cryptic large-bodied comparison and repeated abbreviation
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7830202-q37xm
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The paper knew perplicatus from limited parts of Hapcheon, Sancheong and Uiryeong, with all known populations reported sympatric with notialis. It called the species the largest Korean Hynobius in its comparison, while acknowledging overlap and no discrete known morphological character. Its threshold sentence repeated HL with different cutoffs, 15.81 and 4.39 mm; the second is not silently relabelled as another dimension. The glaciation explanation for its range appeared as an unresolved hypothesis in the etymology, not established biogeographic history.

## Natural history pooled across three species
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7830202-q37xm
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The authors explicitly pooled natural history for H. notialis, H. geojeensis and H. perplicatus because separate breeding ecology was poorly known. They described still-water reproduction in roadside or field ditches and fallow rice paddies, earliest breeding in late January, a late-February to early-March peak and some late-March spawning. Females generally deposited two curved sacs, sometimes looped or nearly straight, and attachment varied with the site. Larvae developed paired balancers and metamorphosed before the following winter; larval overwintering was not known. These pooled observations are not independently measured schedules, clutch sizes or developmental durations for each species.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Two connected sites and a threshold-based conservation proposal
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7830202-q37xm
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The authors estimated perplicatus extent of occurrence below 1000 km² because landscape-use and presence data were unavailable. They described two connected sites and possible occurrence in linked valleys, proposing Endangered under B1ab(i,ii,iii,iv,v) in 2021. Possible valleys are not confirmed additional populations; the range threshold is not measured area of occupancy. Unknown population sizes precluded precise demographic analysis, and the proposal is not a verified current assessment.
