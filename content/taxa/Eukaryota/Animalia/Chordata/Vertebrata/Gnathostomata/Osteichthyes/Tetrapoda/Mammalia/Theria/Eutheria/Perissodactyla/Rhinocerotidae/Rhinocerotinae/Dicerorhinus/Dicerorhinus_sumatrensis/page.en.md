# Dicerorhinus sumatrensis

## Recognizing the Sumatran rhinoceros

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
Small among living rhinoceroses, the Sumatran rhinoceros has two horns and conspicuous hair. Calves have a denser coat, so one coat description does not cover every age. The existing forest account provides morphological and habitat context. The following sections turn to genomic research. Morphology and genetic specimens answer different questions: the account is not a population census, and the genomes do not directly establish present occupancy at every locality.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Comparing 21 genomes

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
The 2021 study compared 21 historical and modern Sumatran rhinoceros genomes. Its population-structure analysis included three additional genomes with lower coverage. Specimens were attributed to populations on Sumatra, the Malay Peninsula and Borneo. Historical material allows comparison of specimens collected at different times. The geographic labels describe analyzed material and should not be read as a map of current occupancy.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Geographic groups in the sample

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
A pairwise genetic-distance tree and principal-component analysis recovered three main groups associated with Sumatra, the Malay Peninsula and Borneo. The tree and clustering analyses also distinguished northeastern and southwestern lineages within Sumatra. Malay Peninsula specimens grouped together in this analysis, contrasting with an earlier mitogenome result. Methods and sampling scope remain part of the finding; different genetic results should not be collapsed into one certain chain of ancestors.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## What the study can answer

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The result supports geographic genetic structure among sampled individuals. It does not directly establish species-level ancestry or formal conservation management units. Sample regions, genetic groups and current wild population numbers are not interchangeable. Return to the Dicerorhinus genus guide to place the study within the pinned accepted-species roster. Complete feeding, reproduction, range-wide conditions and current conservation assessments still require separate evidence.
<!-- /evo:text -->
