## Accepted name and placement
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
COL26.8 accepts Pristimantis moa Oliveira, Alves da Silva, Guimarães, Penhacek, Martínez, Rodrigues, Santana & Hernández-Ruz, 2020 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Strabomantidae (family) → Pristimantis (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## Cited forest-floor and low-vegetation activity
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12654886-scfw4
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The 2025 account described a small nocturnal rainfrog in forested Cerrado environments, found on the ground and low vegetation, drawing on earlier sources. It noted that morphology and advertisement calls were almost indistinguishable from the cited sister species P. giorgii. This is background to the molecular assignment, not newly quantified activity or evidence that visual similarity alone resolves every local specimen.

## One sequenced voucher within a larger collected series
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12654886-scfw4
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
MPEG 42392, collected in Serra das Andorinhas at 125 m on 11 November 2021, was assigned using a 16S fragment. Its uncorrected distance was 0.3% from two cited P. moa comparisons but 3.0–3.2% from two P. giorgii comparisons. The authors called this the first Pará record and about 108 km north-northwest of the nearest prior locality at Araguaína. Although they collected a larger conspicillatus-group series, this paragraph’s explicit molecular evidence concerns one voucher; it is not promoted to molecular verification of the whole series or a recent dispersal measurement.

## Yellow thigh markings and variable belly texture
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/10/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7080247-scfw4
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/10/text/en -->
The moa account described pronounced dark-yellow markings on a brown posterior thigh, basal toe webbing and fringes, large conical subarticular tubercles and a usually centrally smooth but laterally granular belly. Variation included an almost entirely granular belly in ZUFMS-AMP 8575 and a smooth juvenile belly in ZUFMS-AMP 8552, as well as present and absent dorsolateral folds. These are specimen observations, not a universal developmental progression. Female and male averages were reported separately but do not by themselves establish nonoverlapping sex ranges, a complete population size distribution or invariant diagnostic states.

## Tocantins and Maranhão locality records with seasonal observations
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/11/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7080247-scfw4
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/11/text/en -->
The 2020 study listed moa from Palmas, Palmeirante and Araguaína in Tocantins, and Carolina, Balsas and Riachão in Maranhão. Frogs occurred in conserved forest and some fragments surrounded by pasture. Males were described calling in vegetation during the reproductive period, with frogs predominantly on the ground during the dry period. The short account gives no complete annual breeding calendar, quantified height or eggs and larval observations. Named municipality records do not establish continuous distribution, total abundance or harmlessness of habitat fragmentation.

## Three to five notes overlap the other species’ counts
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/12/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7080247-scfw4
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/12/text/en -->
Moa advertisement calls were described with three to five notes. The diagnosis explicitly gives note duration averaging 45 ms, with observed bounds 23.8–64.6 ms; these are note lengths rather than full-call durations. The methods recorded three Palmas and five Balsas males and listed archive numbers MAP-V 320–325, so archive count is not automatically the number of sampled animals. The note-number range overlaps both giorgii and pictus. Identification requires the broader evidence combination, and the record does not measure mate preference or demonstrate an invariant call across the natural range.

## A mitochondrial sister relationship with a limited marker scope
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/13/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7080247-scfw4
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/13/text/en -->
The authors recovered the two Tocantins-basin new lineages, moa and giorgii, as sisters in their concatenated mitochondrial analysis, with reported differences of 2.3% in 16S and 10.5% in COI. These are separate marker comparisons and should not be averaged into a general genomic divergence. Their species argument also used morphology and calls. The result is not a dated split, a full nuclear species tree, a universal species threshold or a direct demonstration that the two species cannot hybridize. Proposed historical contact and acoustic differentiation were interpretations rather than tested mating mechanisms.
