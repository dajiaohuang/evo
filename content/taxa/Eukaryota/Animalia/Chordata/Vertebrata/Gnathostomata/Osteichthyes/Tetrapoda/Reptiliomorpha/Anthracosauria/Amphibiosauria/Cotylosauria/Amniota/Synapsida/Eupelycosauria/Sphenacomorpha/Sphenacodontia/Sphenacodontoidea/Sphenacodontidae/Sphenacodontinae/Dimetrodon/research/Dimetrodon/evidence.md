# Dimetrodon

## claims / statement

<!-- evo:text /records/claims/0/statement -->
A sampled sphenacodontoid analysis places the included Dimetrodon species within Sphenacodontidae and recovers repeated dental-character changes; this is a character-matrix result, not evidence that Dimetrodon was a direct mammalian ancestor.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## claims / confidenceRationale

<!-- evo:text /records/claims/0/confidenceRationale -->
The primary study directly scores Dimetrodon material and optimizes dental characters on a sampled tree. The wording preserves matrix dependence and excludes direct ancestry.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## claims / statement

<!-- evo:text /records/claims/1/statement -->
This dossier is limited to the Dimetrodon and cf. Dimetrodon teeth and skull material explicitly sampled by Brink and Reisz; the study's sphenacodontid placement is a matrix result and not direct ancestry.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## claims / confidenceRationale

<!-- evo:text /records/claims/1/confidenceRationale -->
Named museum specimens and the sampled analysis identify the evidence boundary, but species-level attribution of isolated material and higher placement remain analytical.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## claims / statement

<!-- evo:text /records/claims/2/statement -->
The profile maps only the Early Permian Texas and New Mexico sample, including Archer City, Mount Barry, Briar Creek, Rattlesnake Canyon and the Craddock bonebed; it does not assert a complete geographic distribution.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## claims / confidenceRationale

<!-- evo:text /records/claims/2/confidenceRationale -->
The paper lists the sampled localities and associated Dimetrodon material, while its sampling design is not a global occurrence census.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## claims / statement

<!-- evo:text /records/claims/3/statement -->
The authors interpret sphenacodontids, including the sampled Dimetrodon material, as fully terrestrial apex predators and relate dental-character change to feeding style and trophic interactions; direct prey use and locomotor performance were not observed for each specimen.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## claims / confidenceRationale

<!-- evo:text /records/claims/3/confidenceRationale -->
Dental anatomy and the authors' ecological interpretation support a bounded predator inference, but they do not directly record behaviour, prey or performance.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## claims / statement

<!-- evo:text /records/claims/4/statement -->
MCZ 2779, MCZ 2028, ROM 64021 and UMMP 9722 document the sampled Dimetrodon snout and teeth, including enlarged anterior teeth, carinae and variation in lingual cusp morphology; these observations do not diagnose every species or individual of the genus.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## claims / confidenceRationale

<!-- evo:text /records/claims/4/confidenceRationale -->
The paper figures and describes the named specimens directly; scope is retained at the observed material rather than extrapolated to every Dimetrodon.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## claims / statement

<!-- evo:text /records/claims/5/statement -->
Dimetrodon is displayed at 290.1–273 Ma only as the Early Permian species sample used for dental and phylogenetic analysis, not a complete global genus range. The interval is a source-bounded sample window, not a global FAD, LAD, divergence date, continuous occupancy claim or direct-ancestor assertion.
