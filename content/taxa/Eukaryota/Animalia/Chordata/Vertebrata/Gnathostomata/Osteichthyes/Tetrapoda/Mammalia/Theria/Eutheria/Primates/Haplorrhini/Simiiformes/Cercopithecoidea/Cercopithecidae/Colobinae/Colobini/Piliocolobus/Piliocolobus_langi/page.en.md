## survey

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
A 2019 rapid assessment documented red colobus at six locations in the Kisangani region: living animals were observed at four locations during two reconnaissance walks, and three hunter-killed animals came from two other locations. Five locations showed a sharply marked coat pattern the authors matched to the type description of P. langi; two animals from the right bank of the Lindi River differed and were assigned to the variable form P. ellioti. The report leaves western range limits and the identity of some interfluve populations unresolved.

## assessment_scope

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
The report assessed the broader “Kisangani red colobus” region, estimated at about 69,000 km² and described as including both the authors’ *P. langi* and variable forms assigned to *P. ellioti*; its totals are not a range or abundance estimate for *P. langi* alone. In 2019, two field teams travelled about 1,600 km on foot, bicycle and abandoned roads and conducted 125 interviews in 92 localities. The report separated interview categories such as confirmed presence, probable presence and reported disappearance, and supplemented them with one-to-five-day forest reconnaissance trips. Interview-based polygons are approximate occurrence areas, not continuous occupancy maps.

## range_boundary

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
Under the report’s taxonomy, the sharply bicolored form matching the *P. langi* type description extended from the Maiko River in the south, through the Tshopo basin, to the Lindi River in the north. East of the Lindi, between the Lindi and Aruwimi rivers, the authors assigned variable red colobus to *P. ellioti*. They called for verification of phenotype and taxonomic identity in western populations around Yambuya. The entire “Kisangani red colobus” region therefore cannot be equated with the distribution of pinned *P. langi*.

## threats

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The report recorded illegal hunting at surveyed sites and reviewed mass-killing episodes in parts of the region during the preceding two decades. It described agricultural conversion and logging around Kisangani as sources of habitat loss and degradation, and artisanal mining in the east as a source of forest access and bushmeat demand. These are the report’s 2019 regional findings and historical context, not a current abundance estimate, trend assessment or conservation category for this species.

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
This guide centers on one 2019 regional assessment and its report-era phenotype assignments; the regional totals combine *P. langi* with other red-colobus forms and are not a species census. Several neighboring populations remain taxonomically unresolved. The page has not received external expert review.
