## ecology

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
In Erickson et al.'s 2012 comparison, 1 sexually mature philippine crocodile were tested, with a mean sample body mass of about 69 kg. At the prominent molariform tooth near the back of the upper jaw, the study reported a taxon-representative bite force of 2,736 N; only one sexually mature individual was tested, so no within-species range can be estimated. Researchers used the highest of 3–5 bites per animal. Across species, body mass was the main predictor of bite force, while rostral proportion explained little additional variation. This is a standardized measurement from a limited institutional sample, not a field-performance constant or population range.
