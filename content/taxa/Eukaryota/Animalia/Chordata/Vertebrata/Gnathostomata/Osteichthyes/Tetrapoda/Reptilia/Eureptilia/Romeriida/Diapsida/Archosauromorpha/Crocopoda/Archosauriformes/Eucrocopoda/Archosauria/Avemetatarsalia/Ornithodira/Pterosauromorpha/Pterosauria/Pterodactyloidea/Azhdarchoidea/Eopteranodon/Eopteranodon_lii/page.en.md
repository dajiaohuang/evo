# Eopteranodon lii


## Read Eopteranodon through reassessment of its holotype

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
The 2023 revision compares Yixian Formation Eopteranodon lii with Sinopterus material from the younger, overlying Jiufotang Formation, retaining distinction from S. dongi and Huaxiadraco corollatus. This is a named reassessment of holotype BPV 078, without treating the older directory parent chain as the only phylogeny or establishing direct ancestry from stratigraphic position.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## The authors revise the original identification of skull and jaw remains

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
The authors report that skull and mandibular remains in BPV 078 were originally identified the wrong way round. Reassessment recognizes a downturned rostrum and premaxillary crest, supporting a tapejarid interpretation. Textual captions distinguish the main part, counterpart and cranial details. Images and specimens were not independently inspected here, so the reinterpretation remains explicitly attributed to the authors.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Similar orbits and cervicals coexist with different proportions

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The account describes similarities to S. dongi in a subquadrangular orbit, roughly 160° quadrate reclination and the fourth cervical being longest. Differences include a stouter nasoantorbital fenestra with length/height near 2.5, pteroid/ulna length near 0.56, wing phalanx four/one near 0.45 and metatarsal one shorter than two. This is a comparative character combination from named material, rather than a single-ratio identification rule for every fragment or measured flight performance.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## The revision supports close relationship under its sampling

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
Using an updated matrix, equal character weights and TNT searches, the 2023 study obtained three minimum-length trees. Its strict consensus groups E. lii with S. dongi, with H. corollatus sister to that pair. The authors also distinguish similarity-based clustering from phylogenetic evidence. The matrix was not rerun here, and this result does not overwrite earlier interpretations such as chaoyangopterid affinity or the fixed directory.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Earlier occurrence does not establish direct ancestry

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
Morphological proximity and stratigraphic succession lead the authors to propose that E. lii and S. dongi could be chronospecies along an anagenetic continuum. This is not an established ancestor–descendant chain here; complete sampling, stratigraphy, variation and later studies remain necessary. Relative formation positions neither narrow database age brackets to exact specimen dates nor establish simultaneous coexistence.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Evidence limits

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
Existing sections guide PBDB occurrences dated 2026-10-02; added prose reads the 2023 revision, while a complete monograph remains unfinished. Entries belong to the fixed 276-item pterosaur working roster, retain the current same name and a complete parent chain to the recorded Pterosauria storage anchor, and have usable species-level occurrences without uncertain-identification qualifiers. Database accepted names can connect older combinations; that database correspondence does not decide specimen identifications or all taxonomic disputes and is not merged with same-name COL concepts. Collection sites, stratigraphic intervals, depositional settings and collection-level preservation modes have distinct meanings. They do not directly establish complete range, origin or extinction time, fixed species ecology, biological abundance or the condition of every specimen. Original descriptions, revisions, morphology, diet and behavior remain incompletely assembled; The Eopteranodon account and textual captions and both phylogenetic-analysis sections were read here. Original 2005/2006 full papers, images, specimens and supplementary matrices were not independently inspected, and the entire 2023 paper has not been read.
