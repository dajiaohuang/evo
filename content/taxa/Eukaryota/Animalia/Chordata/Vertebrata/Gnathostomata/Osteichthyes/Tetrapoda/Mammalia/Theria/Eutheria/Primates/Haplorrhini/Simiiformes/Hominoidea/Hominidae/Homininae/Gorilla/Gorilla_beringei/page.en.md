## lifeHistory

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
A comparative study reported a mean age at first parturition of 10.1 ± 1.7 years for mountain gorillas in Virunga (56 females) and 10.5 ± 1.3 years in Bwindi (10 females). The difference was not statistically significant (p = 0.55); this does not demonstrate equivalence and does not cover Grauer's gorillas or every Eastern gorilla population.

## evolution

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
In a mitochondrial-genome comparison focused on Grauer's gorillas, historical samples had 20 haplotypes and modern samples had 11; the authors reported lower haplotype and nucleotide diversity in the modern sample. The comparison included 68 historical and 29 modern individuals. It uses mitochondrial markers, not nuclear genomes; the authors attributed the change to peripheral-population loss, an inference bounded to their samples and model.

## distribution

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
Mountain-gorilla life-history research covered the Virunga volcanoes and Bwindi in Uganda. The Grauer's gorilla genetic study compared historical museum material with modern samples from places including Nkuba and Kahuzi-Biega in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. These are research sites and sampling histories, not a complete survey of the Eastern gorilla's current range.
