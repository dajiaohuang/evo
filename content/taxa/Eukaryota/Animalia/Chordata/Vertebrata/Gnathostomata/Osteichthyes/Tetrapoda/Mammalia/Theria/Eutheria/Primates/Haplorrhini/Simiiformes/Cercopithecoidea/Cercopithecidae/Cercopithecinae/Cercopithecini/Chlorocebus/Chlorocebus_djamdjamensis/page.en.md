## feeding

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
Mekonnen and colleagues compared four groups in southern Ethiopia: two in continuous Odobullu Forest and one each in the Patchy and Hilltop fragments. Bamboo dominated both continuous-forest diets (about 81% of feeding records), while it made up 30.2% for the Patchy group and 1.6% for the Hilltop group. The fragment groups used a wider mix of foods, including graminoids, fruit and cultivated foods.

## evidence-boundary

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
The comparison involved four groups at three sites, not a range-wide sample. Differences describe those groups and habitats; the paper notes that longer-term work is needed before drawing conclusions about the persistence of fragment populations.

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
A four-group feeding comparison; not externally expert-reviewed. Site-specific dietary proportions do not represent all populations or establish long-term persistence in fragments.
