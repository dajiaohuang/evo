# Notharctus (AMNH 143612 and AMNH 143640 foot)

## atlas-profile / overview

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/overview -->

## A foot preserved across two museum numbers
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/0/text/en -->
The study examines a semi-articulated foot of Notharctus from Wyoming’s Bridger Formation, catalogued as AMNH 143612 and AMNH 143640. The bones preserve the positions of several toe elements well enough for the authors to compare individual rays. Treating them as a named, bounded foot sample matters: the paper is not describing every Notharctus foot or a complete skeleton.

## Which toe carried the grooming claw?
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/1/text/en -->
Comparisons of the proximal and middle phalanges led the authors to identify a digit-two grooming claw rather than the digit-one arrangement often assumed for adapiforms. The inference rests on ray-specific bone shape and joint relationships. It is a functional interpretation of preserved phalanges, not an observation of Notharctus grooming itself.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## A functional clue, not a behavior record
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/2/text/en -->
The proposed claw identity helps compare adapiform feet with other primates and evaluate competing evolutionary explanations. But the anatomy does not reveal how often the animal groomed, which social contexts it used, or whether the same condition characterized every individual. A single foot can test a functional hypothesis without settling broader behavior or ancestry.

## atlas-profile / readerLimitations

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerLimitations/en -->
This is a guide to one semi-articulated foot sample and a proposed grooming-claw interpretation. It does not establish observed behavior, the frequency of grooming, population variation or a direct role in anthropoid ancestry.
