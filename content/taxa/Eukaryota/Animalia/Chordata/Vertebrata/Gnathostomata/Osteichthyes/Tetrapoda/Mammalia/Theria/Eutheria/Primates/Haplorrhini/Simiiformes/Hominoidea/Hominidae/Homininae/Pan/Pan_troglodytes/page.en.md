## ecology

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
During follows of a wild western-chimpanzee community at Bossou, Guinea, in 2012–2013, mature forest was the most selected habitat overall. In noncultivated habitat, feeding records occurred more often than expected beyond 200 m from fields and less often than expected in the 0–100 m and 101–200 m bands. The authors suggested that field-associated risk may influence feeding locations. These results describe one site and community.

## lifeHistory

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
Among 36 known-age wild females at Gombe National Park, mean age at sexual maturity was 11.5 years (range 8.5–13.9), and mean age at first birth was 14.9 years (11.1–22.1). First-birth estimates came from a smaller, censored subset and may be biased low because non-dispersing females were overrepresented. These are Gombe population estimates, not fixed species-wide constants.

## development

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
A cross-sectional video sample from three neighboring western-chimpanzee communities at Taï National Park covered 70 individuals, 135 videos, and 1,460 stick-use events. For stick insertion, the model predicted that the multi-digit grip became predominant at about 5.2 years and had a 98% predicted probability of use by age 15. Observations above age 45 came from only three females in one group. This age pattern is specific to the sampled stick task and does not establish a universal developmental trajectory or cognitive cause.

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
This guide brings together three studies from different sites and tasks; it is not a current range-wide synthesis. Bossou and Gombe each represent one population, and tool-use evidence is limited to stick tasks at Taï. This guide is not externally expert-reviewed.
