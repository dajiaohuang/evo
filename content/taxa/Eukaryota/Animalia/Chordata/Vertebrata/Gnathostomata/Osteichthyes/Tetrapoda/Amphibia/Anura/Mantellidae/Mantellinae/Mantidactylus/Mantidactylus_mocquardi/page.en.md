## Dark colour and lip spotting accompany variable limb reach
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10563080-736mk
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/text/en -->
Adult males are reported at 36–48 mm and females at 44–65 mm snout–vent length. The body is dark brown or black, black in preservative, with a few white lateral spots; the paler upper lip has dark brown spotting, denser posteriorly. The snout projects more than 1.75 mm beyond the lower jaw in lateral view. Tibiotarsal reach is between eye and nostril but sometimes beyond the nostril. Preserved colour and variable limb position should remain distinguished from fixed live-colour rules or a single decisive identification character.

## Daytime and degraded-forest records are observational associations
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/10/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10563080-736mk
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/10/text/en -->
The semi-aquatic rainforest species was also recorded in open or degraded forest, especially in northern Madagascar. Encounters along banks and streams with different water speeds span 09:00–23:00, and the authors describe it as apparently more diurnal than nocturnal and more often associated with rocky areas than trees. Tree records include leaves and branches. These observations do not quantify disturbance tolerance, survival in cleared land or experimental substrate preferences. Occurrence in degraded forest is not evidence that forest loss has no population cost.

## The revised account covers central-eastern and northern rainforest
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/11/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10563080-736mk
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/11/text/en -->
The distribution account places mocquardi in low-, middle- and high-elevation rainforest of central-eastern and northern Madagascar at 100–1,550 m. These are summarized occurrence bounds under the revised diagnosis, not a thermal tolerance range or continuous occupancy. Historical cf. or aff. mocquardi vouchers assigned elsewhere in the revision to catalai or tavaratra are not silently pooled with this species.
