## Southern Sichuan Basin locality account
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10083232-v5jy8
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
Male holotype CIB CS20190518031 came from Chishui Nature Reserve, Guizhou, at about 460 m. The monograph examined Chishui females and Sichuan material from Emei at about 1600 m, Wawu at about 1560 m and Laojun at about 1500 m. Its broader interpreted distribution covered hilly localities along the southern Sichuan Basin edge in southeastern Sichuan, southwestern Chongqing, northwestern Guizhou and northeastern Yunnan at 270–1600 m. This wider account incorporates reassigned historical records rather than only newly examined vouchers.

## Twenty-three examined adults
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10083232-v5jy8
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The revised diagnosis reported fifteen adult males at 33.8–41.2 mm snout–vent length and eight females at 43.8–54.1 mm. It described no vomerine ridges or teeth, rough dorsal skin with numerous granules and tubercles, and rudimentary basal toe webbing with narrow lateral fringes. Breeding males had tiny, dense black nuptial spines on finger I and II pads and a subgular vocal sac. These observations concern the examined multi-locality sample, not universal adult size limits or independently established species boundaries.

## Revision of the long-used Emeishan identification
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10083232-v5jy8
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The remarks traced the Emeishan population’s historical treatment as B. minor and cited later evidence distinguishing it from topotypic B. minor at Dujiangyan. They explained that the Chishui description compared Chishui and Emeishan morphology despite earlier work treating those populations as conspecific, making that comparison problematic. The monograph revised the diagnosis and reassigned historical B. minor records from Emeishan, Gulin, Hejiang and Weixin to B. chishuiensis on biogeographic grounds. This attribution is preserved; the historical records are not relabelled as newly molecularly verified vouchers.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## A character cited in the gutu comparison
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12466624-v5jy8
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The 2025 gutu description lists Boulenophrys chishuiensis as without a vomerine ridge. This is an attributed comparator statement within that paper’s taxonomic scope, not a new measurement series for this species. The gutu adult sample spans 34.4–44.7 mm in seven males and 36.2–52.8 mm in eight females; those numbers belong to gutu, not to this comparator. The selected character does not establish population-wide frequency, complete diagnosis, diet or developmental biology.
