## Long tails, a defined standard length and different size scopes
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5119241-56ksw
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
Female holotype MCZ A-137819 was collected south of Sola de Vega on 18 November 1974 at 2200 m. Snout-to-posterior-vent SL was 27.7 mm, anterior-vent length 25.1 mm and tail length 39.2 mm. The variation sample of ten males had mean SL 25.0 mm, range 23.6–28.3 mm; ten females averaged 25.5 mm, range 24.4–27.7 mm. Mean SL/tail ratios were 0.69 and 0.76, not tail/SL. The comparison cited a smallest adult male at 22.9 mm despite the diagnosis saying male SL exceeded 23.5 mm; live notes also recorded a gravid female at 22 mm. These different scopes and printed values are retained without presenting the diagnostic threshold as universal.

## Colour notes from a gravid female and other specimens
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5119241-56ksw
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
October 1997 field notes for gravid female IBH 13998, with a partly regenerated tail, described a tan-reddish stripe with coppery-brassy highlights, whitish lateral markings and a reddish-brown iris. Separate July 1976 notes described dark venters with whitish flecks. Preserved animals had a paler dorsal band sometimes interrupted by herringbone marks or a thin median dark line. These notes describe their named specimens and dates; a regenerated tail is not an intact-tail measurement and the observed pregnancy does not supply egg number, laying site or breeding season.

## Two areas and effort-bound changes in encounters
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5119241-56ksw
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The 2016 account knew areas near Sola de Vega and San Vicente Lachixio, about 40 km apart. The first area was summarized as roughly 2100–2200 m, with later type-locality readings as low as 2085 m; separate Lachixio vouchers included 2615–2730 m. At the type locality, approximately 300 animals were seen in road-bank cracks in November 1974, 75 were found in about two hours in July 1976, and one was found after over two hours in October 1997. Later visits through June 2014 found none. Substrates and search conditions differed, so the counts are not standardized densities, and nondetection does not prove extinction. The stated last live field sighting was March 1998 at Lachixio, a historical endpoint rather than a present-day claim.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## A proposed threat category with unknown total range
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5119241-56ksw
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The 2016 authors recommended Critically Endangered, citing drastic, unexplained declines likely exceeding 80% over 30–40 years and continuing deterioration of montane forest. They explicitly said the full extent of occurrence was unknown despite two known areas around 40 km apart, and suggested occupied area might be very small. Neither the separation distance nor surveyed sites yield a measured occupied polygon. The recommendation and uncertainty remain dated; the reported habitat changes do not alone establish the causal mechanism of population decline.
