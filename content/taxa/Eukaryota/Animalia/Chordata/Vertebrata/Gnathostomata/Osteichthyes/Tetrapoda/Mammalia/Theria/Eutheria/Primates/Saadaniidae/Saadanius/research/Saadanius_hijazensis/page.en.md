## A partial cranium from western Arabia
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/0/text/en -->
Saadanius is known from a partial adult cranium, holotype SGS-UM 2009-002, found in the middle Shumaysi Formation of western Saudi Arabia. The preserved face, palate, teeth and ear-region anatomy make this specimen a rare window into an Oligocene catarrhine-grade primate. Its discovery extended the known record of this part of the primate tree into a region with few comparable fossils.

## A character combination, not a miniature modern ape
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/1/text/en -->
The authors compared the cranium with living and fossil catarrhines using a character matrix. Saadanius combines features associated with catarrhines, including a tubular ectotympanic, without the diagnostic specializations used to identify crown apes or Old World monkeys. In that analysis it falls on the stem side of the catarrhine split, a placement that depends on the sampled characters and comparison set.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## A time window is not a dated split
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/2/text/en -->
The paper places the fossil at roughly 29–28 million years ago using the formation’s biochronology. That age helps frame a possible interval for catarrhine evolution, but it is not a direct measurement of when apes and Old World monkeys diverged. A stem-group position also does not show that this individual or species was their direct ancestor.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## atlas-profile / readerLimitations

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerLimitations/en -->
The account rests on one partial cranium and one published character analysis. Biochronological age is not a direct date for a divergence, and the proposed stem-catarrhine position does not establish direct ancestry.
