## The diagnosis and holotype give different size bounds
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5332023-34s4g
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The diagnosis reports thirteen males at 28–32.4 mm snout–vent length, mean 30 mm, and one female at 33.2 mm. Yet the holotype description gives an adult male at 26.7 mm, below that diagnostic range. The discrepancy is retained rather than broadening a range without knowing which samples were included. Other diagnostic traits include a half-arm axillary membrane, basal finger webbing, toe webbing, single palmar tubercle, moderate pectoral patches and a circular sacral mark with irregular pale dorsolateral bands. One female is not a female population range, and the circular mark alone does not reconcile all historical leucophyllatus-labelled material.

## Reticulated and spotted colour forms occur in the account
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5332023-34s4g
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
Live photographic accounts describe brown to dark-brown dorsal surfaces, sometimes white-spotted, with pale bands and circular marks white or bright yellow and often containing scattered brown spots. Reticulated individuals have a brown background with pale network lines. Limb undersides, thigh surfaces and webbing are orange-yellow to pink at night and described as orange by day; the vocal sac is yellow, belly creamy white and iris bronze. These are specimen-linked descriptive observations, not measured frequencies of morphs, proof of colour adaptation or a claim that every individual changes identically. Preserved and live pale areas remain distinct.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Type II notes are absent from advertisement calls, not all calls
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5332023-34s4g
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
The advertisement account describes a single Type I trill, mean duration 0.19 s, range 0.16–0.23 s, with fifteen to nineteen pulses and mean dominant frequency about 2,655 Hz. Type II notes are absent from that advertisement account. The aggressive-call account nevertheless contains three to four Type II notes, mean note duration 0.035 s, with mean whole-call duration 0.14 s and dominant frequency about 2,669 Hz. Absence must therefore remain specific to the advertisement context. The source classification is retained rather than asserting that all behavioural functions were experimentally established; individual averages and historical reanalyses in the methods also preclude treating each summary as a new independent animal.

## Bolivian lowland observations and an attributed spider predation
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5332023-34s4g
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
The revision recorded arndti in Beni and Santa Cruz, Bolivia, with known elevations 148–529 m, rounded in the prose to about 150–530 m. Nocturnal records came from river shores, forest edges, temporary swamplands and artificial ponds, with perches 0–140 cm above ground or water. That combined reference surface is not an elevation above sea level or a nest measurement. A prior Jansen and Schulze report of Ancylometes spider predation was attributed, not newly observed or quantified in this revision. Regional vegetation categories include savanna, dry forest and Yungas; they do not establish that every specimen used every habitat.

## A larval description was cited under an older lineage label
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5332023-34s4g
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/text/en -->
The species account points to an earlier tadpole description published under the label Dendropsophus leucophyllatus A. This explains where the authors associated larval information with arndti, but the pointer alone supplies no measured larval duration, Gosner-stage series, diet or metamorphic survival data. Those traits are not imported here from an unread referenced paper or from all frogs once labelled leucophyllatus. Any later use of that larval source must retain its voucher and lineage identity, and verify the underlying measurements before adding biological claims.

## A historical range polygon is not occupied habitat
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5332023-34s4g
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/text/en -->
The study estimated a minimum-convex distribution polygon of 66,397 square kilometres for this account. It reported 3,834 square kilometres (5.8%) degraded by human activities. Natural-vegetation estimates used the Amazon Deforestation 2010 layer. A polygon includes intervening space and is not equivalent to occupied area, population size or measured percentage population loss. The paper proposed Least Concern; this dated recommendation is not independent verification of a current Red List assessment. Protection and broad range alone do not demonstrate a stable population or current site security.
