## A source-listed native regional occurrence
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12070909-4s7nb
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The 2025 checklist lists Rhinella horribilis as native in the Sierra Madre Occidental biogeographic province of western Mexico. The authors compile and check state lists, museum-based information, VertNet and GBIF rather than newly surveying every species. Their regional scope crosses parts of Sonora, Chihuahua, Sinaloa, Durango, Zacatecas, Nayarit and Jalisco; it does not imply occurrence throughout all seven states or every habitat in the mountains. The record is attributed to the source taxonomy, not an independently examined voucher, density estimate or complete current range.

## Adult tapeworm associations in a historical North American checklist
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10658874-4s7nb
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
The 2023 review includes Rhinella horribilis among amphibian hosts with records of adult intestinal cestodes. Its named parasite associations are Distoichometra bufonis, Nematotaenia dispar. These describe compiled host–parasite records, not parasites measured in every member of the species. The scope is adult worms in wild continental North American herpetofauna; larval cestode records are excluded. An intestinal association alone does not establish disease severity, host mortality, zoonotic transmission or the species’ role in completing every parasite life cycle. Prevalence, intensity, examined-host denominators and experimental susceptibility are not supplied by this host checklist.
