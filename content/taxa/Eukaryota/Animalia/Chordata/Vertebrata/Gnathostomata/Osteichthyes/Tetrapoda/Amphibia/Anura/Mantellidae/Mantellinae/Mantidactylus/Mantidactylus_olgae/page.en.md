## Grey granulated surfaces and disc proportions are jointly diagnostic
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10563080-qmctj
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The diagnosis gives male snout–vent length of 34–40 mm and female length of 45–52 mm. Grey dorsal and flank surfaces bear black granules, with a lip line and V- or Y-shaped dorsal markings. An inguinal yellow streak, broad pale flank marking and white flank spots are absent in the diagnostic account. The inner side of toe IV has one free phalanx, webbing score is below seven, maximum disc width/base width exceeds 1.8, and tibiotarsal reach is between eye and nostril. Ratios and scores are distinct from millimetres; these characters are not a single-feature identification guarantee.

## Rock-bottom waters and low vegetation supplied observed perches
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10563080-qmctj
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
Encounters around rocky streams and rivers span 10:00–22:30, mainly at night except for juveniles. Females were on rocks at night, with some on leaves 20–50 cm high; males were more often on tree leaves 10–30 cm high. The account describes an association with fast water. This apparent preference is an observational interpretation, not an experimental flow threshold or quantified habitat-selection coefficient. Juvenile daytime records do not establish all juvenile activity, and substrate records do not demonstrate where eggs were deposited.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Northern records span 600–1,700 metres in this account
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10563080-qmctj
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The source lists Ramena in Tsaratanana, Analabe, Andramanalana and northern Sorata, with a reported elevation interval of 600–1,700 m. The interval is a source distribution summary, not an experimentally demonstrated tolerance or complete elevational census. Named areas do not imply that all intervening forest or river systems are occupied. The revision’s concept must be retained when comparing earlier records under other names.

## Nearby Marojejy lacks confirmed material in this revision
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10563080-qmctj
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The comments discuss Andramanalana material but report no morphologically or genetically confirmed olgae samples from Marojejy, around forty kilometres away. Lack of confirmed material is an evidence gap rather than proof of absence, a dispersal barrier or an unsampled population’s identity. Geographic proximity alone cannot assign Marojejy records to olgae, nor can the absence of sequenced specimens certify all historical locality names. The account remains bounded to attributed vouchers and the revision’s examined material.
