## overview

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
A 16-month mark-recapture study investigated the rufous mouse lemur in rainforest at Ranomafana National Park in southeastern Madagascar. Researchers used a quasi-grid with traps about 50 m apart and released animals at their capture sites; the study concerns spatial distribution and capture composition at that site.

## distribution_habitat

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
The paper confines its study to Ranomafana National Park rainforest, with monthly trapping and variable numbers of nights. This long-running site sample alone cannot define the species' full range or occupancy among habitats.

## ecology

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The study captured 102 males and 72 females and reported more male captures at 83% of trap sites; capture sex ratio changed significantly over time. These are captures during that study, not a stable species-wide sex ratio.
