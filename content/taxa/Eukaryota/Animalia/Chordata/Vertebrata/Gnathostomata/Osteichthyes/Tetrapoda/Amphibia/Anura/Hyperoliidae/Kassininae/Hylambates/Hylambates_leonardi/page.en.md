## Two mitochondrial markers and uneven references limit inference
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5683629-6mnbx
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
For Hylambates leonardi, the study’s identification workflow combines COI BIN/ABGD clustering,16S reference comparison and morphology/type-locality context. COI and16S are both mitochondrial and do not constitute independent nuclear confirmation or reproductive-isolation tests. The authors use neighbor joining to cluster similar sequences, explicitly limiting its use for evolutionary-history inference. Most per-species material came from only one or two localities, and not all GenBank vouchers could be morphologically examined. Reference labels and missing sequences therefore remain evidence limits. Fieldwork took place in May–June2012 and March–April2013; GenBank/BOLD comparison queries refer to the2016 source-library snapshot, not a refreshed present-day database audit.

## Study counts and identification scope are not population estimates
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/sourceIds/0 -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
The study reports540 sequenced specimens overall,524COI sequences and52816S sequences, assigned94BINs and85ABGD groups. These totals cover the whole multispecies reference library, not Hylambates leonardi alone. Methods describe80 first-season LMR and97 second-season specimens, whereas Table1 lists79 and97 sequenced/retained entries; methods also describe275 Congo reference specimens while Table1 locality rows total274. These collection-versus-analysis counts are retained without inventing an exclusion reason. The library’s group counts are not a verified census of current accepted species. Raw traces, BOLD assignments and GenBank accessions were not independently reanalysed here.
