# Palaeognathae


## From flighted tinamous to repeated flight loss

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
The companion 2019 studies by Sackton, Cloutier and colleagues compare fourteen palaeognath species: four flighted tinamous, living flightless lineages and extinct little bush moa. Tinamous are nested among traditional ratite lineages, so flightlessness cannot itself define a monophyletic group. Inferences about flight loss and regulatory regions depend on sampling, ancestral states and tree models. This is a research window, not the current catalogue’s full palaeognath roster. Living COL paths and fossil PBDB paths remain distinct.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Evidence limits

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
This route introduces occurrence records for species authored in this batch, not a complete descendant catalogue or a common ecological summary. Record counts are not comparable measurements of specimens, individuals, populations or sampling intensity; outer age bounds are not actual origin and extinction times. PBDB classification paths organize the material while remaining distinct from COL and other phylogenetic schemes.
New genome text uses Sackton et al. (2019), published main text, four figure captions and figure 1 pixels; the read sampling, marker extraction, species-tree methods, selected results and discussion passages of Cloutier et al. (2019); and ten NCBI WGS master records. Companion papers share material rather than independent population replicates. Other figure pixels, supplements, raw sequences and analysis code were not independently examined or rerun; the companion paper was not reviewed paragraph by paragraph in full. Candidate regulatory acceleration, open chromatin and a single-region chicken reporter assay are distinct evidence levels, not functional validation across all species. Complete biology remain unfinished.
