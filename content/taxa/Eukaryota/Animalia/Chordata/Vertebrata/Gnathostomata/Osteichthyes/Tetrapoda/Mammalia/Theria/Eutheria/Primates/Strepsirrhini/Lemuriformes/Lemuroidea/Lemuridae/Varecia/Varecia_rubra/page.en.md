## fieldStudy

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
Martinez and Razafindratsima studied three habituated groups. Fruits accounted for 61% of the recorded diet, and seeds from 75% of consumed fruit species were recovered in collected feces. For 35 gut-passage observations, the reported range was 63–423 minutes, with a mean of 225 minutes.

## seedDispersal

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
Among 194 seed-dispersal measurements linked to potential parent plants, median distance was 48 m and the mean was 83 m (range 0–568 m). Two of the three group home ranges overlapped regenerating forest parcels. Most fecal samples containing seeds were deposited in undisturbed forest, while the lemurs also consumed the non-native shrub Clidemia hirta and dispersed both native and non-native seeds into restoration parcels.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## limits

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The results come from three habituated groups at one restoration site. They describe measured feeding and seed movement, not a population estimate, species-wide seed-dispersal rate or proof of long-term forest recovery.
