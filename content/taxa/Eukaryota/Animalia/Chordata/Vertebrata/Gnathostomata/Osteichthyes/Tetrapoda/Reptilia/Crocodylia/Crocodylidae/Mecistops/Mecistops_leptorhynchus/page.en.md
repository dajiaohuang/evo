## Read the study

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
Which measured skull features helped distinguish two slender-snouted crocodiles? This guide follows the accepted COL26.8 record 8GPZP and reads the study at its stated sample and geographic scale. A comparison of adult skulls from the two living slender-snouted crocodiles found cranial shape differences between the Central African slender-snouted crocodile (Mecistops leptorhynchus) and its West African congener. Nasal shape and the snout length-to-width ratio at the premaxillary notch were especially informative. The paper concerns cranial diagnosis; one ratio is not an absolute field character independent of age, individual variation or measurement method.

Continue with Mecistops cataphractus (COL 3YKJB) to read the congener's field-monitoring guide without treating skull measurements as a census. The Crocodylia group guide provides the wider reading route; the neighboring page retains its own sources and limits.

## Limits of these trait data

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
This supplemental account uses limited trait material and does not count as complete morphology or ecology. This account paraphrases the same-name ReptTraits v1-2 entry, retaining its 2023 taxonomic framework and sources for individual traits. One row per species compresses variation in sex, age, locality and populations; matching name and family do not ensure equivalent later concepts. Reported maximum length is not an absolute species limit, and reproductive means are not fixed counts in every clutch. Many mass entries are estimated from length equations and are not presented here as measured mass. Juvenile-length headers and definitions differ and are also omitted from reader conclusions. Conservation, venom, current distribution and population trends are not updated here; unrecorded fields remain gaps. Dataset citation labels preserve the source chain and do not imply that this project independently reread every original work.
