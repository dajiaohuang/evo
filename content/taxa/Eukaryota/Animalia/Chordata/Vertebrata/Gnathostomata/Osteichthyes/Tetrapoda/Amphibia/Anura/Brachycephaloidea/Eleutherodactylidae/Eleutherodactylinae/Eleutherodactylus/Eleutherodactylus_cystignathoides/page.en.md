## Collection and chemical interpretation limits
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/12/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc11677230-6f4m7
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/12/text/en -->
Eleutherodactylus cystignathoides was sampled in 2024 at Austin, Texas; dorsal and ventral skins were processed separately for UHPLC-HESI-MS/MS after benzocaine euthanasia. This platform offers higher sensitivity but less precise retention-time and structural annotation than the GC-MS pipeline. Standards were unavailable for most frog alkaloids, so absolute quantities cannot be recovered from these reported signal values. The two instrument/pipeline datasets are not directly comparable. Full-skin GC-MS results were not mass-corrected, and co-eluting compounds with the same base peak could not always be distinguished. The authors did not serially sample each species across seasons and a broad range. Raw chromatograms, vouchers and supplementary individual-level records have not been independently reanalysed here.

## Passive accumulation remains a mechanistic hypothesis
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/13/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc11677230-6f4m7
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/13/text/en -->
The paper uses these skin observations in a proposed sequence from dietary toxin exposure and some resistance, through slower elimination and temporary retention, to derived transport or storage associated with sequestration. For Eleutherodactylus cystignathoides, a measured skin profile alone does not establish dietary origin, elimination rate, a binding mechanism or transport specifically to skin. Presence in skin does not by itself distinguish passive retention from specialised sequestration. The proposed ancestral condition and evolutionary sequence require broader sampling and tissue/time-series experiments; they are not direct observations of ancestors or a reconstructed molecular pathway in this sampled species.

## Species-specific detections and their evidence limits
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/14/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc11677230-6f4m7
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/14/text/en -->
The stringent LC-MS criteria yielded no reported lipophilic frog alkaloids in three individuals, while the broad classifier identified55 alkaloid-pathway metabolites. Forty were treated as matching features found in Silverstoneia flotator. Broad computational annotation and cross-sample feature matches are not independent structural confirmation, proof of dietary origin or a positive defence assay; some may be frog or symbiont metabolic products.
