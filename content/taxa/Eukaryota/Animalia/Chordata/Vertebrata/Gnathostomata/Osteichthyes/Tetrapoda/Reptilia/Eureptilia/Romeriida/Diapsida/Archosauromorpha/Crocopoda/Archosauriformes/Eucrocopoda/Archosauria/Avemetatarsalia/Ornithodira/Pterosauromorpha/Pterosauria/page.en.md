# Pterosauria

## origin and relationships

<!-- evo:text /records/readerSections/0/id -->
origin-and-relationships
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/readerSections/0/title/en -->
An archosaur branch near dinosaurs
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/readerSections/0/text/en -->
Expanded anatomical analyses place pterosaurs within Ornithodira, on the archosaur branch close to dinosaurs. Their exact origin and closest relatives remain dependent on the characters and analyses used; this placement does not identify a direct dinosaur ancestor.

## fossil record and geography

<!-- evo:text /records/readerSections/1/id -->
fossil-record-and-geography
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/readerSections/1/title/en -->
A worldwide record with large gaps
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/readerSections/1/text/en -->
The redescription of Carniadactylus documents a Norian pterosaur sample from the Late Triassic, but one dated locality does not establish the group’s first global appearance. A separate analysis of 108 pterosaur taxa reported remains from every continent while emphasizing severe gaps in where and when fossils are known. Geographic patterns therefore depend on an uneven, time-sensitive record.

## preserved eggs and integument

<!-- evo:text /records/readerSections/2/id -->
preserved-eggs-and-integument
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/readerSections/2/title/en -->
What particular fossils preserve
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/readerSections/2/text/en -->
One Hamipterus block preserves many eggs, including embryo-bearing eggs, giving a direct fossil record of reproduction in that deposit. In a separate Crato Formation specimen, melanosome geometry in preserved integument was used to assess a possible signalling role. These are specific fossil cases; behavioral readings and feather-homology claims remain interpretations, not observations of living pterosaurs.

## flight and launch models

<!-- evo:text /records/readerSections/3/id -->
flight-and-launch-models
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/readerSections/3/title/en -->
Testing flight with explicit assumptions
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/readerSections/3/text/en -->
Biomechanical studies use reconstructed body mass, wing dimensions, muscle power and launch kinematics to test whether giant pterosaurs could fly and launch quadrupedally. Their outputs are feasibility results conditioned on those inputs, not preserved motions or measurements that apply uniformly across the pterosaur radiation.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## reader limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/readerLimitations/en -->
This is a selective route through studies of relationships, fossils, geography and biomechanics. The fossil record is patchy; the cited locality and specimen results do not establish a complete global range, life history, diet or uniform flight capability. The profile is not externally expert-reviewed.
