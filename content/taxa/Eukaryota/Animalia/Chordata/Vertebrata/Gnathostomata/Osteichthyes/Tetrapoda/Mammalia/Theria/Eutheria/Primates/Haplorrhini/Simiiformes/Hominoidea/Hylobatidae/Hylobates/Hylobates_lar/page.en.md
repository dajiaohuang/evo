## ecology

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
A close-range call study at Khao Yai National Park, Thailand, analyzed 462 “hoo” calls from 14 males and 11 females. Acoustic parameters differed among adequately sampled contexts; for example, raptor-context calls had lower peak and low frequencies than duet calls. The authors noted that playback tests were still needed to determine whether listeners distinguish these variants.

## ecology

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
Twelve playback trials at the same park involved six habituated groups. Five groups counter-sang after duet-song playback; predator-song playback elicited antipredator responses but no duet singing. The groups responded differently to these recordings under the experiment's protocol, which does not establish the functions of the full natural call repertoire.

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
Source-linked introduction; not externally expert-reviewed. Both studies use bounded samples from Khao Yai National Park in Thailand, and the playback experiment comprised only twelve trials; they do not represent other populations, the full call repertoire or the species' ecology as a whole.
