## overview

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
This page follows COL26.8 in listing Sapajus cucullatus as an accepted species; the fixed MDD crosswalk has no corresponding entry, so both display names are editorial translations. Taxonomic treatments differ: the 2020 IUCN assessment placed it as the subspecies Sapajus nigritus cucullatus, whereas Rylands and Mittermeier’s 2024 review of Neotropical primates lists S. cucullatus as a monotypic species. The field studies below use the historical subspecies name; their geographic and ecological results are reported at that scope and are not treated as a range-wide status assessment.

## distribution

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
Using survey, institutional, field-observation, and citizen-science records, Slomp and colleagues mapped 146 records assigned to the historical subspecies S. nigritus cucullatus in 82 municipalities of Rio Grande do Sul; 37 records were in protected areas. They report occurrence in Atlantic Forest and Pampas ecotones, with the Ijuí and Jacuí rivers and Guaíba Lake marking a southern limit in their analysis. The provenance of the isolated Porto Alegre/Viamão population remained uncertain, and the authors removed inaccurate records. These are compiled occurrence records for the subspecies as treated in 2024, not an abundance estimate or a resolved map of the separate COL26.8 species concept.

## field-study

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
In northern Misiones, Argentina, Martínez de Zorzi, Shanee, and Oklander compared one continuous-forest site with one anthropogenic forest fragment. Surveys covered five days per month in November 2019–March 2020 and November 2020–March 2021, with a gap during COVID-19 restrictions. The study reports group sizes of 12–23 and modeled density of 0.14 individuals/ha in continuous forest, versus groups of 32–36 and 0.62 individuals/ha in the fragment (n=107; ZINB, p<0.05). The authors suggest reduced dispersal could explain the higher fragment density. This two-site comparison is local evidence under the subspecies name S. nigritus cucullatus; it does not establish a range-wide density pattern or by itself resolve the taxonomic boundary used by COL26.8.

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
The studies add regional occurrence and two-site ecological evidence under the historical subspecies name S. nigritus cucullatus, while COL26.8 treats S. cucullatus as a species and the 2024 taxonomic review also recognizes it at species rank. The 2020 IUCN assessment retains subspecies rank, and the fixed MDD crosswalk has no corresponding entry. The occurrence compilation and local comparison cannot be generalized into a current species-wide population or status assessment. Not externally expert-reviewed.
