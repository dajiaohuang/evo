## overview

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
Read this Mico guide by evidence type and study period. First, Mourthé et al. (2022) compared IUCN range-map overlaps across ten Amazonian interfluves; its supplement lists eight Mico species and ten species-by-interfluve overlaps, not point-level field confirmations. Then compare local studies: the 2018 and 2023 Mico chrysoleucos surveys in the middle Madeira and south-central Amazonia; 2004 field records for M. mauesi; Rylands' 1978 observations of M. intermedius at Dardanelos; the 2012–2015 Marmelos–Aripuanã survey of M. marcai; and the dated Brazilian M. saterei assessment published in 2025. Garbino et al. (2013) report two M. humilis records under the historical name Callibella humilis; Costa-Araújo et al. (2021) combine morphology, phylogenomics and locality records to describe M. schneideri. This is a path through selected materials, not a complete species checklist, genus-wide ecology review or phylogeny.

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
This guide links selected Mico studies and is not a complete species checklist, range map, ecological synthesis or phylogeny. Map overlays, historical local observations, later surveys, the dated national assessment and integrative taxonomy answer different questions and should not be collapsed into one evidence type. Source-linked; not externally expert-reviewed.
