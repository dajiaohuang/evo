# Alligator mississippiensis


## Juvenile material and sensory organs on the jaws

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
Integumentary sensory organs (ISOs) are small skin structures associated with sensory nerves. Leitch and Catania’s entire 2012 study used 18 American alligators from Rockefeller Wildlife Refuge in Louisiana, alongside four Nile crocodiles supplied by a commercial breeder. Study material ranged from newly hatched to about three years old; pooled length and mass ranges are not an alligator growth curve, and each experiment had its own subset. Cranial organ counts in three juvenile alligators were approximately 4200±94 (mean±s.d.), with higher densities near the teeth and snout tip. A separate density method used head photographs from two individuals; those subsets cannot be added into a total of independent animals.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## The smallest probe sets a measurement limit

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
In 2012, calibrated von Frey filaments indented moist skin while trigeminal afferent units were recorded. The paper reports 174 receptive fields measured for indentation force across the two study species, spanning applied forces of 0.078–13.725 mN, not 174 animals of this species. Twenty-eight fields responded to the smallest 0.078 mN filament; their actual minimum thresholds were unresolved because a smaller calibrated force was unavailable. A receptive field is the skin area activating an afferent unit, distinct from one sensory organ, an animal count or a water-wave detection distance. Moisture affected thresholds, so these are not fixed sensitivities for every field condition.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Touch without the same postcranial sensory spots

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The studied alligators lacked the Nile crocodile pattern of postcranial sensory organs, yet limb nerve recordings still detected tactile receptive fields, including sensitive distal digit regions. Absence of visible ISO spots does not mean absence of skin sensory nerves or touch. Among 110 cranial afferent units in four alligators, 51% were rapidly adapting and 49% slowly adapting. These classify unit responses to maintained stimulation, not two kinds of animal, and do not establish the same proportions at every age.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Wave localization and capture after contact

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The authors observed juveniles with food pellets and fish in aquaria, using 940 nm infrared illumination and white noise in some trials to limit visual and auditory cues. Both American alligators and Nile crocodiles oriented toward surface disturbances and then swept their heads; capture followed rapidly when an object contacted skin or the open mouth. This links disturbance localization with actions after contact. The passage supplies no complete species-specific behavioral trial total or success rate, and does not establish wild predation rates, exclusive reliance on touch or a fixed response time for every individual. Anatomical and neural recordings supply another evidence layer; behavioral sequences do not test every sensory pathway.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Read the study

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
How do habitat choices change with the scale used to measure them? This guide follows the accepted COL26.8 record BTRB and reads the study at its stated sample and geographic scale. At the Pearl River and Ross Barnett Reservoir in Mississippi, researchers tracked 17 adult male alligators in 2012–2013 and collected 1,145 locations. Habitat selection differed between home-range and within-range patch scales; some individuals selected patches with floating or emergent vegetation.

Continue with Crocodylus niloticus (COL ZKNK) to compare two local habitat studies while keeping observation and selection separate. The Crocodylia group guide provides the wider reading route; the neighboring page retains its own sources and limits.

## Evidence limits

Matching name and family and the presence of same-name sparse rows support the association here; they do not establish equivalence to COL26.8 concepts or remove all inherited components from the reported summaries. ## Distinguish the measured length

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
Recorded dimensions are reported maximum total length (TL) 4600 mm. SVL runs from snout to vent and differs from total length including the tail. Compiled maxima are reported material, not theoretical growth limits or typical individual size. Sex-specific values may come from different localities or studies and alone cannot quantify population sexual dimorphism.

## How to read reproductive records

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/text/en -->
the reproductive-mode field uses the original label oviparous; sex determination is recorded as TSD; consolidated mean offspring or eggs per litter/clutch 39.7; lowest reported clutch/litter mean 20; highest reported clutch/litter mean 50; consolidated litters/clutches per year 1; egg length 76 mm. Clutch/litter summaries can use midpoints of reported means or, when means are unavailable, extrema; they are not necessarily arithmetic averages of observed clutches. Fractions do not describe non-integer young in a litter. The lowest/highest fields are defined as endpoints of reported means rather than guaranteed smallest/largest individual clutches. Reproductive and sex-determination labels do not establish fetal nutrition or uniformity across all populations.

## Limits of these trait data

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/10/text/en -->
This account paraphrases the same-name ReptTraits v1-2 entry, retaining its 2023 taxonomic framework and sources for individual traits. One row per species compresses variation in sex, age, locality and populations; matching name and family do not ensure equivalent later concepts. Reported maximum length is not an absolute species limit, and reproductive means are not fixed counts in every clutch. Many mass entries are estimated from length equations and are not presented here as measured mass. Juvenile-length headers and definitions differ and are also omitted from reader conclusions. Conservation, venom, current distribution and population trends are not updated here; unrecorded fields remain gaps. Dataset citation labels preserve the source chain and do not imply that this project independently reread every original work.
