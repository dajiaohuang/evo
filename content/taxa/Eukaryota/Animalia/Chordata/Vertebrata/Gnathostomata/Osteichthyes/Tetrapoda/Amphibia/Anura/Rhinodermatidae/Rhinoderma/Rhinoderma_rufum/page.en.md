## Vocal-sac care followed by aquatic larval development
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc3680453-78tr2
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The paper’s cited life-history comparison described males incubating young in the vocal sac, then releasing larvae into water before metamorphosis. R. darwinii instead retains its young until metamorphosis. This is a synthesis of earlier observations, and the 2008–2012 survey did not observe breeding by R. rufum.

## No detections during a dated historical-range search
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc3680453-78tr2
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The study surveyed 223 sites across the two Darwin’s frogs’ historical ranges, including 46 in the range of R. rufum alone and 20 in their overlap. It neither saw nor heard R. rufum. Daylight searches were conducted in October–March; the study classified a site as absent only after at least two visits in different years, each lasting at least five hours. This effort supports the reported nondetection, without turning it into proof of global extinction or present-day absence.

## A 2013 possibly-extinct recommendation
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc3680453-78tr2
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The discussion reported the last recorded detection as 1980 and considered surviving populations still possible somewhere in the historical range. It recommended consideration as Critically Endangered (Possibly Extinct). Both the last-record date and the recommendation are the paper’s 2013 evidence and interpretation; no later search outcome or current conservation assessment is asserted here.

## A dated multicriteria conservation priority
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10968267-78tr2
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
The 2024 Chilean assessment assigns Rhinoderma rufum a printed conservation-priority index of 2.21 and places it in the medium category in Table 4. The study reviews 58 selected species using literature, citizen-science records and government plans, rather than newly censusing every population. Its index combines distribution, threat, population trend, taxonomic uniqueness and body-size scores with a protected-area term. The value is a management ranking under this model, not an extinction probability, a current IUCN assessment or a direct measurement of population size. “Non-priority” and “low” do not mean biologically safe or no need for conservation.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## The population and protection scores retain their source limits
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10968267-78tr2
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
Table 3 gives Rhinoderma rufum a population-trend code of 4 (recent decline) and a land-protection code of 1 (the entire population outside protected areas), using the definitions in Table 2. These are the authors’ ordinal assignments, not newly verified census trajectories or measured protected population fractions. A zero denotes unavailable evidence, not a stable population or zero risk. Threat code 3 is also a grouped source category and cannot identify a specific official status by itself. AOO and EOO scores classify area criteria rather than supplying independently reconstructed occupancy maps; protection coverage does not establish effective management.
