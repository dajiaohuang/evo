## A silver-grey iris and yellowish-brown dorsal pattern
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7936570-slxgc
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The diagnosis gives male snout–vent length of 22.2–22.9 mm, yellowish-brown dorsal colour, prominent granules with sharp horny spinules, a weak or discontinuous mid-dorsal ridge, tongue papillae and limited foot webbing. The live holotype had a dark brown X-shaped dorsal mark and scattered spots, dark cross-bands on the limbs, and a silver-grey iris with minute brown speckling. Preserved colours and markings were described separately and were paler in several areas. The holotype appearance is not a population frequency estimate or proof that every individual has an identical X mark. The male diagnostic range does not establish female body size.

## Primary-forest records in the Siruvani and Silent Valley region
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7936570-slxgc
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The 2021 account recorded vellikkannan from its type locality at Singappara, Siruvani, and surrounding Silent Valley National Park regions in Palakkad district, Kerala, north of the Palghat gap. Individuals were observed inside primary forest on vegetation up to four metres high during the breeding season. This is the observed perch range in the account, not proof that frogs never occur higher, a quantitative preference for primary over secondary forest or an identified egg substrate. The description does not measure population trends or validate a present conservation category. Nearby suitable forest should not be added as a confirmed record without observations.

## The acoustic repertoire remained undescribed
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7936570-slxgc
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The species account explicitly states that vellikkannan vocalisations had not been recorded and analysed. The measured 411.2 ms, two-pulse call of its sister species sanjappai is consequently not a vellikkannan observation. Missing recordings do not demonstrate silence or absence of acoustic communication. Calling heights or call summaries for the larger chromasynchysi group also cannot substitute for species-identified acoustic evidence. A reliable repertoire description would require recordings linked to correctly identified individuals and their environmental context.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## A multilocus context for its sister relationship
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7936570-slxgc
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The study assigned vellikkannan to the chromasynchysi group and recovered a sister relationship to sanjappai. Their mitochondrial 16S divergence was 3.1–3.3%, while reported distances to chromasynchysi, ravii and silentvalley were higher. The larger phylogenetic study included mitochondrial and nuclear markers, rather than treating a single percentage as its complete taxonomic evidence. The tree does not demonstrate reproductive isolation, date the split or establish a unique historical dispersal route. Diagnostic morphology and separate locality records remain necessary, especially because vellikkannan calls were not analysed.
