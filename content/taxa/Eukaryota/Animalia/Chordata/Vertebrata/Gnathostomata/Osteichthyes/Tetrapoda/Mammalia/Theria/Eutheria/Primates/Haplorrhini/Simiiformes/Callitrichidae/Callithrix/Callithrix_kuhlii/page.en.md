## feeding

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
Soto da Costa and colleagues sampled hair from 107 Wied’s marmosets in 30 groups across 14 municipalities in southern Bahia, then modelled diet at six sites with isotope baselines for food resources. Both isotope measures were negatively associated with forest cover: animals from less-forested sites had higher hair isotope values. Fruit and insects were the main modelled foods, but their contributions varied by site; fruit was higher in more-forested sites and insects higher in less-forested sites. The authors note that anthropogenic foods may explain part of the isotope pattern, which is a possibility rather than a confirmed food record.

## movement

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
An earlier study followed two groups for three months each in a coastal Atlantic Forest remnant. It described tree-exudate feeding and home ranges of about 10 ha centred on a principal exudate tree. The article uses the older spelling C. kuhli; its small sample and single site should not be treated as a modern range-wide home-range norm.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
Source-linked studies from southern Bahia and one 1980 forest remnant; not externally expert-reviewed. Isotope patterns are indirect dietary evidence, and the older C. kuhli study used a historical spelling and two groups at one site.
