COL26.8 accepts Microhyla irrawaddy Poyarkov, Gorin, Zaw, Kretova, Gogoleva, Pawangkhanant & Che, 2019 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Microhylidae (family) → Microhyla (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## Seven males and six females have overlapping size ranges
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc6680123-6rh3b
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The type-series account reports seven adult males at 12.3–17.1 mm and six adult females at 16.7–20.9 mm snout–vent length, including the holotype in the male count. Females are generally larger, but the ranges overlap. The very slender frog has granular skin, no dorsolateral fold, well-developed finger I, slightly expanded digit tips without conspicuous discs, almost completely reduced toe webbing and tibiotarsal reach to the eye. Small metatarsal tubercles distinguish the diagnosis from the shovel-shaped condition of fodiens. Sample size and sex ranges are not independent population counts or a fixed sex-identification threshold.

## Bright male tubercles and paler female colour vary among vouchers
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc6680123-6rh3b
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The live holotype has a yellow-brown dorsum with dark hourglass-like marking, orange to red granules, barred limbs, cream belly and densely grey-mottled throat. The iris has bronze-green to gold reticulation and green-yellow sclera. Female dorsal colour is generally paler and more grey-olive, with fewer bright tubercles; one female’s continuous dorsal motif is replaced by separate blotches. Males have a darker subgular vocal sac. These sex-associated and individual variations do not define immutable colour states, and unpigmented eggs visible through a gravid female’s belly do not establish clutch size or developmental stage.

## Acoustic sample sizes count calls and pulses
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc6680123-6rh3b
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The rattling advertisement call is reported at 0.184 ± 0.07 s, range 0.004–0.277 s, from fifty calls. Each contains one to five pulses; pulse duration averages 4 ms, with 194 pulses measured. Interpulse intervals and periods have 144 observations, while pulse-rate summaries use 49 calls, with range 11.89–57.14 pulses/s and reported mean 17.3. Call maximum-amplitude frequency averages 3,780 Hz, range 2,810–4,120; the pulse-level mean is separately 3,700 Hz across 194 pulses. These units are not fifty or 194 independent males, and pulse frequency is not silently substituted for the call-level frequency.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Males call from concealed bank hollows
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc6680123-6rh3b
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
Observations come from central Myanmar’s dry zone near temporary or permanent pools, paddy fields and reservoirs. Animals were collected at 18:30–21:00; male calling continued roughly 19:00–02:00, so collection times and calling times are distinct. Males usually hid in bank cracks and holes, sometimes buffalo hoofprints; females hid in nearby grass. These encounters describe substrate use without a controlled concealment preference, disturbance tolerance or proof that hoofprints are obligatory breeding habitat. The regional savanna-like climate does not define measured physiological thermal limits.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Captured-female egg release does not establish natural clutch size
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc6680123-6rh3b
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
The authors state that clutch size is unknown. Female ZMMU A5972 laid approximately thirty eggs in a plastic bag after capture; that event is not certified as a complete natural clutch. Eggs are described as 0.8–0.9 mm in diameter and yellow-white with a brownish pole, but the printed adjective for the pole is malformed and is not used to infer developmental biology. Larval stages and diet were unknown in the paper. The observation does not establish egg substrate in the wild, fertilization, hatch success, parental care or direct development.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Two confirmed areas remain separate from the Sagaing candidate
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc6680123-6rh3b
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
The source confirms Pakokku suburbs on the Irrawaddy, type label 59 m, and Kan Pauk vicinity, paratype label 217 m; the distribution summary rounds these to 60–220 m. Kan Pauk is about thirty kilometres north of the type area. A related Chatthin population in Sagaing, designated Microhyla sp. 2, differs by 2.0% and remains explicitly unresolved as conspecific. The genealogy places irrawaddy as sister to that unnamed population and close to kodial, rather than equating all of them. No candidate locality is promoted to confirmed range, and rounded elevations are not tolerance bounds.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## The 2019 Data Deficient proposal reflects unknown range and trend
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc6680123-6rh3b
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/text/en -->
Because actual range and population trends remained unknown, the authors proposed Data Deficient using the cited IUCN category framework. This is a dated research recommendation, not verification of a current Red List assessment. Two observed areas, calling males and gravid females do not quantify population abundance, long-term recruitment or conservation recovery. Predicted further Irrawaddy Valley sites require independent occurrence and identity evidence; neither their existence nor their absence is established by the proposal.
