# Sinopterus dongi


## Read anatomy and names from an immature holotype

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
The 2023 revision regards the Sinopterus dongi holotype IVPP V 13363 as a juvenile. Cranial and limb comparisons place several older names within this species while recognizing Huaxiadraco corollatus separately. Read material and methods before name interpretations; historical catalogue entries do not establish that all names represent separate biological species.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Diagnosis combines skull, neck and limb features

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
The revision’s combination includes a gently downturned rostrum, elongate nasoantorbital fenestra, approximately quadrangular orbit, near-vertical descending nasal process, fourth cervical longer than the fifth, and elongate first metacarpal. Further diagnostic features include a pteroid shorter than half the ulna and a first metatarsal at least as long as the second. Displacement in the holotype makes some proportions reconstruction-dependent; the combination guides comparisons rather than making one angle invariant across individuals.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Fusion supplies a relative developmental sequence

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
Unfused scapula and coracoid, humeral epiphysis, carpals, first wing-phalanx extensor tendon process and tibiotarsus support a juvenile assessment relative to more mature specimens in the group. The roughly 1.2 m wingspan estimates this specimen, not maximum adult size. Humeral length provides a size proxy and fusion orders relative stages, without establishing exact ages for each stage.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Separate growth signals before comparing morphotypes

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
Using humeral length as a size proxy, the authors examine size correlations and allometry in 21 measurements before clustering and principal-component analyses of proportions judged less affected by growth. Missing values are treated by mean and iterative imputation. Incomplete BPV-077 and the very young Nemicolopterus specimen are excluded from this morphometric dataset. Exclusions limit model scope, and a nonsignificant correlation does not prove complete independence from growth.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Two morphotypes support the authors’ two-species interpretation

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
Quantitative and qualitative comparisons recover two morphotypes, interpreted as S. dongi and H. corollatus. The 2023 work treats S. gui, Huaxiapterus jii, S. lingyuanensis and H. atavismus as junior synonyms of S. dongi. Clustering describes similarity rather than genealogy; a separate phylogenetic analysis informs genus assignment. Specimens and matrices were not independently reassessed here, and fixed database occurrences were not merged.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Crest presence, size and shape answer different questions

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The revision links absent premaxillary crests in the smallest juveniles to growth. Crest size still varies among larger specimens within a morphotype, potentially reflecting individual or sexual variation. Shape corresponds to the body-character groups: a heaped outline in crested S. dongi versus a more trapezoidal crest in H. corollatus. This interprets the sample without determining an individual’s sex or imposing one crest rule on all pterosaurs.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Stratigraphic overlap does not establish shared localities

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
The revision interprets stratigraphic overlap as possible temporal coexistence, while explicitly noting that both species are not known from the exact same localities. Some material is only localized to Chaoyang, with poor bed control. Niche partitioning needs finer locality and sedimentary evidence; two morphotypes do not establish food partitioning, group behavior or a known shared habitat.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Read juvenile-model identities with their study date

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
The 2021 flight study interprets IVPP V-14377, the Nemicolopterus holotype, as a near-hatching S. dongi. The 2023 revision regards its youth as preventing reliable assignment to either S. dongi or H. corollatus and retains it as indeterminate Sinopterinae. Models published under the 2021 name therefore do not establish an uncontested species-specific hatchling performance. Compare preservation and synonymy in [S. gui](../Sinopterus_gui/page.en.md), then the other model inputs in [Pterodaustro guinazui](../../../../../../Lophocratia/Pterodaustro/Pterodaustro_guinazui/page.en.md).
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Evidence limits

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
The existing collection sections guide PBDB occurrence records dated 2026-10-02, while the added account reads the 2023 revision; together they remain short of a complete species monograph. Entries belong to the fixed 276-item pterosaur working roster, retain the current same name and a complete parent chain to the recorded Pterosauria storage anchor, and have usable species-level occurrences without uncertain-identification qualifiers. Database accepted names can connect older combinations; that database correspondence does not decide specimen identifications or all taxonomic disputes and is not merged with same-name COL concepts. Collection sites, stratigraphic intervals, depositional settings and collection-level preservation modes have distinct meanings. They do not directly establish complete range, origin or extinction time, fixed species ecology, biological abundance or the condition of every specimen. Original descriptions, revisions, morphology, diet and behavior remain incompletely assembled; external domain expert review has not occurred. The 2023 front matter, Introduction, Methods through nomenclatural acts, two holotype accounts, statistical results, species interpretations and Tables 4–5, crest sections, Nemicolopterus comments, phylogenetic results, systematic accounts and Conclusions were read. Other specimen accounts, detailed anatomical-variation discussion and part of Methods remain unread. Images, specimens, supplementary measurements and matrices were not independently inspected or recalculated. Original 2003 descriptions, complete life history, diet and external expert review remain incomplete; synonymy, juvenile size and identification retain their sources.
