# Homo sapiens

## atlas-profile / overview

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/overview -->
COL26.8 accepted species usage 6MB3T identifies Homo sapiens (Linnaeus, 1758). This reader connects selected ancient human fossils, isotope evidence and genomic samples from several sites; it is not a complete species or migration history.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## atlas-profile / ecology / diet

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/ecology/diet -->
At Ilsenhöhle in Ranis, collagen isotopes from 10 human remains, interpreted with 52 animal remains, indicated a large-terrestrial-mammal diet in a cold steppe–tundra setting. This single cave sample does not describe Homo sapiens generally.

## Two fossils at Tam Pà Ling
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/1/text/en -->
At Tam Pà Ling cave in northern Laos, TPL 6 is a left frontal-bone fragment and TPL 7 a proximal left tibia, both attributed to H. sapiens by the study. Sediment models gave ages of 70 ± 3 ka for TPL 6 and as old as 77 ± 9 ka for TPL 7; these are contextual sediment estimates, not direct fossil dates. The authors note that TPL 7 may be older than the model’s oldest estimate, and taphonomic alteration limits its measurement.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Diet evidence from one cave at Ranis
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/2/text/en -->
At Ilsenhöhle in Ranis, Germany, researchers analyzed collagen isotopes from 10 human remains with 52 animal remains from the same archaeological setting. The signal points to a diet dominated by large terrestrial mammals in a cold steppe–tundra environment. Layer dates are about 47,500–45,770 and 46,820–43,260 calibrated years before present; they are not direct dates for each human bone. One cave sample cannot define human diets generally.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Ancient genomes south of the Limpopo
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/3/text/en -->
A whole-genome study analyzed 28 individuals from archaeological sites south of the Limpopo River in central and southern Africa, dated from 10,200 to 150 calibrated years before present. In the study’s comparison panel, samples older than 1,400 years fell outside the range of genetic variation represented by its modern-human samples and carried many variants the authors classified as H. sapiens-specific. This result depends on the sampled individuals, comparison panel and variant definitions.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## The directly dated Ust’-Ishim individual
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/4/text/en -->
The Ust’-Ishim 1 femur from western Siberia was directly radiocarbon dated to 46,880–43,210 calibrated years before present at 95.4% probability. Its genome belongs to this one dated individual. The result is not a date for the origin of H. sapiens or a first-and-last occurrence interval for the species.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Keep the sites and questions separate
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/5/text/en -->
The Laos fossils, Ranis dietary signal, Siberian individual and southern African genomes differ in place, age model and research question. Together they offer several windows into ancient H. sapiens, but they do not trace one continuous migration route or provide a current geographic range.

## atlas-profile / readerLimitations

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerLimitations/en -->
This bilingual reader draft selects ancient evidence from four sites and studies; it is not a complete account of living human populations, life history, present-day geographic range or human origins. Site ages, fossil dates and model-based genomic comparisons remain distinct. The dossier is incomplete and has not received independent expert review.
