## Read the study

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
How does genetic structure differ among the localities sampled across the Americas? This guide follows the accepted COL26.8 record ZKNB and reads the study at its stated sample and geographic scale. A study spanning the Americas and Greater Antilles combined mitochondrial control-region data with 11 nuclear microsatellite loci and found strong genetic structure among sampled localities. Differentiation was especially pronounced between continental and Greater Antillean samples, with additional separation between Cuba and Jamaica; the Venezuelan samples yielded three newly reported haplotypes. The results show why American-crocodile genetic relationships should be interpreted by region and sampling locality.

Continue with Crocodylus suchus (COL ZKNT) to compare population structure with a study about taxonomic lineages. The Crocodylia group guide provides the wider reading route; the neighboring page retains its own sources and limits.

## Evidence limits

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
A focused population-genetic study; not externally expert-reviewed, a range-wide census or an abundance estimate. Results depend on sampled localities, marker types and available sequences; no current conservation status is inferred here.
This batch uses historical same-name records from the 2015 life-history archive. Values are reported compilation medians or averages of the two middle values, except that maximum longevity uses the largest record found. They are not direct measurements of one individual, individual variation ranges, mean wild lifespans or current population surveys. The compilation can share and inherit data across historical species and subspecies. Matching name and family and the presence of same-name sparse rows support the association here; they do not establish equivalence to COL26.8 concepts or remove all inherited components from the reported summaries. Wild or captive conditions are not uniformly confirmed here, and underlying publications have not been individually reviewed. Diet, behavior, current range, conservation and missing fields require other sources; no complete monograph or external expert review is claimed.

## Distinguish the measured length

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
Recorded dimensions are female maximum snout–vent length (SVL) 3800 mm; reported maximum total length (TL) 6100 mm. SVL runs from snout to vent and differs from total length including the tail. Compiled maxima are reported material, not theoretical growth limits or typical individual size. Sex-specific values may come from different localities or studies and alone cannot quantify population sexual dimorphism.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Where and how it is active

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
In the entry, microhabitat is Arboreal/Saxicolous/Terrestrial; diet is Carnivorous; active timing is Nocturnal; foraging mode is ACT. These categories summarize activity and resource use rather than an exhaustive habitat list or fixed schedule. Positions such as arboreal and terrestrial can be combined. A carnivorous category does not specify prey, and populations, seasons and life stages may differ.

## How to read reproductive records

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
the reproductive-mode field uses the original label oviparous; sex determination is recorded as TSD; consolidated mean offspring or eggs per litter/clutch 40; lowest reported clutch/litter mean 30; highest reported clutch/litter mean 70; egg length 75 mm; egg width 50 mm. Clutch/litter summaries can use midpoints of reported means or, when means are unavailable, extrema; they are not necessarily arithmetic averages of observed clutches. Fractions do not describe non-integer young in a litter. The lowest/highest fields are defined as endpoints of reported means rather than guaranteed smallest/largest individual clutches. Reproductive and sex-determination labels do not establish fetal nutrition or uniformity across all populations.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Limits of these trait data

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
This account paraphrases the same-name ReptTraits v1-2 entry, retaining its 2023 taxonomic framework and sources for individual traits. One row per species compresses variation in sex, age, locality and populations; matching name and family do not ensure equivalent later concepts. Reported maximum length is not an absolute species limit, and reproductive means are not fixed counts in every clutch. Many mass entries are estimated from length equations and are not presented here as measured mass. Juvenile-length headers and definitions differ and are also omitted from reader conclusions. Conservation, venom, current distribution and population trends are not updated here; unrecorded fields remain gaps. Dataset citation labels preserve the source chain and do not imply that this project independently reread every original work.
