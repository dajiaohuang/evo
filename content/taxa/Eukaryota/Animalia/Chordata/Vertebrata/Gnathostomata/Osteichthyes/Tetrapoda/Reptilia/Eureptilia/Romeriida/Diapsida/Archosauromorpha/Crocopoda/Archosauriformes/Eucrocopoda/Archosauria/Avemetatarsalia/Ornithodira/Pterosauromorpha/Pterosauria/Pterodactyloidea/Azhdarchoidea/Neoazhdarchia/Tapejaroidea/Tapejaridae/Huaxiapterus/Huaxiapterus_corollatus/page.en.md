# Huaxiapterus corollatus


## An older combination leads into the Huaxiadraco revision

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
Huaxiapterus corollatus names the fixed PBDB work record. The 2023 study assigns holotype ZMNH M813 to a new genus as Huaxiadraco corollatus and treats H. benxiensis as its junior synonym. Read anatomy, growth and diagnosis through the holotype and referred material, retaining the older entry for database traceability rather than treating all three entries under the older genus as one biological group.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## A nearly complete holotype still has damaged and obscured regions

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
ZMNH M813 is nearly complete, but the posterior skull, post-cervical vertebrae and feet are especially damaged. Forearm bones cover cervicals four and five, preventing a direct length comparison; the posterior extent and proportions of the nasoantorbital fenestra partly depend on reconstruction from the jaw joint. Near-completeness does not make every character measurable, and images or specimens were not independently inspected here.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## The skull combination includes a downturned rostrum and trapezoidal crest

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The holotype account describes a trapezoidal premaxillary crest, shallow mandibular crest, more strongly downturned rostrum and shorter nasoantorbital fenestra, with a gap between closed jaws. Roughly 21° rostral deflection and a fenestra length/height ratio near 2.2 describe this specimen with some reconstruction, rather than invariant values for every individual. Diagnosis combines further skeletal traits; crest size or shape alone does not establish sex, food or a separate species.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## A short first metacarpal and terminal wing phalanx inform diagnosis

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The holotype’s first and second metacarpals do not reach the wrist and extend roughly one-third the length of metacarpal four; wing phalanx four is about one-fifth the length of the first. This differs from the longer first metacarpal and terminal wing phalanx in S. dongi, helping support a separate morphotype. Skeletal proportions do not reconstruct a complete wing membrane or directly determine maximum speed or flapping style.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Partial fusion supports a subadult interpretation

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
Posterior skull bones, scapula/coracoid and the first wing-phalanx extensor tendon process remain unfused, while the right tibiotarsus is fused and carpals appear to form proximal and distal fused units. Poor preservation prevents assessment of humeral epiphyses. The authors infer a subadult with an estimated wingspan near 1.56 m: relative maturity within the group rather than exact age, sex or maximum adult wingspan.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Skull-less D2525 is referred through limb combinations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
D2525 lacks its skull and parts of the vertebral column and was originally reported as S. dongi. The 2023 revision refers it to H. corollatus using its short first metacarpal, short fourth wing phalanx and metatarsal proportions. An approximately two-metre wingspan and extensive fusion support greater maturity. Skull-less material cannot independently confirm crest shape, referral does not combine it with the holotype into one complete skeleton, and its estimate is not a species maximum.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Compare the two older holotypes before the other morphotype

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
Compare damaged-humerus reassessment and more advanced fusion in [H. benxiensis](../Huaxiapterus_benxiensis/page.en.md), then anatomy, growth methods and crests in [Sinopterus dongi](../../Sinopterus/Sinopterus_dongi/page.en.md). Possible temporal coexistence does not establish shared localities or dietary partitioning. The original 2006 description, all newly reported specimens and dietary evidence remain incompletely read.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Which fossil records this name represents

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
Huaxiapterus corollatus is accepted in the fixed PBDB pterosaur working roster, with database parent Huaxiapterus. This guide uses 1 eligible species-level occurrence record from 1 collection and 1 reference identifier. Occurrences, collections and references are different units, not specimen or individual counts.

## Evidence limits

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
Existing sections guide PBDB occurrence records dated 2026-10-02, while the added account reads the 2023 revision; a complete monograph remains unfinished. Entries belong to the fixed 276-item pterosaur working roster, retain the current same name and a complete parent chain to the recorded Pterosauria storage anchor, and have usable species-level occurrences without uncertain-identification qualifiers. Database accepted names can connect older combinations; that database correspondence does not decide specimen identifications or all taxonomic disputes and is not merged with same-name COL concepts. Collection sites, stratigraphic intervals, depositional settings and collection-level preservation modes have distinct meanings. They do not directly establish complete range, origin or extinction time, fixed species ecology, biological abundance or the condition of every specimen. Original descriptions, revisions, morphology, diet and behavior remain incompletely assembled; external domain expert review has not occurred. The added account reuses the 2023 revision and previously read passages, with complete ZMNH M813, BXGM V0011 and D2525 specimen accounts additionally read here. Images, specimens, original 2006/2007 descriptions, other newly reported specimens, supplementary measurements, histology and diet remain incompletely read or independently checked. Referral and maturity are author interpretations; fixed parent chains, occurrence counts and ages remain intact. Complete monographs, external review and reader-facing delivery remain unfinished.
