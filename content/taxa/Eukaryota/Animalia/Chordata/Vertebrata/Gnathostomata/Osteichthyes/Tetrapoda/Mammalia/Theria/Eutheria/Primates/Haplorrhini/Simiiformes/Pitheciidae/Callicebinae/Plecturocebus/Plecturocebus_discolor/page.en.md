# Plecturocebus discolor


## A rejected historical type attribution

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
The 2025 Naturalis catalogue traces RMNH.MAM.39087, male material labeled from Nouvelle Granade and received from Verreaux in 1873. Jentink’s 1892 Callithrix ornata entry had called it a type of “Callithrix discolor Verreaux.” The authors found no literature publication of that Verreaux name, and the 1887 skeletal catalogue, Schlegel’s 1876 account and relevant accession lists did not support the supposed type attribution. They explicitly reject it as a type of Geoffroy Saint-Hilaire and Deville’s 1848 discolor. An old catalogue or label name does not establish this specimen as the name-bearing material for this page or a confirmed contemporary field voucher.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Dates and citation layers in Paris type reports

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
The 2025 Naturalis catalogue points to a Paris MNHN “holotype” by citing Rode’s 1938 account and gives Tabatinga, Brazil, for the original-description locality. The 2002 account below instead reports Paris type material absent at that time and gives Sarayacu. These are reports from different dates and citation chains. No fresh Paris inventory, original label or 1848 description was checked here, so they are not combined into one newly located specimen or a final ruling on type/locality interpretation. The Naturalis authors explicitly did not actively pursue all type series in other museums; their Paris reference retains that limit.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Type checking still needs a link to historical field groups

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
Rejecting the erroneous RMNH.MAM.39087 type attribution removes one unreliable naming link, but does not independently assign the historical Yasuní/Tiputini discolor groups to pinned COL26.8 concept 4K5Y5. The Naturalis collection catalogue published in June and the separate 2025 northwestern-titi taxonomic revision are different works; the catalogue does not replace a full reading of the latter. Keep name-bearing material, historical field observations and subsequent interpretations separate. Name/ecology correspondence remains unresolved.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## A historical type account is not a current inventory check

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The 2002 account reports that the holotype skin and skull collected in 1847 by Francis de Castelnau and Émile Deville were formerly in the Paris natural-history museum but no longer in its collection at the time. It gives Sarayacu on the left bank of the Ucayali in Peru as the type locality. This is a historical material report without a fresh inventory query or reading of the 1848 original; the older collection-status report is not a confirmed present-day inventory finding.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## The pale forehead and red extremities belong to the historical diagnosis

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The 2002 diagnosis describes monkeys then called discolor with a white or buffy forehead tuft, adjacent dark transverse band, reddish hands, feet and underparts, agouti back and paler distal tail. Figure 9 shows an adult female donated in 1988 with explicitly unknown locality. It is neither a type photograph nor a located wild distribution record. It illustrates historical pelage usage without establishing equivalence to the present COL ID or a concept revised in 2025.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## The mapped Brazilian extension was already unconfirmed

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The 2002 account assembles historical records from Peru, Ecuador and Colombia, explicitly noting that its mapped extension into the Brazilian Putumayo–Içá/Amazonas–Solimões interfluve still required confirmation. Locality lists, proposed extensions and river boundaries do not establish a wholly surveyed continuous range. With subsequent nomenclatural revision, this historical distribution concept is not converted into a current range map, absence evidence or conservation area for 4K5Y5.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Connect naming, material and local ecology as separate evidence routes

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
Compare the unknown type provenance of [P. stephennashi](../Plecturocebus_stephennashi/page.en.md), then return to the named Yasuní and Tiputini study groups below. Historical type reports, unlocated photographs and local behavioral data answer different questions. Shared use of discolor does not assemble a complete monograph for the current ID while concept correspondence remains unresolved. This historical-account addition leaves the first-round classification gap open.

## The 2025 naming revision and pinned accepted species

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
This page follows the fixed COL26.8 accepted-species ID 4K5Y5, Plecturocebus discolor. Taxonomic treatments differ: Vermeer et al. (2025) rename the white-browed titis formerly called P. discolor as P. leucometopus and treat discolor as a synonym of P. cupreus. MDD v2.4 records that change, and the current MDD v2.5 taxonomy-change list contains no replacement for it; its current taxon records retain P. discolor under P. cupreus. COL26.8 still accepts the ID used here, so the page documents the disagreement without treating names in different lists as interchangeable concepts.
<!-- /evo:text -->


## Single-group and local playback studies in Yasuní

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/text/en -->
A Yasuní National Park study published under the older combination Callicebus discolor followed one group from August 2003 to March 2004, totaling 260 observation hours across 168 days. It reported a home range of about 3.3 ha, 30 food-plant species and fruit-dominated feeding records. A separate 2010 Yasuní dataset compiled 68 loud-chorus playback responses and estimated 8.7 groups/km² locally (95% CI 6–12). Both sources are local and use the older taxonomic name; until the name conflict is resolved, these values are not treated as a full-range or population estimate for COL26.8 ID 4K5Y5.
<!-- /evo:text -->


## Read historical field names alongside museum revision

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/text/en -->
Read the Yasuní single-group study as a record of how the name was used in field literature, then compare it with the museum material and taxonomic discussion in Vermeer et al. (2025). The Yasuní measurements remain useful historical observations, but do not by themselves establish that they belong to the species concept currently accepted by COL26.8. This page makes no range-wide ecological or current conservation claim.
<!-- /evo:text -->


## Separate departure from feeding

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/10/text/en -->
The 2019 Tiputini study used Plecturocebus (formerly Callicebus) discolor. Its 2005–2016 multispecies observations principally followed titi groups B, K and L, with 2–6 members each. These are study-group sizes. A feeding bout required five cumulative monkey-minutes, rather than five minutes per individual.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Local morning timing and name limits

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/11/text/en -->
Recorded titi departures and first feeding bouts were never before sunrise. This local result depends on the observation window and event definition. The historical discolor usage does not settle the 2025 naming revision or establish assignment to the fixed COL concept; its classification gap remains open.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Evidence limits

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
English and Chinese display labels are editorial and do not imply MDD acceptance of this ID as a distinct species. The 2002 historical account and Figure 9 were read here; type inventory status retains the period of its report and the Brazilian mapped extension retains the authors’ unconfirmed status. The 1848 original, a fresh inventory audit and full 2025 revision have not been obtained in this pass. Historical field names are not forced onto current concepts. Taxonomic reconciliation for 4K5Y5 and the first-round gap remain incomplete, without a dossier.
New paragraphs use the 2025 Naturalis catalogue’s front matter, selected introduction/methods/nomenclatural discussion (pp. 3–4, 6–8) and titi accounts (pp. 49–50); page layouts 49–50 were viewed, without specimens, original labels, specimen photos or live collection portals. All 164 pages, nineteenth-century originals and other museums’ complete series were not reviewed, nor was the separate 2025 northwestern-titi revision freshly read in full. The catalogue uses its own taxonomic reference; publication-year and type-locality interpretations remain separate from pinned COL data. Rejected historical type attributions do not fill field-concept correspondence.
