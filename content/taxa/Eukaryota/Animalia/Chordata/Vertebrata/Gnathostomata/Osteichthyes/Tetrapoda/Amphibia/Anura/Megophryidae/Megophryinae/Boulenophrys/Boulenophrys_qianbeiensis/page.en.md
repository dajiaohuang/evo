## Accepted name and placement

COL26.8 accepts Boulenophrys qianbeiensis (Su, Shi, Wu, Li, Yao, Wang & Li, 2020) as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Megophryidae (family) → Boulenophrys (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## Northern Guizhou records and a corrected paratype locality
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10083232-v5gyx
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
Male holotype CIB TZ20190608017 came from Huanglian Nature Reserve, Tongzi, Guizhou, at about 1500 m. The monograph recognized Tongzi and Suiyang in the Dalou Mountains at 1500–1600 m and examined Taiyangshan material at about 1600 m. It explicitly described the original Kuankuoshui locality attribution for four named paratypes as a typographical error; those paratypes were listed with the holotype locality in this treatment. This is an attributed locality correction, not an independently re-collected type series.

## Wider spinata records remain proposed assignments
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10083232-v5gyx
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The distribution discussion proposed assigning historical B. spinata records from northwestern Guizhou, southeastern Sichuan and western Chongqing to B. qianbeiensis on biogeographic grounds. It explicitly required additional vouchers with molecular data. Those proposed assignments therefore remain separate from the recognized Tongzi and Suiyang records and are not presented as newly genetically verified occurrences.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Four examined adults with conical male spines
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10083232-v5gyx
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The diagnosis reported three adult males at 50.5–53.7 mm snout–vent length and one female at 65.2 mm. It described vomerine ridges and teeth, rough skin with dense spiny granules, and one-third toe webbing with wide fringes. Breeding males had large, sparse black conical nuptial spines on the dorsal bases of fingers I and II and a subgular vocal sac. The female is one measurement, not a range, and these structures are not measurements of mating performance or swimming ability.

## A character cited in the gutu comparison
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12466624-v5gyx
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The 2025 gutu description lists Boulenophrys qianbeiensis as larger in body size than the gutu sample. This is an attributed comparator statement within that paper’s taxonomic scope, not a new measurement series for this species. The gutu adult sample spans 34.4–44.7 mm in seven males and 36.2–52.8 mm in eight females; those numbers belong to gutu, not to this comparator. The selected character does not establish population-wide frequency, complete diagnosis, diet or developmental biology.
