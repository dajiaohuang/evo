## Read the study

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
How was bite force measured for Melanosuchus niger in a standardized adult sample? This guide follows the accepted COL26.8 record 3ZBL4 and reads the study at its stated sample and geographic scale. In Erickson et al.'s 2012 comparison, 3 sexually mature black caimans were tested, with a mean sample body mass of about 59 kg. At the prominent molariform tooth near the back of the upper jaw, the study reported a taxon-representative bite force of 2,696 N; the sampled values ranged from 1,779–4,310 N. Researchers used the highest of 3–5 bites per animal. Across species, body mass was the main predictor of bite force, while rostral proportion explained little additional variation. This is a standardized measurement from a limited institutional sample, not a field-performance constant or population range.

Continue with Caiman crocodilus (COL PBBP) to compare the same experimental measure while keeping sample size and body mass in view. The Crocodylia group guide provides the wider reading route; the neighboring page retains its own sources and limits.

## How to read reproductive records

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
the reproductive-mode field uses the original label oviparous; consolidated mean offspring or eggs per litter/clutch 39.3; lowest reported clutch/litter mean 30; highest reported clutch/litter mean 65; egg length 67.71 mm; egg width 39.78 mm. Clutch/litter summaries can use midpoints of reported means or, when means are unavailable, extrema; they are not necessarily arithmetic averages of observed clutches. Fractions do not describe non-integer young in a litter. The lowest/highest fields are defined as endpoints of reported means rather than guaranteed smallest/largest individual clutches. Reproductive and sex-determination labels do not establish fetal nutrition or uniformity across all populations.

## Limits of these trait data

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
This account paraphrases the same-name ReptTraits v1-2 entry, retaining its 2023 taxonomic framework and sources for individual traits. One row per species compresses variation in sex, age, locality and populations; matching name and family do not ensure equivalent later concepts. Reported maximum length is not an absolute species limit, and reproductive means are not fixed counts in every clutch. Many mass entries are estimated from length equations and are not presented here as measured mass. Juvenile-length headers and definitions differ and are also omitted from reader conclusions. Conservation, venom, current distribution and population trends are not updated here; unrecorded fields remain gaps. Dataset citation labels preserve the source chain and do not imply that this project independently reread every original work.
