# Spatula hottentota

## Historical Anas combination and the pinned name

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
This page follows pinned COL26.8 Spatula hottentota (9HP5T). AVONET v7 BirdLife 2020 row 399 and the eBird 2021 crosswalk use the same name and Avibase ID AVIBASE-FA831F65; BirdTree gives Anas hottentota, labelled 1BL to 1BT. Pinned COL synonym DGNT points to accepted ID 9HP5T. Name links do not verify every species boundary across dates.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Beak, wing and compiled mass have different denominators

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
Morphology covers 5 birds (2 female and 3 male), with 4 complete measurements. Mean nostril-to-tip beak length is 27.1 mm, beak width at the nostrils 12.7 mm, unflattened wing 147 mm and hand-wing index 42.4. These are species averages; the index is defined by Kipp’s distance/wing length ×100, not flight speed. Mass 276.36 g comes separately from Dunning’s literature compilation and cannot inherit the morphology sample size. Inference=NO retains the workbook’s non-inference flag without supplying individual bounds from unread raw material.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Read feeding niche and trophic level separately

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
Habitat is Wetland and density code 3 means open habitats. Trophic.Niche=Omnivore denotes multiple niches without one dominant category; Trophic.Level=Herbivore denotes a plant-oriented trophic level. The columns have different definitions. Original labels are retained without rewriting either from the other or deriving seasonal diet percentages. Generalist means no single dominant lifestyle; it supplies no proportions for diving, walking ashore or aerial activity.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Movement category and the observations still needed

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
Migration=1 is a sedentary category. It does not establish that every individual remains at its birthplace for life or supply distances for local seasonal movements. Hand-wing index and habitat labels cannot replace behavioural observations. Locality, timing and explicit sources are needed to connect the category to particular populations’ life histories.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Evidence limits

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
Scope is the selected historical AVONET v7 rows, relevant field definitions, two name crosswalks and local COL accepted/synonym records. The whole workbook, individual raw measurements, Dunning’s book and a new full methods-paper reading are excluded. Morphology counts are not literature-mass denominators, and ecological categories are not seasonal diet surveys. Plumage identification, breeding, lifespan, current range and conservation status need other sources; no expert review or complete monograph is claimed.
