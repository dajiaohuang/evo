## Species-specific call structure in the 2021 comparison
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7936570-78ss4
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The article’s ochlandrae species row describes a non-pulsatile call with duration 25.3 ms and dominant frequency 2.7 kHz. Calls are rapidly delivered in long groups. Similar dominant frequency to chalazodes does not imply identical temporal properties. These are source summary values, not a complete range or a universal constant. Type numbers label the study’s call ordering or frequency of delivery, rather than independently demonstrated functions such as alarm or aggression. Temperature, social context and within- and among-individual variation were not fully accounted for in these summaries. Call similarity or difference alone does not establish reproductive isolation or population decline.

## Bamboo-clade skulls in a single-male comparative sample
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10480065-78ss4
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
The 2023 micro-CT study used one adult male per fifty-five available bush-frog lineages/species and thirty-six three-dimensional skull landmarks. It describes reed-bamboo frogs, including Raorchestes ochlandrae, as having relatively broader and dorsoventrally flattened skulls, with posteriorly shifted jaw joints and changes in several bones. These are comparative group patterns, not measured population frequencies, female morphology or a developmental series for this species. Preservation, segmentation and single-individual sampling remain part of the evidence scope.

## A clade-specific history and model-dependent rate shift
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10480065-78ss4
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
The discussion assigns Raorchestes ochlandrae to lineage A of the Ochlandrae clade. Lineage A contains chalazodes, flaviocularis and ochlandrae; the former two are described as narrowly distributed high-elevation sisters, whereas ochlandrae has a broader low-to-mid-elevation range. These are cited historical relationships, not new molecular dating or gene-flow tests. The comparative rate-shift result was recovered in ninety-six percent of one hundred randomized-tree iterations; that statistic is not a ninety-six-percent causal probability for bamboo adaptation.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Bamboo breeding context is cited rather than newly observed here
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10480065-78ss4
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/text/en -->
The paper cites bamboo-internode breeding and egg guarding as life-history context for this clade. Its new data are preserved skull scans, not new observations of egg numbers, care duration or breeding success for this species. A flattened skull could aid passage through narrow openings or blocking intruders, but these proposed functions were not experimentally tested. After phylogenetic correction, the habitat–shape association was not significant (p=0.365), despite a small size–shape association (p=0.021).
