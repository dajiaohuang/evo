## Ecology in the study
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
After release of 18 captive-bred axolotls into two aquatic sites in southern Mexico City, VHF telemetry produced total MCP home-range estimates averaging 2,747 m² for the eight tracked at the artificial La Cantera Oriente wetland and 382 m² for the ten tracked at a restored chinampa in Lake Xochimilco; the difference was statistically significant (p < 0.001).

Study scope: Two aquatic sites in southern Mexico City. Eight animals at La Cantera Oriente were tracked from 2017-10-27 to 2017-12-07; ten animals at a restored Lake Xochimilco chinampa were released on 2018-03-12 and monitored through 2018-04-20. VHF telemetry; sites and seasons differed.

Study material: All 18 study animals were born and raised in captivity before release into the study wetlands. The short post-release monitoring does not establish wild-born ecology, long-term establishment, or a self-sustaining population.

## Evolutionary evidence in the study
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
The authors reported that the assembled axolotl genome lacks Pax3, and that mutation of its Pax7 paralogue produced a phenotype similar to Pax3- or Pax7-mutant mice; the genomic sample came from one three-year-old d/d adult male.

Study scope: A single laboratory-bred d/d male supplied genomic DNA; the 2018 article presents comparative genomic and gene-editing results, not a population survey.

Study material: The source concerns laboratory material and experimental mutants; it does not establish the frequency of these genomic traits across wild populations.

## Outdoor mesocosms supplied the axolotl microbiome samples
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/10/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10571319-cq4m
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/10/text/en -->
Twenty-two newly generated Ambystoma mexicanum samples in the 2023 analysis came from simulated outdoor environments, described as mesocosms in the former native range at Xochimilco Lake. The systems experienced the local climate and used lake-sourced water. The authors therefore retained these samples in all analyses, including mixed models and Mantel tests. This analytical inclusion does not turn managed mesocosm animals into independently observed wild populations or demonstrate a present natural distribution. The 22 samples are not a certified census of unique animals, and these observations do not establish native breeding success or microbiome-mediated protection against disease.

## Sampling environment and climatic grids have different scopes
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/11/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10571319-cq4m
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/11/text/en -->
The study defined sampling habitat by the location and time of collection, explicitly distinguishing it from species life history. Its climatic covariates came from gridded WorldClim data: long-term averages covered 1970–2000, while monthly series covered 1960–2018. For 193 pooled samples collected in 2019–2021 the authors substituted the latest available 2018 climate values. This is a dataset-wide limitation, not evidence that all axolotl samples belonged to that subset. Gridded climate and lake-sourced mesocosm water do not provide measured individual body temperatures, aquatic thermal limits, water-quality thresholds or current habitat conditions.
