## behaviour

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
On Siberut, Tenaza compared songs, choruses and countersinging among 13 family groups whose membership and territories were known, together with other individuals. The paper describes differences associated with sex, age, season, time of day and social or spatial context. Tenaza proposed interterritorial communication and mate attraction as song functions and discussed chorusing as a possible way to reduce predation risk; these remain interpretations from the studied population.

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
The evidence is a 1976 study of known groups on Siberut; proposed song functions are interpretations and do not make this a complete species account. This profile is not externally expert-reviewed.
