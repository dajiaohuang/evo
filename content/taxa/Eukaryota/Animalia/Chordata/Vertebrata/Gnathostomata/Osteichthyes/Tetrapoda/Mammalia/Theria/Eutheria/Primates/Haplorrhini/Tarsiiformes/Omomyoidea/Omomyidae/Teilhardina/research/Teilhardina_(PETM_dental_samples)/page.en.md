# Teilhardina (PETM dental samples)

## atlas-profile / overview

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/overview -->
Separately catalogued Teilhardina dental samples from three correlated PETM sections document a short Asia-to-Europe-to-North-America appearance sequence, not one tracked population or an ancestor chain.

## atlas-profile / evidenceSummary

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/evidenceSummary -->
Smith, Rose and Gingerich document the samples, section correlations and dental comparisons on pp. 11223–11227 (Figures 1–3 and Supporting Tables). Species assignments and correlation underpin the sequence, so it is not a direct record of migration, crown membership or a global first appearance.

## Teeth from three distant regions
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/0/text/en -->
The paper compares small Teilhardina dental samples from the Hengyang Basin in China, Dormaal in Belgium and the Bighorn Basin in Wyoming. The fossils come from separate sections rather than one migrating group. Their identification and the correlation of the rock layers are essential parts of the argument, so the geographic sequence is a reconstruction from several local records.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## A rapid appearance sequence around the PETM
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/1/text/en -->
Smith, Rose and Gingerich compared the teeth and dated sections against the Paleocene–Eocene Thermal Maximum, a major global warming event near 56 million years ago. Their correlations place sampled Teilhardina records first in Asia, then Europe and North America over a short interval. The sequence documents where the sampled fossils occur in correlated strata; it does not follow a single population through time.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Dispersal is an interpretation of the sequence
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/2/text/en -->
The authors interpret the ordered records as evidence of rapid geographic dispersal, but the route is not a direct track of animals crossing continents. Species assignments, fossil sampling and age correlation all contribute uncertainty. These dental samples do not preserve locomotion, habitat use or a complete body, and their first sampled appearances should not be mistaken for a global origin date.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## atlas-profile / readerLimitations

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerLimitations/en -->
This is a guide to a small, section-based dental sample and its correlation model. It is not a continuous migration record, a complete species ecology, or a direct global first-occurrence date.
