## A dated multicriteria conservation priority
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10968267-7bpt4
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The 2024 Chilean assessment assigns Telmatobius fronteriensis a printed conservation-priority index of 2.50 and places it in the high category in Table 4. The study reviews 58 selected species using literature, citizen-science records and government plans, rather than newly censusing every population. Its index combines distribution, threat, population trend, taxonomic uniqueness and body-size scores with a protected-area term. The value is a management ranking under this model, not an extinction probability, a current IUCN assessment or a direct measurement of population size. “Non-priority” and “low” do not mean biologically safe or no need for conservation.

## The population and protection scores retain their source limits

Table 3 gives Telmatobius fronteriensis a population-trend code of 4 (recent decline) and a land-protection code of 1 (the entire population outside protected areas), using the definitions in Table 2. These are the authors’ ordinal assignments, not newly verified census trajectories or measured protected population fractions. A zero denotes unavailable evidence, not a stable population or zero risk. Threat code 3 is also a grouped source category and cannot identify a specific official status by itself. AOO and EOO scores classify area criteria rather than supplying independently reconstructed occupancy maps; protection coverage does not establish effective management.

## The proposed protection actions are recommendations, not completed recovery
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10968267-7bpt4
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
The Discussion places Telmatobius fronteriensis among high-priority desert Telmatobius associated with restricted distribution, threats and limited territorial protection. It proposes consideration of Alto Loa reserve expansion, water protection and restoration of mining-damaged habitat. These are management recommendations based on the source assessment, not evidence that boundaries were expanded, threats removed or a population recovered. The underlying occurrence records and current reserve coverage were not independently reconstructed here.
