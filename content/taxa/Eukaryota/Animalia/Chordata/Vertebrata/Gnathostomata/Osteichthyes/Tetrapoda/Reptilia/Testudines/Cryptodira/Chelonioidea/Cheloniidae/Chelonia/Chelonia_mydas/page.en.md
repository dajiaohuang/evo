## A minimum count at Rose Atoll

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/topic -->
lifeHistory
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
Rapid nesting surveys at Rose Atoll National Wildlife Refuge in American Samoa from 2012 through 2019 recorded a total of 218 female green turtles; permanent tags confirmed a minimum of 138 distinct nesting females. The two numbers answer different counting questions, and 138 is a minimum rather than a complete count. Annual survey effort averaged 7.6 days, so some nesters could be missed. Read this as evidence from free-living nesting females at one rookery over eight seasons; it does not establish global abundance, a population trend or lifetime reproductive output.

## What a local grazing experiment measured

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/topic -->
ecology
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
At a Thalassia testudinum meadow in Little Cayman, Cayman Islands, a 2016 experiment measured five clipped and five reference plots weekly for 12 weeks. Clipping simulated green-turtle grazing. Mean net ecosystem production (NEP) in clipped plots was 79% lower than in reference plots, but the 12-week mean stayed positive; clipped plots had NEP below zero on overcast sampling days in weeks one and nine. Nearby areas continuously grazed by juvenile green turtles for at least a year had mean NEP 92% lower than adjacent ungrazed areas. These are local measurements of ecosystem metabolism. The clipped plots were not a turtle-population experiment, and neither comparison directly measured sediment carbon stocks or establishes a species-wide effect.

## Different stages in the mass fields

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/10/text/en -->
Historical summary values are adult body mass 160000 g; mass at birth or hatching 23.9 g. Adult mass can combine males, females and adults of unspecified sex; birth/hatching mass describes a different life stage. These are not a longitudinal series of the same individuals or a species-wide range of individual masses.

## Distinguish the measured length

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
Recorded dimensions are reported maximum straight carapace length (SCL) 1500 mm. SCL measures the shell in a straight line, excluding head, neck and tail. Compiled maxima are reported material, not theoretical growth limits or typical individual size. Sex-specific values may come from different localities or studies and alone cannot quantify population sexual dimorphism.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Where and how it is active

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
In the entry, microhabitat is Marine; diet is Omnivorous; active timing is Cathemeral; foraging mode is ACT. These categories summarize activity and resource use rather than an exhaustive habitat list or fixed schedule. Positions such as arboreal and terrestrial can be combined. A carnivorous category does not specify prey, and populations, seasons and life stages may differ.

## How to read reproductive records

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
the reproductive-mode field uses the original label oviparous; sex determination is recorded as TSD; consolidated mean offspring or eggs per litter/clutch 112.5; lowest reported clutch/litter mean 85; highest reported clutch/litter mean 200; egg length 55 mm; egg width 45 mm. Clutch/litter summaries can use midpoints of reported means or, when means are unavailable, extrema; they are not necessarily arithmetic averages of observed clutches. Fractions do not describe non-integer young in a litter. The lowest/highest fields are defined as endpoints of reported means rather than guaranteed smallest/largest individual clutches. Reproductive and sex-determination labels do not establish fetal nutrition or uniformity across all populations.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Limits of these trait data

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/text/en -->
This account paraphrases the same-name ReptTraits v1-2 entry, retaining its 2023 taxonomic framework and sources for individual traits. One row per species compresses variation in sex, age, locality and populations; matching name and family do not ensure equivalent later concepts. Reported maximum length is not an absolute species limit, and reproductive means are not fixed counts in every clutch. Many mass entries are estimated from length equations and are not presented here as measured mass. Juvenile-length headers and definitions differ and are also omitted from reader conclusions. Conservation, venom, current distribution and population trends are not updated here; unrecorded fields remain gaps. Dataset citation labels preserve the source chain and do not imply that this project independently reread every original work.
