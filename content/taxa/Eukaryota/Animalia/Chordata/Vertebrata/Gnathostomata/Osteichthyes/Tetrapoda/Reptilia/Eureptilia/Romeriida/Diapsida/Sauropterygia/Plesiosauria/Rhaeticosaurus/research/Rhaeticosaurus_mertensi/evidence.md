# Rhaeticosaurus mertensi

## claims / statement

<!-- evo:text /records/claims/0/statement -->
Rhaeticosaurus is anchored by holotype LWL-MFN P 64047 from a specific Rhaetian Exter Formation bed at Bonenburg; this secure specimen does not establish an exact global plesiosaur or genus origin.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## claims / confidenceRationale

<!-- evo:text /records/claims/0/confidenceRationale -->
The holotype and bed-level provenance are directly documented. High confidence is limited to the named sample, while phylogenetic implications remain separate.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## claims / statement

<!-- evo:text /records/claims/1/statement -->
Rhaeticosaurus mertensi is a new small-bodied plesiosaurian recovered within Plesiosauria as a basal Pliosauridae member in the published 35-taxon, 270-character analysis; that placement is matrix-dependent rather than direct ancestry.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## claims / confidenceRationale

<!-- evo:text /records/claims/1/confidenceRationale -->
The paper directly defines the new taxon and reports the matrix result, but the topology is conditional on taxon sampling and characters; it is not presented as observed lineage descent.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## claims / statement

<!-- evo:text /records/claims/2/statement -->
The Rhaeticosaurus holotype comes from clay pit #3 of the Lücking brick company, 1 km north of Bonenburg in Warburg, North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany, in Rhaetian dark marine mudstones of the Exter Formation; this is a locality record, not a complete distribution.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## claims / statement

<!-- evo:text /records/claims/3/statement -->
Holotype LWL-MFN P 64047 is a partial articulated subadult skeleton preserving the occiput, lower jaw, vertebral column, girdles and left limbs; its diagnosis includes ventrally concave V-shaped cervical neurocentral sutures and foreshortened zeugopodials.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## claims / confidenceRationale

<!-- evo:text /records/claims/3/confidenceRationale -->
The holotype inventory and diagnostic proportions are directly described and illustrated. The confidence applies to the specimen-level anatomy and does not imply an invariant condition across Plesiosauria.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## claims / statement

<!-- evo:text /records/claims/4/statement -->
Rhaeticosaurus preserves four similar pointed flippers and a short tail consistent with plesiosaurian hydrofoil and paraxial-locomotion comparisons, while histological growth and physiological implications remain interpretations rather than directly measured behaviour or metabolism.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## claims / confidenceRationale

<!-- evo:text /records/claims/4/confidenceRationale -->
The paper reports the limb and tail anatomy and compares it with hydrofoil function, while the histology discussion interprets vascularized bone. The claim separates mechanical and physiological inference from direct preservation.
<!-- /evo:text -->
