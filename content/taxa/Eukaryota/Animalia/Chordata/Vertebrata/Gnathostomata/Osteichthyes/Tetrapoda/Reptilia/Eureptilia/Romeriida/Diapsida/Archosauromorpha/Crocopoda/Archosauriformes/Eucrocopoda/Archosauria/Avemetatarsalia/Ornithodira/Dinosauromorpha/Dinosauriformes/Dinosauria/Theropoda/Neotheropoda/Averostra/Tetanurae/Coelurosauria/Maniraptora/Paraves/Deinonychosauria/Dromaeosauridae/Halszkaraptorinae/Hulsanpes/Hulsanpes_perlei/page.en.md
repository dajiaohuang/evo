# Hulsanpes perlei


## Start with the holotype’s preserved material

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
Holotype ZPAL MgD-I/173 was found at Khulsan, Mongolia, in 1970 in the Baruungoyot Formation and named in 1982. Cau and Madzia’s 2018 examination redescribes articulated right metatarsals II–IV with part of the first third-toe phalanx, the first second-toe phalanx and the proximal part of the next, and a small braincase fragment. The skull fragment shares the catalogue number and size class; following the original account, the authors refer all elements to one individual. They do not form a complete skull, leg or body.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Interpreting a braincase fragment about a centimetre long

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
The roughly 10-mm braincase fragment remains partly embedded in sandstone, with sutural contacts and some margins lost. Comparisons with other maniraptorans underpin an interpretation as the inner surface of the right otic region. A deep recess may house the cerebellar flocculus; openings, fossae and grooves may correspond to the inner-ear labyrinth and semicircular canals. Several identifications remain tentative. Specimen photographs and microscope close-ups do not constitute a complete scanned inner-ear reconstruction or measurements of hearing, balance or swimming behavior.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Why metatarsal overlap matters to the diagnosis

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
A medial flange of metatarsal III extensively overlaps the distal shaft of metatarsal II, reducing its exposed area in extensor view. The authors see no deformation at the contact and interpret the overlap as anatomy rather than a preservational artifact, using its extent in the diagnosis. An unconstricted proximal third metatarsal is another part of the character combination. All three proximal ends are eroded and the distal second metatarsal is missing. Preserved and estimated features require separate treatment. Second-toe articulations support hyperextension, but the terminal claw is not preserved and cannot supply a measured sickle-claw size.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## A small specimen and the limits of immaturity inference

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
Table 1 gives metatarsal II as 34 mm and estimated lengths of about 39 and 36 mm for III and IV, marked with asterisks. Foot elements are about 75–80% of their homologues in the Halszkaraptor holotype. Together with rough external bone texture, this supports the authors’ interpretation of an immature individual. It does not measure age or transfer a relative’s age to this specimen. Cross-species bone proportions do not establish adult mass or final body length, and the associated fragments are not a growth series.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Missing characters constrain the relationship hypothesis

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The updated 2018 matrix places Hulsanpes sister to Mahakala, together with Halszkaraptor in Halszkaraptorinae. Most listed family and subfamily diagnostic features are unknown in this specimen; available evidence is concentrated in metatarsals and toe bones. Forcing an avialan position costs four extra parsimony steps, leading the authors to provisionally reject that placement. These are reported results, not a reanalysis here or a statistical probability of placement. The fixed PBDB path organizes pages without replacing additional material or later classification studies.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Foot function is not a measured running performance

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The authors interpret metatarsal flanges in Hulsanpes and Mahakala as reducing relative torsion and dislocation, proposing some cursorial adaptation. Ecological contrasts with Halszkaraptor remain anatomical hypotheses without measured speed or activity observations. The paper also discusses published Mahakala lengths of 82 versus 52 mm, adopting 52 mm and interpreting 82 mm as a typographical error. That is its assessment, without remeasurement of the specimen here. Braincase and foot remains alone do not determine swimming, year-round habitat or diet.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Evidence limits

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
This page combines fixed occurrences with a 2018 redescription of one fragmentary holotype. Braincase identifications remain partly tentative and foot bones cover limited anatomy. Estimated lengths, immaturity, affinities and cursorial adaptation have separate scopes, without establishing adult size, exact age, speed or whole-species ecology. The full 1982 paper and all later material are not assembled, and neither matrices nor disputed bone lengths were independently reanalyzed. Database ages and depositional labels are not death dates or direct habitat records. This is not a complete monograph.
