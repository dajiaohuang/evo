## Southern Sichuan Basin localities
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10083232-lx9m9
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
Male holotype FMNH 49406 came from Mount Omei, now written Emei, Sichuan. Examined adults came from Emei, Wawu, Laojun, Zihuai, Simian and Chishui, including Chishui at approximately 580 m and Emei at approximately 1600 m. The wider summary covered hilly areas of southeastern Sichuan, southwestern Chongqing, northwestern Guizhou and northeastern Yunnan at 400–1600 m. Those examined sites and the wider cited account remain distinct rather than proving continuous distribution.

## Seventeen examined adults
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10083232-lx9m9
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The diagnosis reported ten adult males at 52.1–62.0 mm snout–vent length and seven females at 65.7–71.2 mm. It described vomerine ridges and teeth, rudimentary basal toe webbing with narrow lateral fringes, and dense black nuptial spines on the dorsal bases of fingers I and II in breeding males, with a subgular vocal sac. These are measurements and anatomical descriptions of the examined sample, not universal adult size limits or measurements of the separately listed historical types.

The 2025 paper includes source-labelled Megophrys omeimontis in its Tangjiahe National Nature Reserve inventory in northern Sichuan, China. The total list contains 25 species, with 20 from field surveys and five from literature. This page does not assign every table row to a newly captured 2024 animal without the supplementary species-by-species evidence. ## Regional gradients and survey gains do not certify recovery
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12189923-lx9m9
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/text/en -->
The reserve description spans 1,150–3,830 m, the sampling Methods report 800–2,600 m, and statistical Methods describe observed amphibians at 800–2,500 m. These scopes are retained rather than assigned as this species’ measured range. Richness peaks in two regional bands but its linear elevation association is nonsignificant (reported R² 0.114, p 0.412). Historical recorded-species increase is also nonsignificant (Mann–Kendall p 0.148). Neither result establishes unchanged populations, increased abundance or a causal benefit of management. Discussion and Conclusions use stronger fragmentation, conservation-success and large-population language than these tests alone support; raw records and historical sampling comparability have not been independently reanalysed.

## A historical record without detection during this field survey
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/10/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12189923-lx9m9
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/10/text/en -->
Discussion states that Megophrys omeimontis, known from earlier surveys, was absent during the current field study, although Table 1 retains its habitat assignment in the combined inventory. A literature-supported checklist entry is therefore not automatically a 2024 field detection. Nondetection does not prove local extinction; population variation, behavior and temporary habitat changes are proposed explanations, not independently measured causes. Seasonal repeated monitoring is recommended rather than a certified recovery or disappearance.
