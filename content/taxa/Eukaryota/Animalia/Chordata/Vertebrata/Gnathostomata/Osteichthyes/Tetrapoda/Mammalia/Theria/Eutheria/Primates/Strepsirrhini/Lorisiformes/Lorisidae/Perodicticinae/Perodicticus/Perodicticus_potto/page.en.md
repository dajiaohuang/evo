## fieldStudy

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
In January 2009, Bearder and Oates made 11 nocturnal transect walks totalling 16 hours at five sites in Nigeria’s Okomu National Park and Forest Reserve. The report recorded five sightings as Perodicticus juju; first-sighting height averaged 13.6 m (range 9–19 m), and sightings occurred more often in mature forest and higher in the canopy than the galagos observed there. The authors did not convert five sightings into a density estimate.

## historicalEcology

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
Oates (1984) compared new field observations of West African pottos with earlier diet literature. He proposed careful searching along canopy branches, with gums and slow-moving invertebrates as important foods and gums potentially more important when fruit is scarce. The paper predates the current three-species split, and its abstract does not provide enough locality or sample detail to reconstruct the observations; it is retained as a historical regional interpretation, not a quantitative diet for the current species.

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
The Okomu record comprises five sightings over 16 hours and uses the name P. juju; MDD and IUCN do not present the name at exactly the same rank. The 1984 diet discussion also predates the current species split and has limited sample detail in its abstract. Neither source represents abundance, diet or trend across the full range of current P. potto. The COL–MDD authority-year difference remains unresolved. The Chinese name is editorial; the page has not received external expert review. This page is not externally expert-reviewed.
