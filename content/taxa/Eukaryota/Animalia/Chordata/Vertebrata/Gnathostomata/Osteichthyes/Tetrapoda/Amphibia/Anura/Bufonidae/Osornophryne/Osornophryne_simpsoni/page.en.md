## Two reserve areas reported in the original description

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc3119318-6tbtk

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The 2011 description reported this toad from the type locality and surrounding areas in Reserva Zuñac and Reserve Ankaku-Zona. It placed the sites in montane cloud forest dominated by Clusia trees. These are the description’s locality records, rather than a verified complete present-day range or proof of continuous occupancy between reserves.

## Three females and one juvenile in the Machay sample
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc8763828-6tbtk
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
The 2022 account recorded adult females DHMECN 14412–14414 with reported body sizes of 26.58–33.57 mm and juvenile DHMECN 14415 at 12.38 mm. It described Toes IV and V as longer than I–III, a short rounded snout with a small rostral papilla and conical flank pustules. The juvenile is separate from the adult interval, and this local female sample does not establish a universal size range or sexual dimorphism.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Night perches and a dated third-locality report
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc8763828-6tbtk
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/text/en -->
The adult females were collected at night on bromeliad, palm and Neurolepis leaves 40–170 cm above ground; the juvenile was in a bromeliad at 20 cm. The Machay montane forest records were at 2430–2490 m, with low trees, bryophytes and bromeliads. The authors called Machay the third Ecuadorian locality relative to earlier knowledge. Under handling they observed slow escape movements and falling in a ball-like position. These records do not establish exclusive perch use, a complete modern range or effectiveness of the handling response against natural predators.
