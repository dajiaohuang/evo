## Accepted name and placement

The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## Fourteen types have separate sex and measurement scopes
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc11453160-v5hx4
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The type series comprises a female holotype, five female paratypes and eight male paratypes. The diagnosis reports male snout–vent length 13.8–15.1 mm (n=8) and female length 17.3–20.3 mm (n=6). The holotype is measured more precisely as 20.34 mm. That rounding is retained rather than described as a new size conflict. These dimensions are type-series observations, not population extrema; the paper’s nineteen collected animals and further released encounters are different denominators.

## Variable live patterns change after preservation
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc11453160-v5hx4
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
Living backs range from light brown through grey-brown to dark brown, with inverted-V or irregular darker markings; a faint pale mid-dorsal stripe may occur. The holotype has a dark lateral band, yellow-to-orange groin and yellow underside with white and brown dots. Ethanol descriptions separately record paler cream ventral areas. Some preserved tubercles and folds become less apparent. Neither one colour nor the absence of a conspicuous preserved tubercle is a complete identification rule.

## One cleared male supports the skeletal description
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc11453160-v5hx4
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The osteological description used adult male ZSFQ 1886. It reports finger phalangeal counts 2-2-3-3, toe counts 2-2-3-4-3, eight presacral vertebrae and terminal phalangeal knobs. The diagnosis describes strongly T-shaped terminal phalanges. Cartilage structures were omitted from the osteological description, and the poorly ossified prepollex was difficult to see. One prepared male does not establish frequencies of skeletal variation, female anatomy or a complete developmental sequence.

## Leaf-litter activity has local observation limits
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc11453160-v5hx4
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
The account records Río Blanco and nearby sites at 1720–1890 m in humid montane evergreen forest. Most animals were active at night on the forest floor; three were found in morning leaf litter and four paratypes in pitfall traps. These observations do not yield a complete daily activity budget. No calls were heard during expeditions even though the diagnostic males have vocal slits and an external sac; nondetection does not prove silence.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## A single stomach documents thirteen prey items
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc11453160-v5hx4
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
The stomach of male ZSFQ 1886 contained three ant-mimicking rove beetles classified as Staphylinidae: Pselaphinae, two aquatic fly larvae labelled Diptera: Nematocera, and eight ants classified as Formicidae: Myrmicinae. The thirteen items describe one stomach, not a species-wide fixed diet, preference for ants or feeding rate. Their presence does not establish where each prey item was captured or how prey availability varies among seasons.

## Long 16S inference remains a single-marker analysis
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc11453160-v5hx4
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/text/en -->
The final phylogenetic matrix was 1104 base pairs for eighty-two new and reference individuals. Although the molecular workflow amplified adjoining mitochondrial genes, the relationship analysis and discussion rely on 16S alone. The lineage was placed in the northern clade with references labelled myrmecoides and an unnamed San Martín lineage. Distances of 6.02–10.53% in the focal comparison are not universal species thresholds, divergence dates or nuclear-genome evidence; the paper calls for more markers.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## A dated risk proposal separates occupancy from range extent
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc11453160-v5hx4
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/text/en -->
The authors propose Critically Endangered under B1ab(iii)+2ab(iii), reporting extent of occurrence 1.70 square kilometres and area of occupancy 8 square kilometres. They treat nearby records as one threat-defined locality and infer habitat decline from mining-related impacts. These are distinct assessment quantities and an attributed 2024 proposal, not a freshly verified official IUCN category or measured population trend. Qualitative local abundance and nineteen collected animals do not certify total population size.
