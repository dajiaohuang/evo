## Natural glades in Harenna Forest
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7973069-qfb7m
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The study reported frogs in Ethiopian Harenna Forest at 1400–2300 m, especially natural glades temporarily flooded by seasonal rain. It described ground-dwelling and partly burrowing habits, with dry-season nocturnal observations. Breeding-site use included standing or slow water. Proposed dependence on particular glade vegetation and hydrology remained the authors’ explanation of their observations.

## Co-occurring life stages and a breeding hypothesis
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7973069-qfb7m
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The researchers found an egg-bearing female and individuals near the end of metamorphosis on the same day at the same site, and reported June metamorphs. They inferred that reproduction might occur more than once a year. Co-occurrence of stages does not establish repeated breeding by an individual female or a complete annual reproductive schedule.
