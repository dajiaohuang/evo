## Read the study

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
How did molecular and historical-specimen evidence help distinguish an African crocodile lineage? This guide follows the accepted COL26.8 record ZKNT and reads the study at its stated sample and geographic scale. Comparisons of mitochondrial and nuclear markers and karyotypes from African crocodile samples and Egyptian mummies supported a cryptic lineage corresponding to the West African crocodile, Crocodylus suchus, within the Nile crocodile complex. Mummy haplotypes also indicate that two Crocodylus lineages occurred historically along the Nile. These are phylogenetic and historical-specimen findings, not a current range boundary or abundance estimate.

Continue with Crocodylus niloticus (COL ZKNK) to keep lineage identification separate from a local ecological study under a neighboring accepted name. The Crocodylia group guide provides the wider reading route; the neighboring page retains its own sources and limits.
