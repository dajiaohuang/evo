# Macaca radiata

## atlas-profile / overview

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/overview -->

## atlas-profile / evidenceSummary

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/evidenceSummary -->
Selected evidence includes a 2017 southern India survey and one 2018 interspecific craniodental comparison with 79 bonnet-macaque specimens. The latter does not establish within-species diagnostic variation; life history, species-range limits and current formal conservation status remain unassessed, and this profile has not received independent expert review.

## A forest occupancy estimate at Parambikulam
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/1/text/en -->
In one survey of the Parambikulam landscape in Kerala, observers detected 54 groups in 33 of 64 grid cells. The fitted mean cell-occupancy estimate was 0.51 ± 0.08 (SE), with detection probability 0.25 ± 0.05. These estimates belong to that forest survey and its sampling design.

## Roadside counts connected to Mysore
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/2/text/en -->
The study’s roadside counts fell from 889 in 2003 to 407 in 2015 on roads connected to Mysore. The 2015 resurvey covered 464 km and used a 15-m strip on each side of the road centreline. A roadside count measures animals visible within a defined survey band; the change is not a census of the species across southern India.

## Temple and tourist-site histories
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/3/text/en -->
The same paper compared selected southern Indian temple and tourist locations over historical records and follow-up surveys from 2009–2015. About 31% of assessed locations recorded bonnet macaques; among reported translocation cases, roughly one fifth were followed by reappearance, while more than 48% were recorded as disappearance or removal. These site histories do not describe present-day occupancy across the full range.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Bonnet macaques in a museum comparison
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/4/text/en -->
A 2018 comparison covered 711 museum specimens from 12 macaque species, including 79 M. radiata specimens (46 male and 33 female) attributed to southern and western India; subadults were also included. The paper reported a weak interspecific latitudinal pattern. Its mixed specimen origins and cross-species design do not establish diagnostic traits or variation within bonnet macaques.

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerLimitations/en -->
