## Twenty-point-five millimetres is the single preserved male
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5968859-5bdfy
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
Adult male holotype ZMMU A-5822 is reported at 20.5 mm snout–vent length and in poor preservation condition. Its head is nearly as long as wide, snout shorter than the eye, and first finger 43.0% of finger II length. The small tympanum is near the eye; digit tips are rounded with only toes III–IV weakly enlarged, outer metatarsal tubercle absent, and posterior dorsal tubercles sparse. The diagnosis compares this larger original male with inexpectata and orlovi. It establishes neither a species-wide maximum size nor a female range, and the additional uncollected encounter is not another measured voucher.

## The live holotype has a bright orange-red underside
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5968859-5bdfy
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The live holotype is uniformly dark brick-brown above, including the limbs, with sparse darker tubercles and grey posterior pustules. Head sides are almost black without the whitish jaw mottling described for the other original species. The bright orange-red underside carries weak sparse brown marbling, denser on throat and hind limbs; iris is dark brown. These are one male’s live colour observations. They do not establish colour polymorphism, a female pattern, toxicity or warning-colour function, and are kept separate from the later ethanol-preserved appearance.

## Daytime encounters after rain do not establish a fixed daily schedule
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5968859-5bdfy
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
An uncollected Doi Tung frog was seen under a log around 1,000 m on 10 February 2017. The holotype was collected at 14:00 on 5 April 2017 after heavy rain, at 1,050 m in leaf litter beside a forest trail on a limestone slope, far from streams in the type label. These are two encounters but one deposited voucher. The authors infer concealment in soil or litter; no daily activity budget, burrowing trial or obligatory stream avoidance was measured. The concluding paragraph mistakenly names orlovi when discussing unknown diet and reproduction, so that sentence is not silently treated as newly direct occidentalis evidence.

## Doi Tung records do not confirm adjacent Myanmar occupancy
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5968859-5bdfy
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
The 2018 source confirms a single Doi Tung locality in northern Thailand’s Chiang Rai area, with the holotype at about 1,050 m and earlier unvouchered encounter around 1,000 m. The ridge approaches Myanmar’s Shan State, where future occurrence is anticipated rather than observed. Border proximity alone supplies no confirmed cross-border distribution. The original evidence does not establish continuous mountain occupancy, an area-of-occupancy metric or verified absence from other limestone forests.

## One voucher and one encounter underpin a dated proposal
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5968859-5bdfy
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/text/en -->
The conservation account explicitly bases knowledge on one unvouchered record and the holotype, with range and population status unresolved. It suggests Data Deficient in 2018 and further focused Thai and Myanmar surveys. The proposal is not current Red List verification, a numerical decline or evidence that the species is necessarily absent outside Doi Tung. No calls were recorded during either survey; this evidence gap cannot be used to infer silence, reproductive mode, larval absence or a complete natural history.

## Genus placement is a mitochondrial and morphological hypothesis
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5968859-5bdfy
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/text/en -->
The original study places the three named Vietnamophryne species in a supported Asterophryinae lineage and suggests the genus as sister to Siamophryne. Its 12S–16S mitochondrial fragment does not fully resolve every Asterophryinae lineage. The reported placement belongs to the sampled genealogy and comparative morphology, not a nuclear-genomic isolation test or proof that later named genus members share every diagnostic measurement. Single original male proportions, colours and encounter sites remain species-specific rather than being borrowed across the genus.
