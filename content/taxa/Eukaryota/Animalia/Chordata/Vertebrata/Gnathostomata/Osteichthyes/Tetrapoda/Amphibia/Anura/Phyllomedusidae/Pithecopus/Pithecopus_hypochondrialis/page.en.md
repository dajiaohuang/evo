A larval-development code does not specify larval duration, all larval habitats or the degree of dependence on standing water; those require additional evidence. ## Compiled lengths retain inference and measurement limits
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10688756-4jbpg
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
The row explicitly marks head width and tibia length as inferred, not an observed measurement from a new specimen. The article fills missing traits with a phylogenetic maximum-likelihood procedure; across the full pool, 139 head widths and 146 tibia lengths were inferred. Body length, head width and tibia length are retained numerically in the row-level evidence snapshot. The Methods use means when available and otherwise maxima, while the workbook length headers do not explicitly state units and do not consistently identify sex or stage. These cells are therefore not presented as new diagnostic millimetre measurements, a sex-specific range or measured species-wide variation. Upstream reference entries are preserved, but their original measurements have not been independently checked.

## Compiled habitat and threat labels are bounded descriptions

primary-pmc12654886-4jbpg

The Pithecopus hypochondrialis row prints habitat F,O,Pa and IUCN LC. The source defines F as forest, O as open vegetation and Pa as perianthropic; these are compiled habitat categories, not measured habitat preference, breeding-site selection or demonstrated tolerance of pollution/mining. The printed F Pa spacing in some rows is retained without silently substituting a different classification. LC means the source’s Least Concern label, DD Data Deficient, and a dash remains an unassigned table status rather than a certified absence of threats. The table does not provide an assessment date for each species, and these labels are not a current IUCN refresh. They do not measure local population change or the effectiveness of protected areas.
