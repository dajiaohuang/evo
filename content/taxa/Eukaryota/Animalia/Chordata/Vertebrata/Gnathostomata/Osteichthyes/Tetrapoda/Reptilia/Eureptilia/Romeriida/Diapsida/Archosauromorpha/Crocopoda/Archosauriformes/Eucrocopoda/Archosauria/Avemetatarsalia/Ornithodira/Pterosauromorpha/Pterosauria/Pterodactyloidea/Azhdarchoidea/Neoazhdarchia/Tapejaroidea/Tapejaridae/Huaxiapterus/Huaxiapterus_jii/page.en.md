# Huaxiapterus jii


## An older named entry and revised synonymy

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
The fixed PBDB entry Huaxiapterus jii retains its older name and provenance. The 2023 revision maintains that holotype GMN-03-11-001 is indistinguishable from Sinopterus dongi and treats jii as its junior synonym. Follow preservation, proportions and relative development through this specimen; the database name does not establish another independent biological species.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## A nearly complete body retains incomplete cranial and pedal information

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
The holotype is nearly complete, but its posterior skull is disarticulated and damaged. Only two disarticulated cervicals are visible, preventing assessment of the cervical length sequence; entirely disarticulated pedal elements prevent assessment of metatarsal proportions. An estimated nasoantorbital fenestra length/height ratio near three uses the partly preserved jugal, rather than direct measurement of an intact opening.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Larger crests require comparison with the rest of the body

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The account describes roughly 14° rostral deflection relative to the posterior palatal plane. The premaxillary crest has a parabolic outline like the S. dongi holotype but is larger, as is the mandibular crest. A partly preserved left jugal is triradiate. Original diagnosis emphasized crest depth without precise quantitative comparison; the revision does not retain a separate species merely for larger crests or establish sex or food from them.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## A long first metacarpal and terminal wing phalanx support comparison

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
Metacarpal one extends at least 90% the length of metacarpal four and reaches the wrist; wing phalanx four is about 0.30 the length of phalanx one. The revision identifies these as shared with S. dongi, contrasting with shorter first metacarpals and terminal wing phalanges in corollatus/benxiensis material. Wing proportions are closest to the S. dongi holotype, without directly reconstructing a complete membrane or measured flight speed.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Two juveniles can differ in relative development

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
Posterior skull elements, scapula/coracoid, humeral epiphyses, carpals, the first wing-phalanx extensor tendon process and tibiotarsus remain unfused. Dorsal centra and neural arches are partly fused with visible sutures, unlike some entirely unfused, disassociated dorsals in the S. dongi holotype. The authors consider both juveniles, with jii slightly further developed; this does not establish exact age, sex or complete adult morphology.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Specimen dimensions do not define maximum wingspan

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The 2023 account gives GMN-03-11-001 a 79 mm humerus and a 1,602 mm wingspan, larger than the S. dongi holotype. Both are immature, so larger size and crests require reading alongside fusion states. These are cited author values without independent measurement or inferred body mass; roughly 1.6 m is not a species maximum adult wingspan.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Carry specimen evidence into the two morphotypes

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
First compare the holotype, growth methods and juvenile identification in [Sinopterus dongi](../../Sinopterus/Sinopterus_dongi/page.en.md), then shorter metacarpals, terminal wing phalanges and different crests in [corollatus](../Huaxiapterus_corollatus/page.en.md). The original 2005 full description, images, supplementary measurements and a complete growth sequence remain incompletely read or independently inspected; this page does not adjudicate every historical taxonomy.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Which fossil records this name represents

This guide uses 1 eligible species-level occurrence record from 1 collection and 1 reference identifier. Occurrences, collections and references are different units, not specimen or individual counts.

It combines the oldest early bound and youngest late bound among selected records, rather than a continuous record at every time or measured species origin, extinction or individual lifespan. Each collection retains its own age bracket. Broad intervals are not narrowed to precise specimen ages, and original dating interpretations are not revised here.

## Evidence limits

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
Existing sections retain PBDB occurrence guides dated 2026-10-02; the added account reads the 2023 revised specimen account, while a complete monograph remains unfinished. Entries belong to the fixed 276-item pterosaur working roster, retain the current same name and a complete parent chain to the recorded Pterosauria storage anchor, and have usable species-level occurrences without uncertain-identification qualifiers. Database accepted names can connect older combinations; that database correspondence does not decide specimen identifications or all taxonomic disputes and is not merged with same-name COL concepts. Collection sites, stratigraphic intervals, depositional settings and collection-level preservation modes have distinct meanings. They do not directly establish complete range, origin or extinction time, fixed species ecology, biological abundance or the condition of every specimen. Original descriptions, revisions, morphology, diet and behavior remain incompletely assembled; The complete GMN-03-11-001 account and textual caption were read here; images, specimens, the 2005 full paper and supplementary measurements were not independently inspected, and the entire 2023 paper has not been read. Fusion, synonymy and dimensions retain named source interpretations; reader-facing delivery remains pending.
