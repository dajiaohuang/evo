## overview

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
In 2026 Hart et al. described Colobus congoensis from the Lomami–Lualaba interfluve of the Democratic Republic of the Congo and recommended the local name Likweli, provided by Balanga communities. The description draws on morphology, mitochondrial sequences, acoustic comparisons and field observations; the type locality is on the east bank of Lomami National Park.

## fieldStudy

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
Between 2018 and 2022, the authors recorded 114 field observations across an estimated known area of about 1,700 km². Records were concentrated in tall, closed-canopy forest on high clay pediments. Observed groups ranged from 1 to 20 animals, with a mean of 6.2. The area is the paper’s estimate for the observed range, not the result of a complete occupancy survey.

## ecology

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
Among 62 observations where group composition could be determined, 45 recorded associations with other primates. The authors noted that observations clustered near the Lomami River; only a few records came from sende forest farther east. Both the association frequency and spatial concentration are bounded by survey coverage, opportunities for observation and the known range.

## classification

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The source compares Likweli with Colobus satanas using morphology, genetics and calls, and reports a separation of more than 1,200 km between their known localities. This is a comparison within the species description, not a complete systematic review of the genus. The Chinese common name is editorial; Likweli is retained as the paper’s recommended name.
