# Rhea americana


## The material behind this genome

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
Sackton and colleagues (2019) added a genome for Rhea americana. NCBI master record PTEV00000000 belongs to PRJNA433110 and records sex as male; voucher ROM:ORN:MKP2758; tissue blood; recorded locality Argentina: Viedma, Rio Negro. These fields identify assembly material, not species-wide or wild-population statistics. Missing tissue or locality fields are not inferred here. The main and companion phylogenetic papers use the same genome set, not two independent population samples.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Rhea placement differs between analytical methods

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
Both rhea species enter the comparison. Two summary species-tree methods in Cloutier and colleagues place rheas sister to the kiwi–emu/cassowary clade, whereas concatenation can place them outside the remaining nonostrich palaeognaths. Marker comparisons and retroelements support the former interpretation, but high support alone cannot remove model differences. The discussion concerns lineage sorting during ancient divergences, not an extant rhea being another species’ ancestor.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## One sequence in a chicken forelimb reporter assay

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
Sackton and colleagues select candidate region mCE967994 in a TEAD1 intron, place bird sequences in a GFP reporter system and electroporate chicken embryos at HH19–20, examining them about 24 hours later. The Rhea americana sequence gives 2 strong, 1 partial and 10 absent-expression outcomes in 13 assays; the chicken sequence gives 11 strong, 4 partial and 1 absent in 16 assays. These are experiments on one candidate region in a chicken host, not wild individuals of this species. Reduced reporter expression neither recreates flight nor excludes other regulatory effects in the species’ natural context.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Measured beak dimensions

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
Recorded mean beak measurements are 35.6 mm from the anterior nostril edge to the tip, 27.2 mm in width and 17.2 mm in depth at that edge. These describe separate dimensions at standardized landmarks, not field-identification cutoffs.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Wing, tarsus and mass

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
Mean unflattened wing length from the carpal joint to the longest primary is 604.5 mm; mean tarsus length is 308 mm. Compiled mean mass is 23000 g, with source category Dunning. Mass comes from separate species-average information; this does not mean every morphological specimen was weighed.

## Reproduction and developmental timing

Available compilation fields are female maturity field (defined by the source as age at first conception) 576.47 d; offspring per litter or eggs per clutch 23.71 offspring/eggs; oviposition to hatching 37 d; d denotes days. The litter/clutch field combines offspring or egg counts, and a fractional summary does not mean a single event produced a fractional individual. Timing fields refer to different events and cannot be exchanged for one uniform reproductive cycle.

## Relative weights of food categories

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/14/text/en -->
The archive’s nonzero food weights are invertebrates 20%; fruit 20%; seeds, nuts and spores included in this category 20%; other plant material (the source category also includes fungi) 20%; mammals and birds 10%; reptiles and amphibians 10%. Source code A indicates high compiler confidence in the source and diet information. These percentages standardize verbal descriptions rather than measure diet composition; zero does not prove an item is never eaten. Categories such as Nect, Seed and PlantO are broader than their everyday names and cannot imply exclusively nectar, seeds or plants.
<!-- /evo:text -->


## Foraging strata differ from whole-day activity

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/15/text/en -->
Foraging-stratum weights flagged as species-level in the archive are ground 100%. They estimate relative use of feeding positions, not nest sites, all habitat or whole-day time budgets. Surface-water, understory, mid/high, canopy and aerial categories have different source definitions; they are not elevational ranges and cannot yield flight heights or habitat area.

## Evidence limits

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
This is a guide to historical AVONET v7 data. Morphological values are species averages from the listed samples, not identification diagnoses or ranges of individual variation. Mass uses separately cited literature or weighing sources; morphological sample counts must not be assigned to mass. Habitat, diet and migration are coarse categories; geography concerns historical breeding and resident ranges only. One-to-one historical crosswalks and a matching catalogue name provide the association here, not proof of equivalent species concepts after later splits or lumps. Reproduction, current populations and conservation status require other sources.
This batch uses historical same-name records from the 2015 life-history archive. Values are reported compilation medians or averages of the two middle values, except that maximum longevity uses the largest record found. They are not direct measurements of one individual, individual variation ranges, mean wild lifespans or current population surveys. The compilation can share and inherit data across historical species and subspecies. Matching name and family and the presence of same-name sparse rows support the association here; they do not establish equivalence to COL26.8 concepts or remove all inherited components from the reported summaries. Wild or captive conditions are not uniformly confirmed here, and underlying publications have not been individually reviewed. Diet, behavior, current range, conservation and missing fields require other sources; no complete monograph or external expert review is claimed.
