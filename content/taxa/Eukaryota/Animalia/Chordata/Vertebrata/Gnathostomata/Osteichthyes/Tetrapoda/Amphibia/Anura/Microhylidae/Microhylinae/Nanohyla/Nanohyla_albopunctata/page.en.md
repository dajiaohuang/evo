## Three male types provide limited and conflicting size evidence
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10415765-qmt99
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The holotype ZMMU A-7585 and two male paratypes were collected at Song Hinh Protected Forest, Phu Yen, Vietnam, on 15 January 2021; the type point is at 310 metres above sea level. Females were not recorded. The Diagnosis and Variation give male snout–vent length 18.2–20.2 millimetres for three males, but the holotype description gives 17.6 millimetres. That internal discrepancy prevents certifying one consistent measured range from the text alone. The quoted mean and plus/minus value are not labelled here as standard deviation without the unread supplementary table. This small sample is not a population size distribution.

## Reduced first finger and expanded discs form part of the diagnosis
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10415765-qmt99
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The examined males have a reduced first finger shorter than half the second, no finger webbing, discs on fingers II–IV, prominent grooved toe discs, two metatarsal tubercles and long hindlimbs. The holotype has no maxillary or vomerine teeth and a single subgular vocal sac. Dorsal skin is tubercular, ventral skin smooth, and a mid-dorsal ridge and large superciliary projections are absent. These external observations do not establish skeletal digit loss or larval anatomy. Palmar-tubercle shapes are reversed between Diagnosis and the holotype description, so the number two is clearer than the disputed shape assignment.

## White head spots and live colour retain specimen context
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10415765-qmt99
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
All three types had distinctive pale spots on the top of the head. The live holotype had five white head spots, a brown-grey dorsum with a darker posterior marking, pale sacral spots, striped hindlimbs and fine grey ventral mottling. Its iris had copper-coloured edging and reticulation. After two years in ethanol the colours darkened or faded, while much of the pattern remained. These descriptions distinguish living and preserved material and do not establish that every future animal has exactly five spots or that colour alone is a complete identification rule.

## Calling occurs at a small temporary forest-road pool
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10415765-qmt99
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
Males called after 19:00 from leaf litter at the edge of a temporary pool about two metres across on a forest road. Disturbed males jumped into the water, floated and emitted some calls. Groups of two or three males produced overlapping series; the authors suggest social arousal affects timing and pulse repetition. This is local January observation, not a controlled disturbance or acoustic-social experiment. It does not supply a full nightly activity curve, feeding behaviour or a general avoidance response across populations.

## Series, calls and pulses are not one acoustic denominator
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10415765-qmt99
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
The detailed account describes series of two to six calls, with one to three pulses in the initial call and a broader reported one-to-thirteen range in subsequent calls. The opening summary gives a different successive-pulse range, and the later comparison labels two to six as pulses per call, although that count earlier described calls per series. These conflicts are preserved instead of selecting a single universal pulse count. The rattling impression and reported low peak frequency do not establish a measured species-wide acoustic limit or complete call duration; supplementary recordings and statistical units were not independently checked.

## An integrative sampled lineage is sister to marmorata
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10415765-qmt99
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
The study combined a mitochondrial 12S–16S region with nuclear BDNF and earlier references, totalling sixty-five specimens across the dataset. That total is not sixty-five newly collected albopunctata. Bayesian and maximum-likelihood analyses placed the new lineage sister to marmorata, with 16S uncorrected distance reported as 5.3%. Morphological and acoustic comparisons accompany this placement. A marker threshold cited from biodiversity surveys is not an independent universal rule, and the tree does not measure a divergence date, contemporary migration or reproductive isolation.

## Head ratios and webbing formulae remain disputed in the text
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10415765-qmt99
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/text/en -->
The comparison describes a wider head and shorter foot relative to marmorata, but prints an inverted head-length/SVL interval of 29.2–20.8%. Diagnosis and holotype description agree on one toe-webbing formula while the comparison prints another for albopunctata. No corrected head ratio or definitive formula is invented here. The qualitative comparative claim is retained with its limitation, pending the original measurements and specimen-level check, rather than presenting the disputed values as a precise diagnostic test.

## Lowland occurrence and a proposed DD category are dated evidence
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10415765-qmt99
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/text/en -->
At publication in 2023, the species was known only from its Song Hinh type area, with records around 200–400 metres in low- to mid-elevation tropical forest. The authors report January breeding activity but give no clutch, larval-development or diet data; the actual range is unknown. They provisionally suggest Data Deficient. This is the authors’ dated proposal, not a freshly verified official Red List category, current abundance trend or evidence that all similar forest is occupied. Regional habitat pressure and a conservation call do not measure intervention success.
