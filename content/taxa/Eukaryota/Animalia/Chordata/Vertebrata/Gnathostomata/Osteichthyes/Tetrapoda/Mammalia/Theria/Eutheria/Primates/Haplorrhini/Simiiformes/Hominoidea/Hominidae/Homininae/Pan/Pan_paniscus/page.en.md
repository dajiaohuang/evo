## ecology

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
At the wild Bompusa community at LuiKotale, Democratic Republic of the Congo, researchers used stable isotopes in hair from 23 known adults sampled in 2008–2010. Lower social rank was associated with lower nitrogen-isotope values in males; females showed no corresponding rank relationship across reproductive states. Isotope measurements inform feeding ecology but do not by themselves identify each individual's exact foods.

## ecology

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
A separate study at Kokolopori and LuiKotale analyzed 1,509 known-caller vocalizations from 53 wild adults in four Democratic Republic of the Congo communities. Supervised random-forest classification reliably distinguished 11 of 15 predefined call types; acoustic overlap remained, so the authors described the repertoire as graded. Classification does not establish call meaning or provide a complete range-wide inventory.
