## Rain-associated encounters near marshy ground
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/17/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc11283625-6n64b
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/17/text/en -->
The island survey reported 40 detections near marshy ground, under human-made items beside marshy river overflows and occasionally under rocks in dirt patches near rivers. Encounters were most frequent during or after rain, and the animal was commonly found after digging or gardening in settlements. These observations describe encounter opportunities and refuges, without quantifying an intrinsic rain response or a preference for disturbed ground.

## primaryStudyScopes / sectionEvidence / evidenceSummary

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/primaryStudyScopes/0/sectionEvidence/0/evidenceSummary/en -->
The island survey reported 40 detections near marshy ground, under human-made items beside marshy river overflows and occasionally under rocks in dirt patches near rivers. Encounters were most frequent during or after rain, and the animal was commonly found after digging or gardening in settlements. These observations describe encounter opportunities and refuges, without quantifying an intrinsic rain response or a preference for disturbed ground.

The authors described the caecilian as burrowing and difficult to detect, with records in 2 of 8 settlement, 5 of 13 disturbed-forest and 3 of 11 national-park forest transects. Failure to detect it in the remaining transects is not treated as proven absence, and these counts are not corrected occupancy estimates.
