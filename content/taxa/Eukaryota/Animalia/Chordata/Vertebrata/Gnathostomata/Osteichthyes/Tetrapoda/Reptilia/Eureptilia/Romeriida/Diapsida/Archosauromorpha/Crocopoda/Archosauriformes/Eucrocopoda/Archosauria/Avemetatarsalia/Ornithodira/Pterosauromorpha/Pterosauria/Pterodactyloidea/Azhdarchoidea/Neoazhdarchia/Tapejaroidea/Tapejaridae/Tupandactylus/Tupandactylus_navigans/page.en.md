# Tupandactylus navigans


## Separate the samples in the two analyses

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
The first 2021 morphological matrix contains 64 taxonomic units and 150 discrete characters, using GP/2E 9266 to add body data for T. navigans. The authors initially coded holotype SMNK PAL 2344 separately, reporting no conflicting character scores with the new specimen before using the latter to place the species. A second analysis adds postcranial material MN 6588-V as a separate unit; its supplement header gives 65 units and 150 characters. The units include comparative taxa, so they are neither a specimen count for this species nor a species count for the genus.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Multiple shortest trees and unresolved branching

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
The first analysis reports 132 most-parsimonious trees of 371 steps; adding MN 6588-V yields 469 trees of 373 steps. The strict consensus places T. navigans in Tapejarinae in an unresolved polytomy with T. imperator, Tapejara wellnhoferi, Europejara olcadesorum and Caiuajara dobruskii. MN 6588-V also joins that polytomy in the second analysis. Tree counts and lengths are results of this matrix and search, not species counts or relationship probabilities, and do not establish the two Tupandactylus species as closest sisters.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Missing scores do not establish shared anatomy

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The supplements list T. imperator and T. navigans separately, with MN 6588-V added in the second matrix. Their rows contain missing scores, particularly for the postcranial anatomy of T. imperator. The paper discusses an absence of differences in known scores between the species; this does not demonstrate identical complete bodies or confirm different sexes of one species. Some characters are designated ordered. Interpretation depends on sampling, character definitions, scorable material and search settings; the complete matrix has not been recoded or its tree search rerun here.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Scans and digital models provide different observations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
Medical CT exposes bones still embedded in matrix; the methods report a data voxel size of 0.976 mm and overlap of 0.33 mm. Segmentation and resampling produce meshes that are subsequently smoothed for presentation. These operations cannot recover detail unresolved in the original scan. The digital skeleton does not replace missing bones, remove lateral deformation or directly establish a complete living posture. The paper identifies a raw CT repository, whose slices have not been independently inspected here.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Fused shoulder elements require distinguishing fractures

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
Both scapulocoracoids are preserved, with the left better retained and no visible scapula/coracoid suture. The authors explicitly distinguish a transverse proximal coracoid fracture from the interbone suture; the right scapular end preserves an articular surface for the notarium. These document skeletal support without treating a fracture as nonfusion or deriving exact age or launch performance from fusion.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Different humeral preservation does not measure muscle force

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The left humerus is flattened and lacks part of its distal end; the right is complete, with proximal and deltopectoral regions partly embedded and assessed through CT. The authors describe broad muscle-attachment crests and pneumatic openings. A humerus/femur length ratio near 0.78 is a specimen proportion rather than an invariant for every individual; crest dimensions are not preserved muscle volume or directly measured flight power.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Wrist and metacarpal anatomy separates small fingers from the wing

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
The wrist includes proximal and distal syncarpals and the pteroid among its elements. Slender metacarpals one to three do not reach the wrist, extending only about halfway along metacarpal four, which supports the wing finger; the right pteroid exceeds half the ulna’s length. Exposure differs between sides, with parts of the right hand assessed by CT. These relationships do not preserve a complete membrane or establish particular climbing or flight behavior.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## The pelvis combines maturity indicators with incomplete development

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
The pelvis is nearly complete but lacks the prepubis and minor portions, with interbone sutures not discernible. The long preacetabular ilium may have rotated under lateral compression, so preserved orientation is not automatically living posture. The authors also note apparently incomplete ischiopubic-plate development. This accompanies maturity inferred from other fusion, without making invisible sutures establish reproductive age, sex or a whole-clade fusion sequence.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Long hindlimbs and a four-digit foot do not record running speed

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/text/en -->
Femora and tibiae are preserved, with tibiae approximately half again as long as femora and shorter fibulae. The foot has four phalange-bearing digits, with shorter claws than the hand; the right foot is incomplete distally. Limb lengths and pedal anatomy inform support and locomotor hypotheses without measured speed or diet. The reported fibula/tibia ratio was not recalculated here, and the phalangeal formula is not used to adjudicate all morphological disputes.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## The 2.7 m wingspan is a specimen estimate

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/text/en -->
The authors estimate a wingspan near 2.7 m from humerus, ulna, metacarpal four and wing-phalanx lengths in GP/2E 9266. Tables also give differently preserved dimensions from each side; comparative tables are not combined into one ideal intact individual. Wing phalanx four has a side discrepancy between Table 4 and the preservation account, requiring image/specimen checks. The estimate is not maximum species wingspan or direct flight performance.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Compare an associated body with isolated cranial material

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/10/text/en -->
Compare metacarpals and terminal wing phalanges in [Huaxiapterus corollatus](../../Huaxiapterus/Huaxiapterus_corollatus/page.en.md), then skull-based wingspan scaling in [Europejara olcadesorum](../../../../../Europejara/Europejara_olcadesorum/page.en.md). Associated skeletons supply limb proportions while isolated heads depend on relative comparisons; neither is a direct flight observation. Original descriptions, later research, raw CT, images and supplements remain incompletely assembled or independently checked.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## One skeleton connects the crest with the body

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/11/text/en -->
GP/2E 9266, described by Beccari and colleagues in 2021, is a nearly complete, largely articulated skeleton of Tupandactylus navigans, preserving the skull, postcranial skeleton and soft-tissue traces. It is referred material, not the species holotype SMNK PAL 2344. At publication, it supplied the first postcranial material unambiguously assigned to the genus, connecting body anatomy with known skull morphology. That historical advance does not mean every specimen has now been reviewed.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Six slabs and uneven preservation

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/12/text/en -->
The fossil occupies six matching limestone slabs, with cuts crossing bones and crest tissue. Some broken slabs had been joined with thin metal bars before accession. Compression, weathering and loss vary across the skeleton: the left fourth wing phalanx is missing, and the right foot is incomplete distally. Nearly complete therefore describes abundant associated material, not undamaged preservation throughout. The authors identify Crato Formation provenance from the matrix, while the precise locality and horizon remain unknown. The retained Nova Olinda database collection is not a demonstrated exact locality for this specimen.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Bone and soft tissue together form the crest

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/13/text/en -->
A slender, nearly vertical supra-premaxillary process supports the anterior crest, while the parietal crest is short and rounded. These features help assign the new specimen to T. navigans. Preserved soft tissue comprises a ventral fibrous region and a smoother dorsal region rising far above the skull, although the uppermost extent is not fully exposed. CT also reveals an anterior expansion of the bony crest hidden beneath matrix. These observations describe tissue and support geometry; dark and light fossil patches may reflect oxidation and do not directly establish living colors or sex.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Toothless jaws and a preserved beak sheath

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/14/text/en -->
The jaws are toothless and the rostral tip turns downward. Tissue sheaths cover the rostrum and anterior lower jaw, extending onto the deep, blade-shaped dentary crest; the authors interpret them as a keratinous rhamphotheca. A gap between the upper and lower beak constrains reconstructions of complete closure. Handling small food items or hard plant material is discussed as a possible function, rather than a demonstrated diet. This guide has no stomach contents or direct feeding observations establishing a complete menu.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Neck anatomy, fused dorsals and individual maturity

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/15/text/en -->
Nine cervical vertebrae are preserved, including a fused atlas-axis complex. Mid-neck vertebrae are elongated, posterior ones shorter, and some postzygapophyses bear lateral longitudinal grooves. The centra of the first five dorsal vertebrae form a notarium, with unequal fusion of their neural spines. Combining this structure with fusion of the wing extensor tendon process, the authors interpret the individual as mature. An unfused tibiotarsus supplies a contrasting signal. Incomplete knowledge of tapejarid development prevents converting this assessment into an exact age or a species growth curve.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Clawed fingers beside the elongated wing finger

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/16/text/en -->
The first three left manual digits are complete, with phalangeal formula 2–3–4 and curved unguals retaining a keratinous tissue outline. Digit IV forms the elongated wing finger. Preservation differs between sides: its terminal left phalanx is missing, while the right wing phalanges are observable. Claws document grasping anatomy without independently proving tree climbing or a particular feeding behavior. CT segmentation exposes bones embedded in matrix; smoothed digital meshes are observation and presentation products, not replacements for missing fossil elements.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Flight and ground foraging need mechanical assessment

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/17/text/en -->
The paper relates the notarium, pectoral muscle attachment region and limb proportions to flight and possible quadrupedal launch, and discusses terrestrial foraging. The long neck and large crest also constrain earlier proposals treating the crest as an aerodynamic device. Short-distance flight is considered, with further biomechanical work explicitly needed. The skeleton does not directly provide flight speed, range, launch frequency or an established ground-dwelling lifestyle.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Species differences and relationships retain uncertainty

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/18/text/en -->
The 2021 matrix places T. navigans within Tapejarinae in an unresolved polytomy with T. imperator and other taxa, rather than establishing the two named species as closest sisters. The authors compare crest orientation and dentary crest morphology, while raising an untested possibility that the species represent sexually dimorphic forms. A separate postcranial specimen, MN 6588-V, is assigned only to Tupandactylus sp., not confidently to T. navigans. This page retains the two working-roster species entries without merging names or counting genus-level material as a new species occurrence.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Evidence limits

This page adds a bilingual guide to selected passages of the 2021 associated-skeleton study and retains fixed PBDB occurrences. Exact specimen locality and horizon are unknown. The geological setting says Aptian, whereas the systematic horizon line says Albian; that internal discrepancy is not silently harmonized or used to revise database age brackets. Previously unread anatomical subsections were read here; original descriptions, images and supplements remain incompletely assembled or inspected. No independent specimen inspection, measurement, scanning or matrix reanalysis was performed. Diet, flight, sexual dimorphism and maturity retain the authors’ inferential scope; a full monograph remain pending. This batch adds girdles, forearm, wrist/metacarpus, pelvis, legs/feet and measurement tables without independent scan inspection or remeasurement. The Table 4 wing-phalanx side discrepancy remains unresolved; reader-facing delivery is pending. A complete matrix audit, tree rerun and raw CT inspection remain pending; unresolved branching and missing scores do not establish resolved species relationships.
