## fieldEcology

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
Lahm’s 1986 study drew on observations and feeding remains collected in northeastern and central Gabon from November 1982 to October 1983. Fruit was the main dietary item, alongside other plant parts, insects and occasional small vertebrates; observations also covered primary, secondary, riparian and inundated forests. This is historical multi-site evidence, not a current range-wide diet profile.

## homeRange

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
White et al. used radiotelemetry to follow one wild horde of about 700 mandrills in Lopé National Park for six years. Its documented home range covered 182 km², including 89 km² of suitable forest; the horde used gallery forests and isolated forest patches intensively and avoided savanna and marsh. This exceptional horde does not define a typical group or species-wide range.

## seasonalDiet

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
At Moukalaba–Doudou National Park, Hongo et al. measured fruit availability and camera-trap visits on 11 transects in 2012–2013 and analyzed feces collected over 25 months in 2009–2013. During the fruit-scarce season, feces contained more woody tissue and a wider variety of food types, while visits were distributed more evenly among transects. These seasonal results are local to that study area.
