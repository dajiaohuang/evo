# Caupedactylus ybaka


## A nearly complete skull with limited body material

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
The 2025 Araripe review records holotype MN 4726-V as a nearly complete skull and lower jaw accompanied by a scapula, coracoid, sternum, humerus, part of the right first phalanx of digit IV and rib fragments. This is an incomplete skeleton. The review retains the original study’s adult interpretation, which supplies neither an exact age or sex nor a species-wide growth series.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## A downturned rostrum and a crest extending lengthwise

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
The diagnosis reproduced in the review combines a premaxillary sagittal crest extending anteroposteriorly, a downturned rostrum and a mildly concave ventral skull margin. The anterior jaws leave no gap in closure. The jugal’s lacrimal process inclines posteriorly, and the quadrate lies at about 150° to the ventral skull margin. This specimen-based combination does not directly establish sex, display behavior or a thermoregulatory role for the crest.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Palatal opening names depend on interpreted bone boundaries

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The 2024 study uses CT and laminography of other pterosaurs to reinterpret boundaries among the maxilla, palatine, pterygoid and ectopterygoid. Reassigned elements lead to proposed changes in opening names, with earlier Caupedactylus illustrations included in comparison. The slit-like postpalatinal fenestra in an older diagnosis must therefore be read against that author’s bone boundaries; matching names alone do not establish matching openings.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Included in comparison without a new scan

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
Table 1 of the 2024 paper lists MN 4726-V without marking it as newly scanned, and the caption identifies the Caupedactylus palatal illustration as modified from earlier literature. The authors suggest a possible Y-shaped palatine and discuss its larger pterygoid openings and posteriorly displaced choanal rear margin. These are comparative interpretations, not newly revealed internal sutures from a Caupedactylus CT scan. Raw scans, 3D models and figure pixels were not inspected for this page.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Possible referrals remain conditional

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The 2025 review discusses a complete skull auctioned in 2009 whose whereabouts are unknown, and palatal fragment MPSC R 859. The latter resembles the holotype in having a flat palate anterior to the choanae, but fragmentation prevents a firm referral. The review also compares SMNK PAL 6410 with this species and calls for detailed reassessment of KPMNH DL 84. These discussions do not confirm all material as this species and cannot support a definite individual count or an expanded species distribution.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Read later relationship proposals beside the fixed directory

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
Originally interpreted as an early-diverging tapejarid, the species is placed with Aymberedactylus cearensis in Caupedactylia by later analyses summarized in the 2025 review. Comparative features include the absence of a step-like dorsal eminence of the mandibular symphysis and a flat dentary-fossa surface. The directory’s Tapejarinae parent chain is a fixed database record rather than the conclusion of every later study. This page presents the review’s scheme without rerunning cited matrices or adjudicating possible synonymy involving an earlier name.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## A ground-feeding generalist is a model-based interpretation

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
The review summarizes a 2021 bite-force study interpreting Caupedactylus ybaka as a possible ground-feeding generalist. Jaw features and reduced muscular mechanical advantage support a different functional interpretation from the hard-plant diet proposed for Tapejara. The underlying 2021 study was not independently read in full or recalculated here, so this remains a review-mediated model interpretation rather than gut contents, an observed meal, a fixed menu or demonstrated niche partitioning.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Paper horizon and database stratum remain discrepant

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
The 2025 review assigns the holotype to the Romualdo Formation and explicitly lists its locality as unknown; the 2013 original paper’s title also names Romualdo. The fixed database occurrence instead records Crato and a 119.57–113.2 Ma bracket. Both are retained pending collection-record reconciliation. A database collection entry does not establish the holotype locality missing from the paper, and its broad bracket is neither an origin or extinction estimate nor a direct holotype date.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Which fossil records this name represents

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/text/en -->
Caupedactylus ybaka is accepted in the fixed PBDB pterosaur working roster, with database parent Caupedactylus. This guide uses 1 eligible species-level occurrence record from 1 collection and 1 reference identifier. Occurrences, collections and references are different units, not specimen or individual counts.
<!-- /evo:text -->


## Begin with named collections and strata

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/text/en -->
Collection Between Nova Olinda and Santana do Cariri (Ceará), region code BR, stratum name Crato; database age bracket 119.57–113.2 Ma, early interval Late Aptian. Locality names, region codes and stratigraphic names retain the source wording. This is not a complete species distribution list, and presence in one region does not prove absence elsewhere.

## Age bounds of these records

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/10/text/en -->
Selected occurrences have an outer age envelope of 119.57–113.2 Ma, where Ma denotes millions of years ago. It combines the oldest early bound and youngest late bound among selected records, rather than a continuous record at every time or measured species origin, extinction or individual lifespan. Each collection retains its own age bracket. Broad intervals are not narrowed to precise specimen ages, and original dating interpretations are not revised here.
<!-- /evo:text -->


## Read preservation setting separately from living habitat

Between Nova Olinda and Santana do Cariri has collection-environment label terrestrial indet.; collection preservation-mode label body,original phosphate. These describe database collection settings and assemblages. A depositional setting does not determine this species’ diet or habitat, and an assemblage label does not describe every fossil individually. Unfilled source fields remain unfilled.

## Evidence limits

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
This batch reads all main sections of the 2024 palatal study and the complete genus/species account plus paragraphs naming the genus in the 2025 review. Only publication information and the abstract of the 2013 original description were obtained. Later matrices, the underlying 2021 bite-force study, images, scans, specimens and complete development remain independently unexamined. Conditional referrals, horizon discrepancies and classification revisions remain unresolved. A full monograph, external expert review and reader-facing delivery are pending.
