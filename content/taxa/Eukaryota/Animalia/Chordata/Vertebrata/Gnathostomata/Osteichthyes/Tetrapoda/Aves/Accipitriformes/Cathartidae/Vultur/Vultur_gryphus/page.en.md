## distribution

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
A 2016 Ecuador study modeled 60 roosting locations from seven satellite-tagged condors with seven environmental predictors at 500 m resolution. It estimated an Ecuadorian extent of occurrence of 49,550 km² and area of occupancy of 14,106 km². These are country-level model outputs, not a global range map.

## conservation

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
The same study estimated 94–102 condors in Ecuador from 93 birds counted during a synchronized two-day census in 2015. Its population-viability scenarios identified habitat loss as the greatest modeled threat in that country. The paper discusses an earlier national Critically Endangered listing; it is not a current IUCN or national reassessment.

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
Evidence is Ecuador-focused and dates to 2016: areas are model estimates, and abundance derives from a two-day count plus extrapolation. It does not establish the current global range, population, or conservation category. The Chinese common name is an editorial translation. This guide is not externally expert-reviewed.
