## A small species with a male humeral projection
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7936570-slx78
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The diagnosis reports males at 17–19 mm snout–vent length and a female value of 24 mm; the female value is not a population range. Males have a knobbed bony humeral projection visible externally in preserved specimens. Contrasting white blotches occur on grey to dark-grey flanks and groin, dorsal skin has horny spinules and ridges, and finger and toe discs are yellowish-brown. Six measured specimens varied from light to dark brown or reddish-brown, while a light triangular snout region was retained. These features diagnose a combination relative to other species, not a claim that the male-specific structure occurs in females or that live and preserved colours are identical.

## Leaf litter and elevated vegetation in the same locality
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7936570-slx78
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
At publication, kakkayamensis was known only from Kakkayam in Kozhikode district, Kerala, north of the Palghat gap. Observations at about 750 m elevation came from a primary forest patch and adjoining secondary forest. Individuals were found on ground leaf litter or vegetation 1–3 m high, showing that records were not confined to one perch stratum. The study did not estimate density, quantify preference between forest types or establish a tolerance threshold for disturbance. Its records do not determine natural egg placement or document a new geographic expansion. Only one known locality at that time is not proof that no other population exists.

## A short, usually single-pulse call
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7936570-slx78
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
Males were described as producing one call type, generally with one pulse and not delivered in groups. The typical-call summary gives 25.3 ms duration, 9.2 ms rise time, 15.9 ms fall time and dominant frequency 3.8 kHz. The qualifier generally matters: it does not establish that every call contains exactly one pulse. The study’s acoustic methods acknowledge effects of temperature, social context and individual variation that these summaries do not incorporate. This description supports comparison of sampled call structure, not a playback-demonstrated recognition barrier, a universal frequency or evidence that females share the recorded male call.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Close sequence affinity does not replace the full diagnosis
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7936570-slx78
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
Kakkayamensis was assigned to the bombayensis group, with a well-supported sister relationship to leucolatus. Mitochondrial 16S divergence between them was reported at 2.3–2.4%, lower than its reported distances to several other group members. The wider analysis used both mitochondrial and nuclear markers. A smaller distance does not on its own establish synonymy, recent hybridization or a fixed delimitation threshold; the authors also evaluated diagnostic morphology and call structure. The phylogeny is a sampled relationship estimate and does not date speciation or certify contemporary gene flow between the two species.
