## lifeHistory

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
At Gunung Palung National Park in West Kalimantan, records from 2009–2014 showed that four flanged males sired five offspring. In the broader 2008–2019 sample, paternity was assigned for five of seven offspring. These are paternity results from one completely wild population, not a species-wide reproductive rate.

## ecology

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
Nest transects in and around Danau Sentarum National Park used an updated nest-decay rate to model orangutan numbers: about 202 inside the park (95% CI 75.67–541.79) and about 71 outside it (95% CI 20.76–244.63). These are local model estimates from nest records, not direct counts, and depend on decay-rate assumptions and survey locations.

## evolution

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
Camp Leakey received orangutans that had been reintroduced after displacement. A mitochondrial study found that, among 11 profiles representing founder lineages, nine clustered with the local P. p. wurmbii lineage, while two females clustered with P. p. pygmaeus from northern West Kalimantan or Sarawak; both reproduced with local males. This documents lineage mixing at one human-mediated release site, not gene flow across the species' natural range.

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
This introduction treats studies from three locations as local cases involving Bornean orangutans, not a full species life-history or conservation review. Paternity, nest-based modelling and mitochondrial evidence from reintroduction use different samples and cannot be combined into one range-wide trend or population estimate. Source-linked and not externally expert-reviewed.
