## A diagnosis with explicit fold and fringe exceptions
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7080247-scgjj
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The pluvian diagnosis described a dorsolateral fold, basal toe webbing and fringes, a centrally smooth but laterally granular belly, and a reddish posterior thigh without spots. Its variation paragraph nevertheless listed many specimens lacking the dorsolateral fold, several without finger fringes, some smooth bellies and some immaculate venters. Preservation prevented observing the discoidal fold in two juveniles, which is not certified anatomical absence. The diagnostic combination and exceptions must be read together rather than turning one fold or colour statement into an invariant species boundary.

## Rain-period calling vegetation and dry-period ground use
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7080247-scgjj
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
Pluvian was recorded in Cotriguaçu, Ipiranga do Norte and Paranaíta, Mato Grosso, in conserved forest and some pasture-surrounded fragments. The account described males rising onto vegetation to call during the reproductive period and frogs predominantly on the ground during the dry period. No calling height or complete calendar months were supplied in that paragraph. These observations are not an abundance estimate, proof of equal habitat quality in disturbed and conserved sites or evidence of the egg substrate. The name’s reference to rain is etymology, not an independent seasonal dataset.

## Two-note advertisement calls from a small male sample
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7080247-scgjj
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The diagnosis reported pluvian advertisement calls as two pulsed notes, with three males in the acoustic sample and archived recordings MAP-V 317–319. The methods describe two Paranaíta males, while three archive numbers and diagnosis N = 3 are given; this sample-allocation difference remains unresolved. The two-note pattern distinguishes the cited samples from the three other newly described species’ note ranges, but is not proof that all populations have identical calls or that note number alone produces reproductive isolation. Short diagnosis duration values are not relabelled as whole-call measurements without the corresponding definition.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Unresolved mitochondrial relationships are not a settled radiation
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7080247-scgjj
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The discussion placed pluvian, fenestratus and unnamed Juruti and Borba lineages in a poorly resolved mitochondrial grouping described as a hard polytomy. It suggested rapid speciation and incomplete lineage sorting, while also acknowledging insufficient molecular data as an alternative explanation. The two-marker topology therefore does not independently certify simultaneous divergence, a complete species tree or absence of hybridization. Unnamed lineages remain unnamed source concepts rather than being assigned invented accepted-species identities.
