## Urban call detections count paddies, not frogs
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12789193-37vnm
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The study labels this frog Dryophytes japonica; this reader retains the pinned Dryophytes japonicus name without changing the source spelling or certifying population identity genetically. Call surveys in Machida, Yokohama and Kawasaki detected the frog at 71 of 104 sites in 2022 and 44 of 102 in 2023. Surveys lasted 5–10 minutes at 19:00–24:00, stopping early after detection; no call within ten minutes was coded absent. These counts represent detected calling presence at sites, not numbers of frogs, true absence, occupancy corrected for detection probability or egg production. The same paddies were broadly targeted but ten differed between years and survey points often shifted.

## Nearby forest area is associated with detected calling
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12789193-37vnm
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
The selected landscape model found a positive association between call detection and forest area within a 50 m radius. Forest and paddy area were assessed at 50 and 100 m scales from aerial-image polygons; those radii were chosen analysis buffers, not measured dispersal distances or minimum habitat requirements. Spatial clustering led the authors to include a spatial random effect and pool two years with year as a random effect. Their interpretation that forest–paddy connections support nonbreeding and breeding habitat is biologically contextual, not a randomized restoration experiment or a measured individual movement path. The result is limited to this urban survey design.

## Retained paddy water predicts detection within the fitted study
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12789193-37vnm
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
The microhabitat analysis found a positive association between retained paddy water and calling occurrence. Almost all surveyed sites held water in 2022, whereas roughly half lacked it in 2023. Water status therefore covaries with year, cultivation, fallowing and irrigation timing rather than isolating a single causal water effect. The combined model was not simply the lowest-AIC model for every comparison: the reported final model improved on the landscape model but had slightly higher AIC than the microhabitat model. These associations do not prove a universal water threshold, independence from all soil features or guaranteed breeding success after flooding.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Egg-substrate and seasonal information is cited background
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12789193-37vnm
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/text/en -->
The paper cites earlier accounts for spring-to-summer paddy breeding, May–September male advertisement calling and females appearing mainly during spawning. Its discussion cites eggs on aquatic vegetation and substrates to interpret the water association. Those are referenced natural-history statements, not new clutch observations in the 2022–2023 survey. The study itself did not measure clutch size, egg survival, larval recruitment, mate choice or a complete breeding calendar. Neither a detected call nor a dry survey visit establishes that reproduction succeeded or failed at a paddy throughout the season.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Two unequal survey seasons cannot establish long-term stability
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12789193-37vnm
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/text/en -->
The 2022 survey ran 9 May–4 July and the 2023 survey 8 May–7 June. The authors explicitly note one main survey event per year, shifting points, access restrictions and a shorter second season, although initially dry paddies received additional checks. Their suggestion of stable populations compared with literature around 2000 is an interpretation, not a measured long-term demographic trend. Lower detections in 2023 do not independently quantify decline: calling availability and irrigation schedule can change the result. Forest and water management recommendations remain hypotheses for conservation use rather than verified recovery or a current threat assessment.
<!-- /evo:text -->
