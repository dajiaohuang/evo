## Mitochondrial partitions and taxonomic ranks are separate decisions
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The 2025 Wumeng study recovered a lineage within dafangensis using a 1094-base-pair 16S–CO1 alignment, while CO1 ASAP favoured species separation. The authors nevertheless proposed subspecies rank. Their cited mantzorum–xinduqiao comparison also links low mitochondrial distances with a subspecies interpretation. These selected examples show why a marker partition alone does not certify a rank for every Amolops lineage. The species pages retain the dated names, sample scopes and proposed rank without manufacturing new accepted species identifiers.

## Voucher counts and collection dates must remain traceable
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
The Wumeng diagnosis rests on three male types and one female type, whereas methods describe six sampled animals, four retained and two released. Those denominators have different meanings. Methods and the type account disagree on collection years and months, so the linked dafangensis page records the conflict. A small type series and its variable live coloration cannot supply population-wide body limits, a complete genus identification key or a tested universal sex difference.

## Local breeding signs and a mountain-barrier hypothesis
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
July nuptial pads, stream records in two counties and sympatry with two other frog species concern the described Wumeng population. No amplexus or tadpoles were observed. Early breeding and restricted gene flow across the Wumeng Mountains are author interpretations, not directly measured reproductive timing or gene flow. These records do not establish diet, complete larval development, all-species breeding seasons or the ecological requirements of the genus.

## Regional inventory routes separate field records from literature
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/text/en -->
The selected Amolops routes contain 2 source entries from the 2025 Tangjiahe inventory in northern Sichuan. The full list combines twenty species found during field surveys with five from literature, so its twenty-five rows are not twenty-five new field detections. Thirty-nine adult-search transects on rain-free nights during May–August 2024 do not represent year-round sampling or all genus members. The paper explicitly identifies historical Megophrys omeimontis and Oreolalax nanjiangensis as undetected in the current field survey; the species readers preserve those distinctions. Supplementary per-row evidence and vouchers have not been independently reconstructed.

## primaryStudyScopes / sectionEvidence / evidenceSummary

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/primaryStudyScopes/0/sectionEvidence/0/evidenceSummary/en -->
The 2025 Wumeng study recovered a lineage within dafangensis using a 1094-base-pair 16S–CO1 alignment, while CO1 ASAP favoured species separation. The authors nevertheless proposed subspecies rank. Their cited mantzorum–xinduqiao comparison also links low mitochondrial distances with a subspecies interpretation. These selected examples show why a marker partition alone does not certify a rank for every Amolops lineage. The species pages retain the dated names, sample scopes and proposed rank without manufacturing new accepted species identifiers.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## primaryStudyScopes / sectionEvidence / evidenceSummary

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/primaryStudyScopes/0/sectionEvidence/1/evidenceSummary/en -->
The Wumeng diagnosis rests on three male types and one female type, whereas methods describe six sampled animals, four retained and two released. Those denominators have different meanings. Methods and the type account disagree on collection years and months, so the linked dafangensis page records the conflict. A small type series and its variable live coloration cannot supply population-wide body limits, a complete genus identification key or a tested universal sex difference.
