## Wet-season forest-floor records
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc9848859-t8vdz
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
Eight frogs in the study were found in slope or riparian forest, 1–10 m from water: seven on litter and one on soil beneath a large rock. Encounters including one previously published individual occurred in morning and evening surveys from December to May. No surveys outside that wet-season window were conducted, leaving dry-season activity unknown.

## An attributed bamboo-cavity egg record
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc9848859-t8vdz
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The account cited a 2021 observation of an individual guarding eggs in a water-filled bamboo node, separately from its eight newly studied frogs. A February evening advertisement call during heavy rain, together with the earlier December egg record, suggested wet-season reproductive activity. These few records do not establish year-round reproduction or obligatory bamboo breeding.
