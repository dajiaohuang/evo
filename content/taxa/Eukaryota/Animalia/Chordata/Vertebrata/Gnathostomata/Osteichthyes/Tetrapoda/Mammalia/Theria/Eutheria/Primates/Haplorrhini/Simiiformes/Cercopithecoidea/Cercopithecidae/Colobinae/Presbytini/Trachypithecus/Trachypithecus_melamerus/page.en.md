## name correspondence and classification

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
The pinned COL26.8 record (84J9M) accepts *Trachypithecus melamerus* (Elliot, 1909). MDD v2.5 uses *T. melamera* and records the original combination *Presbytis melamera*, holotype BMNH:Mamm:1888.12.1.64, and type locality Cadu Ciaung. Roos et al. (2020) sequenced that name-bearing specimen (NHMUK.ZD.1888.12.1.64). COL and MDD differ in the final spelling, but give the same author and year; the name sidecar did not match them automatically. The original combination and specimen correspondence support reading the two spellings as combinations of the same name-bearing taxon, while this page retains the fixed COL spelling and ID. Roos et al. also treated *melamera* as a senior synonym of *T. p. shanicus* and elevated that eastern lineage to species rank.

## mitochondrial and type evidence

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
Roos et al. included the holotype of *Presbytis melamera* and the holotype of *Pithecus shanicus* in their mitogenome comparison; both fell within the study's East clade. The authors concluded that *melamera* was not a synonym of *T. p. phayrei* but the older name for *shanicus*. Their species-level interpretation combined mitochondrial lineages with morphological and ecological differences. Mitochondrial DNA records maternal ancestry and cannot by itself represent the full history of gene flow.

## type locality and geographic scope

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
MDD gives the type locality as Cadu Ciaung, Bhamo, northern Myanmar. Roos et al. describe the East clade from Shan State in Myanmar and western Yunnan in China, between the Ayeyarwaddy and Thanlwin rivers; its southwestern boundary may extend into the Kayah-Karen foothills, but the authors say the boundary is not fully resolved. This is a geographic account based on lineage samples and historical locality records, not a continuous range map, a current field-confirmed range, or a population census.

## comparative identification evidence

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
In their comparative description, Roos et al. report that *T. melamera* is generally more fawn than *T. phayrei* and the newly described *T. popa*. Its pale underside contrasts less sharply with the back, the lower arms do not darken markedly, the crown has a whorl or parting, the whiskers point forward, and the head outline is rounder. The authors also note variation in body coloration. These are comparative external characters from the study, not a validated stand-alone field identification rule.

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
The spelling correspondence between COL26.8 and MDD v2.5 is supported by the shared author and year, MDD’s original combination and type specimen, and Roos et al.’s sequencing of that name-bearing specimen; the local name sidecar still does not match them automatically, so the source trail remains explicit. Available evidence centers on phylogeny, type and historical localities, and comparative morphology. It includes no species-level population census, long-term behavior or diet study and does not establish present abundance or trend. MDD v2.5 currently lists Not Evaluated; the Chinese common name is editorial; this page has not received external expert review. This page is not externally expert-reviewed.
