# Callicebinae


## Begin type identity with the evidence chain

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
Three Naturalis titi cases show why type work requires more than repeating old labels: an adult female cupreus paralectotype, two donacophilus syntypes at different stages, and a rejected historical discolor attribution. Registration numbers, skin/skull parts, label localities, bracketed additions and cross-museum references describe different evidence layers. The catalogue did not actively exhaust other museums’ type series and does not replace field ecology or a comprehensive phylogenetic revision.

## Organize titi comparisons by material provenance

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The 2002 bernhardi description connects an adult skeleton of unknown sex with two male paratypes obtained and later kept in captivity, alongside named wild-observation localities. This material chain differs from the imprecisely sourced stephennashi type series; one species’ sites and habitats do not supply another name’s missing evidence. Skull dimensions, appearance photographs, field localities and group behaviour play distinct roles. Historical Callicebus names also need to be read separately from the current genus paths.
