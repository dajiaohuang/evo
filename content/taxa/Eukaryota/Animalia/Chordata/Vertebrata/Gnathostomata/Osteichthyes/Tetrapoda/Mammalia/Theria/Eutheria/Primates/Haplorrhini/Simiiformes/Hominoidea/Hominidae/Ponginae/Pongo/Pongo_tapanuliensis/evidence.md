## distribution / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/distribution/claims/0/text -->
The paper estimated historical extent of occurrence at about 40,796 km² in the 1890s and 21,313 km² in the 1940s, compared with a current range of about 1,023 km². The resulting estimates that the current range is 2.5% and 5.0% of those historical ranges are conditional on the authors' attribution of historical orangutan populations south and southeast of the current range to Pongo tapanuliensis. The authors say northern records are difficult to distinguish between P. tapanuliensis and P. abelii, and there is no robust species identification for records outside the current P. tapanuliensis range; cited historical specimens had not been genetically analysed. These are conditional historical estimates, not a verified complete or present-day range.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## distribution / claims / placeTimeScope

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/distribution/claims/0/placeTimeScope -->
Historical records for Sumatra compiled from the early 1800s through 2009; estimated extents compare 1890s and 1940s baselines with an approximately 1,023 km² current distribution. The ratios depend specifically on attributing historical records south and southeast of the current P. tapanuliensis range to that species. The authors state that northern records may instead represent P. abelii and that records outside the current range lack robust taxonomic confirmation.
<!-- /evo:text -->
