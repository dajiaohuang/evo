## overview

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
The Mozambique Dwarf Galago (also called Grant's Dwarf Galago) is a small nocturnal primate of southeastern Africa. Field identification often uses its incremental loud call. It was historically grouped with other galagos, while taxonomic reviews and a 2020 molecular analysis support treating it separately from the Kenya Coast and Tanzania Coast species.

## fieldStudy

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
Génin et al. (2016) reported preliminary observations from 2011–2014 at Tembe Elephant Park and Tshanini Community Reserve in South Africa. The species was recorded only in dry sand forest and dense woodland. Across 24 survey nights, the paper reports six sleeping sites, all in tree holes 3–14 m above ground: four used by groups of three females, one by a female with an infant, and one by a male. Foraging animals were usually alone. These observations describe a local sample at the southern range edge.

## vocalization

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The South African study recorded the incremental loud call and distinguished it from more conserved alarm calls; the authors used the loud call for field identification. Recordings from one region do not represent the full vocal variation of the species.

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
