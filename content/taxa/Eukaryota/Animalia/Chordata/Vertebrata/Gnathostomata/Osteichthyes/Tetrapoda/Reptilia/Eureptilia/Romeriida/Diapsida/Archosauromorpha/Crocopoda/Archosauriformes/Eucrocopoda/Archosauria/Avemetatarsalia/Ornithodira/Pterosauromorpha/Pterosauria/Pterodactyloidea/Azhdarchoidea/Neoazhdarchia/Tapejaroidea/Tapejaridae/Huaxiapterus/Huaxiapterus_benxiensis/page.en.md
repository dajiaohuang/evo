# Huaxiapterus benxiensis


## BXGM V0011 becomes referred material in the revision

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
The fixed PBDB name Huaxiapterus benxiensis retains the older named record. The 2023 study finds its holotype BXGM V0011 insufficiently distinct from H. corollatus and treats the older name as a junior synonym of Huaxiadraco corollatus. The added account follows this specimen’s preservation and development without counting two catalogue names as two independent biological species.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## A nearly complete body retains damaged trunk and humeral details

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
BXGM V0011 is nearly complete, but post-cervical vertebrae, sternum, ribs and shoulder girdle are damaged or obscured, and both humeri are incomplete. Surface damage limits cranial detail and posterior skull bones remain unfused. Near-completeness does not mean every bone is intact, and a collection-level soft-tissue label does not establish preservation of the entire membrane in this specimen.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Rostral deflection, nasal process and short wing elements support comparison

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The revision describes roughly 20° rostral deflection, a forward-inclined descending nasal process and a tall piriform orbit, with metacarpal one about 40% the length of metacarpal four and wing phalanx four about 20% of phalanx one. The broken crest still appears trapezoidal, matching the corollatus combination. Larger crest size or a longer posterior spine does not establish a separate species, and proportions retain preservation and measurement limits.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Reassessment of an incomplete humerus changes the reported proportion

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The original description gives a humerus only about 55% of femoral length. The 2023 authors attribute that value to an incomplete right humerus and identify damaged ends of the left humerus, giving a comparison near 80%. This illustrates how damage can create an anomalous proportion. The reassessment is cited without independent measurement or image inspection here; the older anomaly does not establish distinctive flight ability.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## More extensive fusion indicates relative maturity

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
Fused humeral epiphyses, carpals, first wing-phalanx extensor tendon process and tibiotarsus contrast with still-unfused posterior skull bones; damage prevents assessment of scapula/coracoid fusion. The authors infer an advanced subadult with a specimen wingspan estimate near 1.6 m. Within-group fusion comparison does not establish exact age, sex or maximum adult size.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Follow both specimens into synonymy and preservation

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
Compare BXGM V0011 with [H. corollatus](../Huaxiapterus_corollatus/page.en.md) in preservation, metacarpals and terminal wing phalanges, then growth and morphotype methods in [S. dongi](../../Sinopterus/Sinopterus_dongi/page.en.md). The original 2007 description remains incompletely read. Fixed occurrences and later referrals retain separate sources without merging sites, ages or counts.
<!-- /evo:text -->

Occurrences, collections and references are different units, not specimen or individual counts.

## Evidence limits

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
Existing sections guide PBDB occurrence records dated 2026-10-02, while the added account reads the 2023 revision; a complete monograph remains unfinished. Entries belong to the fixed 276-item pterosaur working roster, retain the current same name and a complete parent chain to the recorded Pterosauria storage anchor, and have usable species-level occurrences without uncertain-identification qualifiers. Database accepted names can connect older combinations; that database correspondence does not decide specimen identifications or all taxonomic disputes and is not merged with same-name COL concepts. Collection sites, stratigraphic intervals, depositional settings and collection-level preservation modes have distinct meanings. They do not directly establish complete range, origin or extinction time, fixed species ecology, biological abundance or the condition of every specimen. Original descriptions, revisions, morphology, diet and behavior remain incompletely assembled; external domain expert review has not occurred. The added account reuses the 2023 revision and previously read passages, with complete ZMNH M813, BXGM V0011 and D2525 specimen accounts additionally read here. Images, specimens, original 2006/2007 descriptions, other newly reported specimens, supplementary measurements, histology and diet remain incompletely read or independently checked. Referral and maturity are author interpretations; fixed parent chains, occurrence counts and ages remain intact. Complete monographs, external review and reader-facing delivery remain unfinished.
