## Fourteen-point-two millimetres describes one male
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5968859-5bdfx
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The original diagnosis reports the single adult male ZMMU A-5820 at 14.2 mm snout–vent length, not a male or female population range. The stout body has a head about as long as wide, short rounded snout and first finger reduced to a nub, 29.2% of finger II length. Outer metatarsal tubercle is absent and inner tubercle small; digit tips are rounded, only toes III–IV weakly expanded. Posterior dorsal skin is notably warty while the belly is smooth. These features form the source’s comparative diagnosis; a small body alone does not establish species identity, and the unmeasured female size remains unknown here.

## The holotype has grey-beige ventral colour
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5968859-5bdfx
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The live holotype has a grey-brown dorsum, lighter and reddish-speckled anteriorly, with darker posterior tubercles and whitish pustule centres. Head sides are dark brown with beige mottling. The grey-beige underside has weak grey marbling, denser on chest, throat and limbs than belly, and the iris is described as uniformly black. These are one male’s live observations, not immutable species-wide colours, sex comparisons or evidence of seasonal change. Preserved appearance and microtomographic skeletal findings are separate evidence and are not reproduced as though observed in a living population.

## One wet-soil encounter supports a tentative concealed lifestyle
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5968859-5bdfx
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
At 21:00 on 31 May 2016, during heavy rain, the male was found in wet soil at the bottom of a roughly 20 cm hollow under a turned two-metre rotten log, about seven metres from a cascading stream. Air temperature was 21 °C and humidity 100% in the account. The authors infer semi-fossorial or leaf-litter concealment, without measuring time underground, digging performance or a humidity requirement. Seven further field days produced no additional specimen. This local non-detection does not establish rarity throughout the range, and no recorded calling does not demonstrate inability to call.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Only Kon Chu Rang is confirmed in this original account
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5968859-5bdfx
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
The 2018 paper knows inexpectata only from Kon Chu Rang Nature Reserve, Gia Lai, on the Tay Nguyen Plateau in central Vietnam, at about 1,000 m. It anticipates other similar montane sites in central Vietnam and possibly Laos. Those predicted areas are not confirmed occurrences, continuous occupancy or a mapped range. The one type-locality elevation is not a physiological bound or a complete elevational range. Later discoveries would require their own attributable source and identity evidence before being added as confirmed distribution.

## Unknown life history underlies the dated Data Deficient suggestion
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5968859-5bdfx
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/text/en -->
Diet and reproductive biology were unknown, no calling was recorded, and range and population status remained unresolved. Well-developed testes in the examined male do not establish a breeding calendar, clutch size or developmental mode. The 2018 authors suggest Data Deficient under cited IUCN guidance and request further surveys. This is a dated proposal, not a current Red List verification or measured decline. Their explanation that secretive biology may account for few encounters is a hypothesis, not a corrected abundance estimate.

## Genus placement is a mitochondrial and morphological hypothesis
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5968859-5bdfx
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/text/en -->
The original study places the three named Vietnamophryne species in a supported Asterophryinae lineage and suggests the genus as sister to Siamophryne. Its 12S–16S mitochondrial fragment does not fully resolve every Asterophryinae lineage. The reported placement belongs to the sampled genealogy and comparative morphology, not a nuclear-genomic isolation test or proof that later named genus members share every diagnostic measurement. Single original male proportions, colours and encounter sites remain species-specific rather than being borrowed across the genus.
