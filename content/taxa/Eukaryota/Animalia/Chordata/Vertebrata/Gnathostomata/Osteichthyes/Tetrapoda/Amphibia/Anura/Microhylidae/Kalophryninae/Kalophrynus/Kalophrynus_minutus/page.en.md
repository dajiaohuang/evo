## Litter and soil observations
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12423637-v55bk
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
Adults were found on dead leaves or soft forest-floor soil, away from ponds and streams at the type locality. The authors described terrestrial, partly burrowing habits and noted difficulty detecting the small, litter-coloured frogs. Eggs and tadpoles were not observed; these adult observations do not establish the reproductive site or developmental mode.

## Eighteen measured adults and named error statistics
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12423637-v55bk
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The description reported fifteen adult males at 18.2–20.6 mm snout–vent length, with mean ± standard error of 19.52 ± 0.17 mm, and three females at 21.1–24.2 mm with mean 22.87 mm. The holotype’s snout–vent length was 19.6 mm. Standard error is retained as the named statistic, not relabelled as standard deviation. These small sample summaries are not universal adult bounds or a population-wide statistical assessment of sexual size differences.

## Live colour distinguished from preservative markings
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12423637-v55bk
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
The live-colour paragraph described a tan to beige upper body depending on environmental lighting and a lighter underside. The separate preservative paragraph described variable dark dorsal markings and a large round black groin ocellus, while the diagnosis emphasized the ocellus and finger/toe proportions. Males were described with slit-like vocal-sac openings on both sides of the tongue and without dorsal spinules, nuptial pads or arm glands. Preserved pattern descriptions are not substituted for live colour, and those male structures do not establish a breeding calendar.
