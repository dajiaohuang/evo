# Sapajus xanthosternos

## Population evidence

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
Flesher surveyed a roughly 1,000 km² forest–agroforestry landscape in southern Bahia, Brazil, over 17 years, using 754 km of transects alongside reconnaissance, interviews and key-informant reports. The paper found yellow-breasted capuchins mostly restricted to the largest forests; within this landscape, chronic hunting was judged to suppress capuchin abundance and limit distribution. The author also recorded protected populations entering agroforestry lands. These are results for a particular region and study period, not a current range-wide population or status assessment.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Evidence limits

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
Source-linked regional survey; not externally expert-reviewed. The account reports a historical southern Bahia landscape study, not current species-wide abundance, distribution or conservation status.
