## overview

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
COL26.8 lists *Leontocebus nigrifrons*; earlier ecological papers used *Saguinus nigrifrons*, while taxonomic work separated it among the nigricollis-group lineages. A two-group study at Quebrada Blanco, Peru, supplies the main foraging and habitat evidence on this page.

## fieldStudy

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
Kupsch et al. (2014) observed two groups during the 2011 wet-to-dry-season transition: one could enter roughly ten-year-old secondary forest and the other used primary forest only. The groups were recorded for 202 and 191 hours. In secondary forest, the access group captured smaller prey with lower success despite higher nocturnal Orthoptera counts; the result is limited to one site and two groups.

## foraging

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The study compared forest access for two groups of the same species; it was not a test that secondary forest is harmful to the species overall. Researchers observed fewer prey-search substrates and lower capture success in secondary forest, while habitat structure, time of day, prey accessibility and mixed-species association remain part of the interpretation.
