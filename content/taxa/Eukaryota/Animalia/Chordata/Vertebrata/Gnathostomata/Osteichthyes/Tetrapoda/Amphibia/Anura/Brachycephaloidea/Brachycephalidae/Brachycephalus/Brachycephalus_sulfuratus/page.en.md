## Variable yellow blotches limit visual identification
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7937348-5wpyg
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The 2021 review found that the yellow ventral blotches proposed in the original description varied in location and amount and could be virtually absent. Of three specimens collected near the Jurupará dam, two lacked yellow ventral spots but were assigned to B. sulfuratus using advertisement calls. The review also found that the proposed inverted-V and M-shaped cloacal marks were variable or shared. Its authors reported no unique morphological character separating the two species in either live or preserved material. They used live yellow blotches as supporting evidence for B. sulfuratus, while absence of yellow left identity unresolved between it and B. hermogenesi; preservation altered the useful colours.

## A diagnosis depends on the definition of a call
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7937348-5wpyg
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
Under the review’s note-centred approach, a continuous sequence of notes forms a call. B. sulfuratus had at most eight notes per call, all isolated, compared with at least 24 notes and both isolated notes and note groups in B. hermogenesi. The authors found 2–14 pulses per B. sulfuratus note, broader than the original description’s 7–11. Previously reported frequency intervals overlapped, so frequency alone was not the proposed distinction. If each note is instead called a whole call, these call-level note counts cannot be transferred unchanged. The reported limits are the review’s diagnostic sample summaries, not universal experimentally established limits for all populations.

## Reviewed vouchers and acoustic records revise occurrence assignments
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7937348-5wpyg
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The review reassigned vouchered records previously labelled Brachycephalus sp. nov. 1 using live yellow spots and call structure. Unvouchered records generally remained Brachycephalus sp., with B. sulfuratus considered probable; Castelo dos Bugres was an exception because later collected specimens confirmed identity. The Salto Morato reserve record was assigned using call MHNCI 133, and the authors extended that assignment to other historical records at the reserve. Separately, Paraná specimen ZUEC 16602 was left as Brachycephalus sp., probably B. sulfuratus, because reliable live-colour evidence was uncertain. These are different evidence strengths, not equally verified occurrences.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Unresolved São Paulo records are not a certified range union
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7937348-5wpyg
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
For several historical São Paulo records called B. hermogenesi, the review found insufficient details for reassessment and proposed reverting them to Brachycephalus sp., potentially either species. A Piedade genetic record was interpreted as possibly B. sulfuratus, not a universally resolved identity. Six localities listed as B. hermogenesi in the original sulfuratus description were also proposed to remain unidentified because preserved material might not distinguish them. The paper concluded that B. sulfuratus extended farther north than previously thought and proposed investigating possible contact southeast of São Paulo city. These dated interpretations do not convert all uncertain historical sites into confirmed B. sulfuratus localities or demonstrate sympatry of the two named species.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Similar calls do not certify a single species across the range
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc7937348-5wpyg
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
Using available mitochondrial 12S and 16S sequences in a concatenated, midpoint-rooted analysis, the authors interpreted B. sulfuratus as probably including more than one species under the name. They nevertheless reported a similar calling pattern across its reviewed range from southeastern São Paulo to Santa Catarina. This is an attributed 2021 taxonomic hypothesis: similarity of advertisement calls does not independently certify concept equivalence or remove the need for voucher, population and taxonomic revision work. No unnamed lineage is added here as an accepted species.
