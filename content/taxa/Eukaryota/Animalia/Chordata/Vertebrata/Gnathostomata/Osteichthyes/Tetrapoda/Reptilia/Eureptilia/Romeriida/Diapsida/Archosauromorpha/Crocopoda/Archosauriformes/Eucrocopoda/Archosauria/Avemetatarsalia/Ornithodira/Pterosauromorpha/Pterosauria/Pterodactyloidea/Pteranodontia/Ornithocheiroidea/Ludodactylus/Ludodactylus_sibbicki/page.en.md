# Ludodactylus sibbicki


## The type is a skull with a mandible

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
The 2003 naming paper designates SMNK PAL 3828, held at the Staatliches Museum für Naturkunde Karlsruhe, Germany, as the holotype. It is a nearly complete skull and mandible on a limestone slab, with many bones articulated and teeth retained in their sockets. It does not provide an associated body skeleton with complete proportions in this account. Read the described specimen separately from the database occurrence below; neither unit is a population count.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Compression and stone cutting limit crest reconstruction

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
The authors record lateral compression, substantial fractures and rightward rotation of the hyoids. The posterior crest retains its base, but its distal portion was cut away in the stoneyard before museum preparation. Presence of the preserved crest and its complete outline are different questions. The remaining contour does not supply total length or area, a soft-tissue margin or demonstrated aerodynamic function.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Reported commercial provenance has separate stratigraphic support

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The original description states that the specimen came through a commercial source, reported as quarries near Nova Olinda in Ceará, Brazil. The authors use the limestone matrix and small Dastilbe fish on the slab to support attribution to the Nova Olinda Member of the Crato Formation, retaining an Aptian age interpretation. This is not an excavation with a bed-by-bed record made by the authors. The named database collection and broad age bracket remain without deriving exact coordinates or an individual age from the slab.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## A posterior crest occurs together with teeth

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The original description emphasizes a posteriorly directed, laterally flattened parieto-occipital crest and a rounded rostral dorsum lacking a premaxillary crest. Larger curved teeth occur near the front of the jaws, with smaller teeth farther back. Dentition makes resemblance to Pteranodon a partial shape analogy rather than an identification as a toothless pterosaur. The authors also compare a lacrimal projection into the orbit and other cranial openings. Crest and tooth form alone establish neither sex, diet, flight mode nor a complete relationship tree.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## The leaf between the jaws is not identified as Yucca

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
Preparation revealed an elongate, tapering leaf with coarse parallel fibers passing between the mandibular rami. Its ventral end shows physical damage and fraying. The authors compare its appearance with living Cordyline, Yucca or Agave leaves and note that the fossil leaf form had not been formally described. Resemblance is not botanical identification and does not establish habitual leaf eating by Ludodactylus or the full composition of ancient vegetation.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Accidental ingestion and the death sequence remain interpretations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The authors propose that the leaf was mistaken for prey and lodged in soft tissues or a possible gular pouch, interpreting fraying as damage from attempts to remove it. They compare pelicans trapping debris and discuss inability to feed, weakness or infection as a possible route to death. Preserved position and damage provide observations; ingestion behavior, a pouch, starvation and sepsis are not each directly preserved. The narrative cannot become a confirmed diagnosis of cause of death.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Hyoid comparisons inform function without preserving a tongue

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
Li, Zhou and Clarke’s 2018 study includes Ludodactylus among pterosaurs with elongated ceratobranchials. Its S1 Table points to the same holotype, SMNK PAL 3828, and Frey and colleagues’ 2003 description. Comparisons of living-animal bones, muscles and function inform tongue-mobility hypotheses. Contrast-stained CT of living birds and crocodilians in that study is not evidence for muscles preserved in this fossil. Hyoid form constrains inference without directly supplying full tongue length, movement range, swallowing behavior or specific prey.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Which fossil records this name represents

Ludodactylus sibbicki is accepted in the fixed PBDB pterosaur working roster, with database parent Ludodactylus. This guide uses 1 eligible species-level occurrence record from 1 collection and 1 reference identifier. Occurrences, collections and references are different units, not specimen or individual counts.

## Age bounds of these records

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/text/en -->
Selected occurrences have an outer age envelope of 119.57–113.2 Ma, where Ma denotes millions of years ago. It combines the oldest early bound and youngest late bound among selected records, rather than a continuous record at every time or measured species origin, extinction or individual lifespan. Each collection retains its own age bracket. Broad intervals are not narrowed to precise specimen ages, and original dating interpretations are not revised here.
