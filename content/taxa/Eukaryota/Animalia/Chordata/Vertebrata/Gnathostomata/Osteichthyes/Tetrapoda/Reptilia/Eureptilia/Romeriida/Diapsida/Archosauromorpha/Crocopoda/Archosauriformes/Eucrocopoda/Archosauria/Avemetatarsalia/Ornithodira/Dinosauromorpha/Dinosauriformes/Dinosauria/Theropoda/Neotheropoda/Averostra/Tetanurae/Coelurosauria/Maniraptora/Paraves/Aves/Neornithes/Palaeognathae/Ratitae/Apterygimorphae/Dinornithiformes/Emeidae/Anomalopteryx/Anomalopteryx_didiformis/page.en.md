# Anomalopteryx didiformis


## A reference-based assembly of extinct moa

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
The eleven added genomes in Sackton and colleagues (2019) include extinct little bush moa, Anomalopteryx didiformis; the other ten additions are living palaeognaths. The companion study uses a reference-based assembly obtained by mapping moa reads to the emu genome and adds corresponding sequences to noncoding-marker alignments. Bone element, individual age and complete preservation conditions are not established from the passages read here; the species’ fossil age range is not assigned to this DNA material.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## The moa–tinamou link comes from sequence comparisons

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
The 2019 sequence analyses recover little bush moa as sister to tinamous, adding extinct moa to molecular comparisons of palaeognath history. Its sequences enter noncoding-marker alignments, but not the study’s multiway CR1 insertion screen. The full insertion screen cannot therefore be attributed directly to little bush moa. This molecular material is also not DNA testing of each PBDB occurrence below; occurrence totals are not genome sample counts.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Marker coverage is not an individual count

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The companion study adds 12,643 conserved nonexonic elements, 4,982 introns and 3,146 ultraconserved elements from the moa reference assembly, representing 99.7%, 99.3% and 99.6% of their respective marker totals. Retention depends on length relative to the emu reference or at least 200 non-N bases. High coverage means comparable sequence at these markers, not complete recovery of all genes or tens of thousands of moa individuals; reference-mapping limits remain.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Which fossil records this name represents

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
Anomalopteryx didiformis is accepted in the fixed PBDB dinosaur working roster, with database parent Anomalopteryx. This guide uses 9 eligible species-level occurrence records from 9 collections and 4 reference identifiers. Occurrences, collections and references are different units, not specimen or individual counts.

## Evidence limits

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
This batch is a guide to PBDB occurrence records dated 2026-10-02, not a complete species monograph. Entries belong to the fixed 3538-item dinosaur working roster, retain the current same name and a complete parent chain to the recorded Dinosauria storage anchor, and have usable species-level occurrences without uncertain-identification qualifiers. Database accepted names can connect older combinations; that database correspondence does not decide specimen identifications or all taxonomic disputes and is not merged with same-name COL concepts. Collection sites, stratigraphic intervals, depositional settings and collection-level preservation modes have distinct meanings. They do not directly establish complete range, origin or extinction time, fixed species ecology, biological abundance or the condition of every specimen. Original descriptions, revisions, morphology, diet and behavior remain incompletely assembled; external domain expert review has not occurred.
New genome text uses Sackton et al. (2019), published main text, four figure captions and figure 1 pixels; the read sampling, marker extraction, species-tree methods, selected results and discussion passages of Cloutier et al. (2019); and ten NCBI WGS master records. Companion papers share material rather than independent population replicates. Other figure pixels, supplements, raw sequences and analysis code were not independently examined or rerun; the companion paper was not reviewed paragraph by paragraph in full. Candidate regulatory acceleration, open chromatin and a single-region chicken reporter assay are distinct evidence levels, not functional validation across all species. Complete biology and external expert review remain unfinished.
