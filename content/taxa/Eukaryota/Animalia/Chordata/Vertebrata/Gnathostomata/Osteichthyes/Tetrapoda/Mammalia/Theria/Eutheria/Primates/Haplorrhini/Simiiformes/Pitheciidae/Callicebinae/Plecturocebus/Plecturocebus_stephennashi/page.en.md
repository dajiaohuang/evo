## overview

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
This page follows fixed COL26.8 accepted-species ID 4K5YG. Taxonomic treatments differ: the original description introduced Callicebus stephennashi as a species, while Vermeer et al. (2025) interpret its pelage within the phenotypic variation of P. caligatus; MDD v2.4 lumps it into P. caligatus. The current MDD v2.5 taxon record still lists stephennashi among the synonyms of P. caligatus, and the v2.5 taxonomy-change list contains no replacement for the v2.4 treatment. COL26.8 still lists the ID as accepted, and this page presents both treatments without deciding between them.

## morphology

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
Van Roosmalen et al. (2002) described the species from a holotype and four paratypes. Their diagnosis combines a black forehead band, silvery agouti crown through back, bright red lower forearms, and white or buffy hands and feet. The authors reported that the type material was said to come from the middle or upper Purus River in Brazil, without an exact collection locality; this is description and type-material context, not a systematic field survey.

## interpretation

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The 2025 revision compared museum specimens, found material from the Lago Canabouca area similar to the stephennashi type, and interpreted variation in the blaze, dorsum and hands/feet as part of P. caligatus. MDD v2.4 adopts the lumped treatment. Read the original description alongside the museum comparison; until COL26.8 and later treatments are reconciled, this page does not infer range-wide ecology, population status or conservation conclusions for the ID.

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
The English display label follows a familiar historical usage and the Chinese name is editorial; neither implies agreement between COL and MDD on species limits. The original type locality is imprecise, and the later lumping interpretation relies mainly on museum-pelage comparison. This page does not claim to resolve the taxonomic question. It is not yet counted at the first-round species-guide standard; there is no dossier or external expert review. This page is not externally expert-reviewed.
