## overview

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
This reading path follows COL26.8 genus ID 6LP3 and links its three pinned species: P. edwardsi, P. ibeanus and P. potto. Pozzi et al.’s molecular phylogeny, together with morphological evidence they discuss, supports a three-lineage treatment; some older studies use one species or subspecific combinations. Use COL IDs to connect source names to catalogue entries without rewriting the names used in papers.

## readingPath

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
Begin with Pozzi et al. (2015) for the taxonomic evidence, noting that the paper reports limited molecular-marker relationships. Then read Luhrs et al. (2018) for the ten-site behavioural comparison of central and eastern pottos, keeping the 51 and 28 study observations separate from population size. Finish with the 2009 Okomu nocturnal-primate report, which recorded five local sightings as P. juju and noted high-canopy observations. Oates (1984) offers historical feeding context from before the split, not a current species-level diet.

## interpretation

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The sources answer different questions: the taxonomic paper compares lineages, the 2018 study codes behaviour in two species, the Okomu report records short-term nocturnal sightings, and the 1984 article synthesizes regional feeding observations under older names. Their taxonomic frames, periods and sampling units differ and cannot be combined into genus-wide distribution, ecology or population trends.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
This is a short reading path across three species pages, not a genus review. Behaviour sample counts, local observations, historical feeding synthesis and taxonomic markers have different denominators; the sources’ differing rank treatments of P. juju and authority year for P. potto are retained. They do not establish present-day genus-wide range, ecology or conservation trend. The Chinese genus label retains the Latin name; the guide has not received external expert review. This page is not externally expert-reviewed.
