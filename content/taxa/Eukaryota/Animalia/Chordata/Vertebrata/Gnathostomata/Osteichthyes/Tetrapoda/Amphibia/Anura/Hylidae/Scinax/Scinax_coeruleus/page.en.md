## What the historical specimens show
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/sourceIds/0 -->
suarez-mayorga-2022
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
Suárez-Mayorga examined the historical Hyla coerulea specimens ZSM 2710/0/1 and 2710/0/2. Their concealed-thigh spotting resembled S. sateremawe; the author also considered their morphology and colour suggestive of the group associated with S. funereus. These are comparative observations on preserved type material, not a complete diagnosis or evidence that the current names are interchangeable.

## A revived name with unresolved limits
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/sourceIds/0 -->
suarez-mayorga-2022
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The 2022 paper recommended treating the nominal Hyla coerulea as a valid species rather than a synonym of S. x-signatus. It also noted that several named Scinax near the historical Tefé/Solimões locality could be involved. The pinned catalogue spelling is Scinax coeruleus; this dated nomenclatural proposal does not establish every modern population belonging to that concept. Ecology or reproduction attributed to S. x-signatus, S. funereus or S. sateremawe cannot be transferred through resemblance alone.
