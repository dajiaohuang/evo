# Hydrobates tristrami

## Meet this species through Hawaiian breeding nests

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
Pinned COL26.8 lists this species as Hydrobates tristrami; the 2008 paper uses the earlier combination Oceanodroma tristrami. Researchers monitored breeding nests on Laysan, Tern and East islands in the Northwestern Hawaiian Islands: Laysan in 2003–2005 and French Frigate Shoals in 2005–2008, with intermittent East Island visits confined to 2005. This is a colony study rather than a North Pacific diet or distribution survey.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Nests in burrows, vegetation and crevices

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
The 115 Laysan nest sites included earthen burrows, dense vegetation and rock crevices; monitored French Frigate Shoals nests were all in earthen burrows. The 42 measured Laysan burrows were 15–70 cm long, but concealed vegetation nests could be missed and thick-vine areas were left unsurveyed to reduce disturbance. Infrared-probe checks were spaced four, seven or about 21 days apart depending on site and year. Nest proportions and monitoring schedules do not characterize every island.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Winter laying and spring-to-early-summer fledging

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
Recorded breeding extended from boreal autumn into early summer. For 21 usable eggs on Tern in 2005, mean incubation was 44.5 days. Mean nestling periods on Tern in 2005 and 2006 were about 83.7 and 87.1 days, based on 20 and 15 chicks. Dates were estimated within nest-check intervals, and an empty nest counted as fledging only when feathering and age criteria were met. These are not continuous recordings of every hatch or departure.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Three success measures with different denominators

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
Hatching success uses laid eggs as its denominator, fledging success uses hatched chicks, and breeding success is calculated as their product. Reported breeding success was 0.16 on Laysan in 2004 and 0.27 and 0.28 on Tern in 2005 and 2006; these are not adult-survival rates. The small Laysan fledging sample included chicks estimated to be under two weeks old when first found, which were excluded from hatching-success and nestling-period calculations. Analytical samples cannot simply be combined.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Sharing burrows can give way to competition

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
Some Tern chicks shared burrows with Wedge-tailed Shearwaters in 2005 and 2006, without a detected difference in fledging success between shared and unshared nests; more fatal burrow competition was recorded in 2007 and 2008. Abandonment, rain and collapse also contributed to failures. No detected association with check frequency does not establish that every visit method is harmless: researcher-caused collapses were listed separately. Year-to-year differences implicate colony conditions and other seabirds, without proving a particular offshore food shortage caused the changes.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Historical nest estimates and unresolved differences

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The historical estimate of about 700 breeding pairs on Laysan came from nest density and habitat area; about 112–120 Tern pairs largely reflected direct nest counts. These are not 2026 counts and exclude some breeding-age birds without nests. The summary gives Tern 2006 hatching success as 0.61, while the results give 0.62; the Tern 2005 results sample of 86 also differs from Table 2’s 87. No raw-record reconciliation establishes which is correct. The authors’ replacement calculation assumes adult mortality and age at first breeding, so this study alone does not establish a species-wide decline.
<!-- /evo:text -->
