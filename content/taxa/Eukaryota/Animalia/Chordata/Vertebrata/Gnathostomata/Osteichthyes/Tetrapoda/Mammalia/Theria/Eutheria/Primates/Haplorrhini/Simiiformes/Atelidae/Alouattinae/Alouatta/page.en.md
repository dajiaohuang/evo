## distribution

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
Mourthé et al. used IUCN range maps to compare ten major Brazilian Amazon interfluves. Supplementary Table S1 lists five howler species with different map-overlap counts: Alouatta belzebul in 3 areas; A. discolor, A. macconnelli and A. nigerrima in 2 each; and A. seniculus in 5. The comparison shows different mapped distributions within the genus across the selected region; it does not describe shared morphology, intrageneric branching or how individuals cross rivers.

## overview

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
This selective reading path follows five howler species through different field questions. Start with Alouatta pigra to see how mature-fruit patches correspond to leaf-and-fruit feeding patches in two small Tabasco remnants. Compare Alouatta sara across one protected refuge and three cattle ranches in Bolivia. Then read the paired historical censuses of Alouatta arctoidea at Hato Masaguaral, followed by a six-month study of Alouatta guariba movement and encounters with other primates in São Paulo. Finish with Alouatta ululata: a recent local mangrove thesis alongside an older, broader potential-distribution model. These studies use different sites, periods and methods; they are not a common survey or a basis for genus-wide averages.

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
This remains a selective, source-linked introduction, not a complete natural-history account of Alouatta, and it is not externally expert-reviewed. The existing Amazon interfluve comparison uses IUCN range maps, not field abundance or crossing observations; its main-text Table 2 has inconsistent totals and percentages. The added reading path spans separate local studies with different methods and dates. It does not support genus-wide averages or a single shared ecological pattern.
