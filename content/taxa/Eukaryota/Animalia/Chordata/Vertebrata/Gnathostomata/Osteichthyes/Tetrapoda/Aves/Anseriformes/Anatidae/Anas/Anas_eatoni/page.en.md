# Anas eatoni

## The name and this data account

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
Anas eatoni is accepted in COL26.8 within Anatidae. This account summarizes AVONET v7 data under its HBW–BirdLife 2020 name. The source records 4 measured individuals, 4 with a complete morphological set; usable sample sizes may differ among traits.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Measured beak dimensions

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
Recorded mean beak measurements are 24.3 mm from the anterior nostril edge to the tip, 10.3 mm in width and 11.9 mm in depth at that edge. These describe separate dimensions at standardized landmarks, not field-identification cutoffs.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Wing, tarsus and mass

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
Mean unflattened wing length from the carpal joint to the longest primary is 203.2 mm; mean tarsus length is 27.5 mm. Compiled mean mass is 944.62 g, with source category Dunning. Mass comes from separate species-average information; this does not mean every morphological specimen was weighed.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Habitat, feeding and lifestyle

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The dataset assigns habitat Wetland, trophic niche Omnivore and lifestyle Aquatic. These are dominant categories for cross-species comparisons; they do not imply exclusive use of one habitat or food type.

## Evidence limits

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
This is a guide to historical AVONET v7 data. Morphological values are species averages from the listed samples, not identification diagnoses or ranges of individual variation. Mass uses separately cited literature or weighing sources; morphological sample counts must not be assigned to mass. Habitat, diet and migration are coarse categories; geography concerns historical breeding and resident ranges only. One-to-one historical crosswalks and a matching catalogue name provide the association here, not proof of equivalent species concepts after later splits or lumps. Reproduction, current populations and conservation status require other sources.
This batch uses historical same-name records from the 2015 life-history archive. Values are reported compilation medians or averages of the two middle values, except that maximum longevity uses the largest record found. They are not direct measurements of one individual, individual variation ranges, mean wild lifespans or current population surveys. The compilation can share and inherit data across historical species and subspecies. Matching name and family and the presence of same-name sparse rows support the association here; they do not establish equivalence to COL26.8 concepts or remove all inherited components from the reported summaries. Wild or captive conditions are not uniformly confirmed here, and underlying publications have not been individually reviewed.
