## A cold-acclimated captive cohort, not a wild tolerance limit
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/10/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10647746-4rdgx
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/10/text/en -->
Frogs were collected near Arkhara in September2022 and gradually cooled from 14–15°C through 8°C and 4°C to 2–3°C. Twenty days after collection, twelve Rana amurensis were split into six controls and six experimental animals. Controls remained at 5–7mg/L dissolved oxygen. The animals were not fed during captivity; the authors explain this by their seasonal wild feeding pattern, not by a new year-round diet study. These conditions delimit a cold-acclimated sample and do not establish tolerance across all temperatures, seasons or populations.

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/11/text/en -->
All six experimental amurensis shared one sealed10.3L container; oxygen declined through respiration, reaching0.1mg/L after seven days. Methods report ten further days at this level before tissue collection, with normal positions and swimming observed daily. ## Accumulation contrasts depend on tissue and exposure
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/13/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10647746-4rdgx
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/13/text/en -->
Hypoxic amurensis brain accumulated glycerol and2,3-butanediol. In the discussion, lactate concentrations in exposed dybowskii were about three times higher in brain and five times higher in heart and liver despite shorter exposure. Succinate concentrations were similar between species in liver and brain, but higher in dybowskii heart; the abstract-wide similarity therefore cannot be applied to every organ. Proposed protection from reoxygenation and the role of2,3-butanediol remain interpretations, not measured survival benefits.
