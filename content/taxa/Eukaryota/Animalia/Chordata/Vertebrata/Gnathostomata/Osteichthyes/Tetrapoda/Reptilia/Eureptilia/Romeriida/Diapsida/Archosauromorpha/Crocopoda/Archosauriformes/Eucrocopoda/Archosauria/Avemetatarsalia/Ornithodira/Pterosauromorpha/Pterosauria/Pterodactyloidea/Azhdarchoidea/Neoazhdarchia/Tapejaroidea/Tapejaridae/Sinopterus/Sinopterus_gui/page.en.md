# Sinopterus gui


## This entry retains a historically named, revised species concept

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
Sinopterus gui is an accepted name in the fixed PBDB working roster, while the 2023 study refers its holotype BPV-077 to S. dongi and treats S. gui as a junior synonym. This entry retains the database record for traceability alongside the specimen interpretation. Two catalogue entries do not directly establish two separate biological species.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Damaged bone surfaces limit observable anatomy

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
The 2023 account describes damaged, crushed bone surfaces in BPV-077. The skull is mainly exposed from the left, with breakage and displacement posteriorly. The rostrum lacks a premaxillary crest and the mandibular symphysis has a shallow crest; only the first wing phalanx is preserved, and metacarpal proportions and some cervical details cannot be reliably assessed. Missing portions do not supply a complete wing shape or exact skull posture.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Unfused bones support youth while wingspan estimates differ

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
Unfused palatal and posterior skull bones, dorsal centra and neural arches, scapula and coracoid, pelvis and tibia/fibula support a very young individual. Its specimen account cites roughly 64 cm wingspan, while another holotype-comparison passage gives about 0.8 m. This internal difference is retained without choosing a definitive body size. The discussion also rejects the earlier claim of fused dorsals rather than using that old diagnosis to infer adulthood.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Referral to S. dongi does not mean inclusion in every analysis

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The revision interprets differences from S. dongi, especially the absent premaxillary crest, as compatible with growth and maintains synonymy. BPV-077 was excluded from the morphometric dataset because of incompleteness. Its referral therefore relies on qualitative comparison and the broader interpretation, rather than every statistical model directly measuring or independently proving this specimen’s species identity.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Follow specimen, preservation and study-specific referral

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
Read BPV-077 preservation and fusion first, then the anatomical combination and 2023 synonymy account in [Sinopterus dongi](../Sinopterus_dongi/page.en.md). The original 2003 description has not been independently assembled in full, and the fixed database PROXY collection row is not a newly checked holotype locality archive. Synonymy does not merge all old records, recalculate distribution or establish diet here.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Evidence limits

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
The existing collection sections guide PBDB occurrence records dated 2026-10-02, while the added account reads the 2023 revision; together they remain short of a complete species monograph. Entries belong to the fixed 276-item pterosaur working roster, retain the current same name and a complete parent chain to the recorded Pterosauria storage anchor, and have usable species-level occurrences without uncertain-identification qualifiers. Database accepted names can connect older combinations; that database correspondence does not decide specimen identifications or all taxonomic disputes and is not merged with same-name COL concepts. Collection sites, stratigraphic intervals, depositional settings and collection-level preservation modes have distinct meanings. They do not directly establish complete range, origin or extinction time, fixed species ecology, biological abundance or the condition of every specimen. Original descriptions, revisions, morphology, diet and behavior remain incompletely assembled; external domain expert review has not occurred. The 2023 front matter, Introduction, Methods through nomenclatural acts, two holotype accounts, statistical results, species interpretations and Tables 4–5, crest sections, Nemicolopterus comments, phylogenetic results, systematic accounts and Conclusions were read. Other specimen accounts, detailed anatomical-variation discussion and part of Methods remain unread. Images, specimens, supplementary measurements and matrices were not independently inspected or recalculated. Original 2003 descriptions, complete life history, diet and external expert review remain incomplete; synonymy, juvenile size and identification retain their sources.
