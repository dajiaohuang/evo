## hollongaparFeedingStudy

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
Borah and colleagues used focal-animal sampling for a year on two groups in a 21 km² forest patch at Hollongapar, Assam. Adults spent an average 35.2% of the annual activity budget feeding; fruit made up 51% of feeding time on average, varying from 34% to 71% by month. The groups consumed 54 plant species from 32 families. These are observations from two groups in one fragmented forest, not a species-wide diet estimate.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## lawacharaSeasonality

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
At Lawachara National Park, Naher and colleagues followed one group consisting of two adult males, one adult female, one immature male and an infant from April 2019 to March 2020. Scan sampling recorded a diet dominated by fruit (51%, mostly figs) and leaves (35%). The reported home range was 50.79 ha by 90% kernel-density estimation or 47.72 ha by the grid-cell method; 33 daily-path measurements averaged 1,057.2 m. These estimates belong to that group and those methods.

## evidenceBoundary

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The Hollongapar and Lawachara studies use different sites, group samples and observation designs. Their local dietary observations can be read side by side, but they should not be pooled into one population estimate, universal home range or complete account of the species.

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
Ecological evidence is limited to two locally observed groups at Hollongapar and one group at Lawachara; the studies do not establish a range-wide diet, current distribution, population size or trend. Estimates retain each paper’s sampling and home-range method. The Chinese display name is editorial. No dossier or external expert review was added. This page is not externally expert-reviewed.
