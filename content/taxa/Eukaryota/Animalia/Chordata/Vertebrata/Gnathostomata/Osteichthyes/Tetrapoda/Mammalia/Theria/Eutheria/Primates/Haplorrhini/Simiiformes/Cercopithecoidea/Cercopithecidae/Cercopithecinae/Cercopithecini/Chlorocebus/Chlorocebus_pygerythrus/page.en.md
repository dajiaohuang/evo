## behaviour

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
A three-year study of three wild South African troops found that male–male dyads played more often than female–female or mixed-sex dyads. Monkeys also played more with kin and partners of similar dominance rank; the researchers found no preference for grooming partners or individuals that spent more time nearby.

## evidence-boundary

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
The observations came from three troops in South Africa. They support a local account of play-partner choices, not a universal rule for vervet groups or a full description of the species' social system.
