## Microsatellites classify a sampled Japanese hybrid assemblage
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10831114-dtjr
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/text/en -->
Among sixty-eight river samples, fourteen microsatellite loci classified45hybrids and23Chinese-source animals. Hybrids included14F1,22backcrosses and nine ambiguous “other” individuals; no F2 was assigned. NewHybrids used a posterior threshold≥0.8 and reference groups of seven captive davidianus and23japonicus. These are sampled assignments under a two-parent model, not a full estimate of Japanese hybrid prevalence, proof that F2 cannot occur or validation of every Chinese lineage as a separate reference population.

## Reference groups and maternal lineages answer different questions
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/10/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10831114-dtjr
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/10/text/en -->
Andrias japonicus is part of a study comparing Japanese and introduced Chinese giant salamanders. Nuclear PCA of58selected individuals separated japonicus from a Chinese group including davidianus, sligoi and an unnamedU1 lineage, while Chinese groups overlapped in later axes. A mitochondrial lineage can occur in a hybrid, so a maternal sequence alone does not certify unadmixed ancestry. The paper requests broader nuclear and morphological evidence rather than replacing the fixed accepted names with a single distance-based threshold.

## Holocene giant-salamander remains in Ehime
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/11/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12149308-dtjr
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/11/text/en -->
A 2025 study re-examines fragmentary giant-salamander bones from the Shikimizu fissure deposits at a quarry in Ozu, Ehime, Shikoku. Older deposit-age interpretation based on mammal composition was Late Pleistocene or Early Holocene; direct dating of one analyzed vertebra instead gives 4,140–3,587 calibrated years BP. This is one bone’s reported calibrated interval, not proof that the entire deposit, every associated animal or all salamander fragments have the same age. It establishes a study-reported historical occurrence, not present occupancy, a species origin time or a complete Japanese fossil distribution.

## Genus-level bone evidence has preserved and missing characters
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/12/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12149308-dtjr
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/12/text/en -->
The authors use large size, lack of spinal-nerve foramina and unicapitate vertebral transverse processes to place the remains in Cryptobranchidae. Vertebral geometry supports an Andrias assignment, and the slender, slightly curved dentary differs from their described scheuchzeri comparator. However, the maxilla and anterior dentary needed for japonicus versus davidianus sensu lato diagnosis are absent. Bone morphology alone therefore remains provisionally genus-level. A reported roughly40° spine–centrum angle also exceeds the same paragraph’s quoted19–37° Andrias reference interval; the reader retains this discrepancy rather than treating it as a fully validated diagnostic threshold.

## Calibrated dating and short mitochondrial amplification
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/14/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12149308-dtjr
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/14/text/en -->
The reported procedure extracts a greater-than30 kDa collagen fraction from a small bone fragment, measures carbon14 by accelerator mass spectrometry and calibrates with CALIB8.2. The calibrated BP interval is not an uncalibrated radiocarbon age; the read text does not supply enough raw measurements and calibration settings to reproduce its probability distribution. The DNA assay targets93 bp of mitochondrial cytochrome b excluding primers, with45 PCR cycles. This is a short maternally inherited marker, not a whole mitochondrial or nuclear genome and not an independently reconstructed population ancestry history.

## Authors identify the sampled remain with the western japonicus clade
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/15/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12149308-dtjr
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/15/text/en -->
The authors report the short cytb sequence identical to modern japonicus comparisons and recover the remain in the Western Japan clade using maximum likelihood, supporting their species assignment. This conclusion concerns the analyzed sample and the compared marker, not a genomic certification of every bone or proof that no cryptic lineage existed. Extraction took place in a dedicated ancient-DNA facility, negative controls did not amplify and two independent amplifications gave consistent sequences. Those are reported safeguards; the study does not supply genome-wide damage patterns or independent-laboratory replication in the read sections. Table1 reference sequences and the Results’ wider list of matching prefectures have different scopes and are not silently merged into one verified occurrence inventory.

## Human consumption and local loss remain hypotheses
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/16/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12149308-dtjr
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/16/text/en -->
The source describes current Shikoku populations outside the fossil locality and proposes relatively recent local loss in Ehime. Co-occurrence of salamander, fish and mammal remains leads to a suggestion of human food use or ritual, but the authors explicitly report no cut marks on these bones. The study does not directly demonstrate consumption, date an extinction event, identify a causal human pressure or measure a population trend. Cave preservation may have facilitated DNA survival, but one successful sample does not establish preservation probabilities for all amphibian habitats.
