## Fourteen adult measurements in the type series
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5534166-5x296
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
Male holotype MHNCI 10280 came from Serra do Salto, São José dos Pinhais, Paraná, Brazil, at 1120 m and measured 9.7 mm snout–vent length. Ten male measurements had mean ± standard deviation 9.9 ± 0.7 mm and range 8.9–10.7 mm; four females had mean ± standard deviation 10.1 ± 1.7 mm and range 8.3–12.3 mm. Most individuals were dark brown, with some small ventral yellow dots described. These sample summaries do not establish universal size limits or statistically demonstrated population-level sexual dimorphism.

## Patchy local records and habitat-associated density estimates
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5534166-5x296
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The 2017 account knew the species from the type locality in montane, transitional high-elevation and secondary forest, often with Chusquea bamboo. Along approximately 700 m of transect, records were patchy. The authors estimated one individual per about 2–3 m² in bamboo-dominated areas, compared with one per about 6–7 or 15–16 m² where bamboo was less common, and hypothesized a role for litter quality, vegetation and slope. These are local estimates and an attributed hypothesis, not a range-wide census or a demonstrated causal bamboo effect. The cited extent-of-occurrence estimate of 2211 ha is not the area actually occupied by frogs.

## Daytime calling does not make capture rate a density measure
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5534166-5x296
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
Males called beneath leaf litter throughout the day, with stronger activity in the morning and late day and reduced activity during warmer periods around 11:00–16:00. Despite describing the frog as locally abundant, the researchers reported capture success of about 0.5 specimen per researcher per field day and difficulty locating calls in cryptically coloured litter. That effort-based capture rate is not converted to population density, and the daily calling pattern does not establish a complete annual breeding season.

## One stomach observation and an attributed relationship
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5534166-5x296
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
A CT scan of paratype MHNCI 10285 revealed one large isopod in its stomach. This documents one consumed prey item, not a fixed species diet. The remarks cited prior molecular delimitation as consistent with the diagnosis and placed B. curupira closest to B. izecksohni among described species, although the brown B. brunneus looked more similar. This illustrates the paper’s distinction between morphological resemblance and cited phylogenetic affinity; it is not a new independent phylogenetic analysis of every population.

## Sex-specific measurements retain variable-specific sample counts
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12694819-5x296
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
In the 2025 comparative table, male snout–vent length for Brachycephalus curupira is 8.9–10.7 (N = 10) mm and female length is 8.3–12.3 (N = 4) mm, with N retained separately for each sex. Dorsal skin is smooth (n = 17) and lateral skin moderately rough (n = 17). N refers to animals observed for each variable, so the size, skin and colour denominators cannot be pooled into extra specimens. These examined ranges are not whole-species maxima, a statistical proof of population sexual dimorphism or a growth series. Smooth skin in this study can include small, inconspicuous warts; external digit absence does not alone establish skeletal absence.

## Live and preserved colours are separate evidence
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12694819-5x296
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/text/en -->
Brown and yellow ventral patches resemble brunneus, but a black-and-gold iris provides an additional sampled distinction. These are the authors’ comparative observations rather than a standardized colour-chart measurement. Colour and specimen counts vary by trait; similar colours recur among species, so a single hue is not a complete identification rule. Preserved appearance should not be substituted for live appearance.

## Advertisement-call comparisons use distinct acoustic units
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12694819-5x296
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/text/en -->
The note-centred comparison records note groups as Yes (N = 26), attenuated notes as Yes (N = 26), maximum notes per group as 5 (N = 26), and maximum pulses per note as 3 (N = 26). The table’s N counts animals with one or more recorded and analysed calls, not a universal count of notes or complete calls. A call sequence, an isolated note, a note group and a pulse are different units. These observed maxima and presence/absence statements apply to the examined recordings, not invariant limits for every population. Earlier call-centred descriptions may use different units.

## The 2025 assessment separates records, mapped habitat and risk
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/10/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12694819-5x296
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/10/text/en -->
The authors’ Table 7 records an observed elevation range of 980–1,320 metres above sea level for Brachycephalus curupira; this is not a physiological altitude tolerance. Extent of occurrence is 280.3 square kilometres, lower-bound grid area of occupancy 8, and upper-bound AOO 248, with 2 threat-defined location(s). Dashes mark unavailable mapped estimates, not zero occupied area. The study proposes LC under its assessment; this is a dated research assessment, not a freshly checked official Red List category. Records, potential habitat polygons, convex-hull EOO and two-kilometre grid AOO have different meanings. The location count is not a specimen count. Potential management and long-term recovery scenarios are not measured intervention success or a current population census.
