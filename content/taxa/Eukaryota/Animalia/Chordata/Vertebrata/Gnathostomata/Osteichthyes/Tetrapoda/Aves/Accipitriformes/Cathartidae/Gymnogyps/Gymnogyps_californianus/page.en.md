## conservation

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service recovery program counted 607 California condors worldwide on 31 December 2025: 392 free-flying in the wild and 215 in the captive population. The wild count is part of an ongoing management and release program and should not be read on its own as the size of a population independent of management.

## conservation

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
The report divided the wild birds into 98 in Arizona/Utah, 216 in California, 25 in the experimental Pacific Northwest population, and 53 in Baja California. The Pacific Northwest population is spatially separate from the California flocks and is not included in California's count of 216.

## conservation

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The same annual report recorded 16 chicks fledged in the wild, 46 captive-bred birds released, and 22 deaths among the free-flying wild population. The annual total therefore reflects breeding, releases, and losses together, rather than a trend explained by natural births alone.

## conservation

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
Of 22 wild deaths in 2025, nine were confirmed as lead poisoning and nine awaited final necropsy results. The recovery program also reported 161 confirmed lead-poisoning deaths in the wild from 1992 to 2025, representing 50% of deaths with known causes and 28% of all wild deaths. These are historical totals in the report, not a prediction of future risk.

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
This is a guide to an annual recovery-program count and is not externally expert-reviewed; it is also not a comprehensive species account, formal risk assessment, or population-viability analysis. All figures are tied to 2025-12-31 and the report's definitions; the wild total includes managed, released, free-flying birds, and the annual fledgling figure includes remote nests inferred from parental behavior.
