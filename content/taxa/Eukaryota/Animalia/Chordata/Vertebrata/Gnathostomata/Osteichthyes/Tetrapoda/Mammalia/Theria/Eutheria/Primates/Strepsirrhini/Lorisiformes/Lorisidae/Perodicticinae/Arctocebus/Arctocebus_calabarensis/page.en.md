## historicalEcology

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
Jewell and Oates’ 1969 account, based on field work in the lowland forests of southeastern Nigeria, includes the angwantibo among several lorisoid primates. It is useful as a historical regional record and ecological starting point; it predates modern distance-sampling methods and does not describe the species throughout its range.

## fieldSurvey

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
A 2024 nocturnal distance-sampling study in the Oban Hills sector of Cross River National Park, southeastern Nigeria, recorded 41 sightings across 32 transects in closed-canopy and secondary forest. The authors described mostly solitary, patchily distributed detections and observed animals using trees and lianas about 2–4 m high. The paper presents both pooled and broader 'global' estimation sets with different totals, so this page keeps the observation and habitat results local to the survey rather than presenting either total as a species-wide abundance estimate.

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
The 1969 and 2024 studies are regional and separated by decades. The newer study covers one sector of Cross River National Park; its article reports separate pooled and broader estimation sets, so neither is treated here as a species-wide population count or trend. COL26.8 ID 5W42S fixes the page identity; the Chinese display name is editorial. No dossier or external expert review was added. This page is not externally expert-reviewed.
