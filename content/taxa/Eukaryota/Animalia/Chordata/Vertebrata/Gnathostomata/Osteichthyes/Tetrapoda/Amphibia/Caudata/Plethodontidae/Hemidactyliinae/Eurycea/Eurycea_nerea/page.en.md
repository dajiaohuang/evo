## Adult tapeworm associations in a historical North American checklist
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10658874-6hf6m
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The 2023 review includes Eurycea nerea among amphibian hosts with records of adult intestinal cestodes. Its named parasite associations are Bothriocephalus typhlotritonis. These describe compiled host–parasite records, not parasites measured in every member of the species. The scope is adult worms in wild continental North American herpetofauna; larval cestode records are excluded. An intestinal association alone does not establish disease severity, host mortality, zoonotic transmission or the species’ role in completing every parasite life cycle. Prevalence, intensity, examined-host denominators and experimental susceptibility are not supplied by this host checklist.

## How to interpret the compiled parasite evidence

The authors assembled literature records into both parasite-first and host-first lists and supplied an identification key using earlier taxonomic works. The host-first list intentionally omits worms identified only to genus; a shorter list there is not evidence that genus-level records do not exist.
