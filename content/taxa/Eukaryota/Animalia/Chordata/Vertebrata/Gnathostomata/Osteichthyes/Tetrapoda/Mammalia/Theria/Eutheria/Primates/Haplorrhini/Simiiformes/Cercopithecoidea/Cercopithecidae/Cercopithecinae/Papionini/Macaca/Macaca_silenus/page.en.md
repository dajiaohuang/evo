## populationGenetics

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
Ram et al. recovered mitochondrial clades north and south of the Palghat Gap among sampled lion-tailed macaques in the Western Ghats. A fossil-calibrated analysis of 893 mitochondrial bases estimated a mean 2.11 million years to the clades' most recent common ancestor. This is a marker- and locality-bounded model result, not a genome-wide population history or species divergence date. The study also found lower HVR-I diversity at eight sampled Anamalai forest fragments than at a nearby continuous-forest population; this is not a current viability or formal conservation assessment.

## ecology

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
At a forest fragment on the Valparai plateau in Tamil Nadu, researchers followed one lion-tailed macaque troop for more than 480 hours during the 2016 dry season, comparing forest interior, edge, an open patch, and a human settlement. Active foraging was higher in the open patch and edge than in the interior, and lower in the settlement; the study also recorded plant foods from 19 species and non-plant foods. A separate study watched four troops for 375.9 hours at another 92-hectare fragment on the same plateau. Its unreviewed preprint reports that sampled subadults ate a greater diversity of native and non-native fruits than adults and dispersed fewer Ficus seeds than adult females. Both findings are limited to a few groups, one site, and one dry season; the latter is a preprint not certified by peer review.

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
This bilingual guide draws on already audited sources and is not externally expert-reviewed. Behavioural data come from a few troops at two sites; the four-troop study is a preprint not certified by peer review. These samples and mitochondrial findings do not replace a range-wide census, current population trends, genome-wide history, or a complete ecological assessment.
