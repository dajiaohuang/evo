## The male diagnosis retains a malformed head comparison
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12794644-v37rs
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The revised male SVL range is 49.0–55.4 mm. Small webbing reaches the first subarticular tubercle beside toe IV; dorsal skin ranges from sparse to prominent granulation, axillary glands are large, and a canthal stripe is present. The iris is black below and orange-red in its upper third, with bright blue sclera. Brown dorsal blotches, grey flanks with dark spots and a reticulated spotted brown venter join the diagnostic combination. The phrase “head narrower than wide” does not identify the compared dimensions, so it is not converted to a head-length/head-width ratio or silently corrected. No female size range is supplied here.

## The sylheticum sister relationship is mitochondrial evidence
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12794644-v37rs
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The revision places aryatium in the smithi group as sister to sylheticum. Reported 16S distances are 1.8–2.5% from sylheticum, 2.8–4.9% from smithi and 3.6–3.9% from rakhinense. These are marker-specific study comparisons, not universal species thresholds, full genomic reproductive isolation or grounds to pool the species’ biological records. The concatenated mitochondrial analysis contains 120 samples across the wider dataset; that number is not the count of independently sampled aryatium adults or populations.

## Monsoon encounters extend earlier Assam and Meghalaya records
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12794644-v37rs
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
Earlier records came from Garbhanga and adjoining Assam districts and Ri-Bhoi in Meghalaya. The study adds Goalpara at 53 m, Daribokgre in East Garo Hills at 1,117 m and Asanang in West Garo Hills at 486 m. Across tropical moist evergreen, semi-evergreen and moist deciduous settings, the source summarizes occurrence from about 50 m to above 1,100 m. Breeding individuals were encountered in monsoon conditions beneath leaf litter and low vegetation near slow streams. Such encounter substrates do not identify deposited egg sites, measured clutch size or a disturbance threshold; the source’s rounded range remains distinct from exact locality elevations.
