# Cheracebus medemi

## Name and type locality

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
COL26.8 accepts Cheracebus medemi (TXQM) as a species. Hershkovitz originally described the taxon as Callicebus torquatus medemi. MDD records the holotype as FMNH Mamm 70699, from near the mouth of the Río Mecaya on the right bank of the Río Caquetá, Putumayo, Colombia, at about 180 m. A type locality anchors a name; it is not a complete modern range map.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Limits of assigning older regional surveys

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
Palacios and Peres (2005) surveyed three lowland terra firme forests in southeastern Colombia and reported densities under the broader name Callicebus torquatus: 6.8 individuals/km² at Ayo, 4.8 at Caño Pintadillo, and 9.6 at Caparú. The transects were run in 2001–2002 in the lower Caquetá and Apaporis basins. This study predates the current species-level treatment; the figures are regional evidence for the historically broader complex and cannot be assigned to C. medemi as standalone population estimates without specimen-based re-evaluation.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Evidence limits

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
The page covers name and type information, MDD fields, historical regional transects and the 2025 Colombian national assessment. Old survey data remain unassigned to current C. medemi, and the national decline is suspected without a documented population time series. Current species-specific field ecology and abundance trends remain insufficient; this is not a complete species monograph. The Chinese name is editorial, and there has been no external expert review.

The 2020 museum comparison questions hand-colour diagnosis without formally synonymizing medemi or resolving its field ecology and abundance gaps.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## The 2025 Colombian assessment: a suspected decline

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
García’s Colombian national assessment, published in November 2025, lists the species as Vulnerable under A4cd+B1ab(iii). It suspects a decline of at least 30% over 25 years (three generations), citing habitat loss associated with illicit crops and timber extraction in southern Colombia, plus subsistence and local-market hunting. The page supplies no population time series. A suspected decline cannot replace a measured trend or convert historical transect estimates into species-specific estimates. The category belongs to this dated national assessment; this page does not conduct a new global assessment.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## The scope of an eleven-specimen comparison

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
Byrne and colleagues (2020) compared 100 skins across Cheracebus, including eleven assigned to C. medemi. Their focus was C. torquatus; medemi received less detailed treatment. These are museum comparisons, not population counts.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Hand colour does not settle the species boundary

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
FMNH 70700, assigned to C. medemi, has partly yellow fingers. C. lucifer specimens from one locality also vary in black and yellow hand colour. The authors questioned black hands as a sole diagnostic trait and called for further study, while retaining medemi as a species.
