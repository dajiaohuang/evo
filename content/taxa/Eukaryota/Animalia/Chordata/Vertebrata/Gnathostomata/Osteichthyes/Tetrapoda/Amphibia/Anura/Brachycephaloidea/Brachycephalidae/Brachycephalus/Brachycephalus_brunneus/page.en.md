## Sex-specific measurements retain variable-specific sample counts
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12694819-6928v
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/text/en -->
In the 2025 comparative table, male snout–vent length for Brachycephalus brunneus is 8.7–10.6 (N = 16) mm and female length is 9.2–11.4 (N = 10) mm, with N retained separately for each sex. Dorsal skin is smooth (n = 32) and lateral skin moderately rough (n = 32). N refers to animals observed for each variable, so the size, skin and colour denominators cannot be pooled into extra specimens. These examined ranges are not whole-species maxima, a statistical proof of population sexual dimorphism or a growth series. Smooth skin in this study can include small, inconspicuous warts; external digit absence does not alone establish skeletal absence.
