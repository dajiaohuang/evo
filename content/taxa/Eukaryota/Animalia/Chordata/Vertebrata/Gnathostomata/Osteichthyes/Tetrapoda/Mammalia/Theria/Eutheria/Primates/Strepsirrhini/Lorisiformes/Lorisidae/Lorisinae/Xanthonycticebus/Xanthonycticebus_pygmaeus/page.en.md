## lifeHistory

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
A captive study tracked female pygmy slow lorises before and after they moved from solitary housing into group enclosures, recording behavior and fecal cortisol. The paper used the earlier genus name Nycticebus pygmaeus; COL26.8 currently accepts Xanthonycticebus pygmaeus.

## lifeHistory

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
After group housing, the females had lower fecal cortisol levels; one female that showed stereotypic behavior while housed alone no longer showed it after moving to a group enclosure. The study discusses these changes in a captive-welfare context and did not observe the species' social organization in the wild.
