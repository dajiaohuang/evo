## Integrative evidence helps separate similar shrub frogs
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The 2021 Western Ghats study combined mitochondrial and nuclear sequences, diagnostic morphology, eye colour, calls and calling height to examine this diverse shrub-frog radiation. It described five species and proposed species groups, while leaving echinatus and indigo ungrouped and explicitly identifying unanswered questions about reproduction, ecology and natural history. Those groups are hypotheses from the examined dataset, not replacements for the pinned catalogue hierarchy or a complete global account of Raorchestes. Similar colour or a shared genus alone cannot justify transferring a relative’s body-size range, call measurements or breeding behaviour to a species with missing evidence.

## Calls and vertical positions are different evidence
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The study distinguishes pulses, individual calls, call groups and numbered call types; those units cannot be merged into a single duration. Some species have several recorded types, whereas keirasabinae and vellikkannan lacked recorded and analysed vocalisations. Missing recordings do not mean silence. Calling heights are observations above a local ground or vegetation reference, not sea-level elevations or egg-placement measurements. The species pages preserve source-specific acoustic values and table-versus-text discrepancies. Temperature and social context can alter calls, so a summary frequency or pulse rate should not be treated as an invariant recognition test.

## Single-male scans describe comparative external skull shape
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
The study scans one adult male from each of fifty-five bush-frog lineages/species and uses thirty-six external skull landmarks. ## primaryStudyScopes / sectionEvidence / evidenceSummary

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/primaryStudyScopes/0/sectionEvidence/0/evidenceSummary/en -->
The 2021 Western Ghats study combined mitochondrial and nuclear sequences, diagnostic morphology, eye colour, calls and calling height to examine this diverse shrub-frog radiation. It described five species and proposed species groups, while leaving echinatus and indigo ungrouped and explicitly identifying unanswered questions about reproduction, ecology and natural history. Those groups are hypotheses from the examined dataset, not replacements for the pinned catalogue hierarchy or a complete global account of Raorchestes. Similar colour or a shared genus alone cannot justify transferring a relative’s body-size range, call measurements or breeding behaviour to a species with missing evidence.

## primaryStudyScopes / sectionEvidence / evidenceSummary

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/primaryStudyScopes/0/sectionEvidence/1/evidenceSummary/en -->
The study distinguishes pulses, individual calls, call groups and numbered call types; those units cannot be merged into a single duration. Some species have several recorded types, whereas keirasabinae and vellikkannan lacked recorded and analysed vocalisations. Missing recordings do not mean silence. Calling heights are observations above a local ground or vegetation reference, not sea-level elevations or egg-placement measurements.
