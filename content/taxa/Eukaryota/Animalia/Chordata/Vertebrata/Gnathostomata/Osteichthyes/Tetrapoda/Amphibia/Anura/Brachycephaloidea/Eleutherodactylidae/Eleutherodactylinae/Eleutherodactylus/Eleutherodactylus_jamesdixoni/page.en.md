## Accepted name and placement

COL26.8 accepts Eleutherodactylus jamesdixoni Devitt, Tseng, Taylor-Adair, Koganti, Timugura & Cannatella, 2023 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Eleutherodactylidae (family) → Eleutherodactylus (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## Three male type specimens from Sinaloa
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10007972-nnm64
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
Adult male holotype IBH 34852 and male paratypes IBH 34849 and 34851 were collected west of El Palmito on the Concordia–Durango highway, Sinaloa, on 26 July 2011 at 1992 m. The holotype measured 21.961 mm snout–vent length, head width 8.785 mm and tibia length 8.709 mm; vocal slits were present. These measurements belong to one male, not a population range or a female size estimate. Collection date and elevation are locality metadata, not evidence of a complete annual breeding season or altitude tolerance.

## Mottled ventral pattern in the topotype comparison
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10007972-nnm64
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The diagnosis compared jamesdixoni with topotypic E. nitidus. It described contrasting dark-green and tan to pale yellow-orange dorsal markings, a pale interorbital line, dark-and-white mottling on the belly and ventral limbs and a dark chin. The compared nitidus belly was pale and unmarked, although other traits and hidden orange surfaces were similar. The authors used this combination rather than a single colour. These sampled diagnostic differences do not independently establish invariant traits in every population, toxin production or camouflage function.

## Pulsed calls distinguished from a pulsatile comparison
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10007972-nnm64
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The study recorded three jamesdixoni males and four E. nitidus males. It classified jamesdixoni calls as pulsed, with well-defined energy bursts, and nitidus calls as pulsatile, with poorly defined bursts without intermittent silence. Jamesdixoni calls were longer in that comparison, while dominant and fundamental frequencies were similar. Male denominators and the qualitative call definitions remain separate from numbers of recorded calls; the result does not establish an identical frequency in all individuals or a complete reproductive schedule.

## Late-night low-vegetation calls at 15 degrees Celsius
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10007972-nnm64
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The type series was collected in pine–oak woodland between 21:30 and 23:50 at 15 °C, with calling frogs perched about 0.5 m above ground. The authors reported E. saxatilis in the same type locality but associated it with rock outcrops. These are local encounter and coexistence observations, not an experimentally demonstrated substrate partition, a critical thermal endpoint or proof that jamesdixoni never uses rocks. The broader distribution summary remains the 2023 account’s reported geographic knowledge.

## A sister relationship with its reported support value
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc10007972-nnm64
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
The study reported about 3% 16S divergence from E. nitidus and ultrafast-bootstrap support of 78 for the sister pair, lower than the corresponding humboldti–maurus value of 96. It used call and morphological evidence alongside mitochondrial data to describe jamesdixoni. These statistics retain their particular analysis scope; they are not interchangeable with a posterior probability, a universal species threshold or independent genome-wide proof of reproductive isolation.

## A source-listed native regional occurrence
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12070909-nnm64
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
The 2025 checklist lists Eleutherodactylus jamesdixoni as native in the Sierra Madre Occidental biogeographic province of western Mexico. The authors compile and check state lists, museum-based information, VertNet and GBIF rather than newly surveying every species. Their regional scope crosses parts of Sonora, Chihuahua, Sinaloa, Durango, Zacatecas, Nayarit and Jalisco; it does not imply occurrence throughout all seven states or every habitat in the mountains. The record is attributed to the source taxonomy, not an independently examined voucher, density estimate or complete current range.

## Three conservation systems retain distinct dates and meanings
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc12070909-nnm64
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/text/en -->
The source records IUCN NE (Not Evaluated), with the full status/trend cell “NE”; Environmental Vulnerability Score “NE”; and Mexican category NL (not listed). IUCN entries use version 2024-2 accessed in March 2025, while the authors describe the national list as a static 2010 source and EVS as expert-based vulnerability scoring. These are not newly verified current categories, measured local population trends or directly comparable risk probabilities. Data deficiency, an unknown trend, no evaluation and absence from a list are different from demonstrated safety. Differences between systems do not by themselves prove one is wrong.
