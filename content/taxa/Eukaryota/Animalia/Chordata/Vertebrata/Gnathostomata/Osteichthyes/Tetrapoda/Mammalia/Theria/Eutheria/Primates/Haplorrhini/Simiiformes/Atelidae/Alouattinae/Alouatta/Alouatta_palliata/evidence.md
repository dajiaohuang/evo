## catalogue-dossier / evidence / scope

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/evidence/scope -->
A longitudinal field study of 10 groups in the Los Tuxtlas region of Mexico, with 454 monthly group visits from 2000 through 2011. Findings are specific to the monitored population, time interval and methods; they are not a species-wide demographic estimate.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## morphology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/morphology/claims/0/text -->
A comparative CT study found greater nasal-turbinate surface area in eight Alouatta palliata specimens than in 15 Ateles geoffroyi specimens (p = 0.0001); the difference persisted after adjustment for skull size. The Alouatta specimens' geographic populations and subspecies were not reported, and this anatomical comparison alone does not establish sensory function or species-wide variation.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## lifeHistory / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/lifeHistory/claims/0/text -->
For the monitored Los Tuxtlas population, the authors infer that most conceptions leading to births occurred from April to June and weaning peaked in April-May; conceptions were fewer than expected during the November-March energetic-stress period. These seasonal patterns describe this study population and period.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## ecology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/ecology/claims/0/text -->
The study followed 10 howler monkey groups in an anthropogenically fragmented Los Tuxtlas landscape and reported that the monitored population remained relatively stable overall from 2000 to 2011, with an increase in total individuals. This is not a current or range-wide population trend.

In the study's adult sleep records from Nicaragua, 54 of 55 Alouatta palliata records used the sit-in posture (98.2%); 76.4% were on horizontal substrates and 83.6% on large branches. These are observations from the sampled groups and nights, not a species-wide estimate.

## ecology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/ecology/claims/2/text -->
In comparative fruit-investigation observations, Alouatta palliata sniffed shared food plants at about three times the rate reported for Cebus imitator and less often than Ateles geoffroyi. This interspecific comparison is limited to the study's observed animals and sampled foods; it does not establish a species-wide sensory mechanism. The study reports site variation in other tactile and bite/reject behaviors.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## ecology / claims / text

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-dossier/facets/ecology/claims/3/text -->
For one Alouatta palliata group on Barro Colorado Island, the Results section reports step selection positively associated with distance to 8 m canopy gaps and with crown thickness, but not canopy height. Cross-validation accuracy was 0.31 and did not exceed random selection. The abstract and Discussion describe canopy-height relationships differently from this species-specific Results statement, so the weak model does not support a generalized canopy preference.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## ecology / claims / locator
