# Haliskia peterseni

## A species anchored by a partial skeleton

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
The 2024 description names Haliskia peterseni from holotype KK F1426, permanently housed at Kronosaurus Korner in Richmond, Queensland. It preserves the front of the skull, mandible, both ceratobranchials of the hyoid apparatus and parts of the vertebral column, ribs and limbs; the description lists 43 teeth. This is a partial skeleton, lacking major upper-arm and forearm bones. Measurements and functional interpretations first describe this individual, rather than the species’ full variation. The species epithet honors Kevin Petersen, who recovered and prepared the specimen.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## The marine context of the Toolebuc Formation

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
The specimen was collected in November 2021 at Dig Site 3 northwest of Richmond, from Albian strata of the Toolebuc Formation. The paper constrains the horizon through spore and pollen biostratigraphy; this is not direct dating of the bones. Limestone and oil shale in this Eromanga Basin unit record a marine-transgression setting. Associated remains include bivalves and indeterminate ray-finned fishes. The depositional setting explains preservation context without establishing lifelong marine activity, migration routes or the species’ complete range.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Teeth, crests and elongated hyoid bones

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The diagnostic dental combination includes fourth and fifth tooth pairs smaller than the third and sixth, and second and fifth alveolar pairs smaller than pairs three–four and six–eight. Premaxillary and mandibular crests are present, but crest shape alone does not distinguish related taxa; crushing and excavation damage affect some structures. The complete left ceratobranchial measures 280 mm and the mandible 400 mm, giving a ratio of 0.70. Although parts of the paper call this a hyoid-to-skull ratio, this account identifies the actual ceratobranchial and mandibular measurements, avoiding an assumed complete skull length. The ratio does not directly measure the soft-tissue tongue.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## How the approximately 4.6 m wingspan was estimated

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The paper estimates a wingspan of approximately 4.60 m by comparing coracoid, fourth metacarpal and first wing-finger phalanx lengths with osteologically mature relatives. The humerus, ulna and radius are absent, and later wing-finger phalanges are incomplete. This is therefore a comparative reconstruction, rather than a directly measured complete span, a maximum individual size or a species-wide range. The authors also caution that large spans inferred for other Australian pterosaurs from partial mandibles depend on comparator choice and proportions. Preserved lengths, reconstructed elements and final scale estimates should be read separately.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Relationships depend on matrices and unstable taxa

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The specimen was added to two character matrices, with approximately 30.7% and 27.6% of characters scorable. Both support placement within Anhangueria, but finer relationships differ and some results follow exclusion of unstable taxa. The discussion expresses proximity to Ferrodraco through either a sister relationship or a polytomy including Mythunga. No direct ancestor is identified from a tree, and a catalogue parent chain is not a phylogeny. The PBDB snapshot of 2 October 2026 connects the species to Haliskia and Anhangueridae. This is a supplementary catalogue path, without automatic equivalence to pinned COL26.8 concepts.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Feeding hypotheses derived from anatomy

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
No throat or thoracic contents provide direct feeding evidence. Elongated hyoid bones, curved teeth and palatal morphology led the authors to propose that a strong tongue might help restrain slippery prey. Limited wear facets also inform a hypothesis of soft-bodied prey, possibly cephalopods. These remain functional and dietary interpretations, not observed fishing, prey proportions or specialization. The paper explicitly warns that hyoid morphology alone cannot establish food preference, and proposed niche partitioning with contemporaneous pterosaurs requires better temporal constraints and independent evidence.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Evidence limits

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
This page draws on one holotype description and its comparative analyses, rather than a complete species monograph. Wingspan, soft-tissue function, diet and finer relationships remain interpretations limited by specimen and method. The locality context does not establish reproduction, sociality, migration or population size. PBDB and COL concepts remain separate. This is source-bounded editorial prose without external expert review.
This batch is a guide to PBDB occurrence records dated 2026-10-02, not a complete species monograph.
