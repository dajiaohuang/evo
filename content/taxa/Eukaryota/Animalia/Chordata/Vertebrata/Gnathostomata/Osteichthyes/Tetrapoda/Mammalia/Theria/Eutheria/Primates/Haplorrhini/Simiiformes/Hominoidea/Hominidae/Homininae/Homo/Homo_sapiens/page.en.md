## fossil

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
A study at Tam Pà Ling cave in northern Laos attributes the frontal fragment TPL 6 and tibial fragment TPL 7 to Homo sapiens. A site chronology model based on sediment luminescence dating and uranium-series and combined uranium-series–electron spin resonance results from mammal teeth places TPL 6 at 70 ± 3 thousand years and extends the record with TPL 7 to 77 ± 9 thousand years. These estimates describe the cave sequence, not direct dates on the human fossils. The authors note that TPL 7 lies 30 cm below the deepest sediment sample and may therefore be older than the modeled estimate.

## morphology

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
TPL 6 is a partial left frontal bone with a modest superciliary arch and no supraorbital torus of the kind described for archaic humans; the authors’ shape comparison places it among more gracile frontal forms. TPL 7 is a proximal left tibial fragment whose taphonomic alteration prevents accurate osteometric analysis. These two specimens from one site do not describe morphological variation across Homo sapiens.

## ecology

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
At Ilsenhöhle in Ranis, Germany, researchers analyzed collagen stable isotopes from 10 human remains alongside 52 animal remains. The results support a dietary signal dominated by large terrestrial mammals for the sampled people, associated with a cold steppe–tundra setting about 45,000 years ago. This inference comes from archaeological samples at one cave and its stratigraphic context; the isotope results do not show that every animal taxon was hunted or eaten at the site, and they do not describe a general Homo sapiens diet.

## catalogue-profile / limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
This source-linked guide draws on studies from two archaeological sites; it is not a comprehensive Homo sapiens account and is not externally expert-reviewed. Tam Pà Ling ages come from a depositional sequence model, and TPL 7 may predate its estimate; the Ranis dietary result is limited to 10 human remains at one cave. Neither study describes species-wide morphology, diet, current range, population trend, or a formal conservation status.
