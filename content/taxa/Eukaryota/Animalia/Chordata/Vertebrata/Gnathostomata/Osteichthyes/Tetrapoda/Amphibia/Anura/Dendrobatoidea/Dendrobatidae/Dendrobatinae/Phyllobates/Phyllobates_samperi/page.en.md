## Daytime activity in humid-forest understory
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc11420536-v234v
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The description characterized the species as diurnal in southwestern Colombia’s humid tropical forest understory. Adults were mainly encountered in litter near fallen logs, roots and large leaves. The authors also observed frogs in moderately disturbed forest; these encounters do not establish equivalent population performance across disturbance levels or identify the nearby objects as confirmed food sources.

## Male transport of larvae to small water bodies
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc11420536-v234v
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
Males carried tadpoles on their backs and deposited them in ground-level plant-held water, including fallen palm bracts or leaf sheaths, or in semi-permanent rain puddles. All collected back-riding tadpoles were recorded at stage 25. This describes the transported sample and observed deposition settings, without establishing a species-wide transport-stage distribution or development duration.

## Playback responses and an untested mimicry hypothesis
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc11420536-v234v
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
Males called mainly from litter or low perches and approached playback within their territories; one hopped onto a researcher’s leg. The account separately suggested that the similar colour pattern of sympatric Pristimantis gaigeae could represent Batesian mimicry, explicitly requiring study. The playback observations are behavioral evidence, while the resemblance alone does not establish a mimicry mechanism.
