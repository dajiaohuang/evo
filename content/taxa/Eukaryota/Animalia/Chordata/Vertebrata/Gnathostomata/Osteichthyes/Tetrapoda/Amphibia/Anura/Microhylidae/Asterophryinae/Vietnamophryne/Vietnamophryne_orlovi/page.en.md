## One male has a longer head and relatively developed first finger
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5968859-5bdfz
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The diagnosis reports adult male ZMMU A-5821 at 15.4 mm snout–vent length. Head width/length is 86.5%, snout/eye length 141.3%, and first finger length is 47.9% of finger II, contrasting the more reduced first finger of inexpectata. The small rounded tympanum is 5.5% of SVL. Outer metatarsal tubercle is absent, anterior dorsal skin smooth and posterior skin somewhat shagreened with small pustules; the ventral surface is smooth. These are a single male’s diagnostic proportions, not a sex-specific range or a demonstrated developmental trajectory. Female morphology is not established by this account.

## Lemon-yellow ventral colour belongs to the live holotype
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5968859-5bdfz
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The live male has a reddish-brown back, orange-brown anteriorly, densely scattered red speckles and tiny whitish posterior pustules. Narrow pale tubercular lines mark the canthus and upper eyelid. Head sides are dark brown with pale mottling, and the bright lemon-yellow underside has fine dark marbling, denser towards chest and throat. The iris is uniformly dark brown. The account distinguishes this specimen from the other original species by a combination of colours and morphology; it does not document female colour, within-population variation or a toxin-based explanation for the bright underside.

## The soil refuge was observed once, not a measured burrowing preference
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5968859-5bdfz
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
The only specimen was found at 23:00 on 9 June 2017 during heavy rain, about ten centimetres underground around fern roots on a steep Phia Oac slope, twenty metres from a cascading stream, at roughly 1,200 m and air temperature 17 °C. The type label calls the plant a tree fern, while the natural-history account tentatively identifies cf. Dicranopteris; that plant identity is not silently unified. Ten field days found no additional frog. Semi-fossorial behaviour is proposed from this encounter, not measured excavation or obligate underground residence. The surrounding forest’s 1,200–1,400 m context is not additional species-occurrence evidence.

## Phia Oac is confirmed while other ridge sites are predicted
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5968859-5bdfz
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
The original account confirms only Phia Oac Mountain in Phia Oac–Phia Den National Park, Cao Bang, northern Vietnam, at about 1,200 m. It considers similar montane forest elsewhere along the Ngan Son–Yen Lac ridge a likely setting for future records. These prospects are not confirmed occupancy across neighbouring provinces or a complete area of occurrence. The known locality is a sampled point under the original diagnosis, not evidence of absence throughout the rest of the ridge or a complete altitude tolerance interval.

## Diet, reproduction and population status were unresolved
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5968859-5bdfz
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/text/en -->
The source reports unknown diet and reproductive biology and no recorded calls during the survey. The male’s testes are described as developed, without observation of eggs, larvae or mating. The authors propose Data Deficient in 2018 and emphasize surveys elsewhere in northeastern Vietnam. This is a research recommendation, not an independently current IUCN category or quantified decline. Possible secretive biology is an encounter explanation, not proof that the population is abundant, secure or fully protected by the park designation.

## Genus placement is a mitochondrial and morphological hypothesis
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/sourceIds/0 -->
primary-pmc5968859-5bdfz
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/text/en -->
The original study places the three named Vietnamophryne species in a supported Asterophryinae lineage and suggests the genus as sister to Siamophryne. Its 12S–16S mitochondrial fragment does not fully resolve every Asterophryinae lineage. The reported placement belongs to the sampled genealogy and comparative morphology, not a nuclear-genomic isolation test or proof that later named genus members share every diagnostic measurement. Single original male proportions, colours and encounter sites remain species-specific rather than being borrowed across the genus.
