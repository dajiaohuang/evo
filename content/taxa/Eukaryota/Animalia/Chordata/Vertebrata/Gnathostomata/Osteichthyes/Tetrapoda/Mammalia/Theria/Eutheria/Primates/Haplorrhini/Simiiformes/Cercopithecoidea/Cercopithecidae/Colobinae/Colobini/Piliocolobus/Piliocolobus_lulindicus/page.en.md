# Piliocolobus lulindicus


## An adult female holotype and collection locality

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
Turni’s 2024 Berlin primate-type catalogue records holotype ZMB 32739 (old number A 48,09,98) of Piliocolobus lulindicus: an adult female represented by skull and skin, collected by Grauer in October 1908. The locality is the Lulindi River near its outlet into the Lualaba, close to Kasongo in present-day Democratic Republic of the Congo. Registration, preserved parts and collection month trace the name-bearing material; one individual does not establish species-wide variation, modern range or field abundance.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## A male from the same locality is not a paratype

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
The catalogue also lists male skull and skin ZMB 33199/33200 from the same locality and Grauer’s October 1908 collection. Turni explicitly excludes this male from paratype status because it was absent from the original description. Shared locality, month, collector and nearby registration numbers do not establish inclusion in the original type series; skull and skin numbers are not two animals. This follows the 2024 catalogue’s interpretation without a fresh reading of the 1914 description or physical-label inspection.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## The type has not been linked to a modern field concept

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The 2024 catalogue retains lulindicus as a valid species and supplies a concrete lead to name-bearing material. Its taxonomic treatment remains separate from pinned COL26.8 and the other classifications already discussed on this page. The account supplies no new genome, field diagnosis, population survey or independent assignment of historical observations to the pinned concept. It advances name provenance while leaving the first-round taxonomy/ecology correspondence gap open; existing catalogue placement, range and conservation conclusions are not revised from this account.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Precautionary species treatment and catalogue differences

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The 2020 Red List assessment records a long-running classification split: some authors treated lulindicus as a synonym of P. foai or as a form within broader red-colobus groupings, while others retained it separately. A 2016 workshop's precautionary approach kept it at species rank in that assessment. MDD v2.5 lists lulindicus under P. foai, whereas the pinned COL26.8 record accepts a separate species ID. This page preserves both catalog treatments; the material reviewed here is not enough for a full ecological account.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Range and habitat used by the 2020 assessment

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
The 2020 global Red List assessment placed the named form in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, from the Congo–Lualaba River east to a lowland–montane transition near 28.5° E, where it was reported to intergrade with *P. foai*. It gave the Lukuga River near 4.7° S as the southern limit and the Lowa River as the northern limit, with an elevation span of 450–1,800 m. The assessment said observations were from terra firme, lowland moist, and riverine forests. These are the boundaries and habitat summary used by that assessment, not a newly verified range map for the separate COL26.8 concept.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Survey summaries, inferred losses and assessment dates

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
The assessment reported that red colobus were widespread and common in three of five lowland Kahuzi-Biega survey zones in 1994–1995, but were not recorded in periodic surveys from 2004 through 2017, including places where they had earlier been classed as abundant. A 2018 rapid assessment, based on unpublished field data cited by the assessors, found the animals confirmed or highly likely to be extirpated in 76% of 102 habitat blocks covering about 45% of the western range. Hunting was identified in most cases with an assigned cause; shifting agriculture affected about one fifth of blocks assessed. The Red List classified the taxon as Endangered in 2020 and inferred a decline exceeding 50% over three generations. These are assessment-era findings and inference under the assessment’s taxonomic treatment, not a current census or a population estimate for the pinned COL concept.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Evidence limits

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
The range, habitat, and population material is from a 2020 Red List assessment that treated *P. lulindicus* as a separate taxon. MDD v2.5 now places the name under *P. foai*, while COL26.8 retains a separate ID; the source-to-catalogue concept mapping is unresolved. The assessment includes survey summaries and inference, not a current census or a newly verified range map for the pinned COL concept. This remains source-insufficient for a current full species account and has not received external expert review.
New paragraphs use Turni’s 2024 Berlin primate-type catalogue front matter, collector table and methods (PDF pp. 1–3), plus red-colobus accounts (PDF pp. 27–28, printed pp. 177–178); layouts of the latter two pages were viewed. All 44 pages, the 1900/1914 original descriptions, physical specimens/original labels and live inventories were not freshly reviewed. Catalogue valid names, historical original names, part numbers and pinned COL26.8 concepts remain separate. Type identity does not establish field assignment, diet, abundance or conservation trends. The first-round lulindicus gap remains open, without a complete monograph or external expert review.
