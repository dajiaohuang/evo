## An associated holotype among dispersed remains

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The 2019 description designates CP.V 2069, housed at Brazil’s CENPALEO, as the holotype of Keresdrakon vilsoni from bonebed C at Cruzeiro do Oeste. It preserves the anterior skull, an almost complete lower jaw, selected cervical and dorsal vertebrae, sternum, shoulder and pelvic elements and incomplete limbs. Apart from the upper and lower jaws, the bones were associated but disarticulated. Most additional referred material comprises isolated elements. Skulls and first wing phalanges support an author-reported minimum of three individuals, neither a count of every fossil nor the size of a contemporaneous population.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Long toothless jaws and limits on crest reconstruction

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
The described jaws are toothless, with a low skull and a long pointed rostrum. Occlusion leaves no gap of the kind described for tapejarines. No premaxillary sagittal crest is preserved in front of the anterior margin of the nasoantorbital opening; the incomplete posterior skull leaves a more rearward crest unknown. A small thick dentary crest occurs below the mandibular symphysis, while a short blunt ridge near its posterior dorsal end contributes to the original diagnosis. Jaw shape, the observed extent without a crest and whole-head reconstruction have different limits.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Diagnosis combines jaw, wing-finger and pelvic features

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
Beyond the short blunt dorsal dentary ridge, the original diagnosis lists a foramen in the anterior half of the ventral proximal articulation of the first phalanx of digit IV, and a foramen on the lateral ischial surface. Postcranial comparisons also involve large lateral cervical pneumatic openings, the position of proximal humeral foramina and the asymmetric sternal articulation of the coracoid. The paper warns that larger subadult Caiuajara and smaller juvenile Keresdrakon can be difficult to distinguish postcranially. Size alone cannot assign isolated bones, and compression can obscure openings.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Pneumatic cervical vertebrae and the preserved body

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
Some mid-cervical vertebrae are elongate, with tall neural spines and large lateral pneumatic openings. One broken element exposes a hollow interior with the neural canal enclosed in a bony tube supported by trabeculae. A thin sternal plate, slender gastralia, pelvis and incomplete femora and tibia supplement the holotype. Referred first wing phalanges are slightly curved and lack a fused extensor tendon process; some belong to other individuals. These structures support anatomical comparison without supplying the full neck count, soft-tissue wing outline, flight speed or exact gait.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Skeletal fusion supports incomplete maturity

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/6/text/en -->
The holotype has fused premaxillae, some jaw elements and the centra and neural arches of preserved mid-cervicals. Its scapula and coracoid, distal right humerus and pelvic elements remain unfused. The 2019 authors place it between OS2 and OS3 in their pterosaur developmental framework, interpreting the material as late juveniles to subadults. Comparative stage labels do not directly establish age in years, sex or reproductive maturity, and one holotype does not represent the species’ adult skeleton.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Two bone samples reveal growth without a complete age series

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
The study sections the shafts of ulna CP.V 2077 and incomplete CP.V 2374, tentatively interpreted as a first wing phalanx. Both mainly show fibrolamellar tissue; open periosteal vascular canals and the absence of advanced remodeling support ongoing growth. The ulna is interpreted as juvenile but not neonatal, while the other fragment has one deep growth mark. The authors associate the tissue with rapid growth but explicitly cannot rank Keresdrakon and Caiuajara growth rates from vascular arrangement alone. Two samples do not establish a complete growth curve, and a single mark is not converted here into a definite age.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## An approximately three-metre wingspan is comparative

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/8/text/en -->
Comparison of humeral length with known Tupuxuara and Caupedactylus material yields the authors’ estimate of an approximately three-metre wingspan. This is not a tip-to-tip measurement of a complete preserved wing, and the material is interpreted as immature. The estimate does not establish the species’ maximum adult size, flight range or prey-size limit.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Repeated same-bed association supports coexistence

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/9/text/en -->
The 2019 study reports closely associated Keresdrakon, Caiuajara and Vespersaurus material in bonebeds A, B and C. The repeated same-bed pattern supports the authors’ coexistence interpretation. Most bones are disarticulated, motivating a model of death and exposure around an oasis followed by accumulation during high-energy events. Shared horizons offer more specific association than presence in the same formation, without automatically demonstrating simultaneous deaths, predation or assignment of all nearby bones to one individual.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Scavenging, predation and solitary behavior remain hypotheses

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/10/text/en -->
The long laterally flattened rostrum, relatively scarce fossils and associated fauna motivate hypotheses that Keresdrakon was an opportunistic predator or scavenger and less gregarious than Caiuajara. Proposed consumption of carcasses, eggs or young animals belongs to the paper’s ecological speculation. These are not preserved gut contents or direct behavioral observations, and scarcity retains sampling, identification and taphonomic limits. A reconstructed food web therefore does not establish solitary behavior or obligate scavenging.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Relationships and stratigraphic disputes retain their sources

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/11/text/en -->
Adding Keresdrakon to an existing matrix yields six most-parsimonious trees and recovers it as sister to Tapejaridae, a non-tapejarid branch within Tapejaromorpha. This is a result of a particular 2019 analysis, without an independent rerun or a claim of consensus across later studies. The paper also argues for a local Goio-Erê exposure, discusses disagreement with mapped Rio Paraná assignment, and retains competing Early and Late Cretaceous ages. The fixed PBDB parent and broad age bracket remain separate from these published interpretations.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Evidence limits

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/limitations/en -->
The added text follows the original 2019 main article. Captions and measurement-table text were read, without independent inspection of images, fossils or thin sections, a matrix rerun or a complete collection of later material and revisions. Isolated-bone assignments, the minimum of three individuals, juvenile-to-subadult interpretation, comparative three-metre wingspan and ecological hypotheses retain their scopes. Fixed broad database ages do not resolve stratigraphic disputes. Growth, diet, behavior and species-wide coverage remain incomplete; a full monograph, external expert review and reader-visible publication are pending.

The comparative route additionally uses the already-read 2019 shared-bonebed and 2020 dinosaur-histology main texts. Samples and models apply to their respective taxa; cross-links do not complete the site fauna or all developmental research.
