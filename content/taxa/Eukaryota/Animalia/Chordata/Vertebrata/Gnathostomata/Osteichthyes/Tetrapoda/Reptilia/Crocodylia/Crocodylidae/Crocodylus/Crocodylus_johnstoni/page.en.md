## Read the study

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
How do tooth pressure and bite force describe different parts of feeding performance? This guide follows the accepted COL26.8 record C6Q4M and reads the study at its stated sample and geographic scale. In an adult bite-force experiment spanning 23 living crocodilian species, the Australian freshwater crocodile showed relatively high body-size-standardized pressure at its front caniniform teeth and comparatively high pressure at molariform teeth. The authors related its slender snout and pressure profile to feeding on small prey, while the experiment itself directly measured biting force and tooth pressure; the dietary interpretation also draws on earlier studies.

Continue with Gavialis gangeticus (COL 3FFQ3) to compare force and pressure at the measured tooth positions. The Crocodylia group guide provides the wider reading route; the neighboring page retains its own sources and limits.

## Distinguish the measured length

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
Recorded dimensions are reported maximum total length (TL) 4000 mm. SVL runs from snout to vent and differs from total length including the tail. Compiled maxima are reported material, not theoretical growth limits or typical individual size. Sex-specific values may come from different localities or studies and alone cannot quantify population sexual dimorphism.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Where and how it is active

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/3/text/en -->
In the entry, microhabitat is Arboreal/Saxicolous/Terrestrial; diet is Carnivorous; active timing is Nocturnal; foraging mode is ACT. These categories summarize activity and resource use rather than an exhaustive habitat list or fixed schedule. Positions such as arboreal and terrestrial can be combined. A carnivorous category does not specify prey, and populations, seasons and life stages may differ.

## How to read reproductive records

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
the reproductive-mode field uses the original label oviparous; sex determination is recorded as TSD; consolidated mean offspring or eggs per litter/clutch 12.7; consolidated litters/clutches per year 1. Clutch/litter summaries can use midpoints of reported means or, when means are unavailable, extrema; they are not necessarily arithmetic averages of observed clutches. Fractions do not describe non-integer young in a litter. The lowest/highest fields are defined as endpoints of reported means rather than guaranteed smallest/largest individual clutches. Reproductive and sex-determination labels do not establish fetal nutrition or uniformity across all populations.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Limits of these trait data

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/5/text/en -->
This account paraphrases the same-name ReptTraits v1-2 entry, retaining its 2023 taxonomic framework and sources for individual traits. One row per species compresses variation in sex, age, locality and populations; matching name and family do not ensure equivalent later concepts. Reported maximum length is not an absolute species limit, and reproductive means are not fixed counts in every clutch. Many mass entries are estimated from length equations and are not presented here as measured mass. Juvenile-length headers and definitions differ and are also omitted from reader conclusions. Conservation, venom, current distribution and population trends are not updated here; unrecorded fields remain gaps. Dataset citation labels preserve the source chain and do not imply that this project independently reread every original work.
