## Read the study

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/0/text/en -->
How do tooth pressure and bite force describe different parts of feeding performance? This guide follows the accepted COL26.8 record C6Q4M and reads the study at its stated sample and geographic scale. In an adult bite-force experiment spanning 23 living crocodilian species, the Australian freshwater crocodile showed relatively high body-size-standardized pressure at its front caniniform teeth and comparatively high pressure at molariform teeth. The authors related its slender snout and pressure profile to feeding on small prey, while the experiment itself directly measured biting force and tooth pressure; the dietary interpretation also draws on earlier studies.

Continue with Gavialis gangeticus (COL 3FFQ3) to compare force and pressure at the measured tooth positions. The Crocodylia group guide provides the wider reading route; the neighboring page retains its own sources and limits.
