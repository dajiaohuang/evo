## Two immature specimens, one micro-CT scan

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/topic -->
methods
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/1/text/en -->
Jones et al. (2012) describe two L. kempii specimens recovered post mortem from beaches in Wales and Scotland during 2007–2008. Both were immature. The study used specimen M009/08 for micro-computed tomography and dissection-based anatomical work; its scan was reconstructed to examine cranial structures, jaw muscles and neck anatomy. The paper's results are tied to these specimens and comparative material, not a survey of the species.

## Read the anatomy as a specimen-level comparison

morphology
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/2/text/en -->
The authors compare the skull and jaw anatomy of L. kempii with Caretta caretta and discuss broader skull-shape comparisons in Testudines. Their micro-CT account documents internal structures in M009/08, while other observations use separately identified skeletal and dissection material. The paper's interpretation of jaw movement and muscle arrangement is a study result, not a measured behavior of all individuals.

## Distinguish the measured length

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/4/text/en -->
Recorded dimensions are reported maximum straight carapace length (SCL) 750 mm. SCL measures the shell in a straight line, excluding head, neck and tail. Compiled maxima are reported material, not theoretical growth limits or typical individual size. Sex-specific values may come from different localities or studies and alone cannot quantify population sexual dimorphism.

## Limits of these trait data

<!-- evo:text /records/catalogue-profile/sections/7/text/en -->
This account paraphrases the same-name ReptTraits v1-2 entry, retaining its 2023 taxonomic framework and sources for individual traits. One row per species compresses variation in sex, age, locality and populations; matching name and family do not ensure equivalent later concepts. Reported maximum length is not an absolute species limit, and reproductive means are not fixed counts in every clutch. Many mass entries are estimated from length equations and are not presented here as measured mass. Juvenile-length headers and definitions differ and are also omitted from reader conclusions. Conservation, venom, current distribution and population trends are not updated here; unrecorded fields remain gaps. Dataset citation labels preserve the source chain and do not imply that this project independently reread every original work.
