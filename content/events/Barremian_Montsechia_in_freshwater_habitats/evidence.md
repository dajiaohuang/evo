## claims / statement

<!-- evo:text /records/claims/0/statement -->
More than 1,000 Montsechia vidalii specimens from Barremian freshwater limestones preserve flexible leafy axes and closed single-seeded fruits; their morphology, thin cuticle, rare stomata and sedimentary context support a submerged freshwater habit without establishing the ecology of the angiosperm ancestor.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## claims / confidenceRationale

<!-- evo:text /records/claims/0/confidenceRationale -->
The large specimen sample and multiple independent morphological, anatomical and depositional indicators support the habit inference. Medium confidence retains uncertainty in morphology-based placement near Ceratophyllum, distinguishes current Barremian boundaries from the paper's older numerical conversion, and avoids turning one extinct plant into an ancestral-state claim.
<!-- /evo:text -->
