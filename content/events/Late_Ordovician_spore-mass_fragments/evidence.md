## claims / statement

<!-- evo:text /records/claims/0/statement -->
Twelve spore-mass fragments from Ghaba-1 core 21 in northern Oman came from deposits assigned by the source to the Late Ordovician Caradoc using palynology and sequence stratigraphy; the atlas uses a broad Sandbian–Katian display window rather than pretending the legacy stage assignment is an exact numerical date.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## claims / confidenceRationale

<!-- evo:text /records/claims/0/confidenceRationale -->
The core, depth interval, stratigraphic unit and Caradoc assignment are explicit, but Caradoc is a superseded regional unit and the paper does not provide a modern stage-level numerical age for the fossil horizon. The display window therefore remains deliberately broad.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## claims / statement

<!-- evo:text /records/claims/1/statement -->
The Oman fragments preserve masses of naked or envelope-enclosed permanent tetrads or naked dyads, with some presumed sporangial covering; this directly supports land-plant spore production, while lamellate wall ultrastructure only suggests, and does not establish, liverwort affinity.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## claims / confidenceRationale

<!-- evo:text /records/claims/1/confidenceRationale -->
Scanning and transmission electron microscopy directly document the spore masses, covering and wall ultrastructure. The paper itself treats liverwort affinity as an interpretation and notes the absence of elaters, so that narrower qualification is retained.
<!-- /evo:text -->
