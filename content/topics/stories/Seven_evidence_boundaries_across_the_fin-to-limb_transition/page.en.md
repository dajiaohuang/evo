# Seven evidence boundaries across the fin-to-limb transition

<!-- evo:text /records/story/title -->
## Seven evidence boundaries across the fin-to-limb transition
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / dek

<!-- evo:text /records/story/dek -->
Navigate named Elpistostege, Tiktaalik, Acanthostega and Ichthyostega evidence while reading tracks, bodies, internal fin skeletons, digits and mobility models as different evidence—not as a ladder of direct ancestors.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/0/title -->
## A mosaic, not a march
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/0/text -->
A synthesis separates digit-bearing limbs, locomotor changes and full terrestriality. Its broad window organizes unlike evidence; it is not one event or an ancestor-to-descendant sequence.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/1/title -->
## Digits recorded without a body
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/1/text -->
Eifelian Zachełmie surfaces preserve quadrupedal tracks and digit impressions. A trace constrains a trackmaker and gait, but cannot supply a named body taxon, complete anatomy or direct ancestor.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/2/title -->
## One body, aquatic and tetrapod-like characters
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/2/text -->
Tiktaalik combines scales, fin rays and fish-like jaws with a mobile neck, modified ear region and robust ribs. Anatomy and setting make shallow-water hypotheses testable, not observed terrestrial behaviour.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/3/title -->
## A mobile wrist still inside a fin
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/3/text -->
Tiktaalik's pectoral fin has a robust internal scaffold and wrist-like joints that could transmit load, but fin rays remain and no free digits or track are preserved.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/4/title -->
## Digit-like rows beneath fin rays
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/4/text -->
CT imaging of MHNM 06-2067 reveals branched carpals and distal rows interpreted as digits or putative digits. Lepidotrichia still make the appendage a fin.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/5/title -->
## Eight digits do not equal land walking
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/5/text -->
Acanthostega's described forelimb has eight digits. Together with seven-digit Ichthyostega and six-digit Tulerpeton, it rejects a fixed primitive five-digit template without proving a terrestrial gait.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/6/title -->
## A mobility model, not a universal gait
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/6/text -->
Three-dimensional joint reconstruction found that Ichthyostega lacked rotations needed for a typical lateral-sequence walk. It may represent restricted early mobility or a specialization unique to this animal.
<!-- /evo:text -->
