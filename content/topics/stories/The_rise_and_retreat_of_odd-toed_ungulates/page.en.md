## The rise and retreat of odd-toed ungulates
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / dek

<!-- evo:text /records/story/dek -->
From Eocene dominance to a small modern remnant, follow 56 million years of changing herbivore worlds.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/0/title -->
## A rapid Eocene arrival
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/0/text -->
The earliest sampled perissodactyls occur during the PETM on northern landmasses, but those first fossils do not reveal an exact place or time of origin.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/1/title -->
## Browsers in a greenhouse world
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/1/text -->
Eocene brontotheres radiate in North America and Asia while western European Palaeotherium medium provides a separate leaf-browsing sample; one ecology is not assigned to every lineage.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/2/title -->
## Cooling changes the guild
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/2/text -->
Marine records resolve stepwise ice growth, while the Hampshire mammal sequence separates turnover, a hiatus and later immigrant appearances; together they do not prove one simple cause.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/3/title -->
## Specialists and survivors
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/3/text -->
Living horses, tapirs and rhinoceroses are a narrow remnant of former perissodactyl diversity, and even Coelodonta species show that extinct rhinoceros ecology was not uniform.
<!-- /evo:text -->
