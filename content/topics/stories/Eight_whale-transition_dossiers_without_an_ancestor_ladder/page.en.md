# Eight whale-transition dossiers without an ancestor ladder

<!-- evo:text /records/story/title -->
## Eight whale-transition dossiers without an ancestor ladder
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / dek

<!-- evo:text /records/story/dek -->
Follow named fossils, separate specimen assemblages and living molecular datasets while keeping anatomy, ecology, topology, model time and COL26.8 naming coverage distinct.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/0/title -->
## Several samples support one bounded inference
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/0/text -->
RR 207 and RR 208 skulls, separate dense limb bones and tooth isotopes support aquatic wading and raoellid affinity. They are not one skeleton, crown whales or proof that Indohyus directly became a whale.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/1/title -->
## A reconstruction can combine individuals
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/1/text -->
Pakicetus skull H-GSP 96231 and separately catalogued postcranial bones from Locality 62 support a terrestrial body plan. The published outline is a composite, not one articulated animal or a direct ancestor.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/2/title -->
## One holotype, a locomotor mosaic
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/2/text -->
H-GSP 18507 preserves an amphibious combination of vertebral and limb anatomy. Otter-like swimming and sea-lion-like terrestrial motion are functional models, not observed strokes or gait.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/3/title -->
## A dated occurrence is firmer than a route
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/3/text -->
MUSM 3580 fixes a roughly 42.6 Ma protocetid occurrence in Peru and preserves weight-bearing anatomy. Its walking, swimming and westward Atlantic dispersal narratives are tested interpretations, not a filmed journey.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/4/title -->
## A tiny foot is not a walking foot
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/4/text -->
Basilosaurus retains a miniature jointed pelvic limb and foot, and UM 93231 preserves measurable femoral microstructure. Neither establishes terrestrial locomotion; copulatory use remains inferred.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/5/title -->
## Tail propulsion without a preserved fluke
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/5/text -->
CGM 60584 records unfused sacrals, no weight-bearing sacroiliac joint, posterior vertebral elongation and reduced limbs. Greater tail propulsion is inferred, while a fluke is doubtful and not preserved.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/6/title -->
## Living genomes test a branch, not an age
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/6/text -->
Independent retroposon insertions support Hippopotamidae as the living sister group of Cetacea. The sampled topology does not identify a direct ancestor, date the split or place every fossil relative.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/7/title -->
## A 2009 tree is not a 2026 catalogue
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/7/text -->
The 42,335-character supermatrix sampled 87 of 89 cetacean species recognized in 2009. Its tree and clock estimates are model results, whereas COL26.8 is the pinned authority for current accepted-name routing.
<!-- /evo:text -->
