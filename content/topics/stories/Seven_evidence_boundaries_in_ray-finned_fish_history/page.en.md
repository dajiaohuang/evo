# Seven evidence boundaries in ray-finned fish history

<!-- evo:text /records/story/title -->
## Seven evidence boundaries in ray-finned fish history
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / dek

<!-- evo:text /records/story/dek -->
Move from named Devonian and Triassic specimens to molecular models and living genomes without merging fossil occurrence, inferred event time, extant topology and nomenclatural coverage.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/0/title -->
## A named Devonian anatomical mosaic
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/0/text -->
CT scans connect particular Cheirolepis museum specimens to an imperforate propterygium and hyomandibular shaft plus peg-and-socket scales. The late Eifelian occurrence is not the global first ray-finned fish or a direct ancestor.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/1/title -->
## A duplication interval from molecules
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/1/text -->
Thirty duplicated-gene pairs place teleost 3R at 286.18–267.20 Ma in the study's models, before its crown-Teleostei interval. Neither interval is a fossil first appearance or an instantaneous observation.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/2/title -->
## A fossil placement changes a calibration
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/2/text -->
The 2017 analysis places Fukangichthys and other scanilepiforms on the polypterid stem, revising which fossils constrain the living crown. The result is topology-sensitive and differs from the published 2014 placement.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/3/title -->
## Three stem teleosteomorphs at one quarry
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/3/text -->
Named Rüdersdorf holotypes and referred specimens record distinct early teleosteomorph bodies near 244 Ma. Their stem placement and one-locality sample do not establish the crown-Teleostei FAD or a worldwide radiation.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/4/title -->
## Living genomes support Holostei
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/4/text -->
A spotted-gar genome, bowfin transcriptome and comparative synteny support gars plus bowfin as sister to teleosts. These present-day data do not directly date fossils or demonstrate unchanged anatomy.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/5/title -->
## The tail complex crosses the gar–teleost split
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/5/text -->
Gar and teleost developmental series share a hypural-diastema complex, while bowfin and other comparisons expose a mosaic. Descent with loss and latent parallelism remain alternative histories.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/6/title -->
## Independent genomic markers resolve a deep split
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/6/text -->
Gene trees and chromosome-adjacency markers support Elopomorpha plus Osteoglossomorpha as sister to Clupeocephala in the sampled living genomes. This is not a full species tree or a directly observed ancient fusion.
<!-- /evo:text -->
