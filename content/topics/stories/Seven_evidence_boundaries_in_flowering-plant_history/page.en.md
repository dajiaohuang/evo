# Seven evidence boundaries in flowering-plant history

<!-- evo:text /records/story/title -->
## Seven evidence boundaries in flowering-plant history
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / dek

<!-- evo:text /records/story/dek -->
Compare calibration-sensitive clocks, dated whole plants, regional phytolith assemblages and animal-diet isotopes without turning them into one false origin or dominance timeline.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/0/title -->
## One dataset, three clock ranges
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/0/text -->
A 2026 analysis integrates 25,685 fossil occurrences with molecular data, but its crown-angiosperm interval changes radically with calibration treatment; the narrow Late Jurassic result is the authors' preferred model, not a fossil flower.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/1/title -->
## Major lineages in an Early Cretaceous model
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/1/text -->
A separate metacalibrated time tree places several major flowering-plant crown lineages near 135–130 Ma. Those estimates neither validate the atlas's legacy ranges nor record fossil first appearances.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/2/title -->
## Many specimens, one extinct habit
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/2/text -->
More than one thousand Montsechia specimens combine leafy axes, closed single-seeded fruits and depositional evidence for a submerged freshwater plant. They do not make the flowering-plant ancestor aquatic.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/3/title -->
## A dated eudicot whole plant
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/3/text -->
Leefructus links diagnostic leaves and reproductive organs in a 125.8–122.6 Ma Yixian bed, supporting an early eudicot record while leaving Ranunculaceae affinity provisional.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/4/title -->
## Roots and flowers in one monocot
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/4/text -->
Cratolirion preserves roots, leaves, anatomy and reproductive organs together at about 115 Ma. Its crown-monocot placement does not turn it into a modern lily or the first monocot.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/5/title -->
## A regional grass-community proxy
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/5/text -->
Fourteen Great Plains palaeosol assemblages record an 8–5.5 Ma increase in potentially C4 grass phytoliths. The 50–60% value applies within grasses, not to all vegetation.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/6/title -->
## Animal diets reveal C4 biomass
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/6/text -->
Tooth-enamel carbon isotopes record widespread, regionally asynchronous C4 biomass expansion around 8–6 Ma. They do not identify the exact C4 plant lineages or directly measure open-grassland cover.
<!-- /evo:text -->
