# Nine evidence mosaics in turtle and lepidosaur origins

<!-- evo:text /records/story/title -->
## Nine evidence mosaics in turtle and lepidosaur origins
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / dek

<!-- evo:text /records/story/dek -->
Move from named bones and CT volumes to competing matrices, clock calibrations and explicit Mosasauroidea–Mosasauridae boundaries without turning separate fossils into an ancestor ladder.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/0/title -->
## A rib section tests a shell model
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/0/text -->
NHM PV R 4949 shows successive bone deposition in a broadened Eunotosaurus rib, while other specimens preserve shoulder anatomy. The tissue is observed; turtle-stem placement, homology and function are tested interpretations.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/1/title -->
## Paired gastralia without a finished plastron
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/1/text -->
The Vellberg Pappochelys sample combines broad ribs and robust paired gastralia but lacks a fused plastron and complete carapace. Serial fusion is a homology hypothesis, not a frame in a literal ancestor sequence.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/2/title -->
## A complete plastron and incomplete dorsal shell
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/2/text -->
Three Odontochelys specimens preserve a developed plastron with expanded ribs and neural plates but no complete carapace. A plastron-first sequence and marginal-marine life remain interpretations, not anatomy seen changing through time.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/3/title -->
## One fossil sets a minimum, not an origin date
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/3/text -->
Caribemys holotype MNHNCu P-3209 anchors a 155.6 Ma hard minimum for crown Testudines in one best-practice analysis. Its much older posterior estimate comes from a relaxed clock, topology and priors.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/4/title -->
## A three-dimensional skull enters several trees
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/4/text -->
Micro-CT reveals the cranial mosaic of Taytalura holotype PVSJ 698. Multiple analyses place it on the lepidosauromorph stem, but a single skull does not supply whole-body anatomy, direct ancestry or a global range.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/5/title -->
## One skeleton joins morphology and molecules
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/5/text -->
CT anatomy of Megachirella PZO 628 was combined with fossil morphology and living molecular partitions. The analysis recovers a stem squamate and estimates deep divergences, but neither output is a directly observed crown FAD.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/6/title -->
## A near-complete skeleton still gives a mosaic
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/6/text -->
Synchrotron scans of Bellairsia NMS G.2022.1.1 reveal crown-like and retained states in one Jurassic skeleton. Stem placement and proposed cranial motion depend on matrices and functional interpretation.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/7/title -->
## The same block supports competing placements
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/7/text -->
Teams disagree over bones, referrals and character scores for Cryptovaranoides NHMUK PV R36822, producing crown-squamate and non-squamate trees. The atlas therefore keeps the topology contested instead of inventing a secure Triassic crown FAD.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/8/title -->
## A superfamily is not the same as its true mosasaur family
<!-- /evo:text -->

## story / steps / text

<!-- evo:text /records/story/steps/8/text -->
Explicit definitions make Mosasauroidea broader than node-defined Mosasauridae. Dallasaurus retains plesiopedal limbs, so family placement and full paddle specialization remain separate questions.
<!-- /evo:text -->
