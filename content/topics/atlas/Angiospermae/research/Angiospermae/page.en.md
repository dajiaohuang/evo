## Body, life and relationships
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/0/text/en -->
Early Cretaceous Montsechia fossils preserve leafy axes and closed, single-seeded fruits. Their bodies record plants that existed then, rather than the instant the entire group originated. Molecular clocks estimate branching times from living lineages within models: two different timelines in the study of flowering-plant origins.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/1/title/en -->
## Montsechia axes and fruits
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/1/text/en -->
Early Cretaceous Montsechia material preserves flexible leafy axes and closed single-seeded fruits. These specimen-level structures inform discussion of early angiosperm morphology and aquatic life. They do not record the moment the whole group originated.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/2/title/en -->
## Crown ages in molecular-clock models
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/2/text/en -->
Molecular-clock studies combine data from living lineages with fossil calibrations to estimate branching times. A crown group includes the last common ancestor of all living members and its descendants. Calibration and analysis choices affect the estimate, so a crown age is not a first fossil occurrence.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/3/title/en -->
## Different records of origin and ecological expansion
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/3/text/en -->
The presence of flowering plants at an age does not establish dominance across ecosystems. One aquatic fossil cannot represent the ancestral ecology of every early angiosperm. Keep specimens, model estimates and ecological expansion separate.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## atlas-profile / readerLimitations

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerLimitations/en -->
Anatomical observations, interpretations of behaviour and modelled branching times answer different questions. The selected material does not identify a direct ancestor or establish a complete distribution.
<!-- /evo:text -->
