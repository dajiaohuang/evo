# Dinosauria

## overview

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/overview -->
Dinosauria is an atlas route from early fossil samples through living birds. Its displayed 233.2–0 Ma span is a navigation composite: the older end is anchored by a named Late Triassic assemblage, not a demonstrated global first appearance or continuous fossil record. The route keeps that specimen evidence, changing family-tree hypotheses and later fossil examples distinct.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## evidence summary

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/evidenceSummary -->
ULBRA-PVT280 and its Candelária Sequence context are documented specimen evidence. Its age does not set a global dinosaur first appearance. Published morphology matrices recover competing early topologies, and the Yutyrannus integument example is limited to three named skeletons; neither should be generalized beyond its study scope.
<!-- /evo:text -->

## Early fossil sample

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/0/id -->
early-fossil-sample
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/0/title/en -->
A named specimen anchors the early fossil route
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/0/text/en -->
The holotype ULBRA-PVT280 comes from an approximately 233.2 Ma assemblage in the Candelária Sequence of southern Brazil. It preserves a partial skull, axial skeleton, forelimb and nearly complete left hind limb. This is a well-described local sample; it does not establish a global dinosaur first appearance or make Buriolestes a direct ancestor of later dinosaurs.

## Early relationships

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/1/id -->

<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/1/title/en -->
Why dinosaur family trees can differ
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/1/text/en -->
A 74-taxon, 457-character analysis recovered Theropoda and Ornithischia together as Ornithoscelida. A published reanalysis that rescored characters and added taxa recovered the traditional ornithischian–saurischian split. These are competing matrix results: character coding and sampling affect the inferred early branches, and neither analysis directly observes ancestry.
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/2/title/en -->
What three Yutyrannus skeletons preserve
<!-- /evo:text -->

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerSections/2/text/en -->
Three nearly complete Yutyrannus skeletons from the Lower Cretaceous Yixian Formation preserve long filamentous integument. The specimens document those structures in this tyrannosauroid sample; they do not establish complete body coverage, its function, or the integument of every dinosaur lineage.

## Reader limitations

<!-- evo:text /records/atlas-profile/readerLimitations/en -->
This atlas concept is marked unresolved and has no fixed PBDB taxon ID. The page follows a selected fossil sample into the atlas's living-bird navigation; 233.2–0 Ma is not an uninterrupted occurrence interval. The cited specimens and topology analyses do not form a complete dinosaur ecology or evolutionary history, and this reader draft has not received external expert review.
