## What stem, leaf and flower traits are described? {#s-e0a752ee86}
The account describes an erect, virgate subshrub with loose branching above. The last branchlets often end in an inflorescence that bends aside as a younger shoot below grows past it. Branch hairs are mostly fine, star-shaped and sparse, sometimes mixed with short gland-tipped hairs and easily broken, spreading bristles 1–1.5 mm long that arise singly or from bulbous bases. The pointed stipules are about 5–12 mm long, broad at the base, sometimes oblique or nearly clasping the stem, and hairy like the branches. Petiolate leaves have oblong, obovate-oblong or ovate-oblong blades 15–40 by 7–20 mm. Their scalloped edges stop at the wedge-shaped base; three veins arise at the base, branching like a fan. The upper surface has fine star-shaped hairs and tiny papillae, often with longer hairs; the lower surface is usually whitish and woolly when young. Terminal, open panicles bear cymes. Their bracts occur in groups of two or three, sometimes with a long, awl-shaped third bract. Peduncles and flower stalks have branch-like hairs. The thin calyx is about 6 mm long, divided beyond the middle, with broad notches and dense tiny star-shaped hairs at its base; longer bulbous-based or short gland-tipped hairs may also occur. Petals are about 7 mm long, narrowed midway into a claw; the nearly round blade sits above a claw with inrolled, faintly fringed edges. Stamens are about 4 mm long, with filaments joined at the base. The anthers are about 2 mm long and fringed. The five-lobed ovary has star-shaped hairs, especially along its seams, plus longer bristles at the tip; its stalk is 0.5 mm. The capsule is about 6 mm long, with fine star-shaped hairs between its seams and coarser hairs on them.

## How does the revision distinguish its calyx from Hermannia multiflora? {#s-fda2237fa1}
A 1980 revision notes that the calyx of H. repetenda has fine star-shaped or bristly hairs, unlike the dense brownish star-shaped wool on the calyx of H. multiflora.

## Where is it reported and how common is it in the wild? {#s-952ed93e0f}
A 2019 regional account says it is extensively cultivated but rare in the wild, and reports it from Albany Thicket and the Upper Karoo. The excerpt gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency.
