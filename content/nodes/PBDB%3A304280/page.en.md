## A small humeral crest alone does not prove flightlessness {#s-21cafb3e1b}
The 2021 authors reject inferring inadequate flight musculature solely from smaller deltopectoral crests in Hamipterus embryos. Comparable embryonic morphology and smaller absolute muscle requirements at small size weaken that inference. Crest form, muscle volume and flight ability remain separated by inference: this rebuttal neither observes Hamipterus flying nor identifies the hour after hatching when flight began.

## The quantitative models primarily use two other taxa {#s-c3e0f54c83}
The 2021 study models gliding and humeral bending using near-hatching or young Pterodaustro and Sinopterus material. Wing area comes from a living-bird regression, mass from wingspan scaling and shafts from hollow circular-tube approximations. Hamipterus is discussed but is not the species represented in those quantitative models. Their speeds, wing loadings and relative failure forces are not Hamipterus measurements.

## Bone-strength models retain material and preservation assumptions {#s-71453ac8b6}
The models compare loading capacity from humeral dimensions and cortical thickness. Circular sections simplify real shapes, and hatchlings and adults are assumed to share material properties. Some inputs combine specimens, with breakage and compression limiting measurements. Methods and results were read here without recalculation or inspection of original sections. Model support is not a measured flight-performance result for this species.

## Early flight and parental care can coexist {#s-10a8d582cb}
The 2021 discussion explicitly distinguishes precociality from an absence of parental care. Mobile offspring may still follow parents or receive protection or feeding assistance. Eggs and different growth stages buried together do not directly show Hamipterus feeding or guarding young. Care remains unresolved, and missing direct evidence does not prove wholly independent life.

## Flight changes during growth do not establish a complete ecology {#s-07cda67154}
The 2021 models suggest that size-related differences in speed, maneuverability and travel capacity could permit different ecological roles. The authors identify niche shifts as hypotheses requiring dietary and locomotor support. Cross-taxon scaling does not assign Hamipterus hatchlings to forests, adults to open spaces or establish a known age-dependent menu.

## Evidence limits {#s-4e149d6687}
The 2019 embryo study, the 2021 flight-model discussion and correction, and the 2024 palate study answer different questions. The flight calculations use Pterodaustro and Sinopterus, not Hamipterus; embryo staging and a later Sinopterus identification change how far those calculations can be applied. The palate scans describe separate specimens and do not settle age or flight ability. This account does not include the original 2014 description, the full 2017 egg study, supplementary measurements, or direct checks of specimens and images. Exact hatching and flight ages, parental care, diet and a complete growth sequence remain unknown.
