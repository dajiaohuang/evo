## What a 99-character matrix says about ‘Miacoidea’ {#s-649d7dcae1}
A matrix of 99 cranial and dental characters across 40 fossil and living carnivoramorphan terminals tests whether traditional “miacoids” lie outside crown Carnivora. Its preferred trees do not recover Miacidae and Viverravidae as simple stems to the two living carnivore branches.

## From fossils to a branching pattern {#s-985b095892}
The study scores cranial and dental features for 40 fossil and living terminals. A phylogenetic topology is a proposed branching arrangement inferred from those coded characters. In the preferred analyses, sampled Vulpavus, Uintacyon and Miacis-grade taxa fall outside crown Carnivora; the traditional “Miacidae” and Viverravidae do not form the simple successive stems once used to frame feliform and caniform history.

## A result that depends on the sample {#s-d5e92cc8f0}
This is a morphology-based result for the terminals and characters in the matrix, not a directly observed ancestor ladder. Changing which taxa are included, how a character is scored, or the analytical assumptions can move nodes. The analysis therefore challenges a convenient classification scheme without fixing one permanent tree for every early carnivoramorphan.
