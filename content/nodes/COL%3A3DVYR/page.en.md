## Faucaria nemorosa {#s-724b2cd282}
## Reduced leaf teeth appear as tiny tubercles

The 1999 revision describes a branching plant that forms small clumps. Crowded leaf pairs join at the base; the oblong-lance-shaped leaves are 25–45 mm long and have a dull grey-green surface without white flecks. Small dark-green dots visible on the surface correspond to cells beneath the epidermis. Each margin has up to four reduced teeth, sometimes with short bristles or none.

The yellow flowers have 70–90 petals. The revision compares the species with F. subintegra: its leaf teeth are nearly lost, reduced to small tubercles, and its fruit is similar. The revision places F. nemorosa in soil under shrubs and partial shade, unlike F. subintegra in exposed sandstone hollows. A separate habitat record places it in Fish River Scrub, with mainly summer rainfall, on quartzitic sandstone and Dwyka rocks.
