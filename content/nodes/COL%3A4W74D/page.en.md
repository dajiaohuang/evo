## Searsia krebsiana {#s-9e4cd09086}
Regional accounts describe a gnarled shrub or crooked small tree with glossy new leaves, and report different observations of its flowers, fruits and setting.

## A gnarled plant with distinctive leaf and fruit records {#s-ca7b11aede}
The 1993 account, under the name *Rhus krebsiana*, describes a gnarled shrub branching near the ground or a crooked small tree up to 3 m. Dark rough bark, blackish striate branches and chestnut-brown glabrous branchlets lead into three-part leaves. The dull-yellow petiole is semiterete, deeply grooved above and recorded as (11-)23(-30) mm. Sessile, leathery, sticky leaflets are described as concolorous, dark green above and slightly paler below, with stomata on the lower surface. Blades are elliptic to obovate or widely obovate; bases cuneate; tips retuse, rounded, obtuse or rarely acute, with a mucro; margins entire and slightly revolute. Venation is semi-craspedodromous: the midrib is prominent below and impressed above, secondaries are slightly prominent on both surfaces, and tertiaries are strongly reticulate above and impressed below. Terminal leaflets measure (21-)37(-70) × (11-)21(-35) mm and lateral ones (12-)27(-39) × (10-)17(-25) mm. Axillary panicles are up to 60 mm and sit within dense foliage; terminal ones are partly exposed. The account did not see flowers; it cites Engler (1883) for relatively large flowers with 1 mm calyx lobes and 2 mm corolla lobes. Drupes are oblate to obloid, shiny and light to dark brown, measuring 4.3 × 3.0 to 8.0 × 7.5 mm. Flowers and fruits are reported in succession from January through July.

## A tentative comparison and a separate habitat record {#s-ee06285cd3}
The 1993 account calls the gnarled dark stems, coprosma-like leaflets and large fruit recognizable features. It proposes a probable relationship, based on habit and venation, to the more northern *R. tumulicola*, and calls summer deciduousness with new glossy foliage unusual. A separate 2019 Eastern Cape flora lists stony or rocky soil, sand or shale, well-drained ground and full sun, and names Drakensberg Grassland, Sub-Escarpment Grassland, Dry Highveld Grassland and Albany Thicket. These regional records have no dated sampling frame or population-frequency estimate.
