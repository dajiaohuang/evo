## Momordica boivinii Baill. {#s-85fae9f5f3}
## Spindle-shaped fruit and sandy river margins

Momordica boivinii is a prostrate or climbing herb with unbranched tendrils. Its leaves are unlobed or usually shallowly divided into three to five lobes or angles. Male flowers occur in groups of one to eight, while the yellow to deep-orange corollas are darker at the base and pale beneath. Flowering is recorded for July and November through February. The fruit is spindle-shaped, 4–8 mm in diameter and up to 10 cm long. The cited habitat includes woodland, wooded grassland and river margins, mostly on sandy soils.
