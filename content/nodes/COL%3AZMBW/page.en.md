## Name and classification in COL26.8 {#s-b81532f88a}
COL26.8 (Catalogue of Life dataset 316115, released 20 August 2026) records *Crossodactylus dantei* as an accepted species. Its recorded hierarchy is Anura (order) → Hylodidae (family) → Crossodactylus (genus). This is the classification in that release; the name and placement alone do not describe the organism’s biology.

## Development and egg-laying categories {#s-8c874e9e5d}
The Atlantic Forest trait compilation codes Crossodactylus dantei as having larval development and its oviposition as aquatic. A larval-development code does not specify larval duration, all larval habitats or the degree of dependence on standing water; those require additional evidence. These are literature-compiled categories, not newly observed spawning or a developmental experiment for this species. Exact correspondence between the source taxonomy and the current accepted concept remains uncertified.

## Juvenile habitat and egg-site records {#s-01abe0b890}
The same row codes juvenile habitat as aquatic and marks eggs directly in water or on submerged substrate among the seven oviposition-site categories. The positive category summarizes a broad trait; it does not establish a specific nest architecture, substrate species or oviposition height. A juvenile-habitat code is not automatically an adult habitat preference. The compiled record does not newly establish clutch size, egg dimensions, parental attendance, spawning season or offspring survival.

## Limits of the compiled length values {#s-4e37456a03}
The row explicitly marks head width and tibia length as inferred, not an observed measurement from a new specimen. The article fills missing traits with a phylogenetic maximum-likelihood procedure; across the full pool, 139 head widths and 146 tibia lengths were inferred. Body length, head width and tibia length are retained numerically in the row-level evidence snapshot. The Methods use means when available and otherwise maxima, while the workbook length headers do not explicitly state units and do not consistently identify sex or stage. These cells are therefore not presented as new diagnostic millimetre measurements, a sex-specific range or measured species-wide variation. Upstream reference entries are preserved, but their original measurements have not been independently checked.
