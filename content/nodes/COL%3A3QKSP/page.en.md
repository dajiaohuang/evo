## What flowering pattern is described for Jamesbrittenia maxii? {#s-eed38caadb}
Jamesbrittenia maxii is described as a shrublet 15 cm to 1 m tall, with broad opposite leaves and glandular hairs on young growth. Its white, sometimes creamy or yellow flowers form loose to crowded clusters; each has a yellow throat and dark-violet streaks at the base of the lobes. The source says flowers may be present in any month.

## How variable are its stalks and flowers? {#s-d257bb52f7}
The detailed account says flower stalks are usually short, up to about 6 mm, but one cited specimen had stalks to 18 mm. Long stalks were otherwise seen around the Kuiseb River gorge, where short-stalked plants also occur. The corolla tube is usually at least 18 mm, though it can be as short as 8 mm. The account also notes that dried plants may or may not turn black.

## What places does it occupy? {#s-789976b419}
Records cited by the source come from sandy riverbeds and watercourses, with additional mentions of sandy pockets among rocks and gravel plains. These are reported habitat settings, not a complete range statement.
