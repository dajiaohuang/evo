## AODF 876: a partial skeleton in ironstone {#s-aba285e42e}
The holotype of Ferrodraco lentoni is AODF 876, nicknamed Butch, held by the Australian Age of Dinosaurs museum. The 2019 description records the front of the skull and mandible, five incomplete cervical vertebrae, and partial shoulder and wing elements. It is not a complete skeleton. Matching matrix around adjacent bones indicates articulation after fossilization, followed by fragmentation through erosion and soil disturbance before excavation. The genus name’s ferrum refers to ironstone preservation; the species honors Graham Thomas “Butch” Lenton’s support for Winton and the museum.

## The Winton horizon and a possible late survival {#s-2942b05f61}
The specimen was found in April 2017 at Belmont Station near Winton, Queensland; the type locality is AODL 245. The description assigns it to the Cenomanian–early Turonian range of the Winton Formation. Regional detrital-zircon studies motivate discussion of potentially early Turonian deposition in the northern formation, but the authors explicitly note that this type locality lacks refined age constraints. Possible late survival remains an interpretation requiring better stratigraphy, rather than a precise date measured directly from the holotype or proof of an Australian refugium.

## Tooth-size combinations and incomplete crests {#s-db9df765fe}
The two principal diagnostic features are first premaxillary and mandibular tooth pairs smaller than the other anterior teeth, and fourth through seventh pairs smaller than the third and eighth. Preserved material also shows a cranial crest, remnants of a mandibular crest and raised alveolar margins. The cranial crest is incomplete posteriorly, and the complete mandibular-crest height cannot be measured from its remnant. CT data reveal a palatal ridge, mandibular groove and replacement teeth distal and lingual to functional teeth. These observations concern AODF 876; a crest or isolated tooth alone cannot rename all indeterminate material.

## The approximately four-metre span is estimated {#s-994baa2dd2}
The 2019 paper estimates a wingspan of approximately 4 m from holotype measurements and comparison with other anhanguerians. Many tabulated lengths are marked as incomplete; parts of the ulna, radius, fourth metacarpal and first wing phalanx are crushed or distorted. Missing portions in the skeletal reconstruction rely on comparators, so the span is not a direct measurement of a complete wing or membrane. It does not establish maximum species size, an age range or flight performance. Comparisons with estimates in other papers should begin with preservation and reconstruction methods.

## Evidence for skeletal maturity {#s-2e4f29b052}
Fusion of the scapulocoracoid and ossification of the extensor tendon process on the first wing phalanx led the authors to interpret the individual as ontogenetically mature. CT scans nevertheless show unsutured carpals. Both observations remain part of the account; neither is converted into an exact age, sex or reproductive stage. A single skeleton supplies no juvenile-to-adult series from which to infer species growth rate or lifespan.

## Two 2019 analyses and later catalogue names {#s-8e2246da95}
The 2019 study uses two matrices: one pairs Ferrodraco with Mythunga, whereas the other places Ferrodraco outside a polytomy containing Anhanguera, Coloborhynchus and Ornithocheirus, pruning Mythunga because of instability. The paper uses names including Ornithocheiridae; the PBDB directory chain recorded on 2 October 2026 is Anhangueridae–Tropeognathinae–Ferrodraco. The 2024 Haliskia study compares Ferrodraco separately. Results based on different samples and definitions cannot be assembled into a unique ancestor sequence. The directory supports storage and navigation while retaining each publication’s taxonomic scope.
