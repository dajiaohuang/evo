## A species studied at different scales {#s-6bd8c6701a}
The COL26.8 checklist accepts Macaca mulatta under usage 3WWNQ. Studies from China, India and Cayo Santiago answer different questions; together they do not define a single species-wide ecological picture or population trend.

## Seasonal diet in one troop {#s-642b3859c5}
At Buxa, fruit accounted for 70.8% of recorded diet in the study-defined May–September non-provisioning period and 28.8% in the October–April provisioning period for one 64-individual troop.

## One troop's home range {#s-1187ca95fe}
The selected Buxa troop home range included patches of natural forest, mixed-species plantations and residential settlements; this is one local study setting, not a full habitat account.

## Locomotion {#s-f6dd783e0a}
The selected studies do not assess locomotion.

## Body size in the cited samples {#s-327c45c299}
The Buxa troop study did not estimate body size. A separate Cayo Santiago study measured a defined cohort of adult males and female relatives.

## Ecological role {#s-07daa666c3}
The selected studies do not assign an ecological guild.

## Heritability in one Cayo Santiago pedigree {#s-9299875e0f}
In one 2015 Cayo Santiago pedigree sample, most measured male morphometric traits were heritable under the study’s pooled models; this cohort result is not species-wide.

## What the selected studies show {#s-ee6ebcb970}
Selected evidence spans Chinese population genomics, one Buxa troop, managed Cayo Santiago cohorts and one NH-7 road segment. The 54 road deaths recorded in 2009–2010 are a local count, not a population mortality estimate or current conservation assessment.

## Body, life and relationships {#s-e66808fdcf}
Body mass, crown–rump length and canine length can be measured in one pedigreed rhesus-macaque population; seasonal feeding and daily travel require follows of another wild troop. Genetic lineages in Chinese samples address relationships at a different scale. The differences between sites and samples are part of the findings.

## One species, several study populations {#s-7902eeb75a}
The pinned COL26.8 checklist accepts Macaca mulatta. The studies brought together here sampled wild macaques in China and India, plus managed free-ranging groups at Cayo Santiago, Puerto Rico. These settings answer different questions and should not be combined into one range-wide ecological portrait.

## Genetic lineages in Chinese samples {#s-ebd40d19b2}
A population-genomic study sampled 81 wild rhesus macaques at 17 sites across China, representing five subspecies. The analysis identified five genetic lineages and reported evidence of recent gene flow. These results describe the sampled Chinese populations and the study’s models, not a complete global species tree or range map.

## Seasonal feeding at Buxa {#s-5d3d13584b}
Researchers followed one 64-member troop near Buxa Tiger Reserve from October 2013 to September 2014. Fruit made up 70.8% of recorded diet during the study-defined May–September non-provisioning period and 28.8% during the October–April provisioning period. Mean daily travel was 4.72 km and 2.58 km respectively; the comparison does not separate every seasonal influence.

## What a Cayo Santiago pedigree measured {#s-23522bd1bf}
A 2015 Cayo Santiago sample measured 125 adult males and 21 female relatives. Under the study’s pooled pedigree models, several male traits—including body mass, crown–rump length and canine length—met the authors’ heritability criterion; testicular volume did not, and its estimate was imprecise. This managed population is not a morphology survey of macaques throughout their native range.

## Mating-season records at Cayo Santiago {#s-c6f421a06c}
During the 2013 mating season, a study followed 21 high-ranking males aged 7–21 years across nine provisioned, semi-free-ranging groups. Older males were associated with fewer offspring recorded by the study. Paternity was sampled when offspring reached one year, so infants that died earlier were not assigned to sires; the result is not lifetime reproductive success.

## Road deaths on one Indian highway {#s-b3f95a1bc6}
From August 2009 to July 2010, observers recorded 54 rhesus macaques killed along an 11-km section of National Highway 7 near Pench: 27 in summer, 19 in winter and eight in the monsoon. Associations with traffic and roadside feeding are local observations, not a population mortality rate or a current threat assessment.

## Limits of these records {#s-8a5e3f2a0f}
These studies do not establish a current global range or conservation category, a species-wide wild diet and life-history synthesis, or population-wide mortality.
