## Southern-clade placement and study scope {#s-31a8ea7eb6}
The nuclear-genome trees place samples labeled ebenus, hatinhensis and laotum in a southern limestone-langur clade adjacent to the northern three-species clade. Detailed tests of three genetic clusters and lack of extensive secondary gene flow focus on northern poliocephalus, francoisi and leucocephalus, rather than resolving the full southern ebenus–hatinhensis species boundary. Keeping a name on a tree is not a re-identification of all historical field records, and sister clades are not ancestors.

## One homozygous-run value is not a population estimate {#s-9a72d75090}
For the single ebenus comparative sample, the authors report a longest run of homozygosity of about 7.09 Mb; Mb denotes millions of bases, not home range or animal count. Such runs describe agreement between the two genome copies across a region and depend on samples, reference and analysis settings. Population-level ROH comparisons are limited to the three northern species with more than three individuals, so this one ebenus value does not establish low inbreeding, population security or recovery. It supplies one molecular comparison but does not resolve the sample’s relation to field records.

## Taxonomic evidence {#s-fe57fee37a}
COL26.8 lists *T. ebenus* as an accepted species. The current MDD page also accepts it and records the common name Black Langur, the original combination *Semnopithecus (Trachypithecus) (auratus) auratus ebenus*, and a possible type locality near Lai Chau or the Fan Si Pan range in Vietnam. MDD lists Laos and Vietnam and gives its IUCN status as Not Evaluated. The name history does not resolve the taxonomic dispute: authors have treated “ebenus” as a species, subspecies, or black morph of *T. hatinhensis*.

## Disputed black morph {#s-551970fba8}
Nguyen Hai Ha et al. (2020) reported photographs of a fully black-looking group of about 5–7 langurs in Phong Nha–Ke Bang National Park, Vietnam, taken on 9 April 2019. The authors interpreted the photographed animals as the black “ebenus” morph of *T. hatinhensis*, not as a confirmed species-level record of *T. ebenus*. Their review of records near the Lao–Vietnamese border also mentions intermediate forms with differing amounts of white head markings. The photographs are a direct, dated observation at a named site, but do not by themselves establish the presence of accepted species *T. ebenus* in the park.

## Records from Laos {#s-3420fea81d}
Duckworth et al. (2010) described some black-headed langurs in southern Phou Hin Poun and around Hin Nam No, Laos, as fitting *T. (francoisi) ebenus*; they also noted uneven survey coverage and older reports that could not be reliably identified. In a 2021 survey at Phou Hin Poun, Souwideth et al. walked 64.1 km on 21 transects. The reported 35 individuals in nine groups were *T. laotum*, the study species. Separately, two local forestry staff identified “black langurs” during interviews; the paper listed them as a possible species and did not report a direct *T. ebenus* transect sighting. These materials document regional reports and identification limits, not a contemporaneous census or abundance estimate for this taxon.
