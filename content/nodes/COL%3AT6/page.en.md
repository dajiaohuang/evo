## Jaw extensions and lineage history {#brief}
Beloniformes includes sharply different jaw forms, but the evidence here is narrower than a complete order portrait: a 54-species gene tree tested relationships among familiar lineages, while a developmental study examined one halfbeak and one needlefish. Together they make jaw evolution a concrete question rather than a uniform trait assigned to every member.

## An elongated jaw has a base and an extension {#jaw-structure}
Schneider and colleagues examined early jaw development in the halfbeak Dermogenys pusilla and needlefish Xenentodon cancila using morphometrics, transcriptomics and in situ hybridization. In both studied species, elongated jaws comprise a base and a distinct extension. The authors infer that multipotent cells at the anterior dentary tip guide extension outgrowth: cells proliferate and migrate forward, while cells left behind differentiate into osteoblast-like cells and deposit a mineralized matrix with bone- and cartilage-like characteristics. This is a two-species developmental result, not a description of every beloniform jaw.

## A 54-species tree changed the comparison {#lineages-and-jaws}
Lovejoy, Iranpour and Collette reconstructed relationships for 54 beloniform species from two nuclear and two mitochondrial gene fragments, then mapped jaw form and development onto that tree. Their analysis rejected monophyly of needlefishes and halfbeaks as then classified, while supporting flyingfishes and sauries as monophyletic; flyingfishes nested within halfbeaks and sauries within needlefishes. These are results of that 2004 dataset and classification, not a substitute for checking later taxonomic revisions.
