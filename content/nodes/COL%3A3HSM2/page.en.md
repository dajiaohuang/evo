## A 2025 recovery-program count {#s-0fb045dd23}
On 31 December 2025, the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service recovery program counted 607 California condors worldwide: 392 free-flying in the wild and 215 in captivity. The wild count is part of ongoing management and releases, not a population independent of management.

## Regional counts of free-flying birds {#s-b50199de92}
The report assigned 98 wild birds to Arizona and Utah, 216 to California, 25 to the experimental Pacific Northwest population, and 53 to Baja California. It kept the spatially separate Pacific Northwest population outside California’s count of 216.

## Fledging, releases and deaths {#s-0889f364d5}
For 2025, the report recorded 16 chicks fledged in the wild, 46 captive-bred birds released, and 22 deaths among free-flying wild birds. Fledging, releases and losses all contribute to the annual total.

## Lead poisoning among recorded deaths {#s-506a95e923}
Of the 22 wild deaths recorded in 2025, nine were confirmed as lead poisoning and nine awaited final necropsy results. From 1992 through 2025, the program counted 161 confirmed lead-poisoning deaths in the wild: 50% of deaths with known causes and 28% of all wild deaths. These are historical totals, not a forecast.

## The AVONET measurement sample {#s-eb315e5462}
COL26.8 accepts Gymnogyps californianus within Cathartidae. AVONET v7 lists measurements under the HBW–BirdLife 2020 name for four birds, each with a complete morphological set. Usable sample sizes may still vary by trait.

## Bill dimensions {#s-bafa455cda}
Mean bill length, measured from the anterior edge of the nostril to the tip, is 65.3 mm. Mean width and depth at that edge are 33.2 mm and 39 mm. These are standardized measurements, not field-identification cutoffs.

## Wing, tarsus and mass {#s-b8e35e37b2}
Mean unflattened wing length, from the carpal joint to the longest primary, is 788 mm; mean tarsus length is 115.8 mm. The compiled mean mass is 8,442.74 g (source category Dunning). Mass comes from separate species-average information, not from weighing every bird in the morphology sample.

## Broad habitat and diet categories {#s-85133378a9}
The dataset assigns Shrubland as habitat, Scavenger as trophic niche and Terrestrial as lifestyle. These dominant categories support comparisons among species; they do not mean the condor uses only one habitat or food type.

## Migration field and historical latitude endpoints {#s-522630dba8}
The migration field classifies the record as sedentary. Historical breeding and resident maps span latitudes 30.72° to 37.44°. They do not fully describe non-breeding movements or provide a current position or global occupancy estimate. Geographic and morphological summaries come from different compilation steps.

## Mass at different life stages {#s-028e49766d}
The historical summary gives adult mass as 8,800 g and mass at birth or hatching as 185.3 g. Adult values may combine males, females and adults of unspecified sex; the other value describes a different life stage. They are not repeated measurements of the same individuals or a species-wide range.

## Reproductive timing fields {#s-cd283eb79d}
The archive reports female maturity, defined as age at first conception, at 2,185.4 days; one offspring or egg per litter or clutch; 56.25 days from oviposition to hatching; and 192.5 days to flight capability, which some sources define as leaving the nest. The source uses d for days. The litter/clutch field combines offspring and egg counts; a fractional summary is not a fractional individual. The timing fields refer to different events.

## The archive’s maximum-longevity value {#s-4dd2f53fd8}
The archive’s maximum_longevity_y field is 45 years, the largest longevity record found under the source method. It is not a typical or mean wild lifespan, a survival ceiling for every bird, or a record shown to be currently repeatable.

## Food-category weights {#s-0d0b7caa3b}
The archive assigns a nonzero food weight of 100% to scavenged food and related discarded material. Source code A indicates high compiler confidence in the source and diet information. The percentages standardize verbal descriptions rather than measure diet composition; a zero does not prove an item is never eaten. Nect, Seed and PlantO are broader categories than their everyday names and do not imply an exclusive diet.

## Recorded foraging stratum {#s-35fd223c1f}
The archive’s species-level foraging-stratum value is 100% for ground. It estimates relative use of feeding positions, not nest sites, all habitat or a whole-day time budget. Surface-water, understory, mid/high, canopy and aerial categories have different source definitions; they are not elevation ranges and cannot yield flight heights or habitat area.

## catalogue-profile / limitations {#s-63e9f35e04}
The 2025 recovery-program count is a dated management total, not a formal extinction-risk assessment or population-viability analysis. It follows the report’s definitions; the wild total includes managed, released and free-flying birds, and the fledgling count includes remote nests inferred from parental behavior.

AVONET v7 morphology values are species averages for the listed samples, not identification diagnoses or individual ranges. Mass uses separate sources and cannot inherit the morphology sample count. Habitat, diet and migration are broad categories; geography is limited to historical breeding and resident ranges. Name matches across historical classifications support these comparisons but do not prove equivalence after species splits or lumps.

Life-history values come from same-name rows in a 2015 archive. They are compiled medians or means of middle values, except maximum longevity, which is the largest collected record. They are not direct measurements of one bird, individual ranges, mean wild lifespan or a current population survey. Historical data may be shared or inherited among species and subspecies. Wild or captive conditions are not uniformly confirmed, and underlying publications have not been individually reviewed.

EltonTraits 1.0 values are historical same-name, same-family assignments, not a complete species account. Food and avian-stratum percentages are standardized from literature descriptions, not direct measurements; A/B and ABC codes retain source uncertainty. Mammal diet or activity assignments may refer to a congener considered ecologically similar. Genus- or family-level values and non-species-level strata do not support species-specific conclusions here. Individual, seasonal and local variation, current range, population and conservation require other evidence.
