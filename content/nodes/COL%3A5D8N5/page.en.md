## Sample and historical name {#s-8e9f4724b3}
COL26.8 accepts Zosterops mayottensis in Zosteropidae. The AVONET v7 account retains the HBW–BirdLife 2020 name and records 5 measured individuals, 4 with a complete morphological set. The usable sample may vary among traits.

## Beak dimensions {#s-aaa3fd2e40}
Mean beak length from the front edge of the nostril to the tip is 7 mm; width and depth at that landmark average 2.7 mm and 3 mm. These are separate standardized measurements, not field-identification thresholds.

## Wing, tarsus and mass {#s-744f0e27e5}
Unflattened wing length from the carpal joint to the longest primary averages 52.6 mm, and tarsus length averages 15.7 mm. The compiled mean mass is 9.9 g, classed as Dunning. This species-average value comes from a separate source, not from weighing each bird in the morphological sample.

## Habitat and feeding categories {#s-69a7aff23a}
AVONET classifies habitat as Forest, trophic niche as Omnivore and primary lifestyle as Insessorial, a chiefly perching way of life. These broad labels help compare records; they do not mean the bird uses only forest or one food source.

## Migration and historical map {#s-24f1f43fc3}
The migration category is Sedentary. Historical breeding and resident maps span latitude endpoints −13° to −12.66°. This summary omits a complete account of non-breeding movements and does not establish current occupancy throughout the map. Geography and morphology were compiled separately.

## Two EltonTraits field groups {#s-f91785bd29}
EltonTraits 1.0 also has a historical same-name, same-family record for Zosterops mayottensis, with diet and foraging-position fields. It follows an earlier bird classification. The name link to COL26.8 helps locate the record but does not settle later species splits or synonymies.

## Relative weights of food categories {#s-96c3318075}
The diet record assigns 50% relative weight to invertebrates and 50% to other plant material, a category that also includes fungi. Code B indicates reasonable confidence in the source but possible uncertainty in the proportions. The percentages standardize verbal descriptions rather than measure diet composition; a zero category would not prove that the bird never eats that food.

## Feeding positions below the canopy {#s-ef9156f5e0}
The species-level foraging record assigns 60% to understory, edges or shrubs below 2 m and 40% to trees or tall bushes from 2 m upward but below the canopy. These are estimates of relative feeding-position use, not nest sites, a complete habitat description or whole-day time budgets. The categories are not elevation bands and cannot give flight height or habitat area.

## Separate source labels {#s-89ee21935a}
The archive links the diet group to Ref_13 and the foraging group to Ref_50. These are separate source trails for food categories and feeding positions. The labels support attribution; they do not add new field observations or turn the estimates into direct measurements.

## Scope of these historical summaries {#s-fa6bdd5ce4}
The AVONET measurements are sample averages rather than diagnostic values or individual-variation ranges; mass comes from a separate source. Habitat, diet and migration are broad categories, and the map covers historical breeding and resident ranges. EltonTraits weights are standardized estimates from literature descriptions, not direct measurements of intake, energy or observation time; code B also leaves the relative proportions uncertain. Historical names connect the records to COL26.8 but do not establish equivalent species concepts, current range, population or conservation status.
