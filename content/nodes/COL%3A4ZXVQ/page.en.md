## Snout–vent length leaves out the tail {#s-544d998499}
ReptTraits reports a maximum snout–vent length (SVL) of 57 mm and a female maximum of 54.2 mm. SVL measures from the snout to the vent, excluding the tail. These compiled records do not define typical size or a biological maximum. The sex-specific figures may come from different localities or studies, so their difference alone cannot establish the size contrast within one population.

These are broad labels for the cited record, not a full habitat inventory or a schedule followed by every animal. “Carnivorous” does not identify prey, and the row cannot resolve differences among seasons, ages or populations.

## Egg-laying and clutch summaries {#s-3f343ddf24}
The source labels the species oviparous, meaning it lays eggs. Its consolidated clutch/litter value is 2; the lowest and highest reported means are also 2. Such summaries may be midpoints of published means, or of reported extremes when means are absent, rather than arithmetic averages of observed clutches. The endpoints describe reported means, not guaranteed extremes for individual clutches. These categories do not establish embryo nutrition or uniformity among populations.

The first field combines offspring and egg counts; the fractional value is a statistical summary, not a clutch containing half an animal or egg. ## Read the summaries within their limits {#s-df94f03371}
The ReptTraits account summarizes a same-name v1-2 row under its 2023 naming framework. Its size and clutch values are compiled reports, and one row compresses variation in age, sex, place and population. Historical name-and-family matches do not prove equivalence with COL26.8 concepts. Some workbook masses are estimated from length equations; juvenile-length headings also vary in definition, so neither is presented here as direct evidence. This page does not update conservation, venom, current distribution or population trends, and its citations do not mean every original study was independently reread.

The separate 2015 archive reports medians or averages of the two middle values, except for maximum longevity, which uses the largest record found. Those summaries are not measurements of one individual, population ranges, wild-life expectancy or current surveys. Older species and subspecies can share inherited data; matching names and families support the displayed association but do not resolve that history or establish present-day concept equivalence. Wild versus captive conditions are not consistently confirmed, and original papers were not individually reviewed for this page. Other claims about diet, behavior, range and conservation need separate sources.
