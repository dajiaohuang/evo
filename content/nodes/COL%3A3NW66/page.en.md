## Limits of the PanTHERIA, life-history and ecology records {#s-b800214a19}
PanTHERIA 1.0 WR05/2008 is a historical compilation. Continuous traits are generally medians, sometimes after GLM adjustment for sex, definitions or methods; *_EXT extrapolations do not fill missing values here. Input conditions may be wild, captive, provisioned or unspecified, and source types vary by variable. Values are not individual ranges, censuses or diagnoses. MSW93/MSW05 same-name correspondence and a COL name-and-family match associate records but do not establish equivalence after later splits or lumps. Current distribution, survival and conservation need other evidence.

The life-history fields come from same-name records in the 2015 archive. Except for maximum longevity, summaries are medians or averages of the two middle values; maximum longevity is the largest record found. They are not individual measurements, variation ranges, mean wild lifespan or current population surveys. Historical species and subspecies may share or inherit data. Matching names and families and the same-name rows support the association but do not prove equivalence to COL26.8 or remove all inherited components. Wild or captive conditions are not uniformly confirmed, and publications have not been individually reviewed.

EltonTraits 1.0 is a historical guide, not a complete monograph; its concepts are not automatically equivalent to COL26.8. Food and avian-stratum percentages are standardized relative weights from literature descriptions, not direct measures of stomach contents, intake, energy or observation time; avian A/B codes retain source and proportional uncertainty. Mammalian ABC diet or activity may refer to an ecologically similar congener, so not every value is a direct observation of this species. Genus or family typical values, non-species-level avian strata and split records needing review do not establish definite claims. Underlying works have not been individually reread. Individual, seasonal and local variation, current range, population and conservation require other evidence.
