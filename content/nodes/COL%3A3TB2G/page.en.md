## The accepted catalogue record {#s-a1d697426d}
The pinned COL26.8 checklist accepts Lepidochelys kempii (Garman, 1880) under Lepidochelys. This fixes the catalogue name and parent genus for this page; it does not establish whether the species currently occurs in any particular place or describe population condition.

## Two immature specimens, one micro-CT scan {#s-eddcb43426}
Jones et al. (2012) examined two immature L. kempii recovered after death from beaches in Wales and Scotland in 2007–2008. They used specimen M009/08 for micro-CT imaging and dissection-based anatomy, reconstructing structures of the skull, jaw muscles, and neck. The findings apply to these specimens and the paper's comparative material, not to a survey of the species.

## Read the anatomy as a specimen-level comparison {#s-7c784fe66e}
The study compares L. kempii skull and jaw anatomy with Caretta caretta and discusses broader skull-shape comparisons across Testudines. The micro-CT account documents internal anatomy in M009/08; other observations use separately identified skeletal and dissection material. Interpretations of jaw movement and muscle arrangement are the authors' anatomical conclusions, not measurements of behavior in all individuals.

## Different stages in the mass fields {#s-81ff9649ea}
The historical compilation reports adult body mass of 50,000 g and mass at birth or hatching of 19.6 g. Adult records can combine males, females, and adults of unspecified sex; the birth or hatching value describes a different life stage. These are not measurements of the same individuals over time and do not give a species-wide range.

## Reproduction and developmental timing {#s-6ba8dc08bf}
The retained historical fields report female maturity at 3,835.13 days, where the source defines maturity as age at first conception; 110 offspring or eggs per litter or clutch; and 55.1 days from oviposition to hatching. Here, d means days. Litter or clutch counts combine offspring and egg records, so a fractional summary does not describe part of an individual. The timing fields refer to different events, not one interchangeable reproductive cycle.

## Distinguish the measured length {#s-858a620571}
The reported maximum straight carapace length (SCL) is 750 mm. SCL is a straight-line shell measurement that excludes the head, neck, and tail. This is a compiled maximum, not typical size or an absolute growth limit; sex-specific reports may come from different places or studies.

## How to read reproductive records {#s-ef668c330b}
The record labels reproduction oviparous, sex determination TSD (temperature-dependent sex determination), mean offspring or eggs per clutch 110, and clutches per year 1.34. Clutch averages may be midpoints of reported means or, when those are absent, reported extremes; they need not be arithmetic averages of observed clutches. A fractional summary is not a fractional individual. The lowest and highest fields refer to reported means, not guaranteed clutch limits. These labels do not establish one pattern for every population.
