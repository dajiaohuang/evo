## Leaves and inflorescence {#s-3bb75ca3a4}
The source describes long aerial stems growing in clumps. Leaf blades may be whole or irregularly pinnate; their bases meet the leaf axis at an acute angle, and the veins stand out in cross-section. The inflorescence may sit between or below the leaves and branch at the first or second order. Its stalk bract is narrow, elongated, and tubular. The prophyll, a bract at the base of the inflorescence, is recorded with several alternative surface forms: elongated unbranched veins; a short, asymmetric, pointed form with margins curved toward the stem; or a flat, densely hairy surface. The smaller inflorescence branches are not threadlike. Floral pits are arranged spirally, in opposite pairs, or in groups of three; their distal lip is present and their inner surface lacks hairs.

## Flowers and fruit {#s-222a2028c7}
Male flowers fall after flowering, and the pollen sacs (thecae) diverge when the flower opens. Unfertilized female flowers also fall. The fruit apex is rounded; the fruit is black or purple, with a regular, hairless outer layer.
