## What octopus gene expansions can—and cannot—explain {#s-61137b00ae}
The reference genome came from one male Octopus bimaculoides; RNA transcriptomes sampled twelve tissues. The assembly covered more than 97% of expressed protein-coding genes and revealed expansions in protocadherin and C2H2 zinc-finger families, extensive genome rearrangement and tissue-biased gene expression.

These comparisons identify genomic features associated with octopus biology, but they do not show that any one expansion caused arms, suckers, eyes or neural complexity. The study found no evidence for the proposed whole-genome duplication, while noting that duplication followed by extensive gene loss was not ruled out. One genome and its tissue samples cannot stand for every octopus.
