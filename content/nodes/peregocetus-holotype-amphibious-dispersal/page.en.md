## Peregocetus and a South Pacific fossil {#s-dfb5e3d9f1}
The Peruvian holotype MUSM 3580 places Peregocetus pacificus in the record at 42.6 Ma. Its limbs combine weight-bearing features with aquatic adaptations, while its gait and route of dispersal remain reconstructions.

## A substantial skeleton in one holotype {#s-1b52feb459}
MUSM 3580 preserves the mandibles and most major regions behind the skull. Two fused sacral vertebrae, a distinct femoral head and hoof-like tips on the toes support the interpretation that the animal could bear weight, alongside features associated with life in water. The specimen comes from the Yumaque Member of Peru’s Pisco Basin.

## Anatomy is not a recorded journey {#s-6f9c87d8a9}
Walking and swimming are functional interpretations of the skeleton. The paper favors dispersal westward across the South Atlantic, but an eastward route remains possible; neither route is traced by the fossil itself. Protocetidae is treated as paraphyletic in the study, and the presence of a tail fluke is unresolved.
