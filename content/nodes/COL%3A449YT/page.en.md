## Broad petals above a long fruit beak {#s-9b5f11ce12}
The 1979 monograph describes *Monsonia grandifolia* as a few-stemmed, partly woody plant, usually 15–40 cm high, generally erect and rarely lying partly along the ground. Its thick leaves are toothed and sometimes lobed; they are alternate near the stem base and often opposite or nearly opposite higher up. Flowers are usually one or two per inflorescence. The petals are white, pale yellow or cream and 20–30 mm long. The fruit measures 65–80 mm overall, including a narrow beak 50–65 mm long; the smaller fruit segments detach from this axis. This regional account gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency.

## Rocky grassland and higher slopes {#s-1e19e70e81}
The same monograph records rocky or stony grassland on mountainsides and hillsides, and more rarely swampy meadows, at 800–1,800 m. This describes reported habitats and elevations, not a complete range. The account gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency and does not specify whether the plants were wild, cultivated or managed.
