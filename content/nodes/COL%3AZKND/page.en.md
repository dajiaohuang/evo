## Read the study {#s-427ba1ec60}
What can a habitat model assembled from historical records tell us? This guide follows the accepted COL26.8 record ZKND and reads the study at its stated sample and geographic scale. A Colombia–Venezuela regional synthesis reviewed 289 documents on Orinoco-crocodile biology or ecology published from 1900 to 2015 and assembled 1,334 sighting or active-nest records; 125 spatially rarefied locations were used to train its habitat model. Under stated thresholds, the model estimated potential suitable areas and identified conservation and research priorities. Only part of the high-suitability area fell within protected areas. The model represents potential distribution supported by the available records, not a measured population range at that time or today.

Continue with Mecistops cataphractus (COL 3YKJB) to compare modeled suitability with repeated-survey detection. The Crocodylia group guide provides the wider reading route; the neighboring page retains its own sources and limits.

## Different stages in the mass fields {#s-97710be96c}
Historical summary values are adult body mass 107900 g; mass at birth or hatching 66.9 g. Adult mass can combine males, females and adults of unspecified sex; birth/hatching mass describes a different life stage. These are not a longitudinal series of the same individuals or a species-wide range of individual masses.

## Reproduction and developmental timing {#s-31096ae4a1}
Available compilation fields are offspring per litter or eggs per clutch 40 offspring/eggs; oviposition to hatching 82.5 d; d denotes days. The litter/clutch field combines offspring or egg counts, and a fractional summary does not mean a single event produced a fractional individual. Timing fields refer to different events and cannot be exchanged for one uniform reproductive cycle.

## Evidence limits {#s-4ae70eabdd}
The review covered Colombia and Venezuela and used records published through 2015. Habitat suitability, protected-area overlap and priority zones are model outputs from those historical records, not current occupancy, range boundaries or population estimates. Life-history values come from a separate 2015 compilation; same-name and family matches do not establish equivalence to the pinned COL26.8 concept, and the underlying publications were not individually rechecked.

## Distinguish the measured length {#s-9368768ad7}
Recorded dimensions are female maximum snout–vent length (SVL) 2500 mm; reported maximum total length (TL) 4800 mm. SVL runs from snout to vent and differs from total length including the tail. Compiled maxima are reported material, not theoretical growth limits or typical individual size. Sex-specific values may come from different localities or studies and alone cannot quantify population sexual dimorphism.

## Where and how it is active {#s-142d84ba46}
In the entry, microhabitat is Arboreal/Saxicolous/Terrestrial; diet is Carnivorous; active timing is Nocturnal; foraging mode is ACT. These categories summarize activity and resource use rather than an exhaustive habitat list or fixed schedule. Positions such as arboreal and terrestrial can be combined. A carnivorous category does not specify prey, and populations, seasons and life stages may differ.

## How to read reproductive records {#s-98daf1a7f6}
the reproductive-mode field uses the original label oviparous; consolidated mean offspring or eggs per litter/clutch 40; consolidated litters/clutches per year 1; egg length 77.35 mm; egg width 47.22 mm. Clutch/litter summaries can use midpoints of reported means or, when means are unavailable, extrema; they are not necessarily arithmetic averages of observed clutches. Fractions do not describe non-integer young in a litter. The lowest/highest fields are defined as endpoints of reported means rather than guaranteed smallest/largest individual clutches. Reproductive and sex-determination labels do not establish fetal nutrition or uniformity across all populations.

## Limits of these trait data {#s-15782d470e}
This account paraphrases the same-name ReptTraits v1-2 entry, retaining its 2023 taxonomic framework and sources for individual traits. One row per species compresses variation in sex, age, locality and populations; matching name and family do not ensure equivalent later concepts. Reported maximum length is not an absolute species limit, and reproductive means are not fixed counts in every clutch. Many mass entries are estimated from length equations and are not presented here as measured mass. Juvenile-length headers and definitions differ and are also omitted from reader conclusions. Conservation, venom, current distribution and population trends are not updated here; unrecorded fields remain gaps. Dataset citation labels preserve the source chain and do not imply that this project independently reread every original work.
