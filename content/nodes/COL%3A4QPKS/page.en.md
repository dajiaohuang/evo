## Effective-size inference across glacial history {#s-5c64682307}
The chinstrap entry is grouped among lineages expanding early in the Last Glacial Period and declining later, opposite to P. adeliae. The genomic model does not replace the existing global breeding-colony assessment, which uses different units, sampling dates and methods. These curves infer long-term effective size from genomic heterozygosity and depend on mutation rates and time calibration. They are not individual censuses and cannot reliably recover changes in the most recent years.

## A global colony assessment combining different methods {#s-0bc9b4bc1b}
Strycker et al. (2020) combined ground counts with drone and satellite imagery and assembled records for 398 breeding sites. They estimated 3.42 million breeding pairs across 375 extant colonies. About 35% of colonies could not be compared with an adequate historical benchmark; estimates also differed in age and precision. This is a dated, mixed-method compilation rather than a single synchronized census or a present-day 2026 count.

## catalogue-profile / limitations {#s-1202182994}
AVONET v7 summarizes historical samples and species-level values. Morphology is averaged across the listed birds; it is neither an identification diagnosis nor a range of individual measurements. Body mass comes from a separate source and does not use the morphology sample count. Habitat, diet and migration are broad categories, while mapped geography covers historical breeding and resident ranges. Matching names across BirdLife, eBird, BirdTree and COL26.8 supports a crosswalk but does not settle species boundaries after splits or mergers. Reproduction, present populations and conservation require other evidence.
