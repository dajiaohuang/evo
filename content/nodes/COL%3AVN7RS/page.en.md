Mean beak length is 39.4 mm, from the front edge of the nostril to the tip. Mean width is 8.6 mm and depth is 11.5 mm, both measured at the nostril edge. These are distinct dimensions taken at defined landmarks, not field-identification cutoffs.

## Wing, tarsus and mass {#s-9c30dae24e}
Mean unflattened wing length, from the carpal joint to the longest primary, is 248.8 mm; mean tarsus length is 45.3 mm. A separate species-average source gives mass as 994.98 g and is labelled Dunning. The 4 measured birds should not be read as 4 weighed birds.

The archive reports an adult body-mass summary of 1000 g. Its adult records may combine males, females and adults of unspecified sex; the value is not a range of individual masses. Birth or hatching mass is a separate field and is not included here.

The archive gives 4 for its combined offspring-per-litter or eggs-per-clutch field and 49 d for age at flight capability (called fledging age in the source; some source material defines it as leaving the nest). ## Foraging strata differ from whole-day activity {#s-98f0e33cfd}
The species-level foraging record assigns 100% of its weight to feeding below the water surface. This estimates relative use of feeding positions, not nest sites, all habitat or time spent there over a day. The archive's categories describe different positions; they are not elevation bands and cannot be used to infer flight height or habitat area.

## Evidence limits {#s-53d10d62af}
AVONET measurements and averages describe the sampled compilation, not identification cutoffs or individual variation. The mass estimate comes from separate species-average information, not necessarily from the measured birds. Habitat, diet and migration are broad categories; the map summarizes historical breeding and resident ranges. AVONET's HBW–BirdLife name and the COL26.8 match connect these records for this account but do not prove that their taxonomic concepts are identical. Reproduction, current populations and conservation need other sources.

The 2015 life-history values are historical compilation summaries, not direct measurements of one individual, ranges of individual variation, mean wild lifespans or current population surveys.
