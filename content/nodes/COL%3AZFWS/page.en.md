## Mass at adult and birth stages {#s-c63d5c7cd7}
Historical summaries are 1,400 g for adult body mass and 23.1 g for mass at birth or hatching. Adult mass can combine males, females and adults of unspecified sex; the birth or hatching value describes a different stage. These are not repeated measurements of the same individuals or a species-wide range.

## Reproductive and developmental timing {#s-89d51c92e9}
The archive reports female age at first conception as 161.92 d, offspring per litter or eggs per clutch as 3.11, conception or fertilization to birth as 34 d, and age of dependence on food other than maternal milk as 31.04 d. Here d means days. The litter or clutch field combines offspring and egg counts; a fractional summary is not a literal fractional litter. Each timing value describes a different event, not one interchangeable reproductive cycle.

## Relative weights of reported food categories {#s-048c9cfcbc}

## Limits of the historical life-history and ecology records {#s-ab073f48f9}
The life-history fields come from same-name records in the 2015 archive. Except for maximum longevity, summaries are medians or averages of the two middle values; maximum longevity is the largest record found. These are not measurements of one individual, individual variation ranges, mean wild lifespan or a current population survey. Historical species and subspecies may share or inherit data. Matching names and families and the presence of same-name sparse rows support the association, but do not prove equivalence to COL26.8 concepts or remove every inherited component. Wild or captive conditions are not uniformly confirmed, and the underlying publications were not individually reviewed.

EltonTraits 1.0 is a historical guide, not a complete monograph; its historical concepts are not automatically equivalent to COL26.8. Food and avian-stratum percentages are standardized relative weights from literature descriptions, not direct measures of stomach contents, intake, energy or observation time; avian A/B codes retain source and proportional uncertainty. Mammalian ABC diet or activity may refer to an ecologically similar congener, so not every value is a direct observation of this species. Genus or family typical values, non-species-level avian strata and split records needing review do not establish definite claims. Underlying books and papers have not been individually reread. Individual, seasonal and local variation, current range, population and conservation require other evidence.
