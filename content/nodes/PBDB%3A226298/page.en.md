## Research overview {#s-24bf6f6c52}
An exceptionally complete Miguasha fossil of Elpistostege preserves a pectoral fin with both fin rays and a complex internal skeleton. Some distal bones have been interpreted as digits or digit-like elements, but the appendage remained a fin.

## What this evidence can establish {#s-27b2ffccfd}
Specimen MHNM 06-2067 and its CT reconstruction document the fin’s internal structure. Digit homology and the animal’s position among close relatives depend on anatomical interpretation and phylogenetic analysis; this profile does not identify Elpistostege as a direct tetrapod ancestor.

## Bones inside a fin {#s-ef544b2fc1}
The pectoral fin of articulated specimen MHNM 06-2067 contains four rows of radial bones arranged from the proximal end toward the tip. Its carpals, or wrist bones, branch in a complex pattern, and bones nearer the fin tip have been interpreted as digits or possible digits.

Fin rays, or lepidotrichia, remain present in the appendage, confirming that this is still a fin.

## A fin, its setting and movement limits {#s-5576aab850}
Miguasha’s aquatic depositional setting frames this fossil. The fin is described as a propulsive appendage, while load-bearing or digit-assisted use remains an inference; the large-bodied description does not define a genus-wide size range.
