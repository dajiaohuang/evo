## A low score in a 2024 Chilean priority model {#s-dd3286ecc3}
The 2024 assessment prints a conservation-priority index of 1.00 for Batrachyla nibaldoi and places it in Table 4’s low category. It assesses 58 selected species using literature, citizen-science records and government plans; it is not a new census of every population. The model combines distribution, threat, population trend, taxonomic uniqueness and body-size scores with a protected-area term. Its index is a management ranking, not an extinction probability, current IUCN assessment or direct population-size measure. “Low” or “non-priority” does not mean the species is safe or needs no conservation.

## What the model’s component codes mean {#s-8ae3e73330}
In Table 3, population-trend code 0 means trend information is missing; land-protection code 2 means less than half the area is within protected areas. These are the authors’ ordinal assignments, not a newly checked population trajectory or measured share of individuals protected. A zero is unavailable evidence, not stability or zero risk. Threat code 2 groups source categories and does not identify a specific official status. AOO and EOO scores classify area criteria; they do not provide independently reconstructed occupancy maps. Reported protection coverage also does not establish effective management.
