## A species boundary across the Limpopo {#field-study}
Petružela and colleagues combined newly collected and previously published genetic data to examine the Acomys spinosissimus complex across the Zambezian region. Their multilocus analysis supported four currently recognized lineages, including A. selousi, while earlier work had reinstated that name for southern populations. In the authors’ interpretation, A. selousi is concentrated in northern South Africa and southern Zimbabwe, replacing A. spinosissimus south of the Limpopo River.

The study treats these as closely related, cryptic spiny mice and asks how open-habitat history shaped their differentiation. Its account also identifies important holes: DNA sequences were lacking from the putative range of A. spinosissimus sensu stricto and from Zambia, and the status of a possible lineage north of the Zambezi remained unresolved. The source does not justify treating every rocky grassland or woodland record as A. selousi.

The source’s use of A. selousi has not been reconciled with the pinned COL26.8 concept. The shared name alone does not establish that the study and catalogue refer to equivalent taxon concepts.
