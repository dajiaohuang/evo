## Accepted name and placement {#s-453a89a044}
COL26.8 accepts Raorchestes keirasabinae Garg, Suyesh, Das, Bee & Biju, 2021 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Rhacophoridae (family) → Raorchestes (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## Webbed digits and limb fringes in a canopy frog {#s-4ed24fc31f}
The diagnosis gives male snout–vent length of 29–31 mm and combines a vertically outlined snout in lateral view, webbed fingers, nearly fully webbed toes and a distinct dermal fringe along the outer margins of forelimbs and hindlimbs. Morphometric information came from three specimens. Dorsal colour and markings were variable, and spinular projections on the back and flanks appeared more prominent in life. The authors suggested that colour change might help camouflage, but this was not a controlled demonstration of adaptive function. Webbing and skin fringes do not by themselves establish gliding performance, swimming ability or a measured female size range.

## Known canopy records south of the Palghat gap {#s-1a169ed8b2}
The description recorded keirasabinae at 100–1,000 m elevation south of the Palghat gap, including Chathankod-Makki, Ponmudi, Peppara and Shendurney in the Agasthyamalai region, and Periyar Tiger Reserve in Kerala. It was described as inhabiting the highest canopy layers and consequently being difficult to locate. A wider range in southern Kerala and neighbouring Tamil Nadu was suggested, not demonstrated with confirmed additional occurrences. Named reserves identify source localities rather than guarantee present habitat security. These canopy observations do not measure population density, define an upper detection limit or identify egg-laying sites.

## Vocalisations were not recorded and analysed {#s-c4e48d109b}
The species account explicitly states that keirasabinae vocalisations had not been recorded and analysed. Acoustic descriptions of other nerostagona-group members therefore cannot be assigned as measured keirasabinae call durations or frequencies. This is a data gap in the 2021 study, not evidence that the frog is silent, cannot call or lacks a role for calls in reproduction. The difficulty of locating canopy individuals offers field context but does not itself explain every missing record. Describing a call repertoire or its temperature dependence will require species-identified recordings rather than transferring a group summary.

## A sister relationship to nerostagona in the sampled tree {#s-35a05ec599}
The study placed keirasabinae in the nerostagona group and reported a well-supported sister relationship to nerostagona. Mitochondrial 16S divergence was 3.1–3.7% from nerostagona and 6.1–6.6% from crustai. These are sequence distances, not elapsed time or demonstrated reproductive isolation. The wider tree combined mitochondrial and nuclear markers. The paper contrasted known records south of the Palghat gap with nerostagona records north of it; that dated geographic distinction alone neither proves that the gap caused speciation nor establishes exhaustive present allopatry.
