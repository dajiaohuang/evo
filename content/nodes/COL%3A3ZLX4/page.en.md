## What range of forms does the historical account describe? {#s-8b5060cdb7}
The Flora of Panama account describes an erect or sometimes trailing herb, undershrub or shrub 0.6–2 m tall, with young stems covered in simple or star-shaped hairs. Leaves may have petioles up to 5 cm; the ovate blades are heart-shaped at the base, taper to a point, and reach 10 by 7 cm. Their papery edges are toothed or scalloped, and the soft, velvety hairs are denser below. Axillary flower clusters are solitary or grouped, often in short-stalked, umbrella-like cymes. The white flowers are 4–4.7 mm long and heterostylous: individual flowers differ in the relative lengths of styles and stamens. In long-styled flowers, the glabrous staminal tube is 2–2.5 mm; the ellipsoid to oblong ovary is 1–1.5 mm, and the free styles 2.5–2.8 mm. In short-styled flowers, the tube is 1.6–1.7 mm and the free styles 1.7–1.9 mm. Both forms have a densely silky ovary and papillate stigmas. The capsule, enclosed by the enlarging calyx, is flattened-spherical, about 2.2–2.3 mm long and 2.7–3 mm wide; each segment opens along its inner edge. Seeds are about 1.4–1.7 mm long and 1.1 mm thick. This is a historical regional description without a stated population sample or observation date.

## What range and habitats does the historical flora report? {#s-8ce6092df1}
The historical Flora of Panama account calls this a common, often weedy species spread through the American tropics. It reports moist thickets, brushy slopes and weedy fields as habitats. This is the source’s regional-era summary, not a current range survey.
