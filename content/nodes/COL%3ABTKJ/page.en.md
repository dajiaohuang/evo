## Range clues from interviews and brief fieldwork {#s-19ffcc022f}
Gautier’s preliminary 1985 study paired hunter interviews with nine days of field observation in June 1984 at a site where the monkey was common. It reports local records around Mbandaka, Gemena, Mondombé and Lisala; the historical southern record extends to Kasongo-Lunda on the basis of hunters’ reports. These are early local clues, not a standardized range survey.

## Northwestern records {#s-b1e442a06a}
Maisels and colleagues (2006) reported records in northern Republic of the Congo at least 100 km northwest of the previously known range, west of Nouabalé-Ndoki National Park. They noted apparent absence from much of the park interior and proposed movement along the Sangha and Ndoki rivers as an explanation. The article presents local observations and a biogeographic interpretation; it does not measure dispersal or estimate abundance.
