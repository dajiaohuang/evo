## Nevillea singularis {#s-b220f3e810}
## Female flower clusters rise above their coverings

The regional account describes Nevillea singularis as a perennial with underground stems and female and male flowers on separate plants; it reaches 60 cm and has unbranched, hollow stems. Male spikelets (small flower clusters) are 8–14 mm long, and female spikelets rise above the spathes (leaf-like bracts around the flower cluster). Its habitat is reported at marsh edges on shale bands around 1,000 m elevation.
