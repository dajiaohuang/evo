## Southern and southwestern Ethiopian records {#s-064c580efe}
The revision reported southern and southwestern Ethiopian highland records on both sides of the Great Rift Valley, at 1409–2449 m. Named areas included localities near Hagere Mariam, Maji, Gore, Gech’a, Bonga, Jimma and Wondo Genet. This is a study-record range rather than a complete contemporary occupancy survey or physiological elevation interval.

## Night calling in shallow water {#s-502f46691c}
Males were found calling at night in shallow puddles on or beside roads, or in flooded grassy meadows. Several other Ptychadena occurred in the same broader regions. Calling and regional co-occurrence do not establish egg sites, clutch sizes, direct species interactions or a complete annual reproductive calendar.

## A geographically sampled mitochondrial and nuclear dataset {#s-969876f4d8}
The 2023 Ethiopian Highlands study lists 11 mitochondrial samples and 8 nuclear-SNP samples for Ptychadena neumanni in Table 1, with mitochondrial nucleotide diversity π=0.99%. Samples across the twelve-species study were collected in 2011–2019; these are analysed specimens, not a population census. All assembled mitochondrial genomes had the usual two rRNAs, thirteen protein-coding genes and twenty-two tRNAs, but no species had a completely resolved control region because repeats could not be confidently assembled from short reads. The phylogenetic alignment used selected genes (13,937 bp across 303 samples), rather than an alignment of complete control regions.

## A study-defined highland clade and regional distribution {#s-ae06ea6c22}
The paper places Ptychadena neumanni in its P. neumanni clade and reports it from the both sides of the Great Rift Valley. These distributions combine the authors' geographic sampling and earlier range work; they are not independently updated complete range polygons. The twelve-species highland clade is a subset of Ptychadena. Its mitochondrial-clock analysis estimated a common ancestor around 11.7 million years ago, with a reported interval of 8.827–15.494 million years. The east-of-rift ancestral reconstruction had a model probability of 69%; neither that estimate nor inferred crossings is a directly observed migration event or a nuclear-genome divergence date for this species.

## A dated reassessment argument, with remaining demographic gaps {#s-b7a4fe5502}
The 2023 discussion calls for conservation assessment or reassessment using revised species concepts and genetic and geographic evidence. Earlier names and broad distributions can combine lineages that later work separated. The present genomic sample does not itself measure current abundance, fragmentation, decline or reproductive success in Ptychadena neumanni. Any Red List category mentioned in this paper belongs to its dated discussion, not a verified current official listing. Regional land-use and climate concerns support further investigation but do not quantify this species' current threat level.

## Mitochondrial structure lacks matching nuclear geography here {#s-67dafec5f5}
Two mitochondrial clades occurred in the west, with no obvious geographical barrier between them. Nuclear PCA using approximately 1.1 million SNPs did not recover corresponding geographic structure; only four individuals per clade were included in the nuclear comparison. A negative Tajima’s D in the N1 subgroup is compatible with demographic expansion or linked selection, rather than proving a single cause. Absence of detected nuclear structure is not proof of unrestricted gene flow.
