## Different stages in the mass fields {#s-849f401d77}
The archive reports an adult body-mass summary of 2722.5 g. Its adult records may combine males, females and adults of unspecified sex; the value is not a range of individual masses. Birth or hatching mass is a separate field and is not included here.

## Reproduction and developmental timing {#s-d52f35984c}
The archive gives 2.62 for its combined offspring-per-litter or eggs-per-clutch field, 29 d from oviposition to hatching, and 56 d for age at flight capability (called fledging age in the source; some source material defines it as leaving the nest). Here d means days. The litter/clutch summary combines different kinds of counts, so its fraction does not describe part of an individual in one event. The two timing values mark distinct events, not one interchangeable measure of a reproductive cycle.

## How to trace these values to sources {#s-0c0534539e}
Source labels on the same-name archive rows include Dunning (1992), Szekely, Lislevand and Figuerola (2007), Bennett (1986), and Dyke and Kaiser (2010), among others. The retained archive records each field’s row number, value and label, with full reference explanations in its reference table. A label may point to a paper, book or another database. Row counts do not tell how many individuals were measured, and repeated citations are not independent experiments.

## Life-history evidence limits {#s-de05cab206}
This account uses historical same-name rows from the August 2015 life-history archive. Its summaries are medians or averages of the two middle values; maximum longevity instead uses the largest record found. They are not direct measurements of one individual, ranges of individual variation, mean wild lifespans or current population surveys. The archive may share or inherit data among historical species and subspecies. Matching name and family supports this association but does not prove equivalence with the COL26.8 concept or remove every inherited component. Wild or captive conditions are not consistently confirmed, and the underlying publications have not been reviewed individually. Diet, behavior, present-day range, conservation and the missing life-history fields need other sources.
