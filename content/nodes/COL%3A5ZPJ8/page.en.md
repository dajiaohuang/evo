## What features define this climbing species in the regional flora? {#s-394856792a}
The historical Flora of Panama account describes a liana with spiny branchlets that may be hairy or hairless. Its alternate, sometimes nearly opposite, papery leaves are elliptic to broadly elliptic, 5–14 by 3–7.5 cm; the petiole may persist and become spine-like. Leaf hairs are often most noticeable along the veins beneath, though some leaves are nearly hairless. White, five-part flowers occur on short stalks in large terminal panicles. The lower floral cup is about 1 mm long; its upper part is bell- to cup-shaped and 2.5–3 mm long. There are five broad triangular calyx lobes, five hairy petals and ten stamens. The fruit is nearly round, about 1.5 cm across, usually hairless and has five thin, flexible wings, each 5–6 mm wide. This is a historical regional description, not a current or global survey.

## What range did the historical Panama account report? {#s-a09d7f765d}
The Flora of Panama distribution field reports Mexico to Colombia. This is the historical regional statement in the source version retrieved on 8 September 2026; it is not a current range survey or a complete global inventory.
