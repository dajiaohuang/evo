## Form and flowers {#s-2cb4ef5069}
The 2013 regional account describes a nearly stemless corm-bearing geophyte up to 80 mm tall, with a bristly stem. Its inclined leaves are hairy, pleated, and sword-shaped to lance-shaped. Four to nine flowers sit in a low, one-sided spike; they are two-lipped, blue to violet, with white nectar guides and a sweet scent. The cylindrical perianth tube is 25–35 mm long and bends near its tip. The upper tepals are about as long as, or longer than, the tube. The ovary is smooth; the bracts are velvety, with the inner bracts forked at the tip and tapering to a point.

These traits come from a regional South African account published in 2013; the excerpt gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency.

## Distinguishing Babiana curviscapa {#s-50a6048db1}
A 2020 account describes *B. attenuata* as having blue-violet flowers on crowded horizontal spikes close to the ground. It can be confused with the vegetatively very similar *B. curviscapa*, whose reported range from Steinkopf in the north to Bitterfontein in the south includes the entire reported range of *B. attenuata*. The account separates them mainly by the perianth tube: 25–35 mm in *B. attenuata*, versus usually 36–43 mm in *B. curviscapa*. Flowers of *B. attenuata* are strongly scented and usually have tepals 26–35 mm long; those of *B. curviscapa* are faintly scented or unscented, with tepals 20–23 mm long. In the north of its range, *B. curviscapa* has magenta to cherry-red flowers; elsewhere they are blue, like those of *B. attenuata*. The account reports pollination of *B. attenuata* by anthophorine bees whose mouthparts are about 10 mm long.

Plants from the southern Kamiesberg have upright, linear leaves up to 150 mm long with conspicuously long hairs. The account says they appear different from other *B. attenuata* populations and may be a local variant or possibly a separate species, although their flowers match other populations in the diagnostic details discussed.

These diagnostic comparisons come from a regional South African account published in 2020; the excerpt gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency.
