## The Machay Reserve type locality {#s-2da2ff56d6}
The account knew the species only from Machay Reserve in Tungurahua, Ecuador, at 2940 m. The locality was montane cloud forest with abundant epiphytes and a bromeliad-rich understorey. This type-locality record does not establish the full modern range or physiological elevation limits.

## Hidden at bromeliad leaf bases {#s-278b54d37c}
All specimens in the account were found hiding at the bases of bromeliad leaves in the lower forest layer. The observation documents the collection microhabitat, without proving that the species never uses other perches or establishing its egg site, diet or complete activity cycle.

## Six type specimens and two locality elevations {#s-2d0d49a0e9}
Adult female holotype DHMECN 14479 was collected in Machay Reserve on 2 March 2018 at 2970 m; five paratypes comprise two females and three males. Its snout–vent length was 22.63 mm, while the diagnosis reports males at 16.61–17.45 mm and females at 20.81–27.03 mm. These intervals describe the published material, not universal size limits or a population-level dimorphism test. The natural-history account places the type locality at 2940 m, distinct from the material-examined entry’s 2970 m; the discrepancy is not silently harmonized into one value.

## Conflicting head and tympanum measurements remain traceable {#s-454df1f432}
The holotype measurement list gives head length 8.68 mm and width 7.36 mm but later calls the head wider than long using 12.8 versus 11.7 mm. It lists eye diameter 4.7 mm and tympanum diameter 2.5 mm, yet later states tympanum diameter as 35% of eye diameter; the diagnosis instead says 25%. These printed values and incompatible proportional summaries are retained as unresolved source conflicts. The account’s presence of a visible tympanum and weak male nuptial pads is anatomical evidence, not a hearing-performance measurement or an observation of mating success.

## Red hidden surfaces in a banded live holotype {#s-9e2ea87c61}
The live holotype account described dark-brown dorsal surfaces with irregular lighter marks and bands extending to flanks and limbs. Shanks and hidden groin and armpit surfaces were red; other ventral areas were light brown with dark markings, and the iris reddish-brown. The separate 70% ethanol account retained some red regions but described brown, grey and pale banding differently, and other specimens varied in brown tones and thin middorsal lines. The authors compared this colour combination and tubercles with other regional frogs. These observed colours do not alone certify identification, toxicity, signalling or adaptive function.

## Placement in the proposed Cryptomantis framework {#s-59ba156501}
The 2025 study includes Pristimantis burtoniorum in the prolatus species group of its proposed subgenus Cryptomantis. The sampled major clade had bootstrap support 100 and its two group clades 99. Morphological synapomorphies were explicitly unknown. The dated placement does not certify a current catalogue subgenus, a complete range for this species or identity of every historical broadly identified voucher. Unconfirmed candidate lineages remain separate from accepted named species.

## Characters in the paganus comparison {#s-27d4f78163}
The paper distinguishes the new paganus population from Pristimantis burtoniorum using dorsolateral folds absent, a relatively large subacuminate snout and subconical upper-eyelid tubercles. These are comparative taxonomic characters, not a newly sampled population-wide trait-frequency analysis. Sex and body-region wording are retained. A selected fold or tubercle alone is not a complete identification key, and these comparisons provide no new diet, clutch-size or developmental measurements for this species.
