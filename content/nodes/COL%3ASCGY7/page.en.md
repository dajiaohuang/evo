## Sex-specific sizes and contrasting coloration {#s-8f8ff5c974}
Female SVL is17.1–20.7mm (19.3±1.58SD,n4), male14.7–16.9mm (16.0±1.17,n3). The diagnosis combines no tympanic membrane/annulus, evident thoracic/discoidal folds, no dorsolateral folds and only slightly expanded rounded discs. Females are dark brown with irregular white spots and darker venters; sampled males are lighter brown to red without those white spots. These small-sample differences do not establish inheritance or environmental causes of sexual coloration.

## Usually two notes, with a recording caveat {#s-69cc671d15}
Five males from Abra de Zamora and Cajanuma were recorded in2017–2018. Clicking calls usually have two notes, but MUTPL356’s record had one, possibly an artifact according to the authors. Table2 reports116call-duration measurements averaging0.027±0.008s, five-male rate185.9±25.398calls/min and294dominant-frequency measurements averaging3140.3±144.886Hz. These sampling units differ. Comparisons with vidua and tiktik are descriptive, not controlled temperature-normalized tests or proof of reproductive isolation.

## Two neighboring sites and low perches {#s-ed1c07055b}
The revision knows the species from Cajanuma and Abra de Zamora about13km north, with encounters2560–3300m in upper montane forest, subpáramo and shrub páramo. Individuals were found at night, often5–20cm above ground in grassy bases, moss beds or shrub leaves. Males called year round, especially on rainy nights; females were found close to calling males. These are field observations, not an observed mating success or a demonstrated exclusive habitat requirement.

## A supported subgroup but weak closest-relative resolution {#s-28697a2df1}
The species is assigned to the simonbolivari subgroup; its apparent closest association with quintanai and an undescribed species has only weak Bayesian support. The paper reports16S distances4.8–6.0% to tiktik and6.7–7.3% to bambu. Subgroup membership and those comparisons do not settle every internal branch or define a universal species threshold.

## Near Threatened is the authors’2020 proposal {#s-9bd319a380}
Despite two close localities and an estimated area below50km², the authors recommend Near Threatened because encounters were relatively frequent and most reported population habitat lay inside the national park without major then-observed threats. This combines limited distribution with the authors’ habitat interpretation; it does not certify a current assessment or prove immunity to future habitat loss or disease.
