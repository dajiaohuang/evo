## A one-vein leaf and a persistent flower bracteole {#s-842be6c8e5}
A Brazilian Flora 2020 v393.147 field describes lance-shaped or narrowly elliptic, papery leaves with wedge-shaped bases and acute tips. Their flat margins lack a hard, cartilage-like edge. The midrib, the main leaf vein, is grooved on the upper surface, with one vein near the leaf edge. Flowers grow in compact clusters called fascicles; bract status is unknown, while the smaller, inconspicuous bracteoles beside flowers persist. The flower cup is smooth but bears hairs; its calyx is open in bud, with four sepals and four petals. The fruit is round and smooth, and its bracteoles fall away. Seed number is unknown; the field records fused cotyledons (the seed leaves) in the embryo.
