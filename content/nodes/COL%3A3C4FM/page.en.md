## Recurved leaf edges and two flower-cup alternatives {#s-4b0c7a3862}
The Brazilian Flora 2020 v393.147 field describes oblong, leathery leaves whose bases may be wedge-shaped, rounded or blunt and whose tips may be rounded or tapering to a point. The margins roll under and lack a hard, cartilage-like edge. The midrib, the main leaf vein, is grooved on the upper surface, with two veins near the leaf edge. Flowers grow in compact clusters called fascicles. Their supporting bracts fall away, while small, inconspicuous bracteoles persist. The flower cup is described as smooth and either hairy or hairless; its calyx is open in bud, with four sepals and four petals. The fruit is ellipsoid and smooth, with persistent inconspicuous bracteoles. One seed is recorded, with fused cotyledons (the seed leaves) in the embryo.

## A terrestrial category without locality data {#s-ed4baa760c}
The habitat field records “terrícola,” meaning terrestrial. It does not provide a locality, survey date or range coverage and does not identify wild, cultivated, managed or fossil material. The category is separate from the plant structures described above.
