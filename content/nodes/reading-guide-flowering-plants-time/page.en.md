## Why do flowering plants have different origin estimates? {#lead}
A fossil age and a molecular-clock estimate answer different questions. Separating them makes debates about angiosperm origins easier to follow.

## A fossil records a body that already existed {#section-0}
Early Cretaceous Montsechia material preserves flexible leafy axes and closed single-seeded fruits. These specimen-level structures inform discussion of early angiosperm morphology and aquatic life. They do not record the moment the whole group originated.

## A molecular clock estimates branching times {#section-1}
Molecular-clock studies combine data from living lineages with fossil calibrations to estimate branching times. A crown group includes the last common ancestor of all living members and its descendants. Calibration and analysis choices affect the estimate, so a crown age is not a first fossil occurrence.

## Origin, fossil occurrence and expansion are separate questions {#section-2}
The presence of flowering plants at an age does not establish dominance across ecosystems. One aquatic fossil cannot represent the ancestral ecology of every early angiosperm. Keep specimens, model estimates and ecological expansion separate.

## Crown group {#term}
The last common ancestor of all living members of a group, together with all its descendants.
