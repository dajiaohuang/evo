## Euryops decumbens {#s-04669e975a}
## A low, blue-green shrub with silky fruits

Nordenstam describes a much-branched shrublet 5–25 cm tall, sometimes prostrate and mat-forming. It is hairless except in the leaf axils. Short branchlets are leafy near their tips; lower down, leaves fall away and leave persistent overlapping triangular leaf-bases, later leaving dark scars. Leaves crowd the branch ends, spread or bend back, and are linear to narrowly oblong, 3–15 mm long. They are flattened, leathery and dull bluish green, with a distinct midvein; each is entire or divided into three to five lobes above the middle. Their bases partly clasp the stem, and the margins have fine hairs, at least near the base.

Flower heads are solitary at branch tips, sessile or on stalks no longer than 2 mm. Their leathery involucres are narrow to broad bells, 4–8 mm high and 3–10 mm wide, with 5–10 bracts in one row, joined to about the middle. The receptacle is convex and distinctly pitted. Heads usually have 5–10 yellow ray florets and about 8–25 disc florets. A note on specimen Jacot-Guillarmod 2370 records a few tubular female florets without anthers but with fertile, two- or three-lobed styles in some heads.

The white pappus bristles are numerous, 2–4 mm long and minutely barbed. Dry fruits are densely silky-haired, 5–7 by 2–4 mm; their white or light-brown hairs are 2–4 mm long and do not become mucilaginous when soaked.

Nordenstam distinguishes *E. decumbens* from *E. montanus* by its trailing growth, dull bluish leaves, less-joined bracts and hairy fruits, although specimens of both have sometimes been labelled *E. montanus*. Greenhouse-grown plants retained the trailing form and characteristic leaf colour. The species is recorded from open Erica–Helichrysum heath and similar communities on mountain peaks at 2,750–3,400 m, on flat, stony-gravelly ground.
