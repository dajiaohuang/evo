## Studying marmoset fathers: association or cause? {#lead}
A common marmoset study links paternal responsiveness with infant outcomes. Understanding it requires both the observed relationship and the study setting.

## Start with an infant distress call {#section-0}
A study at one Wisconsin research colony included 24 experienced common marmoset fathers and 62 offspring. Researchers compared fathers’ responses to infant distress calls and related responsiveness to later infant outcomes. Paternal care became an observable question: when an infant signals distress, does its father respond?

## What differed among offspring of responsive fathers? {#section-1}
Offspring of fathers classified as responsive had higher survival beyond one month, and the paper reported weight differences between groups at weeks 28 and 30. The finding links paternal responsiveness with infant outcomes. That relationship alone does not establish that responsiveness caused the survival or weight differences.

## How does a captive-colony finding connect to wild life? {#section-2}
These findings come from one captive colony and are not estimates of survival in wild common marmosets. They offer a specific route into paternal care. The species page also covers white ear tufts and pelage, native and introduced records in Brazil, and studies of hybridization with relatives. Each question needs its own evidence.

## Association {#term}
An observed relationship between variables that, on its own, does not establish that one causes changes in the other.
