## Digits inside a fin? {#s-4e88f60907}
CT scans of the articulated Elpistostege specimen MHNM 06-2067 reveal branched carpals and two distal skeletal rows interpreted as digits or possible digits. Lepidotrichia, the fin rays, remain present, so the appendage is still a fin.

## A fin with a complex internal skeleton {#s-e1bd212c57}
Specimen MHNM 06-2067 preserves an articulated body about 1.57 metres long. CT reconstruction of its pectoral fin shows four proximodistal radial rows, branched carpals and two distal rows interpreted as digits or putative digits. These are internal skeletal observations from one exceptionally complete specimen.

## Why the appendage is still called a fin {#s-52c8feacd9}
Lepidotrichia are the bony fin rays, and they remain in the appendage. Whether the distal rows are homologous to digits, and where Elpistostege lies relative to other tetrapods, are interpretations from anatomical comparison and phylogenetic analysis. The Frasnian interval shown for the formation is geological context, not a direct radiometric date on this specimen.
