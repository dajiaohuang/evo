## Leaves and flowers {#s-e7fdf15162}
The 2012 regional account describes an acaulescent, corm-bearing geophyte 5–14 cm tall. The stem is usually underground and shorter than the hairy, lance-shaped leaves. The bilaterally symmetrical flowers are blue to purple with white markings and are usually fragrant. The perianth tube is elongated and straight, the inner bracts are forked at the tip, and the ovary is smooth.

These traits and measurements come from a regional South African account published in 2012; the excerpt gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency.

## Flower and bract characters {#s-793216fb42}
A 2020 diagnostic account recognizes *B. sambucina* by its smooth flower spike near ground level and a distinctive lowermost outer bract that extends upward into a short blade. The flowers have a long, straight tube and nearly equal tepals. Leaves and bracts are often almost hairless or velvety, but can bear sparse, long hairs; silky hairs may occur at the top of the leaf sheaths. The perianth ranges from pale to deep blue or violet. Each lower lateral tepal has a prominent white mark edged in dark blue and sometimes red. Tepals are usually 30–35 mm long, slightly shorter than the tube; the tube is mainly 35–55 mm long and can sometimes equal the tepals. Flowers in most populations are strongly and sweetly violet-scented.

## Comparison with Babiana bainesii {#s-033d687e3b}
The same account notes that *B. sambucina* can be confused with widespread *B. bainesii*—long misidentified as *B. hypogaea*—from summer-rainfall southern Africa. In *B. bainesii* the spike is just below ground, with flowers appearing around ground level; in *B. sambucina* the spike sits slightly above ground. Leaves of *B. bainesii* are longer and usually narrower, 3–10 mm wide, and roughly hairy, although leaf-hair type and density vary in both species. The *B. sambucina* tube is usually 35–55 mm long, similar to that of *B. bainesii*, but *B. bainesii* tepals measure 20–60 × 6–12 mm, are proportionally narrower, and do not overlap in their lower half as they do in *B. sambucina*. The *B. bainesii* tube has a wide throat, and its lower tepals join the upper lateral ones to form a prominent lip. By contrast, the *B. sambucina* tube is cylindrical or gradually widens from the base, with tepals arising at more or less the same level.

## Comparison with Babiana framesii {#s-ad5594c514}
Among species in the southern African winter-rainfall zone, the account considers *B. framesii* of the Bokkeveld Plateau a possible close relative and says the two can be difficult to separate when dry. *B. framesii* also has a long, straight tube, though it curves somewhat below the throat. Its smaller tepals are proportionally narrower and spread at almost a right angle to the tube; its flowers are at most lightly scented.

These diagnostic comparisons come from a regional South African account published in 2020; the excerpt gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency.

## Habitat {#s-fc9b9e1c96}
The regional flora records the species on rocky slopes and flats in fynbos and renosterveld.

This habitat description comes from a regional South African account published in 2012; it does not establish conditions across the full species range.
