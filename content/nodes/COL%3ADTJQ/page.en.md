## Eight source-labelled captive adults at one breeding base {#s-adad3dfa15}
The study selected four males and four females labelled Andrias davidianus, aged seven to eleven years, with reported body lengths 1.03–1.28 m and masses 6.9–12.6 kg. These are selected captive adult measurements, not wild age limits, species maximum size or snout–vent lengths. The facility was in Sangzhi County, Hunan, at 471 m elevation, with an artificial stream and caves. Monitoring ran from July to October in 2020–2022. The selected subjects do not become twenty-four independent animals simply because three years were covered. The methods do not establish genetic equivalence to every currently recognized Chinese Andrias lineage, so the source label is retained with that identity limitation.

## An ethogram distinguishes postures, actions and settings {#s-3c9a7d9bc1}
The PAE framework separately coded posture, action and environment. Investigators reviewed one day of video at two-day intervals, then sampled about twenty percent of marked footage for focal analysis. They defined ten postures, thirty-three actions, eleven environmental factors and forty-five behavioural patterns in nine functional categories. These counts refer to different coding levels and cannot be added as independent behaviours, animals or prevalence estimates. A behavioural category can combine several actions and settings. The ethogram describes the monitored captive breeding period; behaviours unrecorded in that sampling regime are not thereby proven absent from wild individuals or other seasons.

## The monitored breeding period differs from the spawning peak {#s-0c25da1c72}
The base’s breeding period was described as July–October, including preparation, spawning and subsequent care. Spawning in the study occurred mainly in August. The discussion separately cited earlier July–September reproduction and August–mid-October incubation accounts; those cited background periods are not new longitudinal measurements of all eight subjects. A broad breeding period is not the duration of egg deposition by each female or a universal wild calendar. Comparisons with Japanese giant salamanders and hellbenders in the discussion belong to those separate species and do not enlarge the measured davidianus sample.

## Gravel displacement and flowing-water exposure were observed {#s-518719d5b7}
Males displaced gravel outward from the cave floor using the head, limbs, body and tail. They also moved toward the water inlet and exposed parts of the body to flowing water, a category called showering. The authors discussed possible nesting-space, water-quality and egg-retention benefits of gravel displacement, and possible reproductive-development effects of showering, drawing partly on earlier literature. Observing these actions does not experimentally prove each proposed benefit or establish that every wild male performs them in the same order. The facility’s water inlet and cave construction constrain the settings in which the recorded actions occurred.

## Physical contact was coded during courtship and oviposition {#s-adb73a116e}
The ethogram grouped following, side-by-side abdominal contact, riding and other contact under courtship, and described intertwining, cloacal investigation and related actions around oviposition. Male ejaculation and female egg release were included in the source account. These are source-defined behavioural categories in a simulated natural breeding setting, not measured genetic mating success or a complete sequence shared by every pair. Discussion of pheromonal effects and reproductive synchronization relies partly on cited literature; the monitored contact footage alone does not isolate a chemical mechanism, quantify hormone changes or demonstrate causation.

## Male egg care includes movement and egg consumption {#s-94af6fe2a0}
The source described females leaving the cave after spawning and males attending offspring with tail fanning, agitation, shaking and egg-eating. The discussion proposed that water movement improved oxygen supply and that removal of unfertilized or diseased eggs could reduce contamination, citing earlier work. Those proposed functions are not controlled estimates of survival benefit in this study. The category egg-eating does not imply indiscriminate consumption of every viable egg, but the descriptions also do not provide a measured selection rule or an individual-by-individual hatch-success comparison. Captive care observations are not assumed for all wild or genetically distinct Andrias populations.

## Artificial caves describe the study setting, not universal requirements {#s-6641f357a1}
The monitored caves were reported at 0.9–1.5 square metres, with entrances 0.25–0.35 m wide and 0.30–0.35 m high. The discussion recommended water depth around 0.3–0.4 m for the facility’s breeding setting. Cave area, entrance dimensions and water depth are distinct measurements and are not natural-range habitat limits. The study did not randomly compare cave sizes to isolate a minimum requirement, a universal optimal depth or protection from enemies. Earlier claims about space and gonadal development were cited background; they cannot be treated as a dose-response experiment performed on these eight subjects.

## Measured water conditions are not thermal or chemical tolerance limits {#s-14d1034e0d}
Water quality was monitored every thirty minutes. During the study, water temperature ranged 16.9–22.8 °C, dissolved oxygen 5.50–9.25 mg/L and pH 6.58–7.70. These describe conditions encountered in the artificial stream, not experimentally determined critical temperatures, lethal limits or a universal optimum. The discussion cited separate temperature recommendations for gonadal development and incubation; those are not interchangeable with the measured environmental range. Successful spawning under the combined conditions does not identify the independent contribution of temperature, oxygen, pH, diet or cave geometry, and does not establish wild-release success.

## Daytime darkening in a spectral rather than pigment assay {#s-c05642931c}
Under the tested schedules, the authors describe dark-brown juveniles in the light phase and lighter brown in darkness. Illuminated spectra have a short-wave peak around 300–320 nm; dark-phase spectra also have a longer-wave peak around 650–700 nm. Mean brightness B2 is mean relative reflectance over 300–700 nm; chroma variables describe relative spectral contributions. Their UV/violet and green/yellow wavelength bands overlap, so these are not independent pigment concentrations. The Results’ later blanket statement of no daytime differences is broader than the preceding hue-specific tests. The study does not directly quantify melanin, chromatophore counts, hormones or animal visual perception.

## Fitted daily rhythms do not independently estimate free-running period {#s-9500ddac0d}
All forty animals are reported rhythmic, with the rhythm period defaulting to 24 h in the analysis. ## Proposed mechanisms and functions remain unmeasured {#s-59a54b1999}
The Discussion proposes melanosome aggregation/dispersion, melatonin and α-MSH regulation, daytime UV protection and nocturnal energy conservation.
