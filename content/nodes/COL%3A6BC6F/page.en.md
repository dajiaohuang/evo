## A local cloud-forest occurrence and its elevation {#s-60780ebf21}
The 2021 Santa Cruz Tepetotutla checklist reports Craugastor polymniae at 1,100 m during the 2018–2019 survey. It compares this with earlier reports at 1,420–1,500 m and lists photograph catalogue number IBH-RF 598. The locality is in Chinantla, Oaxaca, Mexico. These are reported occurrences, not a surveyed global range, independent authentication of the archived image, or evidence that every intervening elevation is occupied.

## Interpret the record within the survey design {#s-ec71f4bbbb}
Four observers made eleven five-day trips in wet and dry seasons across pristine and managed cloud-forest sites. Their daily survey windows were 09:00–13:00, 15:00–19:00 and 21:00–01:00; the paper reports 2,640 effective field hours for the whole herpetofaunal inventory. Animals were photographed and released, and reported GPS error was 5 m. The species row establishes presence in this survey context, not species-specific detection probability, abundance or trend. The accumulation curve had not reached an asymptote, so a missing record cannot establish absence.

## Six frogs near streams and a habitat exception {#s-dbab1bc7ce}
The species treatment reports six frogs, all within about 3 m of streams; all but one were in primary cloud forest outside disturbed areas. Earlier cited records include two specimens from July 1983, an unsuccessful search in 2000, one individual in 2009 and 21 in 2013. These are historical reports, not new counts from the 2018–2019 survey. The authors call the new locality the fourth known locality, while the Discussion also lists this frog among taxa observed exclusively in primary vegetation. The individual exception is retained, so that discussion summary is not read as absolute habitat exclusivity.
