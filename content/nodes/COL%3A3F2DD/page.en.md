## Ecological evidence {#s-e4de3b84e5}
At Nylsvley Nature Reserve in Limpopo, South Africa, researchers captured 23 Mohol bushbabies from March to August 2009 and monitored skin temperature in free-ranging animals, alongside laboratory and outdoor-enclosure metabolic measurements. The abstract reports torpor in 15% of the 20 animals in its comparison.

## Torpor observations in wild and laboratory animals {#s-8754d5465a}
Detailed results show that one free-ranging subadult male entered torpor on six of nine monitored days in the coldest month, for about four hours each time. Two other animals showed torpor only during laboratory metabolic measurements after food and water restriction. The study supports a physiological capacity for torpor, not a routine behavior or an estimate of how common it is in the wild. The authors discuss possible reproductive or territorial trade-offs, but did not test those explanations as causal mechanisms.

The authors’ explanations involving reproduction and territorial defense remain hypotheses, not species-wide current patterns or a conservation assessment.
