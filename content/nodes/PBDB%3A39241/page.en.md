## A crown group is defined from living birds {#s-197a71e830}
Neornithes includes the most recent common ancestor of all living birds and every descendant of that ancestor. Archaeopteryx falls outside this crown boundary. There is no single fossil feature that defines every crown bird, so Cretaceous candidates such as Vegavis and Asteriornis must be tested in explicit phylogenetic analyses rather than assigned by age or resemblance alone.

## Cretaceous skulls test the boundary in different ways {#s-6e9b311f8e}
The Antarctic fossil Vegavis AMNH FARB 30899 dates to 69.2–68.4 million years ago. A 2025 analysis of its nearly complete skull supports crown-waterfowl affinity, while earlier studies recorded conflicting placements. The European Asteriornis holotype NHMM 2013 008 is younger, at 66.8–66.7 million years ago, and parsimony and tip-dated Bayesian analyses place it differently. The 69.2 Ma crown-bird minimum in this account therefore depends on accepting the 2025 placement of Vegavis; the two fossils do not map a complete Cretaceous range or an origin area.

## One fossil cannot define the ecology of all birds {#s-18903f4a31}
The crown group includes birds with varied diets and habitats, so it has no single ecological guild. Vegavis preserves a narrow beak, an enlarged jaw-muscle attachment area and other skull features used to infer underwater prey capture. That functional interpretation applies to Vegavis alone; it is not evidence that all early or living crown birds shared the same feeding mode.
