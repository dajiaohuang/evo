## Two occurrences, including an older name {#s-7a867a0266}
The fixed Paleobiology Database (PBDB) roster accepts Bennettazhia oregonensis beneath Bennettazhia. This guide uses two species-level occurrences from two collections, linked to two references. One record was originally entered as Pteranodon oregonensis; PBDB connects that name combination to the accepted name Bennettazhia oregonensis. A changed combination is a link between names, not another fossil discovery.

## Two collections in Oregon’s Hudspeth unit {#s-faa32891d5}
The selected collections are Nelson Creek and UO 13522, both recorded in Oregon, United States (region code US), and assigned to the Hudspeth unit. Each has a database age bracket of 113.2–100.5 Ma, with the older bound in the Albian. Two localities in Oregon do not describe the species’ full range.

## The shared age bracket {#s-3814c87768}
Both selected collections carry the broad database bracket 113.2–100.5 Ma (Ma means million years ago). It bounds the recorded material, but does not show a continuous fossil record through that span or directly date the species’ origin, extinction or an individual’s lifespan.

## The two collections have different setting labels {#s-0030be6c5c}
Nelson Creek is labeled “marine indet.” for environment; UO 13522 is labeled “shallow subtidal indet.” Both carry the preservation-mode label “body.” These are collection-level database categories, not direct descriptions of Bennettazhia’s habitat or diet, and they need not characterize every fossil individually.

## Citations attached to the collections {#s-dc8449f426}
The database links Nelson Creek to Gilmore (1928), reference 14574, and UO 13522 to Retallack et al. (2023), reference 84064. These citations are attached to the respective collection records; the database attribution alone does not show which paper originally named the species.

## Evidence limits {#s-caadcb0c4e}
This guide selects two species-level records from the fixed 276-name PBDB pterosaur roster. Database acceptance and name combinations organize records; they do not settle every specimen identification or taxonomic dispute, and they remain separate from same-name Catalogue of Life concepts. The two localities, their broad age bracket and their collection-level setting labels do not establish Bennettazhia’s complete range, exact origin or extinction, abundance, habitat or diet. These occurrence records are not a full anatomical or ecological account.
