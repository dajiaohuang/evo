## Tropaeolum peregrinum {#s-d4add87d09}
## Hooked floral spurs rise from a climbing vine

A historical Flora of Panama account describes a high-climbing annual vine, usually hairless though hairy forms also occur. Its leaves have 3–7 lobes, measure 3–7 cm long and 3.5–8 cm wide, and are green or sometimes pale-glaucous below. Flowers grow in leaf axils, often forming false racemes. Their petals are yellow, often with purple spots near the base, and a short, thick spur ends in a dark hook. The larger upper petals are 15–20 mm long; the lower petals have long claws fringed with long purple hairs.

The fruiting carpels are dark brown to blackish and 10–14 mm long. The account’s distribution field says the species originated in Peru, is commonly cultivated worldwide, and often escapes cultivation.
