## Leaves, flowers and segmented pods {#s-4d53572a90}
The historical Flora of Panama account describes this plant as an herb or shrub 2–4 m tall, with few branches. Its round, ridged stems carry dense hooked and soft hairs. Each leaf has three leaflets: the narrow oval leaflets have blunt, short-pointed tips. Terminal leaflets are 5–9 cm long; lateral ones are 3.3–6 cm. The upper surface is glossy, with hairs along the midrib and scattered elsewhere; the lower surface is duller and more densely hairy. Dense, branched flower clusters grow at shoot tips and leaf axils; the individual flower stalks (pedicels) are 5–6 mm long. The hairy calyx has two lips, and the petals, especially the standard, have tiny raised bumps; that standard is obovate, notched at the tip, and 5.5–8 mm long. The fruit is a stalked, segmented pod (a loment) with up to six sections; each section is bluntly triangular with narrow, off-centre joins and a hairy surface. Seeds are blunt-oblong, about 2 × 1.3 mm. This historical regional description gives no population sampling frame or observation date and is not a current or global account.

## A historical range note {#s-b5a869eff9}
The same Flora of Panama source says the species is scattered in southern Mexico and Panama, the West Indies, and the northern half of South America. This is a historical regional statement, not a current range or a complete global inventory; the source does not classify these records as native, introduced, cultivated, escaped or wild.
