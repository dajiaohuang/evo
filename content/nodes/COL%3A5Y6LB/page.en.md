## One sequenced individual and a separate DNA-content estimate {#s-4ad77208a2}
The study extracted liver DNA from one Chiropterotriton priscus specimen as part of a sixteen-species, three-genus comparison, targeting ten gigabases of short-read data per species. Repeat analysis randomly sampled merged reads at roughly 0.1-fold coverage. Table 1 gives a genome C-value estimate of 32.9 picograms, based on blood-smear densitometry and species means from earlier genome-size work. Picograms of DNA are not gigabases, body mass or a complete genome assembly; the sequenced individual and the species-mean DNA-content estimate have different evidence roles. The sample does not determine variation among sexes, populations or ages.

## Detected repeat abundance is a method-dependent lower estimate {#s-96a05eb4c1}
Table 1 reports 45.3% detectable repeat abundance for Chiropterotriton priscus, including uncategorized repeats. Table 2 reports LTRs at 11.41%, and Table 3 separates ERV at 5.682% from Ty3/Gypsy at 5.727%. These are percentages of sampled bases identified by the study's repeat library, not percentages of animals, newly inserted elements or measured gene expression. Mutation can degrade repeats beyond recognition; the authors regard short-read masking as likely underestimating true repetitive content. Rounded table categories need not sum exactly, and uncategorized repeats should not be silently assigned to a known TE superfamily.

## Repeat divergence suggests relative expansion histories {#s-af510c2f64}
Priscus shared an older ERV divergence peak with larger-genome Bolitoglossa and had a Gypsy/Ty3 peak around Kimura distance twenty-seven. That contrasts with lower-distance peaks in dimidiatus. The comparison supports different repeat histories within the sampled genus, without establishing exact elapsed years, a body-size effect or a causal regulatory mechanism. Inferred common-ancestor expansion and lineage-specific silencing or deletion remain interpretations.

## Selected Mexican survey localities, 2017–2018 {#s-b697e8129b}
Figure 1 associates source-labelled Chiropterotriton priscus with Cerro Potosí. Researchers searched 26 localities in five Mexican states from March 2017 to November 2018; salamanders were encountered at sixteen. Identification used external size, tail proportions, feet and color pattern. These sites are observed study links, not the species’ complete distribution or proof of present occupancy. No individual voucher or molecular re-identification has been independently checked. The additional Aquiloeurycea sp. is left unnamed and its observations are not redistributed among accepted species.

## Skin swabs describe a filtered bacterial community {#s-fc1094e047}
For Chiropterotriton priscus and the other sampled hosts, the study changed gloves between animals, rinsed each with 100 ml sterile water and swabbed the whole body for thirty seconds. A separate ventral-body/tail/leg swab was used for Bd testing. The V4 16S bacterial assay retained forward reads, filtered very rare variants and rarefied to 17,536 reads per sample. It is not a complete skin metagenome, cultured viable-cell census or fungal community survey. Eight environmental swabs represented cave walls; each cave supplied only one wall sample, with no matched forest or karst environmental sampling. Negative bacterial controls did not amplify and were not sequenced, which is not independent proof of absence of contamination.

## Bd detection is not a test of resistance or disease survival {#s-6d8bb679eb}
The Results report 0/5 Bd-positive Chiropterotriton priscus samples. This is a small, dated sampled proportion, not regional prevalence, an experimental infection challenge or proof of resistance. No obvious disease signs or dead/dying animals were observed during the sampling visits; absence of observed signs does not establish later survival or population safety. Bd was quantified as ITS copies with synthetic DNA standards, not directly counted zoospores. ITS copy number differs among strains, so copy values cannot be equated to a cited fatal-zoospore threshold. Aggregate Results 31/86 differs from Methods microbiome comparison groups 30 positive/57 negative; skin-sequencing and diagnostic denominators remain unresolved without the individual supplement.

## Antifungal capacity is inferred rather than demonstrated in this host {#s-744114373f}
The study matches bacterial marker sequences to a reference database of previously tested Bd-inhibitory isolates and predicts functions with PICRUSt2. It does not culture this Chiropterotriton priscus sample’s bacteria to demonstrate inhibition, measure antibiotic production or test a probiotic treatment. Cave and forest samples did not differ significantly in abundance/diversity of potentially inhibitory bacteria; this does not establish equivalent protection. Pooled microbial co-occurrence edges are statistical associations, not observed cooperation or competition, and the networks were not estimated for each host species. The authors warn that reference-genome predictions may miss real functions or infer absent ones in poorly represented cave communities.
