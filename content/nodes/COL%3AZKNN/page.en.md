## Read the study {#s-c0ea1d563c}
How was bite force measured for Crocodylus palustris in a standardized adult sample? This guide follows the accepted COL26.8 record ZKNN and reads the study at its stated sample and geographic scale. In Erickson et al.'s 2012 comparison, one sexually mature mugger crocodile was tested, with a mean sample body mass of about 207 kg. At the prominent molariform tooth near the back of the upper jaw, the study reported a taxon-representative bite force of 7,295 N; only one sexually mature individual was tested, so no within-species range can be estimated. Researchers used the highest of 3–5 bites per animal. Across species, body mass was the main predictor of bite force, while rostral proportion explained little additional variation. This is a standardized measurement from a limited institutional sample, not a field-performance constant or population range.

## Limits of these trait data {#s-1664be3b77}
This account paraphrases the same-name ReptTraits v1-2 entry, retaining its 2023 taxonomic framework and sources for individual traits. One row per species compresses variation in sex, age, locality and populations; matching name and family do not ensure equivalent later concepts. Reported maximum length is not an absolute species limit, and reproductive means are not fixed counts in every clutch. Many mass entries are estimated from length equations and are not presented here as measured mass. Juvenile-length headers and definitions differ and are also omitted from reader conclusions. Conservation, venom, current distribution and population trends are not updated here; unrecorded fields remain gaps. Dataset citation labels preserve the source chain and do not imply that this project independently reread every original work.
