## The deposited caecilian record {#s-06157dbc77}
This page summarizes the CaecilianTraits version 4 record for Rhinatrema ron, filed under Rhinatrema in Rhinatrematidae (species row 173). That is the source’s identification; its match to a current COL26.8 species concept has not been checked. The compilation keeps individual, population and species entries distinct.

## Measurement material {#s-bf4b78a738}
There are two measurement rows, both classified as species-level; the source lists no individual-level or population-level rows. These are records in a compilation, not a count of animals or evidence that two populations were measured. The source cautions that individual identifiers and measurement conditions need row-level review; body measurements cannot be compared safely without knowing whether they are fresh, preserved or summarized.

## Compiled habitat categories {#s-2a258c6e5e}
The compilation assigns the broad habitat categories Terrestrial and Freshwater (inland waters), and the microhabitat category Fossorial, meaning associated with burrowing. These labels do not show how often the animal uses either setting or define its full range.

## Compiled reproduction category {#s-f012069ec7}
The source category is oviparous, meaning egg-laying. A cited passage says the species is suspected to lay eggs in soil at the edge of permanent forest lakes and ponds; “suspected” marks this as an interpretation, not a confirmed observation. The category does not describe parental care or timing.

## Compiled anatomical counts {#s-7e952743e5}
The species-level rows give 298 dorsal annuli, 4.2 annuli behind the vent and 5.4 annuli interrupted by the vent. The fractional values indicate summaries, not literal counts on one animal. These records do not establish fixed counts for every individual.

## Water-dependent larval development {#s-39e083c022}
The compilation records a water-dependent larval stage. The cited passage says larvae hatch from eggs and are believed to finish development in water; “believed” preserves the source’s uncertainty. This concerns development, not where adults always live.
