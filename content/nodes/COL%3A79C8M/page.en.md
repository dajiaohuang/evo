## What does the historical account describe about the willow? {#s-2d66e30b03}
The Flora of Panama account describes a shrub or tree up to 15 m tall. Slender, flexible branches have sparse hairs and dry reddish; the youngest growth dries yellowish. Narrow, lance-shaped leaves are about 5–6 cm long and roughly 4 mm wide, with pointed tips, blunt bases and toothed edges. The underside is hairless, while the upper midrib is hairy and later loses its hairs. The petiole is 3–5 mm long, channeled above and hairy; the toothed, leaf-like stipules are 3–4 mm long and fall away. Loose catkins (flowering spikes) 3–10 cm long grow at the tips of short side shoots; flowers are crowded when open and more spaced in fruit, with their individual stalks reduced. The account describes female flowers only: each has a deeply two-lobed gland, a narrow elliptic ovary on a short stalk and four finger-shaped stigmas in two pairs. The ovoid to ellipsoid fruit is about 6 mm long and dries brown. Compressed, trapezoid-shaped seeds are about 1 mm long and bear long white hairs. Male flowers were not seen in the source account. This is a historical regional description without a stated population sample or observation date.

## What range does the historical flora report? {#s-6d72ca7c20}
The Flora of Panama account reports a broad range across South America and north through Central America into Mexico. This is the historical source’s summary, not a current survey or complete range inventory.
