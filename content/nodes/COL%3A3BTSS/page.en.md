## Evergreen leaves and separate male and female flowers {#s-b80a348268}
The 1963 Ebenaceae treatment describes *Euclea coriacea* as evergreen, growing there as shrublets 1–3 m high or trees 3–10 m high. Its stiff leaves are usually 2–5 × 1–3 cm, with a distinctly wavy edge that is entire or minutely scalloped. The species is dioecious: male and female flowers occur on separate plants. The flowers are white to cream; male flower clusters are looser and larger, with longer stalks than the female clusters. The fleshy fruit is 1.2–1.5 cm across and changes from green through yellow to deep red as it ripens; it usually contains one seed, occasionally two or three. This regional account gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency.

## A 1963 account compares the leaves and flowers {#s-fae47cb20f}
The same account says complete material makes the species distinct from others. It compares *E. coriacea* with *E. crispa* var. *ovata*: some leafy forms can be distinguished by vegetative characters, and their flowers differ, with the corolla of *E. crispa* var. *ovata* split more than halfway down. It also compares *E. coriacea* with *E. tomentosa*, whose leaves it describes as elliptic to obovate and without the wavy edge. These are comparisons from that treatment, not a claim that the named taxa are interchangeable.

## A later guide contrasts fruit size and the corolla opening {#s-878e343960}
A 2002 southern African tree guide also compares *E. coriacea* with *E. crispa* subsp. *ovata*. It describes the latter as usually having opposite leaves and smaller fruits, about 5 mm across. In *E. coriacea*, the corolla tube is only slightly lobed at its opening; in *E. crispa* subsp. *ovata*, the lobes extend at least halfway down the tube. This is a separate source’s comparison using its own taxonomic name for the other taxon.

## Two grassland habitat categories {#s-d9c9107609}
A separate 2017 regional guide lists “Dry Grassland” and “Mesic Grassland”; mesic means moderately moist. These are habitat categories, not individual collection sites or a complete range. The guide gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency and does not state whether its records concern wild, cultivated or managed plants.
