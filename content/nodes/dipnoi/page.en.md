## How genes test lungfish relationships {#reading-introduction}
A sampled lungfish liver became a library of gene sequences. Comparing retained sequences with other living vertebrates tests the position of lungfishes relative to coelacanths and tetrapods. A calibrated molecular clock asks a further question about divergence, without turning that estimate into a lungfish fossil record.

## A lungfish liver became a gene library {#s-7d4b120929}
To test where living lungfishes fit among jawed vertebrates, Hallström and Janke sequenced 1,200 random expressed sequence tags (ESTs) from 1 g of freshly frozen liver tissue from *Protopterus aethiopicus*, the African marbled lungfish. The study reports 771 unique nuclear EST sequences not previously recorded for this species.

After sequence selection and filtering, the final alignment contained 78 genes and 65,247 nucleotide positions across 12 vertebrate species; it supported a lungfish–tetrapod sister relationship. These are results from a sampled living-lineage analysis, not a date for the lungfish fossil record or evidence that one fossil species directly gave rise to another.

## A clock boundary, not a fossil endpoint {#s-bb52059f67}
The calibrated analysis uses 24 calibrations and estimates a boundary near 409 Ma for the lineage containing living lungfishes and tetrapods relative to coelacanths. The displayed interval carries this model result to the present; it is not a complete fossil history or an exact Dipnoi origin date.
