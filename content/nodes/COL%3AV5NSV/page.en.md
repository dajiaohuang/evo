## Accepted name and placement {#s-7e9b0194a1}
COL26.8 accepts Boulenophrys dupanglingensis Xiao & Mo, 2025 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Megophryidae (family) → Boulenophrys (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## Low-elevation forest-litter records at Dupangling {#s-b9dc4fb8fb}
The 2025 description placed male holotype HUNU 22SA01 at Dajiangyuan, Daoxian, Hunan, at 380 m, collected on 20 April 2022. Female paratypes came from Daguyuan, Jiangyong, at recorded elevations of 442 and 451 m. The account knew the species only from Dupangling National Nature Reserve and observed frogs in leaf litter of evergreen secondary forest, typically near mountain streams, at 380–451 m. These surveyed localities and substrates do not establish the complete current range or an obligatory habitat requirement.

## Diagnostic measurements and a separate six-male comparison {#s-f1b2dbdef3}
The diagnosis reported seven adult males at 37.6–40.2 mm snout–vent length and three females at 41.8–45.9 mm. Its male mean was 38.9 with an accompanying ±1.3 value, whereas the sexual-size paragraph used six males with mean 38.8 and ±0.9 and called females larger. These different male denominators and summaries are retained rather than pooled; the error-statistic type is not certified from these paragraphs. Anatomy included a vomerine ridge without teeth, a rounded unnotched tongue margin, and rudimentary toe webbing without lateral fringes. The description’s dimorphism conclusion remains attributed to this small type sample.

## Holotype colour before and after preservation {#s-751a1eafef}
The live holotype account described red-orange groin colour, a light-salmon throat, chest and ventral body with dark-brown patches, and a yellowish-brown iris. The preservative account described dorsal fading to greyish brown, an interorbital triangular marking and dorsal V mark, and ventral fading to greyish white with markings becoming indistinct. These are observations of the holotype under different conditions, not interchangeable live-colour descriptions or a quantified species-wide colour distribution.

## Calls and mature eggs support a dated breeding inference {#s-1ce6ddf11b}
The authors recorded male calls between April and June and found mature creamy-white eggs in females collected during that period. They suggested that breeding includes those months; the observations do not establish its complete beginning or end. Adult males had a single internal subgular vocal sac and developed prominent black nuptial spines at the dorsal bases of fingers I and II during breeding condition. No tadpoles were encountered, so the account did not establish larval habitat or development.

## A character cited in the gutu comparison {#s-f12f9223e8}
The 2025 gutu description lists Boulenophrys dupanglingensis as without a vomerine ridge. This is an attributed comparator statement within that paper’s taxonomic scope, not a new measurement series for this species. The gutu adult sample spans 34.4–44.7 mm in seven males and 36.2–52.8 mm in eight females; those numbers belong to gutu, not to this comparator. The selected character does not establish population-wide frequency, complete diagnosis, diet or developmental biology.
