## Senecio oerstedianus {#s-4896715eb5}
## A creeping rhizome supports woolly yellow heads

The historical *Flora of Panama* description gives an upright herb 30–50 cm tall, arising from a coarse, creeping rhizome 10–15 mm wide. Stems may grow singly or in loose clusters—the source leaves this uncertain. Dense white woolly hairs cover most of the plant; upper leaf surfaces become irregularly hairless early, and stems lose hairs unevenly.

Leaves are spaced along the stem and become smaller upward. The lower and middle leaves are thick and leathery, at least in dried specimens, with lance-shaped to narrowly ovate blades 6–10 by 2–5 cm, toothed edges and sheathing leaf stalks. A single terminal cluster bears 10–20 yellow radiate heads on stout, densely woolly stalks. Each head has about 21 woolly outer bracts, around eight ray florets and 20–30 disc florets. The column-shaped dry fruits are hairless and 2–3 mm long; abundant white pappus bristles are as long as, or slightly longer than, the bracts.
