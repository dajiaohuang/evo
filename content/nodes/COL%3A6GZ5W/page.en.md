## What stem and flower traits are recorded for Euphorbia chamaerrhodos? {#s-9c8be8ba92}
The cylindrical stems are pubescent. Leaves are opposite, with palm-like veins, entire margins, and leaf-like stipules between the petioles. Green bracts surround solitary or paired cyathia, Euphorbia’s cup-like flower units, at the upper stem nodes. The involucre is bell-shaped and hairy, with four or more transverse-elliptic glands; each gland has a matching, petal-like appendage that spreads outward. Female flowers lack a pseudoperianth, and their ovaries are pubescent; the styles are two-lobed. The fruit is bristly, and the seeds lack a caruncle.
