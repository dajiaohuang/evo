## A Chinese species described from field collections {#s-381b5d6998}
Catalogue of Life COL26.8 lists Amanita circulata Y.Y. Cui, Q. Cai & Zhu L. Yang as an accepted species usage. The linked Fungal Names and Index Fungorum record is used to check the name identity; it is not a source for the biological description.

The biological claims come from a named species account and phylogeny in a 2023 taxonomic study of Amanita section Vaginatae in China.

The species diagnosis highlights threadlike fungal filaments (hyphae) that dominate the inner remnants of the volva, the veil tissue around the mushroom's stem base. The paper also describes the name-bearing type specimen and microscopic characters; the available account does not provide a broader survey of variation across populations.

The type collection was gathered at Puer, Lancang Lahu Autonomous County, Yunnan, China, on 20 August 2016. The paper also lists dated specimens from Anhui and Yunnan. Its stated range is the authors' 2023 account, not a global inventory.

The authors report mushrooms growing alone or scattered on soil in subtropical mixed forests associated with beech-family (Fagaceae) and pine-family (Pinaceae) trees. This field association does not establish that the fungus depends on, or forms a physiological partnership with, either tree family.

The habitat observations come from wild field collections.

In the paper's combined multilocus phylogeny, A. circulata falls in a monophyletic clade with A. flavidocerea, A. pekeoides, A. verrucosivolva and three unnamed or provisionally identified taxa. This describes the sampled tree in that study; it does not establish ancestry or a dated evolutionary history.

The placement is limited to the study's sampled taxa, genetic markers and analyses. The account does not assess broader phylogenomic evidence or divergence times.

The authors report A. circulata from eastern and southwestern China and list type and additional dated collections in Anhui and Yunnan. These published localities describe the paper's records, not an exhaustively surveyed global range.

The single taxonomic study describes its sampled material but does not establish developmental or broad population variation, or a complete diagnostic synthesis.

The account does not establish interaction mechanisms, dependence on a tree resource, seasonal patterns or population-level ecology.

No exhaustive global occurrence search or assessment of native versus introduced status is included.

Life history, fossils and conservation are not assessed in this account, which is based on one original taxonomic study rather than a comprehensive species review.
