## Read the study {#s-5f2b05a5d5}
How much can a blunt snout tell us about biting performance? This guide follows the accepted COL26.8 record BTRF and reads the study at its stated sample and geographic scale. An adult bite-force study covering 23 living crocodilian species grouped the Chinese alligator with the broad-snouted caiman as a blunt-snouted, blunt-toothed type associated with hard-shelled prey. The similarly sized species still spanned much of the molariform tooth-pressure range among crocodilians of comparable size, and snout shape alone did not reliably predict bite force. The feeding description synthesizes earlier literature; the experiment measured biting performance in adult research, conservation and display specimens.

## How to read reproductive records {#s-ebf8de7f37}
the reproductive-mode field uses the original label oviparous; sex determination is recorded as TSD; consolidated mean offspring or eggs per litter/clutch 20.25; lowest reported clutch/litter mean 20; highest reported clutch/litter mean 30; consolidated litters/clutches per year 1; egg length 60.8 mm; egg width 33.6 mm. Clutch/litter summaries can use midpoints of reported means or, when means are unavailable, extrema; they are not necessarily arithmetic averages of observed clutches. Fractions do not describe non-integer young in a litter. The lowest/highest fields are defined as endpoints of reported means rather than guaranteed smallest/largest individual clutches. Reproductive and sex-determination labels do not establish fetal nutrition or uniformity across all populations.

## Limits of these trait data {#s-ab13d36698}
This account paraphrases the same-name ReptTraits v1-2 entry, retaining its 2023 taxonomic framework and sources for individual traits. One row per species compresses variation in sex, age, locality and populations; matching name and family do not ensure equivalent later concepts. Reported maximum length is not an absolute species limit, and reproductive means are not fixed counts in every clutch. Many mass entries are estimated from length equations and are not presented here as measured mass. Juvenile-length headers and definitions differ and are also omitted from reader conclusions. Conservation, venom, current distribution and population trends are not updated here; unrecorded fields remain gaps. Dataset citation labels preserve the source chain and do not imply that this project independently reread every original work.
