## Genome searches constrain the SWS2-loss hypothesis {#s-017f407f65}
The published imitator scaffold includes LWS and upstream MECP2, but the authors did not detect SWS2 between them. Searches across the genome also failed to identify a gene or recognizable pseudogene; reciprocal candidate searches returned related opsins rather than SWS2. Positive-control searches could recover SWS2 in other frogs. This supports the proposed ancestral loss, while distinguishing genome-search evidence from the pumilio pooled-eye transcriptome. The proposed 70–40-million-year interval is inherited from dated broader phylogenies, not a directly sequenced deletion date for imitator.

## A selected RH1 site does not give a measured visual shift {#s-3ce1833bea}
The discussion identifies imitator among branches with serine replacing alanine at RH1 site124, numbered against bovine rhodopsin. MEME detects a selection signal at this branch-site combination. Bovine mutagenesis provides a possible functional analogy, but this paper does not experimentally measure the spectral shift or colour discrimination of imitator pigment. Numbering is a shared alignment reference rather than a certified species-local residue index; a candidate functional effect remains a hypothesis.

## Repeat annotations are computational observations {#s-d38891a1b8}
The authors built species-specific repeat libraries with RepeatModeler2, classified unknown elements with RFSB and supplemented the libraries with vertebrate repeats before applying RepeatMasker. Its hits are termed insertions in the paper, but a hit is not independently demonstrated to be a complete, expressed or mobile element. Superfamily labels can aggregate distinct families. Element counts, total occupied length and genome proportions are separate quantities; overlapping hierarchy categories cannot safely be added as independent fractions. No new repeat annotation or expression experiment for Ranitomeya imitator was performed here.

## Across-species correlations do not prove this species mechanism {#s-c136c1d107}
The study compared61 assemblies using phylogenetically adjusted models, an earlier pruned tree, contig-N50 covariates and multiple-comparison correction. Recently active Ty3/DIRS1-associated sequences correlate with genome size across that sample, but recent activity is a divergence-based proxy, not direct observation of current transposition in this species. Length and open-reading-frame similarity filters likewise do not demonstrate active protein expression. The authors' suggested developmental constraints are comparative hypotheses; they do not establish a causal TE effect on this species' larval duration or clutch size.
