## Stem surface {#s-6692a96bee}
The Brazilian Flora 2020 record lists the stem as pubescent or strigose, with hairs lying close to the surface and pointing backward. It records simple hairs and unbranched bristles flattened from front to back; branched and glandular hairs are absent. Prickles are given in four forms: spreading and scattered, backward-pointing and scattered, spreading and infranodal (below a node), or backward-pointing and infranodal. The field does not assign these alternatives to individual specimens. Short lateral shoots (brachyblasts) are absent.

## Leaves and inflorescences {#s-63545f571b}
Stipules, small structures at leaf bases, are caducous and fall early. Extrafloral nectaries (nectar-secreting glands outside flowers) and projections between pairs of pinnae (the leaf’s primary side divisions) are absent. The inflorescence, or flower cluster, is globose; floral bracts (small leaf-like structures) are shorter than the flowers before they open (anthesis).

## Flowers {#s-1fd8ae0515}
The perianth (calyx and corolla together) is recorded as having four parts. The membranous calyx, the outer sepal whorl, has triangular lobes. Corolla (petal-whorl) lobes have one to three veins and are hairy. The androecium (the stamens collectively) is isostemonous, with stamens equal in number to the petals; the filaments are pink.

## Habitat category {#s-125e4d7e1d}
The regional dataset labels the habitat “Terrícola,” Portuguese for terrestrial. This category does not specify a collection locality or establish the species’ full range.
