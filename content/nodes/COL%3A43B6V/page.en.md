## Mikania clematidifolia {#s-0b92cda592}
## Three basal veins and interpetiolar stipules

The Brazilian Flora 2020 record describes smooth, twining branches with shallow lengthwise lines and solid pith. Opposite leaves have stipules—small appendages—between the leaf stalks, longer than 2 mm. Their blades are lobed, with elliptic to ovate lobes; margins are recorded as entire or lobed. The base narrows, the tip is shortly tapered, and both surfaces are nearly smooth, with a few glands on the lower face. Three main veins radiate from the base, and the blades are leathery.

Loose panicles or corymb-like clusters carry stalked heads. Lance-shaped bracts occur near the top of each head stalk; the bracts around each head are oblanceolate, fringed and shaggy, with acute tips. The tubular corolla has triangular, tapered, glandular lobes as long as the tube. The dry fruits (cypselae) are smooth. A separate habitat field says terrestrial; this regional record gives no locality or complete range.
