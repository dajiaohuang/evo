## Separate the holotype from probable referrals {#s-969a1e8f61}
The original description designates FRDC-GS GJ (06) 01–18, an associated partial postcranial skeleton housed at FRDC in Lanzhou, as holotype. Additional material includes unnumbered hindlimb elements of a large individual and isolated paired pubes FRDC-GS JB(07)01-01. The pubes are only probably referred because the holotype lacks them for direct comparison. No skull is preserved in this described material. Three material entries are not three complete skeletons, and a probable referral cannot automatically establish the holotype’s missing anatomy.

## Grey mudstones and overlying red beds {#s-f2b9dae624}
The holotype came from White Ghost Castle in the Yujingzi Basin of Gansu, China, in grey mudstones near the lower part of the Xinminpu Group, described as Early Cretaceous Aptian–Albian. A second partial skeleton came from overlying red beds, and the probable pubes from near the holotype at approximately the same level. The existing occurrence guide retains PBDB’s Xiagou label and 121.4–113.2 Ma database interval. These source descriptions are not automatically a new precise age determination. Associated fauna and facies provide context, not quantitative habitat preference or a complete range.

## Tail vertebrae, hand claws and the metatarsus {#s-400f3c5e2b}
The diagnostic combination includes a notched anterior caudal neural spine, ventral keels on middle caudals, and a more curved first manual claw compared with straighter other manual claws. Metatarsal III is pinched proximally but remains visible along the extensor surface; the authors call the foot subarctometatarsal, rather than identical to the condition in highly derived ornithomimids. Pedal digit I remains present, and the short broad pedal claws are slightly curved. These features inform diagnosis and phylogenetic comparison without directly measuring grip force, running speed or use of each digit.

## Thirteen to fourteen lines and continued growth {#s-bdb4157b1e}
A transverse thin section from half of the holotype’s right fibular shaft, sampled 27 cm from its proximal end, was examined with polarized and reflected light. Thirteen to fourteen presumed annual growth lines span the inner cortex to the periosteal surface: true lines of arrested growth (LAGs) occur internally and externally, with mainly annuli in the middle and some transitions between forms. Narrowing outer spacing and changing vascularity, together with open neurocentral sutures and unfused ankle bones, support continued growth approaching somatic maturity. The authors use young adult and subadult in different passages. This is neither a fully grown individual nor a hatchling, and line counts are not assumption-free exact ages or lifespan estimates.

## 626 kilograms belongs to an estimation model {#s-00d87be265}
The authors estimated 626 kg for the holotype using a femoral-length/body-mass regression, noting that robust forelimbs might imply greater mass than Late Cretaceous ornithomimids at a given femur length. This is not direct weighing or an adult maximum. Different comparison taxa used femur length or distal tibial width, and Gallimimus was recalculated rather than simply retaining a 490 kg model value from another work. Cross-species numbers require their landmarks and equations, not treatment as wild population means measured by one uniform method.

## Unresolved relationships in the 2009/2010 analysis {#s-b31953dd65}
The original analysis used 293 characters and 72 theropod taxa, displaying a strict consensus. Beishanlong was more derived than Pelecanimimus and Shenzhousaurus but outside the clade of Garudimimus plus Ornithomimidae; incomplete overlap in preserved parts left its relationships with Harpymimus and others incompletely resolved. Keel-like caudal anatomy could also depend on position within the vertebral series, so one feature does not settle sister relationships. The 2014 Deinocheirus study used another matrix and more complete material. Changing analyses and current catalogue ancestry remain separate, without declaring Beishanlong a direct ancestor of another species.

## Discussing diet when the material lacks a skull {#s-744a87277b}
The paper discusses Beishanlong alongside other large, possibly herbivorous coelurosaurs in patterns of Asian gigantism and environmental association. Its described material is postcranial, so toothless beaks, teeth or direct stomach contents from relatives are not transferred to this species. Associated fauna in grey mesic deposits and another specimen in red beds also show why preservation and sampling matter for ecological comparisons. Plant species, omnivorous proportions, social behavior and obligatory wetland use are not established here; Deinocheirus in situ fish remains do not supply Beishanlong diet.
