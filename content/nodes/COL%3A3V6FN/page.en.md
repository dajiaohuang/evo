The pinned COL26.8 checklist accepts Liolaemus fabiani in the family Liolaemidae. ReptTraits brings records together as one row per species, so these values describe that compilation, not every population as if it were identical.

## What the length numbers measure {#s-e56d2bb94d}
The compiled record gives a reported maximum snout–vent length (SVL) of 90.3 mm and a female maximum of 68.5 mm. SVL measures from the snout to the vent and leaves out the tail, so it is shorter than total length. These are maxima found in the cited material, not a growth ceiling or a typical size. The two sex-specific values may come from different studies or places; by themselves they do not measure how much males and females differ within one population.

## Three broad labels for activity and food {#s-d401c35174}
For this species, the row labels its microhabitat terrestrial, its diet omnivorous, and its activity diurnal. In plain terms, the entry groups it with ground-associated microhabitats, animals that eat both plant and animal material, and daytime activity. These are broad categories: the row gives no detailed habitat description or prey list, and it does not show whether activity or diet varies among places, seasons, or life stages.

## What the litter figures mean {#s-9809de7f4b}
The source labels the reproductive mode viviparous, or live-bearing. It gives a consolidated litter-or-clutch mean of 2.5, with reported-mean endpoints of 2 and 3. ReptTraits may calculate a summary from the midpoint of published means, or use the midpoint of reported extremes when means are missing; 2.5 is therefore not a count from one observed litter. The low and high fields mark endpoints of reported means, not guaranteed smallest and largest individual litters. These labels do not tell us how embryos are nourished or establish that every population shares the same reproductive pattern.

## Limits of this account {#s-f51800fdbe}
This page paraphrases the same-name ReptTraits v1-2 row and keeps its 2023 name framework and trait-specific citations. A single row compresses differences in sex, age, locality, and population; the same name and family do not prove that a historical record matches every later taxonomic concept. A reported maximum is not an absolute size limit, and a reproductive mean is not a fixed litter size. ReptTraits also includes mass estimates derived from length equations; they are not presented here as measured mass. Juvenile-length fields have differing headers and definitions, so they are not used as conclusions. This page does not update conservation status, venom, current distribution, or population trends. The citation labels retain the dataset's source chain; they do not mean this project independently rechecked every original study.
