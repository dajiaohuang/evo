## overview {#s-1417281497}
Plains zebras use African grasslands and open woodland and feed mainly on grasses. Stripe patterns vary within the species. This entry concerns the entire species; extinction of the quagga must not be assigned to all Equus quagga.

## Different stages in the mass fields {#s-0eb9a61bfd}
Historical summary values are adult body mass 300000 g; mass at birth or hatching 32000 g. Adult mass can combine males, females and adults of unspecified sex; birth/hatching mass describes a different life stage. These are not a longitudinal series of the same individuals or a species-wide range of individual masses.

The archive’s maximum_longevity_y value is 40 years, taking the largest record in the collected material under the source method. It is not typical lifespan, mean wild lifespan, a survival ceiling for every individual or a current repeatedly attainable record.

## Evidence limits {#s-76a556b8cf}
Quantitative traits, complete ranges, divergence dates and current conservation assessments require separate evidence. Catalogue counts are not counts of living wild biological species.
The 2015 archive compiles historical species- and subspecies-level life-history records: non-longevity values are medians or interpolated medians, while maximum_longevity_y is the largest compiled record. Historical taxonomic inheritance means a same-name match does not establish equivalence to the pinned COL26.8 concept; these records do not yield a uniform wild-only or captive-only estimate. EltonTraits 1.0 converts literature descriptions into diet weights and categorical activity/foraging fields; mammalian ABC fields may draw on congeners, so they are not direct species-level observations in every field. Neither archive establishes current distribution or conservation status.
