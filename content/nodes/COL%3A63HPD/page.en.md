## Records represented for Otus {#s-17b0729652}
AVONET v7 has measured records for 21 Otus species. Examples include Otus bakkamoena, O. brucei, O. capnodes, O. collari and O. elegans. The hierarchy follows pinned COL26.8, while the biological values retain the source's historical names and sampling scope.

## Differences between records {#s-6d203cdc83}
Among those records, compiled mean mass ranges from 50.3 g for Otus ireneae to 144 g for O. insularis. The represented habitat categories include Forest and Woodland, and the trophic-niche category includes Invertivore. These are comparisons among source records, not individual mass ranges or traits shared by all owls.

## Historical life-history coverage {#s-6e85aad81c}
Fifteen historical life-history accounts are represented, including Otus bakkamoena, O. brucei, O. elegans, O. everetti and O. fuliginosus. Adult body mass and offspring-per-litter or eggs-per-clutch fields occur in all 15 accounts; a maximum-lifespan record occurs in 13, and birth or hatching mass in 8. These are coverage counts within the archive, not a complete denominator for accepted species. Compare each field with its event, unit and source in view.

## Historical feeding ecology: two field groups {#s-8d585e8a7e}
One historical subset has 24 entries with both food-category weights and foraging-stratum estimates, including Otus ireneae, O. balli, O. spilocephalus, O. angelinae and O. longicornis. None has a mammalian activity-period field. The weights are source-based comparisons, not measured proportions of each species' diet or daily schedule.

## Historical feeding ecology: stratum-only records {#s-7ec6ff7002}
Another historical subset has 17 entries with foraging-stratum estimates but no food-category weights or mammalian activity-period fields. Examples are Otus sagittatus, O. rufescens, O. icterorhynchus, O. umbra and O. manadensis. These stratum records do not fill the missing diet or activity information.

## Evidence limits {#s-db2651c337}
The AVONET summary covers 21 measured species with historical one-to-one crosswalks and matching names and families. The 50.3–144 g endpoints are compiled species means, not individual minimum and maximum masses. They do not cover every accepted descendant or remove the sampling and taxonomic limits described on species pages.
The life-history summary covers 15 historical same-name, same-family accounts. Its medians, averages of middle values, maximum-lifespan records and possible taxonomic inheritance are properties of the archive, not direct measurements of current species concepts or every descendant.
The 24-entry feeding subset has both food and foraging-stratum groups; the separate 17-entry subset has strata only. Both are historical same-name, same-family records, not a complete descendant catalogue. Percentages standardize literature descriptions and do not establish shared genus-wide traits; source taxonomy and per-record interpretation still matter.

Among those 15 limited accounts, habitat categories appear in 14, feeding-niche categories in 10, primary-lifestyle categories in 15, and migration categories in 15. The beak, wing, tarsus, tail and mass fields on these accounts are not interpreted here and do not support a genus-wide size comparison.

## Limits of these historical data {#s-d01a1c4399}
These 15 entries cover limited historical ecology records, not every accepted descendant. COL26.8 navigation, same-name same-family associations and two one-to-one same-name crosswalks do not resolve later differences in species concepts. Coarse categories are not independent behavioral observations, and historical maps are not current occupancy or conservation assessments. Original morphology inferences and mass sources are retained but are not treated as direct measurements of the species.
