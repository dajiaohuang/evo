## Accepted name and placement {#s-eb59e1a976}
COL26.8 accepts Hynobius bambusicolus Wang, Othman, Qiu & Borzée, 2023 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Caudata (order) → Hynobiidae (family) → Hynobius (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## Reading route {#s-becd76ee35}
Begin with the accepted record and its parent genus, then use the family and order pages to locate related catalogue entries. This page supplies a taxonomic orientation. Species-specific morphology, ecology, life history, distribution, evolutionary evidence and conservation information still require reviewed biological sources.

## Two released adults and four immature vouchers {#s-b97c8a9d27}
Table 4 separates wild adults 22HyF001 and 22HyF002 from four immature type vouchers, 22HyF003–006. The two adults have reported total lengths 191.57 and 196.94 mm and nine and ten costal grooves. Total length includes the tail and is not snout–vent length. The table calls the types juveniles, while nomenclature calls them subadults. Neither two adults nor those immature types establish an age-complete population size range; an adult total-length criterion cannot identify small juveniles by itself.

## Age-related colour and a model with limited anatomical scope {#s-5330a92c96}
The account describes brown juveniles with variable blue dorsal speckles that disappear with age, and dark chocolate adults with pale grey/bluish ventral speckles. Variable vomerine-tooth counts in immature vouchers were not used as a fixed diagnostic character. The downloadable adult model was manually built from photographic references, not a CT-derived internal anatomical specimen or a measured developmental series. Colours and model geometry do not establish all sex-specific variation.

## Egg sacs observed in shallow bamboo-forest pools {#s-da13d31b93}
The source reports egg sacs in shallow water-filled tyre ruts, with puddles 5–12 cm deep, many sacs near 10 cm depth and 21–27 eggs per sac. Adults were found under cover in wet soil and humus, not observed on the surface even at night during these visits. Field methods report eight pairs of sacs, whereas discussion refers to eight sacs and four females. Those denominators conflict and are not silently converted into a counted breeding population or a species-wide clutch distribution.

## Captive larval milestones are temperature-bound observations {#s-68b65aa2ed}
Larvae were kept at 26 degrees Celsius in prepared water. The paper illustrates free-swimming larvae with balancers at day 16 and later hindlimb development at days 69, 74 and 77. Methods list a later observation at day 87, while the account labels gill regression before metamorphosis at day 84. These timings remain source-specific and inconsistent at the last label; they are not a fixed wild metamorphosis duration. One sampled sac is not a temperature-response experiment or complete development across populations.

## Cannibalism and tail posture were observed in reared animals {#s-5586b27fe6}
Reared larvae were fed bloodworms, yet some consumed siblings. Recently metamorphosed individuals raised their tails when startled; the proposed diversion of attacks away from vital organs is an interpretation. Captive food and cannibalism do not establish natural diet, feeding preference, wild cannibalism frequency or an experimentally demonstrated protective effect of the posture.

## One provoked recording is not a complete call repertoire {#s-d0a332a50b}
A January male sometimes vocalized when probed, and only one recording was obtained while it was partly submerged. The reported duration is 153.40 ms, peak frequency 129.20 Hz and four strong harmonics. The authors suggest alarm, submissive or agonistic functions. This is one provoked sound, not an advertisement-call survey or a controlled functional experiment; the reported amplitude units are not calibrated sound-pressure decibels.

## Mitochondrial trees disagree about the closest branching order {#s-89db002152}
Three new genetic samples came from two adult swabs and a dead embryo. Single 16S, Cytb and COI trees and a concatenated analysis place the lineage within southern Chinese Hynobius but differ in branching order and support. COI reports sister relationship to amjiensis with posterior probability 97%, while the concatenated placement has weaker 65% support. These linked mitochondrial markers are not independent nuclear evidence or a direct estimate of reproductive isolation, introduction history or gene flow.

## A small-population scenario is not a census {#s-b2dc58348d}
The paper considers the known Fujian population potentially Critically Endangered and constructs a breeding-female scenario from few waterbodies and assumed egg-sac numbers. Fewer than 200 breeding animals is an author inference, not a measured census; eight-pair versus eight-sac wording limits the denominator. Historical site drying, bamboo harvest and climate concerns are discussed as threats, without a quantified population trend in this sample. The 2023 interpretation is not a newly verified official IUCN listing. Exact breeding-site details are not reproduced here.
