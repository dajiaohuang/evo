## Leaves and growth {#s-c221fdc1ca}
Scott's 1985 regional revision describes a stemless plant with a basal rosette up to 12 mm across. New growth from the base forms clusters. There may be up to 20 leaves: young ones stand upright and are more or less recurved, while older leaves spread outward. They are 3–10 cm long, 2–4 cm wide, and up to 6 mm thick; their shape ranges from ovate or lance-shaped to triangular, tapering sharply to a pungent tip. The upper surface is flat to concave, with transverse callosities and raised, shiny tubercles that merge with the leaf colour in wavy crosswise rows; raised lengthwise lines are often present. The convex lower surface is similar. One keel, tubercled or marked by transverse callosities, lies centrally or obliquely in the upper third of the leaf. The margins curve upward and are sharp and rough.

## Flowering structure {#s-7586d46fe4}
The inflorescence reaches 35 cm tall. Its simple stalk is about 2 mm in diameter and up to 20 cm long. The erect, keeled sterile bracts are membranous, ovate, pointed, and about 7 mm long. The loose raceme reaches 15 cm and bears about 12–25 flowers and buds, arranged spirally or along one side; usually 1, occasionally up to 4, are open at once. The membranous floral bracts are about 3 mm long, triangular and pointed, with a keel and pinkish to reddish veins; they clasp the pedicels but are shorter than them. The erect, green pedicels are up to 6 mm long and up to 1 mm in diameter.

The straight perianth is about 14 mm long, white, and funnel-shaped, compressed at the base, with grey-brown or green keels on its segments. Its tube is about 3.5 mm across and narrows to 2.5 mm above. The segments closely cohere and form a two-lipped limb. The posterior segments are erect or recurved, white with grey-green or brown-green veins; the outer portion is recurved and spreading, with brown veins. There are 6 stamens in two lengths, 5 and 6 mm, inserted inside the perianth tube. The green ovary is 2.5 mm long and 1.5 mm in diameter; the curved, yellow-green, head-shaped style is 3 mm long.

These measurements come from Scott's 1985 regional species account. The excerpt gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency.

## Habitat and climate described in the account {#s-6746f312c1}
The regional account places *H. limifolia* mainly on north-west-facing slopes or atop the Ubombo Mountains, at 300–800 m elevation. Annual rainfall in its described range is 800–1,400 mm, falling in summer mainly as thunderstorms; summers are hot and humid. Plants grow mostly on rocky shale outcrops, often in full sun for most of the day, with some shade from surrounding grass. Higher on the mountains, loose stones and rocks shelter plants in pockets and crevices, sometimes beneath an overhanging rock. At higher elevations, plants occur at the edge of the mist belt. The account says this extra moisture is associated with plants that differ somewhat from those in the lower, drier region. It reports a preference for soil with pH 6.2.
