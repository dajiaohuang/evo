## A seven-species sample pairs different kinds of evidence {#s-6550747e25}
The paired-stage shape study includes 7 *Eupsophus* species. Tadpole data for 3 rows come from study photographs; the other rows use literature images. Adult entries combine images of listed material, measurements from published figures and literature values. The table records 1 female and leaves 3 entries unspecified for sex; these are source-row counts, not a population sex ratio. Researchers marked 32 larval landmarks and used 10 linear measurements for adults. The stages are paired by species, not by following the same frog through metamorphosis. Tail corrections address fixation artifacts, and principal component analysis (PCA) estimates some missing adult values; not every cell is a direct observation.

## The sample uses different habitat codes for each life stage {#s-ff007b1153}
Among the 7 retained species rows, larval codes are LE 0, LO 0, END 7 and ST 0; adult codes are SAX 0, SEMAQ 0, TER 7 and TOR 0. These stand for lentic, lotic, endotrophic and semiterrestrial larvae, and saxicolous, semi-aquatic, terrestrial and torrential adults. The counts describe this subset, not the whole genus. END is the study’s endotrophic category and is distinct from direct development. These broad water- and land-associated labels do not measure flow tolerance, diet, breeding success or habitat at every stage.

## What the cross-stage shape comparison found {#s-5901dece32}
Across the study sample, larval–adult shape differences have a weak but significant association (Mantel r = 0.16, p = 0.003), while disparity patterns vary among families. The authors interpret the result as support for adaptive decoupling; it does not demonstrate complete genetic independence or a cause of development. The phylogenetic analysis retains 74 of 83 sampled species. PC1 ancestral reconstructions and geological dates are model estimates, not new fossil observations. Adult K is lower but has a reported p = 0.001. The source also records family-level sample-size and statistic inconsistencies; size or convergence effects cannot be assigned as diagnostic features of every linked species.

## How the selected tadpoles compare with other frogs {#s-cddb81fbb5}
In the shape-space analysis, the selected *Eupsophus* tadpoles fall among mostly lotic hylodid larvae. The study codes these *Eupsophus* larvae as endotrophic, but similarity in body and tail proportions does not establish the same feeding strategy or habitat. The authors compare their relatively long tails with those of *Alsodes* and call the overlap intriguing; they do not test its cause experimentally. Endotrophic tadpoles remain a larval stage, so the code does not mean direct development.
