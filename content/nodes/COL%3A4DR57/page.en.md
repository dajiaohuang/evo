## Name and this data account {#s-392dd2975f}
Paroedura tanjaka is accepted in pinned COL26.8 within Gekkonidae. This account uses its ReptTraits v1-2 record, retaining the Reptile Database 2023 name framework and trait-specific sources. The dataset consolidates information into one row per species; this describes the cited record rather than a uniform state of all populations.

## Distinguish the measured length {#s-d7b634e11d}
Recorded dimensions are reported maximum snout–vent length (SVL) 102 mm; female maximum snout–vent length (SVL) 84.2 mm. SVL runs from snout to vent and differs from total length including the tail. Compiled maxima are reported material, not theoretical growth limits or typical individual size. Sex-specific values may come from different localities or studies and alone cannot quantify population sexual dimorphism.

## A scale contact shared with P. neglecta {#tanjaka-diagnostic-character}
The 2021 revision reports the nostril contacting the rostral scale in both Paroedura tanjaka and P. neglecta. This character separates the pair from other members of the species group considered, but does not distinguish the two species. The paper does not establish the full range of morphological variation in P. tanjaka.

## Where and how it is active {#s-5e0735f890}
In the entry, microhabitat is Saxicolous; diet is Carnivorous; active timing is Cathemeral. These categories summarize activity and resource use rather than an exhaustive habitat list or fixed schedule. Positions such as arboreal and terrestrial can be combined. A carnivorous category does not specify prey, and populations, seasons and life stages may differ.

## How to read reproductive records {#s-857d1b8e60}
the reproductive-mode field uses the original label oviparous; consolidated mean offspring or eggs per litter/clutch 2; lowest reported clutch/litter mean 2; highest reported clutch/litter mean 2. Clutch/litter summaries can use midpoints of reported means or, when means are unavailable, extrema; they are not necessarily arithmetic averages of observed clutches. Fractions do not describe non-integer young in a litter. The lowest/highest fields are defined as endpoints of reported means rather than guaranteed smallest/largest individual clutches. Reproductive and sex-determination labels do not establish fetal nutrition or uniformity across all populations.

## Sympatry recorded at Bemaraha {#bemaraha-cooccurrence}
The revision reports P. tanjaka and P. neglecta together at Bemaraha. In the sampled material, the two formed distinct mitochondrial lineages and shared no nuclear haplotypes in the markers used. This is a record from the studied locality, not a range-wide pattern or a complete ecological account.

## The locality reported in the 2021 revision {#recorded-locality}
The authors describe P. tanjaka as known only from the karstic Tsingy de Bemaraha in central-west Madagascar. This is the study-era distribution statement, not a current exhaustive occurrence inventory.

## A sister-species hypothesis from sampled loci {#sampled-sister-hypothesis}
The paper's mitochondrial and nuclear analyses place P. tanjaka and sympatric P. neglecta as distinct lineages and sister species. The authors call for more comprehensive phylogenomic study to confirm relationships in this group; the placement remains that study's sampled hypothesis.
