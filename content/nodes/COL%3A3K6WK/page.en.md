## Helichrysum lingulatum {#s-b33478dcde}
## Woolly rosettes and a pink-to-white bract crown

Hilliard's *Flora of Southern Africa* account describes a mat-forming perennial with crowded leaf rosettes on woody, branched underground stems. Solitary flowering stems rise from the rosettes, reaching 180 mm and covered in gray wool. Basal, tongue-shaped leaves reach 35 by 8 mm, have rounded tips and woolly surfaces; the narrower stem leaves are tipped with a white, papery bract.

Bell-shaped heads are solitary, about 20 mm long, and have radiating bracts. Roughly seven loose rows of glossy bracts shade from pink to white, with a crimson mark inside near the base. Heads contain 152–180 florets. Barrel-shaped achenes are about 1.5 mm and bear paired mucilage-producing hairs; many pappus bristles have barbed tips and bases that cohere in bundles. The source places its mats on hard bare ground or stony turf at about 1,500–1,950 m and distinguishes it from *H. argentissimum* by leaf shape and hair texture.
