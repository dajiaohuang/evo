## Horses, tapirs and rhinos occupy separate branches {#s-74d938cdca}
Perissodactyla includes living horses, tapirs and rhinoceroses alongside many extinct branches. Fossils place an early radiation during the Paleocene–Eocene Thermal Maximum (PETM), but the sampled record and analyses do not resolve the order's exact origin.

## A branching history without a settled origin date {#s-930fc79579}
The PETM occurrence study and broader fossil synthesis trace branching histories across northern continents. They do not date the order's exact origin or establish one way of life for every branch.

## Living forms differ across a branching order {#s-3f75ff2541}
Horses, tapirs and rhinoceroses look very different today. Early fossils are often separated by finer tooth and skull features, and extinct branches add further variation. Together they form a branching record, not a sequence leading only to the living forms.

## 71 terminals and 102 characters leave basal positions open {#s-3d7cb3ed91}
The earliest-occurrence study analyzes 71 terminals and 102 morphological characters. Alternative placements near the base show why the first sampled fossils do not settle the order's origin.

## Separate branches changed teeth, skulls and limbs {#s-b918ae4686}
Many extinct groups sit within the order's branching history. Teeth, skulls and limbs changed along different lineages; arranging fossils as successive ancestors would hide that branching pattern.

## PETM dispersal does not identify the order's origin {#s-63d78c4df5}
The earliest sampled perissodactyl records fall around 56–54 Ma, during the PETM. Their distribution supports rapid dispersal among North America, Europe and Asia, but does not identify the order's exact place of origin.

## Limits of the sampled order history {#s-27a0e8bb60}
This early radiation is reconstructed from sampled fossils and comparative anatomy. Basal placements depend on analysis, and the selected records do not identify direct ancestors, a precise origin date or a complete distribution.
