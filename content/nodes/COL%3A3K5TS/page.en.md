## Helianthus annuus {#s-32f91491d4}
## Yellow rays frame dark flower discs

Hilliard's 1977 account describes an annual herb up to 4 m tall, usually branching from the base. Leaves are alternate, though lower leaves may be opposite. Upper blades reach about 10 by 6 cm; lower ones are often much larger. They range from ovate to triangular, with long-pointed tips and heart-shaped to truncate bases that descend abruptly onto the petiole. Both surfaces are rough and hairy; petioles reach about 10 cm.

Flower heads usually measure 8–10 cm across the expanded yellow rays, but can be much larger. They grow singly on long stalks at upper branch tips. The bracts around each head form three rows and are rough, ovate-lance-shaped and mostly drawn abruptly into long points. The central disc is brownish or purplish black. Dry fruits are about 5 mm long, narrow-obovate and somewhat compressed, with sparse bristles; two small, narrow pappus scales soon fall away.

In Hilliard's regional account, the plant sometimes occurs on disturbed ground. The small-headed form was well established in sandy waste ground at the head of Durban Bay; selected giant-headed garden and agricultural forms were occasionally recorded on waste ground near habitation or roadsides.
