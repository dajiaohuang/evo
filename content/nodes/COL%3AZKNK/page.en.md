## Separate juvenile samples in two studies {#s-70601e2790}
Integumentary sensory organs (ISOs) are small skin structures associated with sensory nerves. The four Nile crocodiles in the entire 2012 study came from a commercial breeder in Florida, not a wild Nile transect; trigeminal recordings used two of them. The 2013 electrophysiological experiment separately used five Nile crocodiles and five spectacled caimans, all juveniles weighing 700–1200 g. Their samples are not pooled into one population survey, and 2013 embryonic and neonatal tissues are not automatically additional electrophysiological individuals. Nile crocodile sensory organs were visible on jaws and postcranial scales; counts, density and anatomical sampling sites require separate interpretation.

## Separate brushing, temperature and pH stimuli {#s-598437e86f}
In 2013, electrodes were placed near individual skin sensory organs in anesthetized juveniles: jaw and dorsal regions in Nile crocodiles, and jaws in spectacled caimans. Brushing, local warming or cooling, and acidic and alkaline droplets elicited electrical activity. Visible thermal responses summarized as above about 43°C or below about 15°C depend on the stimulation and recording setup; they are not preferred habitat temperatures, lethal limits or freely chosen temperatures. RT-PCR from neonatal tissues and embryonic in situ hybridization showed relevant channel expression. Expression, local electrical activity and whole-animal perception or choice remain distinct evidence levels.

## The scope of an undetected salt response {#s-3933767cb2}
The 2013 study applied 0.5 M NaCl or KCl to recorded skin sensory organs without detecting corresponding electrical responses. Selected neural units in the 2012 study likewise showed no responses to its salt solutions or battery configurations in water. Negative results are bounded by sites, individuals, stimuli and recording methods; they do not show that the whole animal cannot sense salinity, nor does a pH response mean detection of every chemical. The 2013 authors proposed lingual salt glands or other pathways as possible salinity sensors but explicitly called for further experiments. Salt secretion and direct salinity detection are different conclusions.

## Head sensory organs precede the head-scale pattern {#s-202d5b1900}
In Nile crocodile embryos incubated at 29.5°C in the 2013 study, facial and jaw ISOs began forming around day 38 after oviposition, whereas the head-scale groove pattern began around day 55. Postcranial sites differed in the timing of organs and scales, so ISOs cannot uniformly be described as additions after scales form. These are observations under one incubation condition linked to morphological stages, not a fixed developmental calendar for every nest temperature. The caiman’s relative timing was reported as data not shown, not an equally illustrated series. Molecular morphogenetic mechanisms remained unresolved.

## Wave localization and capture after contact {#s-d9b3fa0f85}
The authors observed juveniles with food pellets and fish in aquaria, using 940 nm infrared illumination and white noise in some trials to limit visual and auditory cues. Both American alligators and Nile crocodiles oriented toward surface disturbances and then swept their heads; capture followed rapidly when an object contacted skin or the open mouth. This links disturbance localization with actions after contact. The passage supplies no complete species-specific behavioral trial total or success rate, and does not establish wild predation rates, exclusive reliance on touch or a fixed response time for every individual. Anatomical and neural recordings supply another evidence layer; behavioral sequences do not test every sensory pathway.

## Monthly encounters in the Victoria Nile delta {#s-71b96afbce}
In the Victoria Nile delta of Lake Albert, western Uganda, researchers made monthly surveys along five transects from October 2017 to September 2018, recording 186 crocodile observations. Records were most frequent in January–February and April–May and least frequent in July–August and October–November. Crocodiles were encountered most often along woody north-shore riverbanks, followed by papyrus–Vossia habitat. Hatchlings had the narrowest habitat niche breadth and subadults the widest.

## The smallest probe sets a measurement limit {#s-5ec40d06d8}
In 2012, calibrated von Frey filaments indented moist skin while trigeminal afferent units were recorded. The paper reports 174 receptive fields measured for indentation force across the two study species, spanning applied forces of 0.078–13.725 mN, not 174 animals of this species. Twenty-eight fields responded to the smallest 0.078 mN filament; their actual minimum thresholds were unresolved because a smaller calibrated force was unavailable. A receptive field is the skin area activating an afferent unit, distinct from one sensory organ, an animal count or a water-wave detection distance. Moisture affected thresholds, so these are not fixed sensitivities for every field condition.
