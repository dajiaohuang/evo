## Postglacial expansion in one tentative population {#s-a73133dc66}
Chakraborty et al. (2014) used a mitochondrial-DNA Bayesian skyline plot for the tentative Upper Subansiri population and inferred expansion after the Last Glacial Maximum, around 15,000 years before present. Ten of 12 dried skin samples from old hunting trophies were successfully sequenced. The study could not rule out effects of local migrants. A small apparent decline 5,000–7,000 years ago may fall within the credibility interval and was not treated as conclusive. This model describes one geographic population, not a census or species-wide history.

## A modeled effective-population decline around 3,500 years ago {#s-c0c6ae1adc}
Approximate Bayesian computation favored decline Scenario 2 for the tentative Upper Subansiri population. It estimated effective population size at about 50,600 (95% HPD 19,600–68,300) before a roughly 30-fold reduction to 1,700 (95% HPD 476–4,090), centered around 3,500 years before present (95% HPD 520–13,800). This is a model estimate, not a head count. The analysis could not rule out unsampled migrants and did not establish climate or human activity as the cause.

## Evidence limits {#s-a33b8f0402}
Both demographic accounts describe modeled history for the tentative Upper Subansiri population; neither is a census or a reconstruction of every M. munzala population. The focal analysis used old trophy-skin samples, with 10 of 12 successfully sequenced. A captive Tawang blood sample occurred in the wider study but is outside these Upper Subansiri claims. Local migration could not be ruled out, and the study did not establish a climatic or human cause. Other ecology, current range-wide distribution and conservation were not assessed in this study.
