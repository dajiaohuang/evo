## What does the historical account describe about this herb and its flowers? {#s-a79a0e5556}
The Flora of Panama account describes a herb or undershrub 0.6–1.5 m tall. Stems are nearly hairless or sparsely covered in appressed, star-shaped bristles; sometimes a woolly line runs lengthwise and changes direction at each node. Leaves have slender petioles 0.5–2 cm long and filiform stipules. The blades may be triangular-ovate or narrowly so, up to 9 cm long, with several possible base shapes, pointed to tapering tips and scalloped-toothed edges. They are softly hairy above and more bristly beneath, with five main veins. Solitary flowers grow in leaf axils on slender, jointed stalks 1–5 cm long. The epicalyx has about ten linear bracts, as long as the calyx or sometimes longer, up to 25 mm. The calyx is 7–12 mm long and deeply divided. Petals are 1.4–2 cm long, usually crimson and rarely white, with appressed star-shaped hairs outside. The staminal tube is shorter than or about as long as the corolla; the style branches project beyond it, and the stigmas have dense fringes. The nearly spherical capsule has a small tip, is about as long as the persistent calyx and bears appressed hairs. The angled, nearly kidney-shaped seeds are 2–3 mm long and covered in long white hairs. This historical regional account gives no population sample or observation date.

## What range and habitats does the historical flora report? {#s-7f7fd9c67e}
The historical account reports the species from the West Indies and from Mexico south to Colombia and Venezuela. It also records open forest and shrubland. These are the flora’s reported range and settings, not a current survey.
