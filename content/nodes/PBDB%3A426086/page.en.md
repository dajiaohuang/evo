## Two jaw fragments support a species referral, not an associated skull {#s-d511938d94}
Holotype FSAC-KK 5075 is a partial anterior rostrum missing its tip; paratype FSAC-KK 5076 is part of the anterior mandibular symphysis, also missing its tip. Both are held at the Faculté des Sciences Aïn Chock, Université Hassan II, Casablanca. The original description supports referral to one species from similar size, long low outlines, thick-walled semicircular sections, foramina and a corresponding ridge-and-groove complex. The 2023 account explicitly gives no reason to regard them as one individual. Neither a complete skull nor an associated body skeleton is available.

## 48 and 67 mm are preserved lengths {#s-9a4023b5c6}
The manuscript and table 1 record 48 mm of preserved rostrum and 67 mm of preserved mandible. Assuming an evenly tapering tip, the authors estimate about 70 mm and 35 mm missing anteriorly, respectively. Those are missing-length estimates, not preserved lengths or measurements of a complete beak. The rostrum breaks anterior to the nasoantorbital fenestra; the mandible stops before the rami diverge. Repair also accentuates curvature near the mandibular tip, so the entire observed curve cannot be treated as original anatomy.

## Low angles, a median ridge and a corresponding groove {#s-aabba4f11d}
A median ridge extends along the preserved upper-jaw occlusal surface. The lower jaw changes from a flatter anterior surface to a shallow posterior median groove. The authors distinguish upper and lower jaws partly by lateral-angle differences, given as about 2.5° and 2.0° in the 2023 comparison. Both are dorsoventrally flattened, approximately semicircular in section, and have thick cortices around reduced cavities with elongate slit-like foramina. This character combination informs comparison; a corresponding ridge and groove do not prove that the fragments were joined or directly preserve feeding behavior.

## XCT describes internal cavities and canals {#s-0547adcf2b}
The authors used X-ray computed tomography on holotype FSAC-KK 5075, digitally removing matrix and constructing a three-dimensional model. The manuscript describes a flattened central void extending along the rostrum, thin trabeculae crossing it locally, and canals opening into elongate external foramina. Imaging and models reveal internal architecture rather than preserved nerves or sensory receptors; the canals do not measure sensory performance.

## Small size does not establish a juvenile {#s-a131321003}
The original description interprets the relatively smooth surfaces of both specimens as suggesting osteological maturity. This comes from local surface comparison, not exact age determination. The material remains two jaw fragments, without a growth series, complete wings or sex evidence. Preserved lengths of only a few centimeters do not by themselves establish juveniles, and the maturity inference supplies neither complete adult proportions nor a fixed wingspan.

## Relationships are limited by few codable characters {#s-86da2126fd}
The original manuscript favors Azhdarchoidea from the combination of toothlessness, elongate foramina and thick cortices, while leaving family attribution unknown. Its phylogenetic analysis adds five characters to an existing dataset. The authors report few codable characters and homoplasy, yielding unresolved or conflicting results under unordered and differently ordered treatments, including alternatives near ctenochasmatids and Lonchodectes. The 2023 account also leaves the family position indeterminate. The published analyses therefore do not support treating the directory parent chain as a settled phylogenetic result.

## Sediment probing is a morphological hypothesis {#s-11a4c7ec3c}
The original description compares the long, narrow, dorsoventrally flattened jaws and strengthened cortices with probing birds, proposing that Leptostomia inserted its beak into soil or sediment to obtain small invertebrates. The authors consider the freshwater-to-brackish Kem Kem setting compatible with this hypothesis. Bird analogies, bone form and environmental consistency are not stomach contents, direct feeding observations or tracks assigned to this species. Worms, crustaceans and molluscs discussed as potential prey cannot become a confirmed menu.

## Missing tips leave remote touch as a testable prediction {#s-5b71eae597}
Some probing birds detect vibrations or pressure changes from hidden prey through sensory structures near the bill tip. The authors discuss this remote-touch ability as a possible adaptation in Leptostomia but explicitly state that both type specimens lack their anterior tips, preventing assessment of concentrated tip foramina that might support it. Additional specimens could test the prediction. The preserved foramina and canals are not themselves sensory receptors, so the proposed touch ability remains unconfirmed.

## Evidence limits {#s-5f9caeccee}
The account rests on two incomplete jaw fragments and published comparisons of their form, age, maturity and possible function. Their proposed referral to one species does not establish that they came from one individual; family placement, probing behavior and remote touch remain uncertain. Estimated missing lengths are not measurements of complete jaws, and the database age range is not a direct date for the species' origin or extinction.
