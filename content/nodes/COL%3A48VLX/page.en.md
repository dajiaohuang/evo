## Oedera squarrosa {#s-7b8084d918}
## Leaf shape and fruit help distinguish close relatives

Manning and Goldblatt's 2012 account compares *Oedera squarrosa* with *O. genistifolia*. In *O. squarrosa*, the leaves are obovate (broader toward the tip), curve backward and end in a small point; young leaves are arranged in four ranks.

Bremer's 1976 treatment of *Relhania* records considerable variation in *O. squarrosa* foliage. It describes most specimens as distinguishable by larger, elliptic leaves that curve backward and wider, bell-shaped involucres (the bract cups around each head), often with visible spots. Young shoots often have opposite leaf pairs arranged at alternating right angles. When these features are absent, the two species can look similar; the account points to their more slender dry fruits and a pappus (bristles attached to the fruit) shorter than one-fifth of the fruit length as distinctions. Fruits from ray florets are almost always hairless and only occasionally sparsely hairy. Bremer notes one geographic association: specimens from Piketberg and just north have wider, nearly round leaves. The 2012 account records stony sand or clay slopes and flats as habitat.
