## One record, several kinds of evidence {#s-2b9e603acb}
COL26.8 places Monilesaurus rouxii among the agamid lizards. The ReptTraits v1-2 entry follows the workbook’s 2023 Reptile Database name framework and cites sources for individual traits. Because the workbook brings reports together in one row per species, the values below describe that compiled record; they do not establish that every population shares the same measurements or habits.

## Maximum lengths leave out the tail {#s-710643b8e5}
The compiled record gives a reported maximum snout–vent length of 82.4 mm and a female maximum of 65 mm. Snout–vent length measures from the tip of the snout to the vent; it leaves out the tail. These are reported maxima, not a typical adult size or a biological growth limit. The two measurements may come from different places or studies, so their difference alone cannot show how body size varies between males and females in a population.

## Trees, rocks, and the ground {#s-faac27d093}
The entry records activity in trees, on rocks, and on the ground; “saxicolous” means associated with rocky surfaces. It classifies the diet as carnivorous and activity as diurnal. These labels give a broad picture of where the lizard is active and when it feeds, but they are not a complete habitat map or a fixed daily schedule. The diet category does not identify prey, and habits can differ among populations, seasons, and life stages.

## What the clutch figures summarize {#s-cce6b2a3dd}
The workbook labels this species oviparous, meaning it lays eggs. Its consolidated clutch or litter figure is 4.15; the lowest and highest reported means are 1 and 9. These summaries can be midpoints between published means—or, when means are missing, between reported extremes—rather than averages of individual clutches. A fractional value is therefore a summary, not a count of part of an egg or offspring. The listed low and high values are endpoints of reported means, not guaranteed smallest and largest clutches. These fields also do not establish how embryos are nourished or show that reproduction is identical across populations.

## What these records cannot establish {#s-adea1e806c}
The one-row summary compresses differences in sex, age, locality, and population, and the workbook’s 2023 name framework may not match every later taxonomic concept. A reported maximum is not the species’ absolute size limit, while a reproductive mean does not predict every clutch. Length-based mass estimates are not measurements of mass, and the workbook’s juvenile-length headings and definitions differ, so neither supports a conclusion here. The listed traits also provide no basis for conclusions about venom, conservation, current distribution, or population trends. References identify the workbook’s source chain; they do not mean every underlying study has been independently reassessed here.
