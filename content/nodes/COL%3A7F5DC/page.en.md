## What range of flower and trap forms is described? {#s-2e767d7c8d}
Utricularia amethystina is described as a perennial, or probably sometimes annual, terrestrial herb. Underground stolons—creeping stems—bear traps; leaf-like photosynthetic organs usually form a small rosette at the base of the flowering stalk. The stalk bears 1–6 flowers, whose corolla is usually violet or mauve, sometimes white with a yellow palate. The spur is typically two to three times the lower lip’s length. The account gives typical dimensions but explicitly allows smaller or larger flowers, so these are not fixed limits for every plant.

## What range did the historical flora give? {#s-10d8e41e89}
The 2013 Flora of Panama distribution field says Utricularia amethystina ranges from tropical Florida to Brazil. This is the historical regional account in the source version retrieved on 8 September 2026; it is not a current range survey or global inventory.
