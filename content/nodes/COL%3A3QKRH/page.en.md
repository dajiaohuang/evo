## What does the description record about Jamesbrittenia bergae? {#s-8493556cb4}
This shrublet reaches about 20 cm and grows from a perennial rootstock. Its leaves are grey-green, alternate or occasionally opposite near the stem base, and edged with coarse teeth or irregular lobes. The leaves and calyx carry glandular hairs. The scarlet flower limb spans about 16.5–25 mm, with a bright-yellow throat; the capsule is hairless and 4–7 mm long. The description reports no nectar secretion observed.

## Which traits distinguish it from Jamesbrittenia accrescens? {#s-cc37e5d142}
The source says an earlier key leads to J. accrescens, but distinguishes J. bergae by its scarlet, medium-textured corolla limb, shorter and narrower tube, usually notched petal tips, and hairless ovary and capsule. Its capsules are also smaller: 4–7 × 2–3 mm versus 7–10 × 3.5–4.5 mm in J. accrescens. The source separately notes that J. accrescens occupies rocky scrub and forest margins farther north and east.

## What growing site is described? {#s-d0442aceb4}
The account places J. bergae in full sun, in crevices on south-facing ferricrete outcrops in the southern foothills. This is the source’s site description.
