## What does the record describe for Croton cuneatus? {#s-5855eb78d3}
Young branches have scale-like hairs. Long-stalked oval-lanceolate, elliptic, or oblong leaves also have scale-like hairs on both surfaces and looping side veins. Their margins are entire, and a sessile nectar gland occurs at the base. Young branch tips lack leaf-like stipules. Terminal thyrses have bisexual flower clusters near the base. Male flowers are nearly sessile; the record does not specify their shape. Female flowers are nearly sessile or shortly stalked, with fused sepals, a four-part style, and equal calyx lobes. Their petals are threadlike, and the bud arrangement is valvate. The fruit columella has weakly distinct apical lobes.
