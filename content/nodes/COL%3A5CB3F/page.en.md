## Reassessed elevations without converting predictions to records {#s-7954c162ef}
The monograph flagged the original Zhangmu type elevations of 700–1000 m as erroneous and proposed altimeter error as a possible explanation. It examined Lixin, Zhangmu, specimens at about 2180 m. Its distribution paragraph stated 1800–2100 m, while the remarks explicitly gave 1800–2180 m based on earlier and current information. These differing endpoints and the attributed reassessment remain visible rather than silently harmonized. Neighbouring Nepal and India were expected areas in this account, not confirmed localities supplied by this treatment.

## Separate historical male and newly examined adult samples {#s-9e38c5a462}
The diagnosis cited seven historical males at 32.5–40.0 mm snout–vent length and separately reported its own single male at 39.5 mm and two females at 44.5–50.2 mm. It described vomerine ridges and teeth, toes without webbing but with narrow lateral fringes, and dense grey nuptial spines on the dorsal bases of fingers I and II in breeding males, with a subgular vocal sac. The historical and current measurements are not pooled into one uniformly measured series, and the single new male is not a range.

## Mitochondrial and nuclear relationships require broader sampling {#s-9a4113b039}
The remarks placed X. zhangi close to the mid-elevation X. monticola population in the cited analyses, with minor mitochondrial divergence but distinct nuclear divergence. That grouping differed from the high-elevation X. monticola population. Available molecular data came only from near the two type localities, more than 230 km apart; the authors called for population genetics across all relevant populations in three countries and retained the two species. These are attributed phylogenetic results and a provisional taxonomic decision, not a resolved assessment of every population or evidence of reproductive compatibility.
