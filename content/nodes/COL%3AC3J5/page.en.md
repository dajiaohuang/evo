## Aloe condyae {#s-b80cdb66a9}
Aloe condyae is described from quartzitic sandstone cliffs, where its small groups grow in rock crevices among lichens and moss. The rosettes have densely white-spotted leaves and short, crowded racemes of orange-red flowers. The regional account places the sites in a summer-rainfall area receiving about 1,500–1,750 mm annually; it suggests sunbirds as possible pollinators from the tubular flowers, rather than reporting direct pollination observations.
