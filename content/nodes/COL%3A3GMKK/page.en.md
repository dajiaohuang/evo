## What leaves and flowers are described in the historical account? {#s-665169c322}
A 1925 regional account describes nearly hairless, threadlike branchlets about 1 foot long, with 1–2 line internodes. The alternate leaves are erect or slightly spreading, linear-lanceolate, pointed, ash-green and hairless, with one vein; they measure 4–6 lines by 0.5–1 line, and may be flat or have recurved margins. Upper leaves are nearly whorled. Flowers are surrounded by white hairs at the base. The hairless calyx is 6–7 lines long, with a slender tube that narrows at the middle and widens narrowly like a funnel above. Its ovate, tapering lobes are about 1.5 lines long and sparsely hairy outside. The account describes eight fleshy, oblong, blunt petals, but the petal-length number is missing from the excerpt. Anthers are about half as long as the petals, with the upper one protruding. The historical source uses “line” as a length unit (about 2.1 mm).

## Where does the historical account place it? {#s-2b72a7fd13}
The 1925 account records it in grassy places near the mouth of the Umtentu River. This is a specific historical locality note, not a complete range statement.
