## What does the historical account describe about this flexible herb or shrub? {#s-f885f4bc9d}
The Flora of Panama account describes a herb or small shrub 0.6–2 m tall. Young stems are grey and softly woolly. Leaves have 0.5–3.3 cm petioles and narrow threadlike stipules; the papery, narrowly ovate to ovate blades are 3.5–12 by 1.5–7 cm, with rounded, truncate or slightly heart-shaped bases and blunt or slightly pointed tips. Their toothed or scalloped edges and both surfaces are grey and softly woolly, especially below; the veins are impressed above and prominent beneath. Tight, nearly spherical flower clusters occur in leaf axils or at shoot tips, on stalks ranging from very short to long. Flowers are sessile, with linear-lanceolate bracts about as long as the flower. The 4–4.5 mm tubular-bell-shaped calyx has five angles and five long-silky outer lobes; the inner surface is hairless except for the lobes. Yellow or orange petals are 4–4.5 mm long and clawed; their outside is hairless, with sparse cobweb-like hairs inside near the claw. The staminal tube is about 2 mm; the nearly stalkless anthers are about 0.8 mm. The ovary is silky on its upper half; the style bends at the middle, and the stigma is feather-like with papillae. The capsule is enclosed by the calyx and persistent corolla, about 2.5–3 by 1.5–2 mm, silky above and one-seeded. The obovoid seed is about 1.8–2.1 by 1–1.3 mm, with a smooth coat. This historical regional description gives no population sampling frame or observation date.

## What range and settings does the historical flora report? {#s-9172607393}
The historical account calls Waltheria indica a polymorphic tropical weed, also reaching some subtropical regions. It reports the species as common in secondary growth, along roads, in dry thickets and open forests. This is the flora’s historical summary, not a current global survey.
