## Sleeping sites {#s-20e1effbf7}
A 2024 field note describes 24 sleeping sites in fragmented and relict landscapes of Colombia’s eastern Llanos. In Villavicencio, Acacías and Cumaral, nests were in forest fragments, along their edges or in living fences, usually 2–8 m above ground; in San Martín, sites were in or beside forest fragments and morichales at 8–18 m. The records show several locally used settings, not a species-wide rule.

## Scope of the evidence {#s-20fce9f01f}
The study combined landscape censuses at San Martín with interview-assisted searches for sleeping sites in three other landscapes. Its 24 characterized sites inform nest-site use; they do not estimate population size or establish current distribution.

## Evidence limits {#s-94810846d3}
The 24 sleeping sites come from landscape censuses and interview-assisted searches; they show local nest-site use, not abundance or a full habitat survey. The 2007 Quindío type has unknown wild provenance. The 2010 study’s karyotype, specimen table and external GenBank sequences are distinct evidence sets, and its eight historical taxon names do not map one-to-one to all COL26.8 accepted species. Wild ecology for A. jorgehernandezi remains undocumented.
