## Plant and flower description {#s-ce74f4e44d}
The SANBI account gives plant height as 0.50–1.05 m; plants grow singly or a few together in small tufts. The corm is about 20 mm across. The lowest peduncle is reported as (70–)100–230 mm long. There are a few foliage leaves, 350–650 × 3.0–4.5 mm, and about four reduced sheathing leaves. Spikes are pendulous: the terminal spike bears 7–12 flowers, with 2–5 lateral spikes, each bearing 4–9 flowers.

Bracts are elliptic-lanceolate, tapering to a point, 15–25 × 6–9 mm, and about two internodes long. They are membranous and pure white, or have a few flecks along the main vein; about five lateral veins lie on each side of the main vein. Flowers are bell-shaped, with tepals upright or scarcely flaring at the tip. They are creamy white to pale yellow, rarely tinged pink or lilac. The perianth tube is reported as (4.5–)5.0–6.0(–)7.0 mm long, tepals 9–12 × 4–5 mm, anthers 5–6 mm, and stigmas 3–6 mm below the top of the perianth.

## Diagnostic comparisons {#s-a2b38e0ff9}
A separate SANBI account describes solitary stems or small tufts, pointed pure-white bracts, and small, rather closed flowers that are creamy white to pale yellow, rarely tinged pink or mauve; it gives the perianth as 14–18 mm long. *Dierama sertum*, from northern coastal KwaZulu-Natal, is described as having similar flowers but bracts strongly flecked in the lower part and along the midline. *Dierama argyreum* also has white bracts, but its flowers are larger, bell-shaped, white or ivory, with an 18–35 mm perianth and flaring tepals.
