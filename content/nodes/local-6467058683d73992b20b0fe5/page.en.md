## The deposited caecilian record {#s-6889e8a9de}
The fourth version of CaecilianTraits lists Schistometopum thomense in the genus Schistometopum and family Dermophiidae. It keeps individual, population and species records separate. Whether this source concept matches a current Catalogue of Life (COL26.8) concept has not been established.

## Measurement material {#s-98c9332ff3}
The compilation lists 20 measurement records for this source concept, all at individual level and none at population or species level. These row counts are not estimates of population size or proof of 20 distinct specimens. Each record retains its specimen identifier, sex code and reference. Body-size comparisons also need to distinguish fresh, preserved and summary measurements.

## Compiled habitat categories {#s-41a5d59ffd}
The compilation labels the broad habitat “Terrestrial” and the microhabitat “Fossorial.” These are source categories: they do not show how often each setting is used or describe the species’ complete geographic range.

## Compiled reproduction category {#s-039039ddfa}
The compilation classifies the species as viviparous, meaning that it bears live young. This category does not tell us about parental care, when reproduction occurs or how it varies among populations.

## Compiled anatomical counts {#s-9c03372ca6}
At species level, the compilation gives a primary-fold count of 97.775 and a secondary-fold count of 29. The decimal value may summarize records rather than describe one animal; these entries do not establish that every individual has the same diagnostic counts.

## Water-dependent larval development {#s-63aeb195f4}
The compilation marks a water-dependent larval stage as absent. This is the dataset category described in its accompanying data paper; it does not mean adults never use water. The reproductive metadata and original reference are needed to understand the observation’s scope and qualifications.

## Compiled clutch counts {#s-624606ef65}
The source species record lists a minimum clutch size of 2 and a maximum of 7. These counts come from cited reproductive accounts; they describe the compiled source material, not universal limits or annual reproductive output. The associated reproductive metadata remains part of their interpretation.
