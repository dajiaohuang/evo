## Accepted name and placement {#s-e6e58f016a}
COL26.8 accepts Epipedobates currulao Betancourth-Cundar, Ríos-Orjuela, Cannatella & Tarvin, 2025 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Dendrobatidae (family) → Epipedobates (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## Observed Colombian lowland localities {#s-a0f8101f87}
The description reported Pacific-lowland records in Valle del Cauca, including the Buenaventura area and upper Anchicayá basin, at 0–260 m elevation. A proposed extension to Timbiquí based on photographs depended on consistent colour traits and required further investigation. It is retained as a conditional lead rather than a confirmed extension or a reassignment of every older E. boulengeri record.

## Daytime activity near slow water {#s-bc2f60f5a7}
The researchers observed terrestrial frogs by day in agroforestry, secondary-forest edges and disturbed fragments near marshes or slow streams. Individuals moved through grass and litter and called at water edges, with greater calling activity in morning and late afternoon than during warmer sunny periods. These are local observations, not experimental proof of habitat preference, disturbance tolerance or temperature limits.

## Skin alkaloid measurements retain individuals and assay scope {#s-76ae43d5b0}
In the 2024 study, Epipedobates currulao is represented by 2 frogs analysed with GC-MS. Table1 reports 99–105 detected alkaloid compounds per individual, with median 102.5. Its printed log-total-integrated-area range is 19.49–19.68, with median 19.59; these are chromatographic signal summaries, not micrograms, tissue concentration or a toxic dose. The logarithm base is not inferred from the column label. Compound counts include the source’s isomer distinctions and should not be read as numbers of independently tested toxins. The phenotype column prints defended; defended/undefended are prior broad chemical-content bins, not measured predator-deterrence outcomes, while NA is an unassigned source category.

## Collection and chemical interpretation limits {#s-ebb5b55902}
The GC-MS sample of Epipedobates currulao belongs to the 2014 Ecuador/Colombia collection series. Whole skins were placed in methanol after lidocaine euthanasia; species sample sizes were opportunistic, with random subsets chosen within Epipedobates. Standards were unavailable for most frog alkaloids, so absolute quantities cannot be recovered from these reported signal values. The two instrument/pipeline datasets are not directly comparable. Full-skin GC-MS results were not mass-corrected, and co-eluting compounds with the same base peak could not always be distinguished. The authors did not serially sample each species across seasons and a broad range. Raw chromatograms, vouchers and supplementary individual-level records have not been independently reanalysed here.

## Passive accumulation remains a mechanistic hypothesis {#s-706e98e180}
The paper uses these skin observations in a proposed sequence from dietary toxin exposure and some resistance, through slower elimination and temporary retention, to derived transport or storage associated with sequestration. For Epipedobates currulao, a measured skin profile alone does not establish dietary origin, elimination rate, a binding mechanism or transport specifically to skin. Presence in skin does not by itself distinguish passive retention from specialised sequestration. The proposed ancestral condition and evolutionary sequence require broader sampling and tissue/time-series experiments; they are not direct observations of ancestors or a reconstructed molecular pathway in this sampled species.

## Species-specific detections and their evidence limits {#s-ff566c3bda}
The authors identify this species as one for which they provide alkaloid data for the first time. Its two-individual chemical sample is not an estimate of all-site or all-season variability, and the report is not a new taxonomic diagnosis.
