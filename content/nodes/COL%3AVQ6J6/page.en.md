## Original description and type locality {#s-09b0b17bc0}
Rengifo et al. (2022) described Cheracebus aquinoi using morphological comparisons and compiled known locality records in Loreto, Peru. MDD records the holotype as MUSM 59736, collected about 2.5 km northeast of Villa Belén in San Juan Bautista District, Maynas. The paper supports the taxonomic diagnosis and its listed locality context; it is not a population census or a complete natural-history study.

## Locality records and assessment fields {#s-5e53bd5f6e}
The fixed catalogue and MDD list the species in Peru. Its type locality and the sites compiled in the original description are traceable local evidence, not a continuous or complete range boundary. MDD v2.5 lists no IUCN Red List assessment for this species. This page has no independent population estimate, long-term trend or complete ecology study with species-level confirmation.

## Limits of counts from unidentified regional titis {#s-44267bd17d}
Aquino et al. (2021) ran diurnal line transects at six sites across the Itaya, Nanay and Tigre basins; from May to November 2019 they covered 1,659 km over 1,897 survey hours and recorded 32 groups identified as *Cheracebus sp.*, including 17 in the Nanay basin. The survey preceded the 2022 description and does not reidentify those groups as *C. aquinoi*; it also included Itaya. Its counts and density estimates are therefore regional context, not results assigned to this species.

## Habitats and foods reviewed in the description {#s-57b8a931dd}
The original description also synthesizes earlier observations: the monkeys used forest strata around 15–25 m high and were reported in white-sand scrub forest (varillal), gallery forest, flooded forest and palm-dominated forest. Reported foods included buds, flowers, fruit pulp, insects, leaves and seeds. These habitat and diet details come from studies cited in the taxonomic paper, not a new standardized field survey in 2022; they do not define the full range or establish a current population trend.

## Evidence limits {#s-f682b249ff}
The habitat and diet account summarizes earlier studies cited by the 2022 taxonomic description. A 2019 regional transect survey recorded unidentified *Cheracebus sp.*; there is no basis here for assigning its counts, trends or habitat profile to *C. aquinoi*. The type locality and sites in the original description do not constitute a complete range map. The Chinese name is editorial.
