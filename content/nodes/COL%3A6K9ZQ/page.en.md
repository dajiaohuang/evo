## Gasteria croucheri: spotted rosettes and variable flower clusters {#s-5f6563e6f6}
A South African regional account describes stemless plants, from decumbent to erect, 250–400 mm tall and up to 600 mm across. They may be solitary or divide and proliferate at the base into dense groups. Succulent roots reach 6 mm in diameter.

The rosettes have leaves 200–360 mm long and 30–100 mm wide at the base. Leaves are triangular to linear-lanceolate, rarely strap-shaped, and spread upright; they are rarely horizontal or recurved. The upper surface is channelled and flattens toward the tip; the lower surface is somewhat convex with an off-centre keel. Both surfaces are dark green, often glaucous, with dense white spots in crosswise bands. The epidermis is smooth, rarely slightly rough. Margins are tuberculate-serrulate, rarely simply toothed; tips are blunt or acute with a short point. Juvenile leaves are two-ranked and strap-shaped, horizontal to upright-spreading, smooth or rough, with acute, rarely blunt, short-pointed tips.

The inflorescence varies: it may be a flat-topped panicle up to 500 mm tall, or a raceme with or without a pair of side branches. Racemes are horizontal, rarely upright-spreading, and often droop. The flattened scape is 6–10 mm wide at the base. Bracts are hairy and 5 mm long; pink pedicels are 6–7 mm long. The perianth is 28–40 mm long, on a stalk up to 6 mm; its narrow-elliptic, stomach-shaped basal portion occupies about half its length and is 5–9 mm wide, often triangular in cross-section. This portion is pink, while the upper half is white with green striations; both are similarly inflated, with slight constriction in the middle. The tips are erect, then spread upright and blunt. Inner segments are free and channelled at the base for 5–6 mm, then diverge gradually. Stamens are 28–30 mm long, with 2 mm anthers that are included or project slightly. The ovary is 7–12 mm long and 3 mm in diameter; the style is 20–25 mm long. The minute stigma curves upward and is included or slightly exserted. The capsule is 18–25 mm long with a blunt tip; seeds are 3–4 mm long and 2–3 mm wide. Flowering is reported from November to February.

The diagnostic account describes *G. croucheri* as variable but distinct, with a large perianth 35–40 mm long, inflated in both lower and upper halves and slightly constricted in the middle. Its spreading rosette leaves are triangular-lanceolate or triangular, rarely linear, and often glaucous; juvenile leaves may be rough or smooth. Habit and inflorescence vary. In floral characters it is closest to *G. batesiana* and *G. acinacifolia*. *G. batesiana* is smaller, with a similar perianth, a distinctly wrinkled-tuberculate leaf epidermis and a simple raceme. *G. acinacifolia* is larger, with taller panicles and 20 mm bracts; it also has tuberculate juvenile leaves, capsules 30–43 mm long and seeds up to 8 mm in diameter. *G. croucheri* may also be confused with the large, robust *G. excelsa*, whose smaller perianth has a stomach-shaped base extending over more than half its length.

The habitat account describes dry, hot, rocky, wooded river valleys, usually associated with sandstone spelled “quartztitic” in the source. Plants occur on sheer cliffs in the shade of the northern valley-bushveld variant, and also on rocks in coastal forest and thornveld. Soils are shallow and humus-rich; the plants seem confined to southern and eastern aspects. Rainfall is reported as 500–1,000 mm per year, mainly in summer; summers are hot and winters mild. At Oribi Gorge in southern Natal, plants occur on sheer cliffs shaded by short xerophytic trees and shrubs.
