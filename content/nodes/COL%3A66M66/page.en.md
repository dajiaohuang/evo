## Accepted name and placement {#s-9135f88447}
COL26.8 accepts Anaxyrus debilis (Girard, 1854) as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Bufonidae (family) → Anaxyrus (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## A source-listed native regional occurrence {#s-384495b3f9}
The 2025 checklist lists Anaxyrus debilis as native in the Sierra Madre Occidental biogeographic province of western Mexico. The authors compile and check state lists, museum-based information, VertNet and GBIF rather than newly surveying every species. Their regional scope crosses parts of Sonora, Chihuahua, Sinaloa, Durango, Zacatecas, Nayarit and Jalisco; it does not imply occurrence throughout all seven states or every habitat in the mountains. The record is attributed to the source taxonomy, not an independently examined voucher, density estimate or complete current range.

## Distribution categories are dated checklist assignments {#s-7f179715bf}
The row’s global-distribution code 2 classifies the source taxon as shared between the United States and Mexico. Its total-province field is printed as 5; this is a biogeographic-list entry, not an area of occupancy, number of individual populations or a measured elevational range. The SMO’s regional extremes below 200 m and above 3,300 m cannot be assigned to this species. Endemism is the authors’ source assignment and does not independently establish every current occurrence or reconcile later taxonomic splits.

## Three conservation systems retain distinct dates and meanings {#s-8c9617a15d}
The source records IUCN LC (Least Concern), with the full status/trend cell “LC (=)”; Environmental Vulnerability Score “L (7)”; and Mexican category Pr (subject to special protection). IUCN entries use version 2024-2 accessed in March 2025, while the authors describe the national list as a static 2010 source and EVS as expert-based vulnerability scoring. These are not newly verified current categories, measured local population trends or directly comparable risk probabilities. Data deficiency, an unknown trend, no evaluation and absence from a list are different from demonstrated safety. Differences between systems do not by themselves prove one is wrong.

## Adult tapeworm associations in a historical North American checklist {#s-ec40323580}
The 2023 review includes Anaxyrus debilis among amphibian hosts with records of adult intestinal cestodes. Its named parasite associations are Distoichometra bufonis. These describe compiled host–parasite records, not parasites measured in every member of the species. The scope is adult worms in wild continental North American herpetofauna; larval cestode records are excluded. An intestinal association alone does not establish disease severity, host mortality, zoonotic transmission or the species’ role in completing every parasite life cycle. Prevalence, intensity, examined-host denominators and experimental susceptibility are not supplied by this host checklist.

## How to interpret the compiled parasite evidence {#s-80757468bf}
The authors assembled literature records into both parasite-first and host-first lists and supplied an identification key using earlier taxonomic works. The host-first list intentionally omits worms identified only to genus; a shorter list there is not evidence that genus-level records do not exist. The article reports 88 named amphibian hosts overall, alongside unresolved and hybrid labels in its host list; that source total is not the number of pinned COL26.8 pages linked here. This page uses only an exact source-name match to the frozen accepted name, without certifying every historical specimen’s identity. The source does not document a dated, reproducible database-query protocol sufficient to treat a missing association as a systematic negative finding.
