## Domestic, feral and breed variation {#s-56a9574c57}
MDD describes domestic horses as a domestic derivative of E. ferus and distinguishes domestic and feral occurrence. Breed formation and artificial selection produce substantial variation; one breed’s mass or lifespan cannot represent the entire taxon.

## The 2015 life-history entry and this name {#s-0379a810ad}
Equus caballus is accepted in pinned COL26.8 within Equidae. This page compares the same-name historical life-history entry using 7 focal fields, each supported by cited species-level sparse-table rows with matching name and family. Reported summaries can still include historical taxonomic inheritance; a matching row name does not remove that influence.

## Different stages in the mass fields {#s-d8a1a95e2f}
Historical summary values are adult body mass 250000 g; mass at birth or hatching 37900 g. Adult mass can combine males, females and adults of unspecified sex; birth/hatching mass describes a different life stage. These are not a longitudinal series of the same individuals or a species-wide range of individual masses.

## Reproduction and developmental timing {#s-ec5daef60e}
Available compilation fields are female maturity field (defined by the source as age at first conception) 914 d; offspring per litter or eggs per clutch 1 offspring/eggs; conception or fertilization to birth 337.99 d; age of dependence on food other than maternal milk 274 d; d denotes days. The litter/clutch field combines offspring or egg counts, and a fractional summary does not mean a single event produced a fractional individual. Timing fields refer to different events and cannot be exchanged for one uniform reproductive cycle.

## A longest record differs from usual lifespan {#s-4288c121d0}
The archive’s maximum_longevity_y value is 62 years, taking the largest record in the collected material under the source method. It is not typical lifespan, mean wild lifespan, a survival ceiling for every individual or a current repeatedly attainable record.

## How to trace these values to sources {#s-3140933fe9}
The field summaries draw on source labels such as Jones, K., Bielby, J., Cardillo, M. et al. (2009), de Magalhaes and Costa (2009), Ricklefs (2010b) and Garratt et al. (2014). A label can point to a paper, book or another database. A row count is not a count of measured horses, and the same citation appearing in multiple rows does not make those rows independent experiments.

## Read this historical record through food and foraging position {#s-bdb79e65ff}
COL26.8 accepts *Equus caballus* in Equidae. A separate EltonTraits 1.0 entry under this same name groups three ecological field types: food, foraging position and activity period. It uses historical taxonomy, so the name match alone does not resolve later splits or synonym disputes.

## Relative weights of food categories {#s-aad51a8c2c}
The archive’s nonzero food weights are other plant material (the source category also includes fungi) 100%. Code ABC pools high, reasonable and partial confidence; some estimates can borrow from an ecologically similar congener. ABC cannot be rewritten as direct observations of this species. These percentages standardize verbal descriptions rather than measure diet composition; zero does not prove an item is never eaten. Categories such as Nect, Seed and PlantO are broader than their everyday names and cannot imply exclusively nectar, seeds or plants.

## Mammalian foraging-stratum assignment {#s-9be9182eb0}
The entry is assigned G, the ground category; the source definition also includes some aquatic foraging. Code A records compiler confidence in the source and assignment. The mammalian stratum reference is Nowak (1999). This is a category, not a percentage of time spent in each stratum, and it does not mean the species acts only there.

## Activity periods can occur together {#s-823d164d60}
The archive marks feeding activity during night, twilight, daytime as 1. Several fields can be 1 together; they are not proportions adding to a daily time budget or schedules shared by every day, season and site. Activity certainty is ABC and can still use ecologically similar congeneric material; genus/family typical activity values do not fill gaps here.

## Trace each field group to its source labels {#s-435d804a79}
The used groups carry labels Ref_1 and Nowak1999. Diet, foraging position and activity may draw on different material, and a Ref number in a bird table is not necessarily the same work as that number in a mammal table. The underlying books and papers have not been individually read; source labels are pointers to cited material, not additional observations.

## catalogue-profile / limitations {#s-3c9f315ce7}
The life-history values summarize a 2015 archive: fields use medians or, for an even number of inputs, the mean of the two middle values; maximum longevity uses the largest record found. They are not measurements of one horse, ranges of individual variation or current population surveys. Historical species and subspecies records may share or inherit data, so matching name and family do not establish identity with the current COL26.8 concept or remove inherited components. The cited underlying publications have not been individually reviewed, and wild versus captive conditions are not uniformly known.

The EltonTraits entry is also a historical same-name, same-family summary, not a complete species account. Its food weights standardize literature descriptions rather than measure intake, energy or observation time. ABC food and activity fields may include material from an ecologically similar congener, so they are not necessarily direct observations of this species. The underlying books and papers have not been individually read. Neither dataset establishes current range, local or seasonal variation, population status or conservation status.
