## Leaves and flowers {#s-c1f0c69e53}
The 2012 regional account describes a corm-bearing geophyte 5–15 cm tall. Its hairy, lance-shaped leaves may have thickened margins and can be shorter or longer than the stem. The flowers are bilaterally symmetrical; the lower tepals are somewhat claw-shaped, meet at the base, then diverge obliquely. They are pale blue with white to yellow marks on the lower tepals and have a scent. The inner bracts are divided to the base, and the ovary is hairy.

These traits and measurements come from a regional South African account published in 2012; the excerpt gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency.

## Colour, scent, and comparisons {#s-4929f134c4}
A 2020 account describes *B. mucronata* as one of the most common species in the interior valleys and lower mountain slopes of the western half of Western Cape. Among section Babiana species with densely hairy ovaries, it is recognized by pale to deep-violet flowers with white to pale-yellow blotches on the lower lateral tepals. The anthers are pale yellow to lilac, sometimes turquoise. Flowers usually have an acrid to metallic odour, though sometimes appear scentless.

Most other blue-flowered section *Babiana* species in this area are more or less stemless, have leaves taller than the stem, and smooth or sparsely hairy ovaries. The account excepts the relatively tall *B. lineolata*: it favours wet sandy ground and can be distinguished by rigid, narrow, deeply pleated leaves and short floral bracts, mostly 7–10 mm long.

These diagnostic comparisons come from a regional South African account published in 2020; the excerpt gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency.
