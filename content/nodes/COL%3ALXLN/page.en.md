## Name and this data account {#s-90eb4e8145}
COL26.8 accepts Bitis worthingtoni in Viperidae. Its ReptTraits v1-2 entry follows the Reptile Database 2023 name framework and retains citations for each recorded trait. The single species row summarizes compiled records, not one uniform state across populations.

## Distinguish the measured length {#s-838927405f}
The reported maximum total length (TL), including the tail, is 560 mm. Snout–vent length (SVL) ends at the vent and is a different measurement. This compiled maximum describes source records, not typical size or an absolute growth limit. Values for different sexes may come from separate studies or places and do not establish size differences within a population.

## Where and how it is active {#s-39602b814f}
The entry records terrestrial microhabitat and diurnal activity. These broad labels do not list every habitat or set a fixed schedule, and the compiled row does not establish whether populations, seasons, or life stages share the same pattern.

## How to read reproductive records {#s-e0e401798a}
The entry labels reproductive mode as viviparous and reports a consolidated mean of 9.5 offspring or eggs per litter or clutch. Such summaries may be midpoints of reported means, or of reported extremes when means are unavailable; they are not necessarily averages of observed clutches. A fractional value is a summary, not part of an individual. The dataset defines lowest and highest values as endpoints of reported means, not the smallest or largest observed clutches. The labels do not establish fetal nutrition or uniformity among populations.

## Evidence limits {#s-0f02ca6725}
This account paraphrases the same-name ReptTraits v1-2 entry, retaining its 2023 taxonomic framework and sources for individual traits. One row per species compresses variation in sex, age, locality and populations; matching name and family do not ensure equivalent later concepts. Reported maximum length is not an absolute species limit, and reproductive means are not fixed counts in every clutch. Many mass entries are estimated from length equations and are not presented here as measured mass. Juvenile-length headers and definitions differ and are also omitted from reader conclusions. Conservation, venom, current distribution and population trends are not updated here; unrecorded fields remain gaps.
