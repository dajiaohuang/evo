## Leaves and flowers {#s-6369d95449}
The 2012 regional account describes a short-stemmed, corm-bearing geophyte 2–8 cm tall, with hairy, lance-shaped leaves longer than the stem. The bilaterally symmetrical, fragrant flowers range from mauve to blue with yellow markings, or may be entirely dull yellow. The inner bracts are divided to the base, and the ovary is smooth.

These traits and measurements come from a regional South African account published in 2012; the excerpt gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency.

## Flower colour and comparisons {#s-9ab90c2967}
A 2020 diagnostic account describes *B. patula* as typically low-growing, with relatively small, strongly scented flowers and often unusual colouring. Flowers may be white to creamy yellow, but are more often violet or light mauve to purple; the lower lateral tepals are deep yellow and tipped with purple or mauve. The account suggests an affinity with the southern Cape species *B. fourcadei*, which is taller and has blue to violet flowers marked with white, a longer 20–30 mm perianth tube, often a spicy, acrid scent, and a smooth ovary.

The account's phrase describing *B. patula*'s ovary as “sparsely hairy on in the ribs” is malformed, so the precise hair pattern is unclear. It contrasts *B. patula* with superficially similar *B. odorata*, whose cream and yellow flowers, sometimes flushed with mauve, are also strongly scented, but whose ovary is densely hairy. *B. patula* may also be confused with *B. ambigua*, typically a short or stemless plant of sandy habitats. *B. ambigua* has dark-blue, rarely mauve, flowers with white nectar guides; its ovary is smooth or sparsely hairy on the ribs. The account identifies a well-developed fibrous collar around the stem base in *B. patula*, absent in *B. ambigua*, as the clearest difference.

These diagnostic comparisons come from a regional South African account published in 2020; the excerpt gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency.
