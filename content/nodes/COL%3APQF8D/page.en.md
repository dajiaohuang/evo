## Marker availability in the 2023 island-origin comparison {#s-983add7897}
The paper's Table 2 lists 12S, 16S, COI, RAG-1, TYR, BDNF for Kurixalus raoi. The combined study uses three mitochondrial and three nuclear gene fragments, but missing entries mean that this species is not necessarily represented by all six genes. Several rows combine vouchers or previously deposited sequences; this is not a newly collected complete genome or an independently verified population survey. The concatenated analysis partitions the six loci into five groups, with 12S and 16S together. No new diet, breeding behaviour or physiological tolerance follows from this sequence row.

## Its relationship to the reconstructed island lineages {#s-46a4d8df67}
The combined phylogeny places this species in clade I with lenquanensis, idiootocus, silvaenaias, inexpectatus and raoi. Clade I, the island clade II and gracilloides group together, but relationships among those three branches remain unresolved. The authors therefore analyse three alternative placements of gracilloides. Membership in this clade does not imply that all five species independently colonized Taiwan or share a measured island-arrival date.

## New nuclear fragments, rather than a new whole genome {#s-b57ae9149e}
The authors newly sequenced RAG-1, TYR and BDNF fragments for Kurixalus raoi, alongside two other named species, with the nine study accessions OQ719606–OQ719614. These new fragments supplement existing mitochondrial information. The study does not provide new nuclear data for every genus member, nor population-wide whole-genome evidence for this species.
