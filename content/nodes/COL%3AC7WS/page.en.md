## What the conservation index measures {#s-c606384126}
The 2024 Chilean assessment gives Alsodes barrioi a printed conservation-priority index of 1.35 and places it in Table 4’s low category. It evaluates 58 selected species using literature, citizen-science records and government plans; it is not a new census of every population. The index combines scores for distribution, threat, population trend, taxonomic uniqueness and body size with a protected-area term. The value is a management ranking under this model, not an extinction probability, a current IUCN assessment or a direct population-size measurement. “Low” or “non-priority” does not establish biological safety or remove the need for conservation.

## Shape comparisons do not trace this species’ development {#s-692e590355}
Across the sampled species, larval–adult shape dissimilarities have a weak but statistically significant Mantel association (r 0.16, p 0.003), while disparity correlations vary among families. A Mantel test compares patterns of pairwise distances. The authors interpret the overall result as support for adaptive decoupling, but it does not show zero correlation, genetic independence, an experimentally selected trait or the developmental trajectory of Alsodes barrioi. The phylogenetic analyses include 74 of the 83 sampled species. Reconstructed ancestral shape and timing come from models fitted to living-species data, not newly measured fossil morphology. Adult K 0.44 with p 0.001 indicates less similarity among close relatives than the reference expectation; the paper’s phrase “not phylogenetically structured” does not mean the test was nonsignificant. Supplement S3 repeats family N values that sum to 112 for both stages, so adult individual denominators and imputed cells cannot be confirmed without the raw matrices.
