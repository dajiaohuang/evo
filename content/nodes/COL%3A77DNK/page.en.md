## Workshop vocabulary and later species naming {#s-4f37b11922}
The underlying workshop used an opportunity sample of 13 volunteer native isiZulu speakers, aged18–55, from five Zululand areas: ten participants on28November and three on1December2016 at Tembe Elephant Park. Participants viewed frog photographs and discussed names collectively, resolving disagreements by discussion. The six broad names elicited during the workshop did not individually distinguish all58 species. Species-level names were assigned afterwards with Bongani Mkhize, combining this vocabulary with earlier published names and naming guidelines. These group discussions do not supply independent per-person frequencies, dialect-wide agreement, or a test of recognition accuracy for Phrynobatrachus natalensis.

## Species Index and site index have different meanings {#s-9f69d1801c}
SI is the sum DBS+TBS+SBS for one species. Site RABI sums the SI of every recorded species, without dividing by species richness. Consequently, SI1 for Phrynobatrachus natalensis is not the RABI of a site containing it, nor a probability of extinction or an effect size. Successive input categories use doubled weights: DBS1/2/4/8, TBS0/1/2/4/8 and SBS0/1/2/4. Those weights express the authors' prioritization design; they are not evidence that extinction risk or ecological sensitivity actually doubles between categories. The positive correlations reported among sub-indices also mean that their inputs are not empirically independent in this sample.
