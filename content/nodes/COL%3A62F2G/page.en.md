## Larval and adult measurements use different source material {#s-b1a325cb3e}
The morphology dataset retains three Batrachyla species rows. Its larval data use 32 landmarks (reference points for describing shape), while its adult data use ten linear measurements, so the stages were measured in different ways. None of the larval rows uses photographs taken for the study; the larval images come from literature. Adult data combine images of listed material, measurements taken from published figures, and literature values. No retained adult row records a female, and two leave sex unspecified; these are source-row counts, not a population sex ratio. Stages are paired by species rather than by following the same frog through metamorphosis. The study also corrects for specimen fixation and uses principal component analysis (PCA) to fill some adult values, so not every cell is a direct observation.

## Larval and adult habitat codes describe different categories {#s-99f6048d39}
For the same three retained rows, the larval codes are LE 3, LO 0, END 0, and ST 0; the adult codes are SAX 0, SEMAQ 0, TER 3, and TOR 0. The larval labels refer to still-water (lentic), flowing-water (lotic), endotrophic, and semiterrestrial categories; the adult labels refer to rock-dwelling (saxicolous), semi-aquatic, terrestrial, and torrent-related categories. These counts describe retained source rows, not the genus as a whole. Endotrophic tadpoles are a larval category, distinct from direct development. Broad habitat codes do not measure flow tolerance, diet, breeding success, or habitat use at every life stage.

## Shape decoupling remains a comparative hypothesis {#s-d079189ef0}
Across the study’s full comparative sample, a Mantel test comparing larval and adult shape-distance patterns found a weak but statistically significant association (r = 0.16, p = 0.003); patterns of shape disparity also differed among families. The authors interpret this as adaptive decoupling, but the comparison does not demonstrate complete genetic independence or a causal developmental mechanism. Phylogenetic analyses retained 74 of 83 sampled species. Reconstructions along PC1 (the first principal component) and geological dates are model estimates, not newly observed fossils. The study also reports a lower adult K statistic (p = 0.001), while family sample sizes and statistics remain inconsistent in the source. These comparative results do not establish size or convergence as a universal diagnostic property of each linked species.
