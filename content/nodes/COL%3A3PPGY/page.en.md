## Leaves, flowers and an immature pod {#s-9a1924da81}
An older Flora of Panama account describes a tree with densely woolly, dark rusty-brown branchlets. Its large compound leaves carry six leaflet pairs. The roughly 2.5 cm leaf stalk (petiole) is cylindrical and as woolly as the branches; the central axis (rachis) is about 16 cm long, unwinged and similarly hairy. Above the points where the small leaflet stalks attach, it bears a large, unstalked, oval, concave gland. Leaflets are mostly elliptic to lance-shaped; the terminal ones are about 13 × 4 cm and the basal pair somewhat smaller. Their bases are rounded or blunt, and their tips pointed and somewhat long-tapering. The upper surface has coarse short hairs, except that the midrib is woolly; the underside is woolly. Veins stand out below and are slightly sunken above, forming a net pattern. The author did not know the stipules (small leaf-base appendages). Flower clusters are stalked spikes from the leaf axils; each cluster stalk is 4–5 cm long, and its thick flower-bearing part about 4 cm. The small bracts fall away. Flowers have no separate stalk; the tubular calyx (outer floral cup) is 6–7 mm long and reddish-brown hairy. The corolla (petal whorl) was not known. The only pod described is immature: slender and twisted, rusty-woolly and grooved lengthwise; thickened margins make it roughly cylindrical. This historical regional description gives no population sampling frame or observation date.
