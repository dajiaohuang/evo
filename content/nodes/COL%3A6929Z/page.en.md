## Accepted name and placement {#s-58eed725e0}
COL26.8 accepts Brachycephalus hermogenesi (Giaretta & Sawaya, 1998) as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Brachycephalidae (family) → Brachycephalus (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## Isolated notes and note groups in the reviewed calls {#s-b3759b003b}
The 2021 review diagnosed B. hermogenesi by at least 24 notes per call, with isolated notes and groups, using a note-centred definition of a call. Its own groups contained 2–6 notes, compared with the previously reported 2–7; notes had 1–3 pulses in the review. Weak preceding notes provisionally termed attenuated notes were detected in only three calls, MHNCI 167, 183 and 215, and were not counted as part of the note groups. These counts describe the reviewed acoustic evidence, not a number of frogs or universal call limits; frequencies alone overlapped those previously reported for B. sulfuratus.

## A possible second species in part of the type series {#s-fcbd00781b}
Calls at Trilha do Corisco and part of Corcovado were attributed to an unidentified third flea toad, with groups usually of two notes, occasionally three, and up to 16 pulses per note. The review reported that this unnamed form and B. hermogenesi occurred together at Corcovado. Its conclusion suggested that the hermogenesi type series could include a second species associated with a paratype locality, probably not the holotype locality, and did not infer that the name was a synonym of another species. These are the authors’ dated assignments and uncertainty; the unnamed form is not treated as an accepted taxon or confused with B. sulfuratus.

## Historical names require locality-specific reassessment {#s-dae3567295}
The review retained several São Paulo records formerly called B. hermogenesi as Brachycephalus sp. because adequate diagnostic evidence was unavailable. It proposed that previous southern-Brazil records instead belonged to B. sulfuratus, while its individual-record discussion explicitly retained uncertain or unvouchered records. Available mitochondrial evidence placed the Paraibuna specimen with hermogenesi specimens from the type locality, but did not certify every historical population. A coherent calling pattern across reviewed localities likewise did not establish that the name contained only one biological species. Range summaries should therefore preserve these record-level and dated taxonomic limits.

## A source-coded development mode and oviposition environment {#s-8619144ffd}
The Atlantic Forest trait compilation codes Brachycephalus hermogenesi as direct-developing and its oviposition as terrestrial. In the article, direct development means no free-living larval stage, with a froglet hatching rather than a free-living tadpole; it does not mean that embryonic development is absent or that humidity is unnecessary. These are literature-compiled categories, not newly observed spawning or a developmental experiment for this species. Exact correspondence between the source taxonomy and the current accepted concept remains uncertified.
