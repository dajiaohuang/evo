## Reading an older ecology record {#s-9e3ee6db3d}
COL26.8 accepts Dendropicos elachus in Picidae. EltonTraits 1.0 contains a same-name, same-family entry under an earlier taxonomy. This page reads its diet and feeding-position fields with their source labels; the name match does not decide later splits or synonymies.

## Diet-weight estimate {#s-99a6f05de1}
Invertebrates carry all of the nonzero diet weight (100%). Code A signals high compiler confidence in the source and diet entry. The percentage is a standardized interpretation of published descriptions, not measured intake; zero weight elsewhere would not prove a food is never taken, and broad categories do not indicate an exclusive diet.

## Mid and upper canopy feeding positions {#s-c2afdec55f}
The species-level foraging entry divides position weights equally: 50% in trees or tall bushes from 2 m to below the canopy, and 50% in or just above the canopy. These are relative feeding positions, not nest sites, all habitat use or a full-day budget. Water-surface, understory, mid/high, canopy and aerial classes have distinct definitions; they do not give elevation or flight height.

## Provenance of the selected groups {#s-5576a1242a}
Both selected groups cite Ref_7, the Handbook of the Birds of the World, volume 7. Other entries may use different materials for diet, strata and activity, and a repeated reference number across bird and mammal tables need not identify the same work. The archive preserves citation strings by file, row and field group. The Handbook was not independently reread here.

## Limits of the historical record {#s-867c61e25c}
EltonTraits is a historical same-name, same-family comparison, not a complete species account. The name match does not prove equivalence between its older taxonomy and COL26.8. Diet and foraging percentages are standardized interpretations of literature descriptions, not measurements of intake, energy or time. The selected bird fields are species-level, but the Handbook was not independently reviewed here. Individual, seasonal and local variation, current distribution, populations and conservation require other evidence.
