## Highland records with a southwestern exception {#s-ef6afdd514}
The revision reported populations on both sides of Ethiopia’s Great Rift Valley. An elevation interval of 2387–3394 m was based on 156 barcoded individuals, while a few southwestern individuals between Tippi and Gech’a were reported at 2270 m. The exceptional lower locality is retained separately, without replacing it with a claim that every population occurs within the barcoded-sample interval.

## Rain-associated calling and water-body eggs {#s-9b8e81682b}
Males called in shallow puddles, flooded grassland or agricultural fields, often close together. Calling was greatest in rainy months, usually starting after 22:00 but sometimes as late as 02:00 in dry weather, and ending before dawn. The account reported numerous small bicolored eggs in the same water body, without supplying clutch counts or development duration.

## A geographically sampled mitochondrial and nuclear dataset {#s-bef74f77ab}
The 2023 Ethiopian Highlands study lists 25 mitochondrial samples and 16 nuclear-SNP samples for Ptychadena erlangeri in Table 1, with mitochondrial nucleotide diversity π=0.64%. Samples across the twelve-species study were collected in 2011–2019; these are analysed specimens, not a population census. All assembled mitochondrial genomes had the usual two rRNAs, thirteen protein-coding genes and twenty-two tRNAs, but no species had a completely resolved control region because repeats could not be confidently assembled from short reads. The phylogenetic alignment used selected genes (13,937 bp across 303 samples), rather than an alignment of complete control regions.

## A study-defined highland clade and regional distribution {#s-d862cb6c4e}
The paper places Ptychadena erlangeri in its P. erlangeri clade and reports it from the both sides of the Great Rift Valley. These distributions combine the authors' geographic sampling and earlier range work; they are not independently updated complete range polygons. The twelve-species highland clade is a subset of Ptychadena. Its mitochondrial-clock analysis estimated a common ancestor around 11.7 million years ago, with a reported interval of 8.827–15.494 million years. The east-of-rift ancestral reconstruction had a model probability of 69%; neither that estimate nor inferred crossings is a directly observed migration event or a nuclear-genome divergence date for this species.

## A dated reassessment argument, with remaining demographic gaps {#s-1a8f719cca}
The 2023 discussion calls for conservation assessment or reassessment using revised species concepts and genetic and geographic evidence. The present genomic sample does not itself measure current abundance, fragmentation, decline or reproductive success in Ptychadena erlangeri. ## Three sampled regional groups agree across markers {#s-161f4f4e35}
The study found one western and two eastern populations in both mitochondrial data and nuclear PCA using approximately 0.6 million SNPs. Their geographical structure is a sample-level finding, not evidence that every locality has been genetically surveyed. The inferred western dispersal at about 0.57 million years ago comes from the mitochondrial-clock and ancestral-area model, not direct observation.
