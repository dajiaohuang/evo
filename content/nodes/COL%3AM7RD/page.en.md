## Type series and name-bearing specimen {#s-67580a5c6f}
The name-bearing holotype is female ZSI_A-12521, SVL27.4mm, collected12December2010 near Mt.Harriet at approximately175m in evergreen forest. Nine adult paratypes ZSI_A-12522–12530 and seven larval paratypes WII-115 are listed separately; the larvae came from a Rutland phytotelm. Referred adult WII-113 had an extra toe on both feet and is not described as the usual digit condition. The genus was monotypic when described. Etymology attributes beryet to a Great Andamanese expression for a small frog; the authors’ expectation of prior community knowledge is not an independently documented ethnobiological interview.

## Adult characters and measurement disagreements {#s-09d4525602}
The diagnosis combines small size, a visible tympanum, no cranial ridges or vomerine teeth, elongate paired parotoid glands, webbed fingers/toes and expanded digit tips without circum-marginal grooves. Diagnosis SVL is mean24.0mm, range21.8–27.4mm; the holotype-description paragraph instead opens with mean24.2±0.6mm even though the holotype is one female of27.4mm. ## Skeleton and living versus preserved coloration {#s-edeff92782}
The osteological account is based on one paratype, ZSI_A12527: six presacral vertebrae, an arciferal pectoral girdle, no omosternum, a coccyx not fused to the sacrum, finger phalanges2-2-3-3 and toe phalanges2-2-3-4-3. ## Leaf-perching and secretion observations {#s-de57e0aff9}
Adults were observed on shrub leaves at night and under forest-floor litter by day. Calling males perched about1–1.5m above ground. The article reports a white, viscous, pungent parotoid secretion during handling, and mortality of other frogs confined in the same bag. That is an uncontrolled observation, not an experimentally identified toxin, quantified dose, verified cause of death or demonstration of dietary sequestration. The suggested ecological niche and gland-related evolutionary interpretation are hypotheses; they do not establish competitive exclusion or a tested chemical-defense mechanism.

## A monitored phytotelm cohort {#s-f8fc995696}

This is one observed cohort, not a species-wide clutch-size distribution, survival estimate or complete-development duration. The statement that breeding begins in June must be kept alongside the May cohort, not converted into a June-only season.

## Alternative island-origin hypotheses remain open {#s-830bedcaeb}
