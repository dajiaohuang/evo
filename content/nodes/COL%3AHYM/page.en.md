## form {#s-d7535eda1b}
This order includes ants, bees, wasps and sawflies. Winged adults couple their wings with small hooks and development includes a pupa. Wingless workers still belong here; broad-waisted sawflies show why a narrow waist is not universal.

## ecology {#s-9643fb88f0}
Flower visiting, herbivory, predation and parasitoid life histories coexist. Parasitoid larvae develop at a host’s expense and ultimately kill it. Ant and honey-bee colonies do not represent all wasp and bee lifestyles.

## What the evidence supports {#s-d1fb3bc607}
The oldest undisputed xyelid fossils fall at about 242–237 Ma, a fossil minimum rather than an estimate of when Hymenoptera began. Molecular divergence estimates depend on sampled taxa, loci, fossil calibrations and clock models.

## A tree built from sampled lineages {#s-c60d52e1f6}
Peters and colleagues used transcriptomes, the expressed RNA copied from genes, to compare relationships across major hymenopteran lineages. The analysis supports many relationships and supplies fossil-calibrated divergence estimates. A calibration helps set a time scale for a tree, but the estimate remains tied to the included lineages, loci, fossil choices and clock model; it is not a measured first appearance for the whole order.

## Triassic fossils set a minimum age {#s-959cc25ed7}
A systematic review identifies the oldest undisputed xyelid fossils at approximately 242–237 Ma, based on preserved body and wing characters. The older edge of that interval is a fossil minimum: it shows that the lineage existed by then, not when the entire order originated. Other early fossil assignments and molecular estimates answer different questions and should not be collapsed into one date.
