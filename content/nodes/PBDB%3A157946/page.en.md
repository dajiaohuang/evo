## One database occurrence for this species {#s-af2d74cbb8}
The fixed Paleobiology Database (PBDB) pterosaur roster accepts Darwinopterus modularis beneath the database parent Darwinopterus. This guide uses one eligible species-level occurrence, linked to one collection and one reference identifier. These count database records and citations, not bones, specimens or animals.

## A collection from the Tiaojishan Formation {#s-e6be09b51c}
The selected collection is Daxishan, Linglongta, recorded in Liaoning under region code CN and assigned to the Tiaojishan Formation. Its database bracket spans 168.2–152.21 Ma, from the Late Bathonian at the older end to the Early Kimmeridgian at the younger end. This broad interval and single locality do not map the species’ full distribution.

## A broad span between recorded age bounds {#s-b1eb70b980}
The selected occurrence’s outer database bracket is 168.2–152.21 Ma (Ma means million years ago). It joins the older and younger bounds recorded for the collection; it is not a continuous fossil record or a direct date for the species’ origin, extinction or any individual. The breadth of the interval should not be mistaken for a precise specimen age.

## Collection setting and preservation labels {#s-21fff4d48b}
The Daxishan, Linglongta collection is labeled “lacustrine indet.” for environment and “body,” “adpression” and “soft parts” for preservation mode. These are collection-level database categories. A lake setting does not establish Darwinopterus’s habitat or diet, and the labels need not apply to every specimen in the collection.

## The reference attached to the collection {#s-f95b08f9bb}
The database attributes Daxishan, Linglongta to Lü et al. (2010), reference 31222. This is the citation attached to the collection record; the attribution alone does not establish whether it is the original description of Darwinopterus modularis.

## Evidence limits {#s-e89ccd8a3e}
This guide uses one eligible species-level occurrence from the fixed 276-name PBDB pterosaur roster. Database acceptance and parentage organize the record; they do not settle specimen identifications or every taxonomic dispute, and are kept separate from same-name Catalogue of Life concepts. The locality, broad age bracket, collection environment and preservation labels answer different questions. None alone establishes a complete range, exact origin or extinction, abundance, habitat, diet or the condition of every specimen. The selected record does not provide a complete anatomical or behavioral account.
