## Name and this data account {#s-6921a535c3}
Pinned COL26.8 accepts Graptemys sabinensis in Emydidae. This account follows its ReptTraits v1-2 row, which retains the Reptile Database 2023 name framework and trait-specific sources. As the dataset summarizes each species in one row, its categories describe that record rather than every population.

## Distinguish the measured length {#s-389ce88f5c}
The compiled maximum straight carapace length (SCL) is 250 mm; the female maximum is also 250 mm and the male maximum is 120 mm. SCL measures the shell in a straight line, excluding the head, neck and tail. These are maxima in the reported material, not theoretical limits or typical individual sizes. Sex-specific values may come from different localities or studies and do not alone measure sexual dimorphism across a population.

## Where and how it is active {#s-2d427b79a2}
The row classifies the microhabitat as Aquatic, diet as Omnivorous and foraging mode as active (ACT). These are broad categories, not a complete habitat inventory or fixed schedule. They do not identify particular foods or show differences among populations, seasons or life stages.

## How to read reproductive records {#s-b8f9123997}
The entry records an oviparous reproductive mode and a consolidated value of 3 litters or clutches per year. This annual count is a dataset summary, not a guarantee that every individual or population produces the same number. Other reproductive measures are not interpreted here.

## Evidence limits {#s-372c6aed1c}
This limited trait account paraphrases the same-name ReptTraits v1-2 row, retaining its Reptile Database 2023 framework and individual trait sources. One row per species compresses variation in sex, age, locality and populations; a matching name and family do not guarantee the same concept in later taxonomies. A reported maximum is not an absolute length limit. Many mass values in the dataset are estimated from length equations and are not presented here as measured mass. Juvenile-length fields have differing headers and definitions and are not used in the conclusions. Conservation, venom, current distribution and population trends are not updated; missing fields remain gaps.
