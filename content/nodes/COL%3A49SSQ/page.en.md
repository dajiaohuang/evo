## Morphology in the study {#s-fb387e55f4}
Researchers measured skin reflectance in 255 live Oophaga pumilio from six populations in Costa Rica and Panama between December 2008 and June 2011. Their visual models found population differences in color and brightness contrast among sampled dorsal and ventral coloration. These measurements describe the sampled populations; they do not establish that selection caused the divergence or characterize every population of the species.

Study scope: Six populations: Sarapiquí and Hitoy Cerere in Costa Rica; Río Gloria, Tierra Oscura, Isla Colón, and Isla Solarte in Panama. Field measurements were collected between December 2008 and June 2011; the article was published 2015-06-25.

Study material: Live, free-living frogs were measured in the field and released at their capture sites. No captive or domesticated frogs support the claim.

## Ecology in the study {#s-ad28ac433e}
In 20-day experiments at six sampled populations, researchers deployed 1,600 standardized clay frog models per population. Attack rates varied among populations and were higher at the two sampled Panama island populations than at several mainland populations; local versus non-local model origin had no significant overall effect. These are predator interactions with clay models, not mortality or survival measurements of live frogs.

Study scope: The six model-placement populations were Sarapiquí, Hitoy Cerere, Río Gloria, Tierra Oscura, Isla Colón, and Isla Solarte in Costa Rica and Panama. The main article gives the live-frog field-work period as December 2008 to June 2011 but does not date each model experiment separately; exact experiment dates remain unknown.

Study material: The focal frogs are free-living; attacks were recorded on non-toxic clay models placed in their habitat, not on live or captive frogs.

## Three complementary searches support a missing opsin hypothesis {#s-0d52a62c3f}
The study did not recover SWS2 in dendrobatid bait-capture assemblies. For pumilio it also pooled equal RNA concentrations from one eye sample from each of eleven populations into a single transcriptome, recovering RH1, LWS and SWS1 but not SWS2. This is one pooled expression dataset, not eleven independent absence tests. Searches of published and rescaffolded genomes did not identify an SWS2 gene or recognizable pseudogene, including upstream of LWS. Gaps and possible assembly errors in the pumilio scaffold remain acknowledged limitations. Together these observations support gene loss or degradation, rather than proving every individual lacks every possible remnant.

## Visual consequences remain partly inferred from earlier measurements {#s-27c227ecee}
The discussion cites earlier retinal work reporting no green rods in pumilio and a short-wavelength cone absorption peak near 466 nm. It proposes that loss of SWS2 could reduce dim-light colour discrimination and that RH1 might contribute to a middle-wavelength cone receptor. This paper does not newly measure behavioural colour thresholds, cone expression by cell type or visual-pigment absorbance. Similar absorbance curves motivate the RH1 proposal; they do not demonstrate a duplicated RH1 gene or establish the molecular identity of every receptor.

## Colour-mediated behaviour is cited background {#s-9b7395f136}
The authors cite earlier studies of maternal-colour imprinting in tadpoles, female colour-assortative preferences and male aggression toward similarly coloured rivals. These are species-specific research contexts used to motivate an opsin hypothesis; the present work did not perform new mate-choice, imprinting or territorial trials. It does not establish that a particular selected amino acid causes these behaviours or that every population expresses the same preference.
