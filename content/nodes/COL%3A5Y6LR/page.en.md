## Accepted name and placement {#s-e8904a7837}
COL26.8 accepts Chiropterotriton infernalis Rovito & Parra-Olea, 2015 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Caudata (order) → Plethodontidae (family) → Chiropterotriton (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## Selected Mexican survey localities, 2017–2018 {#s-d57494f949}
Figure 1 associates source-labelled Chiropterotriton infernalis with General Zaragoza; Conrado Castillo. Researchers searched 26 localities in five Mexican states from March 2017 to November 2018; salamanders were encountered at sixteen. Identification used external size, tail proportions, feet and color pattern. These sites are observed study links, not the species’ complete distribution or proof of present occupancy. No individual voucher or molecular re-identification has been independently checked. The additional Aquiloeurycea sp. is left unnamed and its observations are not redistributed among accepted species.

## Skin swabs describe a filtered bacterial community {#s-18709ca54d}
For Chiropterotriton infernalis and the other sampled hosts, the study changed gloves between animals, rinsed each with 100 ml sterile water and swabbed the whole body for thirty seconds. A separate ventral-body/tail/leg swab was used for Bd testing. The V4 16S bacterial assay retained forward reads, filtered very rare variants and rarefied to 17,536 reads per sample. It is not a complete skin metagenome, cultured viable-cell census or fungal community survey. Eight environmental swabs represented cave walls; each cave supplied only one wall sample, with no matched forest or karst environmental sampling. Negative bacterial controls did not amplify and were not sequenced, which is not independent proof of absence of contamination.

## Bd detection is not a test of resistance or disease survival {#s-e6378ab2df}
The Results report 6/12 (50%) Bd-positive Chiropterotriton infernalis samples. This is a small, dated sampled proportion, not regional prevalence, an experimental infection challenge or proof of resistance. No obvious disease signs or dead/dying animals were observed during the sampling visits; absence of observed signs does not establish later survival or population safety. Bd was quantified as ITS copies with synthetic DNA standards, not directly counted zoospores. ITS copy number differs among strains, so copy values cannot be equated to a cited fatal-zoospore threshold. Aggregate Results 31/86 differs from Methods microbiome comparison groups 30 positive/57 negative; skin-sequencing and diagnostic denominators remain unresolved without the individual supplement.

## Antifungal capacity is inferred rather than demonstrated in this host {#s-61647b8d66}
The study matches bacterial marker sequences to a reference database of previously tested Bd-inhibitory isolates and predicts functions with PICRUSt2. It does not culture this Chiropterotriton infernalis sample’s bacteria to demonstrate inhibition, measure antibiotic production or test a probiotic treatment. Cave and forest samples did not differ significantly in abundance/diversity of potentially inhibitory bacteria; this does not establish equivalent protection. Pooled microbial co-occurrence edges are statistical associations, not observed cooperation or competition, and the networks were not estimated for each host species. The authors warn that reference-genome predictions may miss real functions or infer absent ones in poorly represented cave communities.

## Within-species cave–forest comparisons retain their sample sizes {#s-1e4bdbac61}
The reduced dataset includes Chiropterotriton infernalis with 7 cave and 5 forest samples. Its within-species Shannon pairwise comparison has reported p 0.08, not a significant increase in cave bacterial diversity. Across the three dual-habitat species, unweighted UniFrac composition differs by habitat (p 0.007), while weighted UniFrac structure does not (p 0.11). Those pooled results are not separate significance tests for Chiropterotriton infernalis. Observational habitat contrasts cannot isolate every site, season or host difference, and nonsignificance is not proof that microbiomes are identical.

## High ITS-copy observations without a measured fatal threshold {#s-f56d08a092}
Three infernalis samples GZ1, GZ6 and GZ8 have a reported mean 18,388 ITS copies, compared with a reported 637-copy mean for most other positive samples. These are molecular load estimates in a limited field sample, not an inferred count of 18,388 zoospores. The Discussion cites a 10,000-zoospore mortality threshold from other work, while Methods explicitly warn that ITS copy number varies among Bd strains. No death was observed at the visit; neither a universal fatal threshold nor this species’ long-term tolerance is established.
