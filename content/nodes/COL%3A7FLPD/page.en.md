## Relative weights of food categories {#s-fb5735d3d5}
EltonTraits assigns 100% of the nonzero food weight to invertebrates. Its A code indicates high compiler confidence in the source and diet information. The percentage is a standardized reading of written accounts, not a measured diet share; zero does not show that a food is never eaten. Nect, Seed and PlantO are wider categories than their everyday names and do not imply that the bird eats only nectar, seeds or plants.

## Foraging strata differ from whole-day activity {#s-0267c0941b}
The species-level feeding-position weights are 50% in trees or tall bushes from 2 m upward but below the canopy, 40% in or just above the canopy, and 10% in understory, edges or shrubs below 2 m. These figures estimate relative use while feeding; they do not locate nests, describe all habitat or allocate an entire day. Surface-water, understory, mid/high, canopy and aerial categories have different source definitions. They are not elevation ranges and cannot be turned into flight heights or habitat area.

## Life-history evidence limits {#s-3078d82cbd}
This page is a guide to an older same-name, same-family EltonTraits 1.0 record, not a complete species monograph. The match does not prove that its historical species concept equals COL26.8. Food and bird feeding-position percentages are coarse relative weights standardized from written literature, not direct measures of stomach contents, intake, energy or observation time. Bird diet codes A/B retain source and proportional uncertainty. Mammal ABC diet or activity codes may also use a congener judged ecologically similar by the compilers; this page does not claim a direct observation of *V. kirkii* for every field. Genus- or family-level typical values, bird positions not marked species-level and split records requiring individual review do not support definite conclusions. The underlying books and papers have not been individually reread. Other sources are needed for individual, seasonal and local variation, current range, population and conservation.
