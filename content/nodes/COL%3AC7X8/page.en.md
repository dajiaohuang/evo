## Stage comparisons do not trace this species’ development {#s-77e676802f}
Across the species sampled in the study, differences between larval and adult shapes were weakly but significantly associated (Mantel r = 0.16, p = 0.003; this test compares patterns of distance), while disparity correlations varied among families. The authors interpret the overall pattern as supporting adaptive decoupling. It does not show zero association, genetic independence, experimental selection, or the developmental trajectory of Alsodes norae itself. The phylogenetic analysis used 74 of 83 sampled species; its reconstructions of ancestral shape through time were models based on living data, not new measurements from fossils. For adults, K = 0.44 (p = 0.001) indicates less similarity among close relatives than the reference expectation. The paper’s phrase “not phylogenetically structured” therefore does not mean that the test was nonsignificant. Supplement S3 repeats family N totals of 112 for both stages; without the raw matrices, the adult individual denominator and imputed cells remain uncertified.
