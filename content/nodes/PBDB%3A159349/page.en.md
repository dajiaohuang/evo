## Holotype BPV 078 was re-read as a tapejarid {#s-901690864f}
The 2023 revision re-examines Eopteranodon lii through its holotype, BPV 078, from the Yixian Formation. It compares that fossil with Sinopterus from the younger, overlying Jiufotang Formation while keeping E. lii distinct from S. dongi and Huaxiadraco corollatus. One central change is the identification of the skull and jaw remains.

## The original skull and jaw identifications were reversed {#s-a4ff9ec590}
The authors report that the skull and mandibular parts of BPV 078 had originally been identified in reverse. Their re-examination recognizes a downturned snout and a crest on the premaxilla, supporting placement among tapejarids. The account distinguishes the main slab, its counterpart and cranial details. The specimen remains the same; the revised identification is the authors’ interpretation.

## Similar features coexist with different proportions {#s-4feed83e65}
The revision notes similarities to S. dongi: a nearly quadrangular eye socket, a quadrate angled back by about 160°, and a fourth neck vertebra longer than the others. It also records differences: E. lii has a stouter nasoantorbital opening (length-to-height ratio about 2.5), a pteroid-to-ulna ratio near 0.56, a fourth-to-first wing-phalanx ratio near 0.45, and a first metatarsal shorter than the second. Together these measurements compare named specimens; no single ratio identifies every fragment or measures flight performance.

## One matrix analysis places three species together {#s-c79f060bdd}
With an updated character matrix, equal weighting and searches in TNT, the 2023 study found three shortest trees. Their strict consensus groups E. lii with S. dongi, with H. corollatus as the sister species to that pair. The authors distinguish a cluster based on overall similarity from a phylogeny inferred from character evidence. Other placements, including an earlier chaoyangopterid affinity, have also been proposed.

## A direct ancestor link remains a hypothesis {#s-97638e60ed}
Because E. lii is from the older Yixian Formation and resembles S. dongi from the younger Jiufotang Formation, the authors propose that the two may be chronospecies along a gradual evolutionary sequence. This is a hypothesis, not a demonstrated ancestor–descendant chain. Stratigraphic succession and resemblance alone cannot establish direct ancestry, exact fossil ages or whether the two species ever coexisted.

## One selected record in the Yixian Formation {#s-748f9056e8}
The fixed Paleobiology Database (PBDB) pterosaur roster accepts Eopteranodon lii beneath the database parent Eopteranodon. This guide uses one eligible species-level occurrence, linked to one collection and one reference identifier. These count database records and citations, not bones or individual animals.

## Beipiao collection and the Yixian Formation {#s-9b94a4b896}
The selected collection is Beipiao pterosaurs (PROXY), recorded in Liaoning (region code CN) and assigned to the Yixian Formation. Its database bracket is 125.77–119.57 Ma, from the Late Barremian at the older end to the Early Aptian at the younger end. One collection is not a complete distribution map for the species.

## A broad database age interval {#s-2f6a006119}
The selected occurrence has a broad database bracket of 125.77–119.57 Ma (Ma means million years ago). It bounds the collection record, not a continuous record at every moment, and does not directly date the species’ origin, extinction or an individual’s lifespan. It is not a precise specimen date.

## Collection labels do not say where the animal lived {#s-fbd90be0de}
Beipiao pterosaurs (PROXY) carries the database environment label “lacustrine indet.” and preservation-mode labels “body” and “adpression.” These describe a collection and its fossil-preservation categories, not Eopteranodon’s living habitat or diet. Collection-level labels need not apply to each fossil individually.

## The citation attached to the collection {#s-cba628da92}
The database attributes Beipiao pterosaurs (PROXY) to Lü et al. (2006), reference 31359. This is the citation attached to the collection record; it is distinct from the 2023 revision that discusses the holotype’s anatomy and classification.

## Evidence limits {#s-507c2ea304}
This account summarizes the 2023 revision’s interpretation of holotype BPV 078 alongside one selected PBDB occurrence from the fixed 276-name pterosaur roster. The skull and jaw reassignment and the phylogenetic placement are interpretations attributed to that study. The proposed chronospecies relationship between E. lii and S. dongi remains a hypothesis: formation order does not prove direct ancestry. The broad database age bracket is not an exact specimen date, and a collection environment is not a species-wide habitat. The original 2005/2006 descriptions and the full range of later anatomical and ecological studies are outside this account.
