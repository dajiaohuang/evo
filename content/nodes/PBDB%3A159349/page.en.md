## Holotype BPV 078 was re-read as a tapejarid {#s-901690864f}
The 2023 revision re-examines Eopteranodon lii through its holotype, BPV 078, from the Yixian Formation. It compares that fossil with Sinopterus from the younger, overlying Jiufotang Formation while keeping E. lii distinct from S. dongi and Huaxiadraco corollatus. One central change is the identification of the skull and jaw remains.

## The original skull and jaw identifications were reversed {#s-a4ff9ec590}
The authors report that the skull and mandibular parts of BPV 078 had originally been identified in reverse. Their re-examination recognizes a downturned snout and a crest on the premaxilla, supporting placement among tapejarids. The account distinguishes the main slab, its counterpart and cranial details. The specimen remains the same; the revised identification is the authors’ interpretation.

## Similar features coexist with different proportions {#s-4feed83e65}
The revision notes similarities to S. dongi: a nearly quadrangular eye socket, a quadrate angled back by about 160°, and a fourth neck vertebra longer than the others. It also records differences: E. lii has a stouter nasoantorbital opening (length-to-height ratio about 2.5), a pteroid-to-ulna ratio near 0.56, a fourth-to-first wing-phalanx ratio near 0.45, and a first metatarsal shorter than the second. Together these measurements compare named specimens; no single ratio identifies every fragment or measures flight performance.

## One matrix analysis places three species together {#s-c79f060bdd}
With an updated character matrix, equal weighting and searches in TNT, the 2023 study found three shortest trees. Their strict consensus groups E. lii with S. dongi, with H. corollatus as the sister species to that pair. The authors distinguish a cluster based on overall similarity from a phylogeny inferred from character evidence. Other placements, including an earlier chaoyangopterid affinity, have also been proposed.

## A direct ancestor link remains a hypothesis {#s-97638e60ed}
Because E. lii is from the older Yixian Formation and resembles S. dongi from the younger Jiufotang Formation, the authors propose that the two may be chronospecies along a gradual evolutionary sequence. This is a hypothesis, not a demonstrated ancestor–descendant chain. Stratigraphic succession and resemblance alone cannot establish direct ancestry, exact fossil ages or whether the two species ever coexisted.
