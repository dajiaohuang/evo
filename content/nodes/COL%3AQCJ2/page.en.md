## Reading an older ecology record {#s-753e868e0c}
COL26.8 accepts Campethera cailliautii in Picidae. EltonTraits 1.0 has a same-name, same-family entry under an earlier taxonomy. This page reads its diet and feeding-position groups with their source labels; the name match alone does not settle later splits or synonymies.

## Food-weight estimate {#s-8906651fb6}
Invertebrates account for all of the nonzero diet weight (100%). Code A marks high compiler confidence in the source and diet entry, but the proportion is a standardized reading of published descriptions rather than measured intake. Zero weight elsewhere would not prove a food is never eaten; broad categories should not be read as exclusive diets.

## Feeding positions around the canopy {#s-f44df9467d}
The species-level entry assigns 80% of foraging weight to the canopy or just above it and 20% to trees or tall bushes from 2 m up to the canopy. These are relative feeding positions, not nest locations, all habitat use or a daily activity budget. Water, understory, mid/high, canopy and aerial classes have distinct definitions; they do not give elevation or flight height.

## Provenance of the selected fields {#s-1ac6f2bb23}
Both selected groups cite Ref_7, the Handbook of the Birds of the World, volume 7. Other records can use separate materials for diet, strata and activity, and the same number in bird and mammal tables may identify different works. The archive preserves the full citation string by file, row, field group and table. The Handbook was not independently reread here.

## Limits of the historical record {#s-b801bfbe90}
EltonTraits offers a historical same-name, same-family comparison, not a complete species account. Matching name and family do not establish that its older species concept equals COL26.8. Food and foraging percentages are coarse, literature-based estimates rather than measurements of stomach contents, intake, energy or observation time. These selected bird fields are marked species-level; the cited Handbook was not independently reviewed here. Individual, seasonal and local variation, current distribution, population and conservation require additional evidence.
