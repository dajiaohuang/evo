## Overview {#s-65ddff4eda}
The articulated Eomaia scansoria holotype CAGS 01-IG-1a,b preserves skull, teeth and much of the postcranium, including feet and claws. Its anatomy was used in a basal-eutherian placement, but later combined matrices can place Eomaia outside Theria; the result depends on the sampled characters and analysis.

## Diet {#s-d54f71c37d}
Dentition supports a small-prey or faunivorous interpretation; feeding was not observed.

## Habitat {#s-7ca384c250}
The known occurrence is in the Yixian Formation; habitual microhabitat is not established by the lake deposits alone.

## Locomotion {#s-22134e7840}
Scansoriality is inferred from limb and foot proportions, not observed climbing.

## Body size {#s-d432410875}
A small mammal represented by one articulated skeleton; population variation is unknown.

## Ecological role {#s-a55f28d23d}
A small scansorial faunivore is an anatomical inference, not an observed guild.

## Preserved features {#s-05d4c7cb57}
Articulated skull and postcranial skeleton

## Preserved features {#s-f9969a2f9c}

## Preserved features {#s-a87d2ad476}
Therian placement changes across sampled phylogenetic matrices

## Evidence and scope {#s-a541a8321f}
The single articulated holotype supports detailed anatomy and the original basal-eutherian test. Later combined analyses can move Eomaia outside Theria; the 125.8–124.1 Ma interval is the sampled Yixian context, not a universal therian first appearance or direct specimen date.

## Limits of this account {#s-ee60f0b6b5}
The 125.8–124.1 Ma interval is the Yixian sample context, not a direct date on the holotype or a universal therian first appearance. Small-prey feeding and scansorial movement are anatomical interpretations; this single skeleton cannot establish population variation or a habitual microhabitat.
