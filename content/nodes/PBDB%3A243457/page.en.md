## Why is Strudiella's insect identity disputed? {#s-1964a5bb87}
The nearly complete Strud fossil was first described as a Late Devonian insect. A later re-examination rejected that identification, leaving its place among arthropods uncertain. The specimen preserves a small segmented body with antennae, legs and contested mouthpart evidence.

## atlas-profile / evidenceSummary {#s-b02dc281eb}
Garrouste and colleagues described Strudiella as an insect. Hörnschemeyer and colleagues later rejected that identification after re-examining the specimen. Its arthropod affinity remains contested, so it is not a secure insect calibration.

## What the specimen preserves {#s-808e7308cb}
The nearly complete, millimetre-scale specimen preserves a segmented body, antennae and legs. Its mouthparts helped support the original insect diagnosis, but the proposed orthopteroid and other insect characters were challenged when the same material was re-examined.

## Ecology follows a disputed reading {#s-699ec9a331}
The original study inferred a terrestrial animal and possible omnivory from its mandibles. No feeding event is preserved, and the later reassessment weakens ecological conclusions that depend on calling those structures insect-like.

## atlas-profile / readerLimitations / en {#s-a2a32058a8}
The record is one contested specimen from Upper Devonian freshwater deposits at Strud. The deposit alone does not establish its usual habitat or gait, and it cannot define a population-wide ecological guild or a genus-wide range.
