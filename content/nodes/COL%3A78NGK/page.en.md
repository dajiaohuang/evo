## Accepted name and placement {#s-78c9d6ba3a}
COL26.8 accepts Ptychadena amharensis Smith, Noonan & Colston, 2017 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Ptychadenidae (family) → Ptychadena (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## The Amhara Plateau sample {#s-a315c6dc11}
The revision recorded the species on Ethiopia’s Amhara Plateau at 1824–2642 m, with several localities north of the Blue Nile. It had not found the species south of the river. This survey boundary is retained without treating non-detection as proof of permanent absence.

## Calling in flooded grass fields {#s-56715fd28a}
Males were observed calling at night from flooded grass fields, sometimes in large aggregations. Other Ptychadena occurred at some sites. These calling and co-occurrence records do not establish clutch sizes, larval development or direct interspecific effects.

## A geographically sampled mitochondrial and nuclear dataset {#s-f81fc77c3f}
The 2023 Ethiopian Highlands study lists 33 mitochondrial samples and 15 nuclear-SNP samples for Ptychadena amharensis in Table 1, with mitochondrial nucleotide diversity π=0.32%. Samples across the twelve-species study were collected in 2011–2019; these are analysed specimens, not a population census. All assembled mitochondrial genomes had the usual two rRNAs, thirteen protein-coding genes and twenty-two tRNAs, but no species had a completely resolved control region because repeats could not be confidently assembled from short reads. The phylogenetic alignment used selected genes (13,937 bp across 303 samples), rather than an alignment of complete control regions.

## A study-defined highland clade and regional distribution {#s-68cb1c3178}
The paper places Ptychadena amharensis in its P. cooperi clade and reports it from the western side of the Great Rift Valley, north of the Blue Nile valley. These distributions combine the authors' geographic sampling and earlier range work; they are not independently updated complete range polygons. The twelve-species highland clade is a subset of Ptychadena. Its mitochondrial-clock analysis estimated a common ancestor around 11.7 million years ago, with a reported interval of 8.827–15.494 million years. The east-of-rift ancestral reconstruction had a model probability of 69%; neither that estimate nor inferred crossings is a directly observed migration event or a nuclear-genome divergence date for this species.

## A dated reassessment argument, with remaining demographic gaps {#s-abf7eafc64}
The 2023 discussion calls for conservation assessment or reassessment using revised species concepts and genetic and geographic evidence. Earlier names and broad distributions can combine lineages that later work separated. The present genomic sample does not itself measure current abundance, fragmentation, decline or reproductive success in Ptychadena amharensis.
