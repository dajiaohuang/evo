## Read this historical record through food and foraging position {#s-a9b1e0321f}
In pinned COL26.8, *Colaptes campestris* is an accepted member of Picidae, the woodpecker family. EltonTraits 1.0 has an older record with the same name and family, which is the comparison used here. Its taxonomy follows an earlier bird or MSW3 mammal framework; the name match cannot resolve later splits or synonymies.

## Relative weights of food categories {#s-adbe30adba}
The nonzero food weights in the archive are invertebrates 90%, and mammals and birds 10%. Source code A marks high compiler confidence in the source and diet information. The percentages standardize verbal descriptions; they are not direct measurements of diet, and a zero does not show that a food is never eaten. Categories such as Nect, Seed and PlantO are broader than their everyday names and do not mean an animal eats only nectar, seeds or plants.

## Foraging strata differ from whole-day activity {#s-1f9db3be2d}
The archive marks its foraging-position record as species-level and assigns ground 100%. This is a relative estimate of where feeding occurs, not a nest-site description, a map of all habitat or a whole-day time budget. The source uses separate definitions for surface-water, understory, mid/high, canopy and aerial positions. They are not elevation ranges and cannot be converted into flight heights or habitat area.

## Life-history evidence limits {#s-fe30623e52}
This is a guide to an older same-name, same-family foraging record in EltonTraits 1.0, not a complete monograph. Matching names and classification do not establish that the historical species concept equals COL26.8. Food and bird foraging-position percentages are coarse relative weights standardized from literature descriptions, not direct measurements of stomach contents, intake, energy or observation time. Bird diet codes A and B retain uncertainty in their sources and proportions. Mammal ABC diet or activity codes may use information about a congener that compilers judged ecologically similar; this page does not claim that every field comes from a direct observation of this species. Genus- or family-level typical values, bird positions not marked species-level and split records awaiting individual review do not support definite conclusions here. The underlying books and papers have not been individually reread. Other sources are needed for individual, seasonal and local variation, current range, population and conservation.
