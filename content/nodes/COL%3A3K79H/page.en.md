## Helichrysum sessile {#s-4142515716}
## Cushion growth and a thick white leaf covering

Hilliard's 1983 flora account describes a compact cushion-forming dwarf shrub 40–100 mm tall. Its old main stems are woody and gnarled, while the densely tufted branches carry dead, dry leaves below and closely overlapping leaves above. The narrow elliptic or oblong leaves measure about 5–40 by 1–4 mm, with tips from nearly sharp to blunt. Both surfaces have a thick white covering that feels silky, dries like crumpled tissue paper and breaks down into felt with age.

Bell-shaped flower heads measure about 5–7 by 8–12 mm across their radiating bracts. They grow alone or in groups of 2–12 on leafy stalks up to 100 mm long. The bracts form four to six loose overlapping rows, with woolly bases, silvery translucent surfaces that may be crimson above the base, and opaque milk-white, triangular tips. Each head has a scarcely honeycombed receptacle and 20–38 yellow florets. The 1.5 mm dry fruits bear elongated, paired mucilage-producing hairs; many rough pappus bristles are about as long as the corolla, with thickened tips and bases that cohere by spreading cilia.

The source also notes that heads vary: small crowded heads have been described as *H. ernestianum*, while larger heads may be solitary or few together. Stalk length responds to environmental conditions. Hilliard distinguishes *H. sessile* from *H. sessilioides* by the thick white silky covering on both leaf faces; the latter has a thin tissue-paper-like covering above and white felt below. Small cushions are recorded in cracks and pockets of weathered rock faces.
