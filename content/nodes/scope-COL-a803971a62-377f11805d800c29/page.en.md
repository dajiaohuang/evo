## A shell interior, not an animal in full {#s-740f12c93f}
The cited study examines more than fifty Pojetaia specimens, including shell sections from South Australia. Those sections preserve a layered inner fabric that the authors compare with foliated aragonite. They discuss how the material bears on the history of nacre, but do not claim undisputed Cambrian nacre in these specimens. Shell sections reveal structure; they do not preserve this animal's feeding or movement.

## A regional sample with a bounded age {#s-dae7e721ed}
The paper places the sampled shells in the Ajax Limestone and the lowermost Erkeket Formation of South Australia. Their correlations are described as probably Botomian and lowermost Botomian, respectively. Using the Cambrian Stage 4 boundaries in the International Chronostratigraphic Chart v2026/06 gives a 514.5–506.5-million-year sample envelope. It is a dated window for the cited material, not a complete global first or last appearance for the species or genus.

## What the shells cannot establish {#s-b4f6b16a1b}
The shell material supports a species-level account of the studied specimens and a comparison of their mineralized layers. Isolated sections do not establish whole-animal size, diet, locomotion or ecological role. The higher evolutionary position of early bivalves and any relationship to later nacre remain comparative questions, not evidence that Pojetaia was a direct ancestor of living bivalves.
