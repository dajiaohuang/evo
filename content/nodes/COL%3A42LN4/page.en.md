## A historical life-history entry {#s-690a11455c}
COL26.8 accepts Micaelamys namaquensis in the family Muridae. The August 2015 life-history archive has a same-name, same-family entry with six cited fields. Because the compilation can inherit data under historical taxonomy, the matching name does not prove that every value describes the species as classified today.

## Adult and newborn mass describe different stages {#s-7419ea5e84}
The archive reports an adult body-mass summary of 57.3 g and a birth-or-hatching mass of 2.5 g. The adult field can combine males, females and animals of unspecified sex; the second value belongs to a different life stage. They do not follow the same individuals through growth or define a range of individual masses.

## Litter size, gestation and transition from milk {#s-a2053c67f7}
The archive reports 3.75 offspring or eggs per litter or clutch, 21.99 days from conception or fertilization to birth, and 24.35 days for the age associated with dependence on food other than maternal milk. The first field combines offspring and egg counts, so its fractional summary is not a literal fraction of an animal. The two time values describe different events and do not define one uniform reproductive cycle.

## The longest record is not a typical lifespan {#s-0928034177}
The archive reports 4.7 years for maximum_longevity_y, using the largest record in its collected material. This is not a typical lifespan, a mean wild lifespan, a survival limit for every individual or evidence that the same record is currently reproducible.

## Following the life-history source labels {#s-9b3f24feb9}
The same-name rows carry labels including Jones, Bielby, Cardillo and colleagues (2009), de Magalhaes and Costa (2009), Ernest and colleagues (2003), and the BC mammals compilation citing Savage and colleagues (2004). The archive retains row locators and labels by field. A label may point to a paper, book or another database; rows are not counts of measured animals, and repeated citations are not independent experiments.

## A separate record of foraging position and activity {#s-514e4d9f9b}
EltonTraits 1.0 has a historical same-name, same-family entry with fields for foraging position and activity. Its diet group is withheld under source code D2 because of uncertainty, interpolation or source-link limits. The older taxonomic match does not resolve later splits or synonym disputes.

## A broad foraging-position category {#s-cda891f4c0}
The entry assigns the species to G, the ground category; the source definition also includes some aquatic foraging. Code A records compiler confidence in the source and assignment. Archive metadata attributes mammalian stratum labels collectively to Nowak (1999). This is a broad category, not a measure of time spent there, and it does not mean the animal uses no other position.

## An activity label, not a daily schedule {#s-0b249de75d}
EltonTraits marks nocturnal activity as 1. Several activity fields can be 1 at once, so this code is not a share of a 24-hour budget or a schedule guaranteed across days, seasons and sites. Its ABC confidence code can include a related species in the same genus; no genus- or family-level typical value is used to fill this entry.

## Following the EltonTraits source labels {#s-a26a683085}
The foraging and activity fields carry the labels metadata:Nowak1999 and Ref_1. Different field groups may come from different material, and the same Ref number in bird and mammal tables does not necessarily identify the same work. Original labels are retained by file, row and field group. The underlying books and papers have not been individually read, so these labels are trace information rather than additional observations.

## Life-history evidence limits {#s-de90f00390}
This page combines two historical sources. The 2015 life-history archive reports compilation summaries that may inherit data across historical species or subspecies; the values are not individual measurements, and the underlying publications have not been individually reviewed. EltonTraits 1.0 converts older literature descriptions into categorical fields, which may include material from related species. Its diet fields are withheld here under code D2 because of uncertainty, interpolation or unresolved source links; they are not used to infer a diet. Neither historical name match establishes equivalence with COL26.8 or provides a complete account of current ecology, range, population or conservation.
