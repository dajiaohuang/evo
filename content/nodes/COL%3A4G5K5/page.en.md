## High-elevation forest perches with an imprecise lower boundary {#s-9e596426d1}
The 2016 account described calling from varied perches in primary forest above “700 or 800” metres and cited records from eastern to western Mindanao mountains. The alternative lower values are retained without choosing a precise physiological threshold. The authors interpreted the frog as a higher-elevation original-forest specialist, but naturally discontinuous montane occurrence was not equated with severely fragmented habitat. These are survey and interpretation statements, not a full contemporary range or an experimentally measured habitat requirement.

## A negative chytrid result does not establish immunity {#s-21810b0f65}
At one high-elevation Mt. Lumot site, the authors found substantial chytrid infection in other treefrogs but reported P. poecilius as negative. This site-specific negative observation does not establish species-wide absence of infection, resistance or future protection; the paragraph supplies no sample denominator. The authors withheld a conservation-status downgrade because available data suggested restricted original-forest habitat. This is their 2016 assessment reasoning, not a verified current official category.
