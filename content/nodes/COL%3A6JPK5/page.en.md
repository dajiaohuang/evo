## Accepted name and placement {#s-542dc11035}
COL26.8 accepts Fritziana ohausi (Wandolleck, 1907) as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Hemiphractidae (family) → Fritziana (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## Development and egg-laying categories in the study {#s-7a083fed80}
The Atlantic Forest trait compilation classifies development as larval and oviposition (egg-laying environment) as terrestrial. These are literature-compiled categories, not observations from a new breeding or developmental study. The larval code does not give the duration of the larval stage, all larval habitats or dependence on standing water. The source taxonomy has not been independently reconciled with the COL26.8 concept.

## Separate codes for juvenile habitat and egg site {#s-2fd1fac21b}
The same row codes juvenile habitat as aquatic and selects “eggs carried by terrestrial adults” from seven oviposition-site categories. These labels describe juvenile habitat and a broad egg-site mode; they do not establish an adult habitat preference, nest structure, substrate species or egg-carrying height. The record does not add evidence about clutch size, egg dimensions, parental care, breeding season or offspring survival.

## Length values include estimates and unit uncertainty {#s-a8686efe11}
The workbook row marks head width and tibia length as inferred rather than newly measured on a specimen. The article estimates missing traits with a phylogenetic maximum-likelihood method, a statistical procedure that uses inferred evolutionary relationships; across its full pool, it inferred 139 head widths and 146 tibia lengths. The row also includes numeric body-length, head-width and tibia-length values. The Methods use means when available and otherwise maxima, but workbook length headers do not explicitly state units and sex or life stage is not consistently identified. These cells cannot be treated as diagnostic millimetre measurements, a sex-specific range or measured variation across the species. The upstream measurements have not been independently checked.
