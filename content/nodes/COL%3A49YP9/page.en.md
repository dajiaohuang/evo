## Subterranean stems and compact, red-marked flowers {#s-0d6a56ae47}
The regional description records stems 30–150 mm long and 3–5 mm thick, often partly underground or beneath stones, then thicker after emergence. Some stems spread as thin runners underground for 80 mm or more. Exposed portions may become nearly black; stems are usually darker brownish green, with less distinct tubercles than O. arcuata. Stems are otherwise difficult to distinguish, while the flowers are described as markedly different.

Flowers arise in small inflorescences near stem bases, with as many as four clusters around a stem, especially on young, short stems. Underground peduncles are slender, white and knobbly, up to 10 mm; aboveground peduncles are rarely longer than 1 mm. Underground pedicels reach 6 mm, compared with about 1.5 mm for many aboveground flowers. Flowers developing below the soil reach the surface with a cage formed by their lobes projecting above ground; sand and leaf litter may partly fill them. Sepals are about 1 mm long, 0.25–0.50 mm wide at the base, translucent white, ovate-acuminate and papillate. Glabrous, ascending pedicels are 1.5–6 mm long and 0.50–0.75 mm thick.

Flowers are bud-like, broadly elliptic to nearly spherical or depressed-globose, 3.5–7.0 mm long and 4–6 mm broad, usually widest just beyond the tube mouth. The white exterior is pale-red-speckled above, with markings coalescing on the lobes; it is glabrous apart from small, obtuse conical papillae toward the upper parts. Inside, a smooth white surface has bold irregular deep-red spots on the lobes and three concentric deep-red rings in the tube. Stiff, erect, cylindrical white to red hairs reach 1 mm in the tube and about halfway along the lobes. The shallow bowl-shaped tube is 1.5–2.0 mm long and 3.0–4.5 mm broad, not thickened at the mouth. Deltate, acuminate lobes are 3–5 mm long and 2.0–3.3 mm wide at the base; they ascend, then become horizontal or descend toward tips that remain fused. The account notes that each exterior papilla is a raised stoma.

The bright-yellow corona is about 1.2 mm high and 1.5–2.5 mm broad, on a short cylindrical stipe about 0.5 mm long; red flecks may mark the backs of the inner lobes. Outer lobes are about 0.5 mm long, ascending and lanceolate with obtuse to acuminate tips; they usually exceed the inner lobes’ dorsal projection and join laterally to a flat, horizontally spreading projection divided into four or five obtuse teeth. Inner lobes are about 0.25 mm long, deltoid to nearly square, notched, pressed against the backs of the anthers and usually shorter than the anthers. The outer corona is described as unlike O. arcuata, with slender lobes beneath the guide rails and thin horizontal projections rather than solid bosses.

The habitat account contrasts these sites with the flat, low-lying habitat of O. arcuata. It places O. willowmorensis on steep, north-facing sandstone slopes, often under small bushes in rock-slab crevices, grass tufts, clumps of Dolichothrix ericoides or other rocky shallow soils. The sites are described as succulent-rich and surrounded at higher levels by arid fynbos; the account also notes occurrences with Euphorbia polygona. Plants may remain hidden in leaf litter, with only occasional stem sections exposed. These regional observations do not establish the full range or the frequency of these conditions.
