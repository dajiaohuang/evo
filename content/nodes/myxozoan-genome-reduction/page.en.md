## Tiny parasites in the cnidarian tree {#s-b38419d6f4}
A 77-taxon, 51,940-amino-acid analysis places sampled Myxozoa within Cnidaria as sister to Polypodium and links their miniature parasitic bodies with extreme genome and gene-content reduction. This is a comparative result from sampled species, not a complete reconstruction of every myxozoan ancestor.

## A surprising place on the animal tree {#s-9a9bc93251}
The study combined four new myxozoan assemblies with Kudoa, Myxobolus and Polypodium data in a 77-taxon amino-acid matrix. The sampled myxozoans fall within Cnidaria, as sister to Polypodium. Genome-size estimates and gene-category comparisons associate their microscopic parasitic body plans with extensive genomic reduction.

## Reduction is reconstructed comparatively {#s-66f38c81e8}
The study infers gene loss and parasite adaptation by comparing living samples and annotations. Polar capsules and cnidarian nematocysts are treated as homologous structures, but this does not make their life histories identical. The result supports placement and reduction in the sampled lineages; it does not document every step in an ancient transition.
