## A2021 description from the Teles Pires region {#s-02f0204f32}
Santana and colleagues described Proceratophrys korekore in 2021 using morphology, calls and mitochondrial evidence. The male holotype ZUFMS-AMP08100 was collected on 8 November 2016 at Jacareacanga, Pará, Brazil (09°19′1.00″ S, 56°46′35.76″ W; 200 m; SAD69). The paper cites earlier concavitympanum usage only in part; it does not equate all historical records with korekore. The epithet means frog in the Munduruku language. Proposed Portuguese names are sapo-korekore and sapo-de-chifre-dos-mundurukus.

## A combination of diagnostic traits {#s-a89c7a462e}
The diagnosis combines small, pointed, fused warts at the upper eyelid border; an eye-diameter/eye–nostril-distance ratio of 1.1–1.3; and one forearm tubercle row of unequal sizes with sparse surrounding tubercles. Korekore lacks a developed long palpebral or rostral appendage and postocular swellings. The holotype has vomerine teeth in two short rows and no hand interdigital webbing. These reported characters and taxonomic comparisons are not independently authenticated histology or proof that one character identifies every age and population.

## Preserved colour andindividual wart variation {#s-8c72318364}
The preserved holotype has a brown dorsum with a lighter area inside the ocular-dorsal wart ridge, bordered by four dark triangular marks on each side. Its cream ventral surface has dark mottling and a darker throat. Live and preserved type-series backgrounds vary from light to dark brown, and some individuals lack a complete ocular-dorsal ridge. These variations do not support an invariant colour pattern or universally complete ridge. Captions were read; figure pixels have not been independently inspected.

## Eight calls fromone recordedmale {#s-eded13de9e}
The analyzed recording is paratype ZUFMS-AMP13680 at the type locality, around 20:00 h, at 24.3°C air temperature and 86% humidity, recorded as 44.1 kHz/16-bit WAV and archived as MAP-V 329. Eight calls contain a single pulsed note lasting 0.162–0.332 s (mean 0.251±0.053), with 96.4–111.1 pulses/s (mean 103.9±5.9) and dominant frequency 861.3 Hz without observed variation. These are repeated observations of one male. The frequency result is limited by that sample; raw sound and the archived recording have not been independently reanalyzed.

## A421bp mitochondrial analysis {#s-bcb1dbbcac}
The authors sequenced 16S fragments from three new-species individuals and four strussmannae from its type locality, assembling a 421 bp alignment with other GenBank sequences. BEAST under HKY+I+G ran for 50 million generations; the paper reports ESS above 200. Results places korekore sister to concavitympanum within a supported clade, but deeper nodes have low support. This is one mitochondrial locus, not a validated genome-wide species tree or proof of no introgression. Figure 6’s million-year scale alone does not establish an independently reviewed calibrated divergence date.

## Two banks oftheTeles PiresRiver {#s-8bd0eedbb4}
The 2021 source restricts known occurrence to Jacareacanga, Pará and a few sites across the Teles Pires River at Paranaíta, Mato Grosso. It reports dense ombrophilous forest on both banks. Rainy-season surveys from 18:00–21:00 h, October–June, found males calling from temporary-stream leaf litter and occasionally bare soil near water. Other individuals were observed by day and night or trapped far from water. These records do not establish absence of calling at other times, exclusively aquatic habitat, larval development or thermal tolerances.

## Local threats andsympatry aredated observations {#s-d188078b4d}
The source names Ameerega munduruku, Ceratophrys cornuta and Lithodytes lineatus as sympatric and reports logging, cattle-related clearing, illegal gold mining and hydroelectric fragmentation in the region. Mercury use and fish poisoning are discussed with attributed literature, not a chemical assay of korekore. These dated threats are not a current IUCN category, measured population decline, diet study or proof of interactions among sympatric species. The authors request more surveys and integrative taxonomy.
