## Twelve dinosaur evidence sets without an ancestor ladder {#s-cc5a342753}
Compare named fossils, anatomical matrices, growth evidence, traces and mechanical models. Each supports a different kind of conclusion; together they do not make a simple ladder of direct ancestors.

## Two matrices test different dinosaur roots {#s-a4de6304bf}
Baron et al. analysed 457 characters and recovered Ornithoscelida. Langer et al. added taxa and rescored characters, recovering the traditional split instead. These are competing tests of character patterns, not observations of a branching event.

## Teeth inform an ancestral-diet model {#s-ccb4d14b56}
ULBRA-PVT280 preserves recurved, serrated teeth. A model places faunivory—feeding on animals—near the sauropodomorph root. The teeth are direct evidence; the ancestral diet is a reconstruction, not a feeding ancestor found in the rock.

## A partial skeleton does not set a global first {#s-049abe889a}
SAM-PK-K8025 is a partial skeleton that informs an early ornithischian branch. Its matrix placement and the age of its formation constrain this specimen’s context, but do not establish the group’s first appearance worldwide.

## One species changes stance as it grows {#s-efe2a7d7b4}
Digital models of Mussaurus combine specimens at several growth stages. They support a shift from bipedal to quadrupedal posture during growth in this species; the modelled change is not an evolutionary sequence between species.

## A giant with flexed limbs {#s-995a7855dc}
BP/1/7120 supports an adult giant whose limbs were flexed. The estimate of about twelve tonnes and the interpretation of habitual quadrupedality come from comparisons with a partial skeleton, not a direct measurement of a complete animal.

## Preserved armour and inferred covering {#s-1374931cf6}
NHMUK R1111 preserves associated osteoderms, the bony plates in the skin. Some armour is missing and a keratin covering is inferred. Its position on the ankylosaur stem comes from character analysis, separate from what the specimen preserves.

## A complete skeleton tests a character combination {#s-1d9187f660}
IVPP V14530 preserves a nearly complete early ceratopsian skeleton. Its proposed place in the dinosaur tree is the result of a character-matrix analysis, not evidence that it was a direct ancestor of later horned dinosaurs.

## Skin and feeding marks constrain one carcass story {#s-b643038f61}
NDGS 2000 preserves skin impressions and traces interpreted as feeding marks. The authors propose that scavenging helped desiccate this carcass. That taphonomic pathway explains one fossil’s preservation; it is not a general rule for dinosaur carcasses.

## Filaments do not settle flight or function {#s-7a8316d767}
Three Yutyrannus skeletons preserve filamentous integument. The fossils document those structures, but not their original function, full-body coverage or a flight capability. Those questions are separate from the animal’s placement relative to birds.

## A pose supports, but does not show, brooding {#s-8ff1c3395f}
IGM 100/979 lies over a clutch in a bird-like posture. The association strongly supports a brooding interpretation, but behaviour is inferred from the fossil position rather than directly observed.

## Growth marks feed a fitted model {#s-9d621adf1f}
Growth marks in tyrannosaurid bones are observations. The ages and masses assigned to individuals, and the fitted logistic growth rates, are outputs of a model built from those marks.

## Traces show bone consumption; models estimate force {#s-c1a46dd804}
MOR 799 trace fossils and a coprolite—a fossilized dropping—containing bone support osteophagy, or bone eating. Bite force and pressure estimates come from a biomechanical model of seven skulls; they are not direct measurements of every tyrannosaur.
