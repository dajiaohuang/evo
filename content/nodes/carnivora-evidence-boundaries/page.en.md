## Eleven dossiers across carnivoran origins, form and function {#s-8c8f67cef3}
Follow named teeth, skulls, skeletons, sequence matrices and digital experiments without converting separate branches into an ancestor ladder.

## Teeth and ankle bones expand the original sample {#s-a85de152ca}
IRSNB M2095–M2099 underpin a reconstruction of the permanent dentition. Separate ankle and hindfoot bones include astragali IRSNB M2107–M2108 and heel bone IRSNB M2109.

## A character matrix tests the traditional “miacoid” grouping {#s-b63039893b}
The study scores 99 cranial and dental characters for 40 sampled taxa. In its preferred trees, traditional “Miacidae” and Viverravidae fall outside crown Carnivora rather than forming two tidy stems within it.

## A cranium and associated bones test Caniformia {#s-e3fba9c8b6}
Holotype UCMP 85202 preserves the cranium and teeth. Associated paratypes SDSNH 107446 and SDSNH 107447 add bones from the rest of the skeleton to the study of early Caniformia.

## Six gene regions sample living carnivorans {#s-eeb92f5172}
The combined sequence matrix contains 6,243 DNA base pairs from TR-i-I, TBG, IRBP, ND2, CYTB and 12S rRNA. It samples 76 living species; the resulting branches depend on those taxa and gene regions.

## Texas skull and jaw material is re-examined {#s-dc9a99ad10}
The study CT-reconstructs the Gustafsonia cognita holotype cranium, TMM 40209-200. Angelarctocyon australis is represented by specimen TMM 41850-1 and referred jaw material.

## A digital skull tests simulated biting loads {#s-7fb248d772}
The complete young-adult holotype skull B-4071 is tested under simulated canine, p4 and m1 bites, as well as pull-back and head-shaking loads. The 2026 study deposits its meshes and scripts on Zenodo; the force patterns are model results.

## Three dental specimens are compared in the panda-affinity analysis {#s-77410e6498}
The sample includes left upper fourth premolar MNCN-CSIC NV-2-42, right upper first molar NV-2-40, and partial mandible IPS 46473 with canine-to-second-molar teeth and an associated fourth premolar. These specimens form the anatomical evidence considered in the panda-affinity analysis.

## Three localities locate the sampled fossils {#s-be3575f9ea}
The UCMP and SDSNH specimens come from member C of the Santiago Formation in San Diego County. The map marks the study localities; it does not show a continent-wide range.

## Two matrices resolve different branches {#s-77c9d4d60e}
The 50-taxon analysis leaves some close relationships unresolved. In the Paleogene-only consensus, Lycophocyon falls on the caniform branch outside crown Canoidea; that placement is specific to the sampled analysis.

## A classifier estimates diet from living reference species {#s-c5f8295e4f}
Using six variables and 82 living reference species—46 carnivores, 21 omnivores or hard-object feeders, and 15 insectivores—the classifier assigned the fossil holotype an 83% posterior probability of carnivory. This is a model-based classification, not an observed meal.

## Three dental specimens come from two Spanish basins {#s-01378cd426}
The Nombrevilla 2 basin contains the fourth-premolar holotype and first-molar paratype. The partial mandible and associated fourth premolar come from ACM/C6-Camí.

## The tree places Kretzoiarctos but leaves branches unresolved {#s-4a953239cc}
The combined fossil-and-living matrix places Kretzoiarctos within Ailuropodinae. Its majority-rule bootstrap leaves relationships among several ailuropodine branches unresolved.

## The teeth and jaw remain separate specimen records {#s-307c9a8caa}
Figure 1 shows the left fourth premolar NV-2-42, right first molar NV-2-40 and partial mandible IPS 46473. The route begins with these preserved specimens before considering dietary interpretations.

## Simulated loads compare skull mechanics, not behaviour {#s-4907682af8}
Finite-element analysis compares canine, p4 and m1 bite positions with standardized external loads. The reported stress and force patterns are simulated results under the study's stated constraints.

## A Tibetan skull and fragments enter the combined sample {#s-0f301c1352}
Adult partial cranium IVPP V18788.1 is the holotype; IVPP V18788.2 is a jaw-ramus fragment and IVPP V18788.3 a partial premaxilla. The original magnetostratigraphic framework places the locality sample between 5.95 and 4.10 Ma.

## Inner-ear shape is compared with inferred locomotion {#s-03ed7219b9}
The 36-specimen sample includes Field Museum skulls of Hesperocyon gregarius and Aelurodon. Researchers segmented the left inner-ear bony labyrinths and compared their shapes with PCA, CVA and MANOVA; these analyses test functional patterns rather than preserve a hunt.

## A lake-deposit skeleton preserves much of the body {#s-9f8126377f}
Holotype NUFV 405 preserves about 65% of a skeleton, including skull, teeth, vertebrae, and substantial fore- and hind limbs. It comes from an early Miocene lake deposit; the setting alone does not establish how the animal lived.

## A stem-pinniped skeleton supplies limbs for comparison {#s-69d68f45c3}
LACM 4321 preserves a skull and much of the rest of the skeleton. Its limb proportions and joint shapes can be compared with living pinnipeds and land-dwelling carnivorans; comparison does not directly record its swimming behaviour.
