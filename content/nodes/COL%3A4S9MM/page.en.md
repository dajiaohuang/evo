## Overview {#s-1ba121a1b9}
This page follows the COL26.8 accepted name Rhinopithecus brelichi. The English common name comes from the locally exact-matched MDD v2.5 record; the Chinese rendering is editorial. The selected studies concern social organization, elevational activity and genetic samples at Fanjingshan, Guizhou; they are not a synchronized multi-site population survey.

## Research observations {#s-fdab4ea43f}
A field project begun in 1979 included ten months of intensive observation in 1991. The study described family groups typically containing 5–10 monkeys centered on one adult male; several groups could travel in large, loose bands and occasionally aggregate into groups of more than 400. These are early Fanjingshan observations, not current social structure or abundance.

## Research observations {#s-5902503cee}
From September 2011 to August 2013, researchers at Fanjingshan National Nature Reserve recorded activity between 1,432 and 2,100 m and documented feeding on 104 plant species. Autumn and winter use spanned a broader elevational band, while activity from April to September was more concentrated at 1,700–1,900 m. These seasonal patterns apply to that site and observation period.

## Genetic evidence {#s-b280c8ed2c}
A mitochondrial control-region study analyzed 141 fecal samples collected from wild monkeys and reported five haplotypes. This describes one genetic marker and sample set; it cannot by itself establish current abundance, population health or adaptive capacity.

Historical summary values are adult body mass 14500 g. Adult mass can combine males, females and adults of unspecified sex and does not provide a species-wide range of individual masses. Birth/hatching mass is not added to this account here.

## Reproduction and developmental timing {#s-b776e5aebe}
Available compilation fields are offspring per litter or eggs per clutch 1 offspring/eggs; conception or fertilization to birth 199.67 d; d denotes days. The litter/clutch field combines offspring or egg counts, and a fractional summary does not mean a single event produced a fractional individual. Timing fields refer to different events and cannot be exchanged for one uniform reproductive cycle.

## A longest record differs from usual lifespan {#s-225d94e95b}
The archive’s maximum_longevity_y value is 29.5 years, taking the largest record in the collected material under the source method. It is not typical lifespan, mean wild lifespan, a survival ceiling for every individual or a current repeatedly attainable record.

## Evidence limits {#s-70250ddd28}
The selected behavioral and elevational studies are from one reserve, and the genetic result comes from one sample set and one mitochondrial marker. Early findings are not generalized to range-wide patterns, current abundance or a complete conservation assessment.
The 2015 archive compiles historical species- and subspecies-level life-history records: non-longevity values are medians or interpolated medians, while maximum_longevity_y is the largest compiled record. Historical taxonomic inheritance means a same-name match does not establish equivalence to the pinned COL26.8 concept; these records do not yield a uniform wild-only or captive-only estimate. EltonTraits 1.0 converts literature descriptions into diet weights and categorical activity/foraging fields; mammalian ABC fields may draw on congeners, so they are not direct species-level observations in every field. Neither archive establishes current distribution or conservation status.
