## What does the historical account describe about the tree and its flowers? {#s-41be89a747}
The historical Flora of Panama account describes a shrub or small tree 3–10 m tall, with smooth bark and branches covered in whitish woolly and long hairs. The petioles are 1–1.5 cm long. Two very unequal stipules occur at each leaf: one is threadlike and 1–1.2 cm long; the other is nearly stalkless, shield-shaped, almost round and up to 4.5 cm across. The oblong to obovate-oblong leaf blades are somewhat asymmetric, slightly heart-shaped at the base and tapering at the tip; they are 10–20 by 3–8 cm, membranous, irregularly toothed, dark green and sparsely hairy above, whitish-woolly beneath, with five to seven main veins. Flower stalks are 4.5–5 cm long. The triangular, tapering sepals are 1–1.6 cm long before their appendages; each edge bears four or five threadlike appendages 0.7–1 cm long, plus a 1–1.2 cm appendage at the tip. Thin, bright yellow obovate petals are 3–4 cm long and hairless. The unequal staminal filaments are 0.3–0.7 cm long; the oblong anthers are 1.2–1.5 mm. The ovary has dense woolly, cobweb-like hairs near its tip. The thick style is about 0.8 cm long and expands at the base; the stigma has five teeth. The hairy, cobweb-like berry is transversely elliptic, about 1 by 1.5–1.8 cm; seeds are about 0.4–0.5 mm. This is a historical regional description without a stated population sample or observation date.
