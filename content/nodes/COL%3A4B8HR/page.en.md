## A historical EltonTraits record {#s-9c87709339}
COL26.8 accepts Otus magicus in Strigidae. EltonTraits 1.0 contains an older record under the same name and family, with 2 ecological field groups. The historical taxonomy may differ from COL26.8; a name match does not settle later splits or synonym disputes.

## Relative weights for food categories {#s-20953cc3bc}
The archive assigns 90% of its nonzero food-category weight to invertebrates and 10% to unspecified vertebrates. Code A records high compiler confidence in the source and diet information. These percentages standardize verbal descriptions; they do not measure actual diet composition, and a zero would not prove that the owl never eats an item. Labels such as Nect, Seed and PlantO cover broader categories than their everyday wording and do not mean exclusive feeding on nectar, seeds or plants.

## Relative use of four feeding positions {#s-3dc73e4c07}
The species-level foraging weights are 40% for the ground, 20% for understory, edges or shrubs below 2 m, 20% for trees or tall bushes from 2 m upward but below the canopy, and 20% in or just above the canopy. These estimate relative use of feeding positions, not nest sites, all habitat or a whole-day time budget. The source defines surface-water, understory, mid/high, canopy and aerial categories separately; they are not elevation bands and cannot be converted into flight heights or habitat area.

## Follow the citation labels {#s-204a2accee}
Both EltonTraits groups carry Ref_5 in the bird-source table, which identifies Handbook of the Birds of the World, volume 5 (1992–2011). Diet, foraging and activity may cite different material, and the same Ref number in bird and mammal tables need not identify the same source. The archive retains file, row, field-group and reference-table labels for tracing. The cited volume was not read here; a source label is not another observation.

## Life-history evidence limits {#s-e01cd08272}
EltonTraits 1.0 is a historical same-name, same-family record, not a complete species account or proof of equivalence with COL26.8. Its diet and foraging percentages standardize literature descriptions; they are not direct measures of stomach contents, intake, energy or observation time. Code A reflects compiler confidence in the source and diet information, not direct measurement. Elsewhere in the archive, avian A/B diet codes retain source and proportional uncertainty, while mammalian ABC diet or activity codes may borrow from a congener considered ecologically similar. Genus- or family-level values, non-species-level strata and unresolved split records do not establish traits for this species. The underlying books and papers were not individually read. Individual, seasonal and local variation, current range, population and conservation status need other sources.
