## BXGM V0011 becomes referred material in the revision {#s-a70f6569a0}
The fixed PBDB name Huaxiapterus benxiensis retains the older named record. The 2023 study finds its holotype BXGM V0011 insufficiently distinct from H. corollatus and treats the older name as a junior synonym of Huaxiadraco corollatus. The added account follows this specimen’s preservation and development without counting two catalogue names as two independent biological species.

## A nearly complete body retains damaged trunk and humeral details {#s-17d4ce4592}
BXGM V0011 is nearly complete, but post-cervical vertebrae, sternum, ribs and shoulder girdle are damaged or obscured, and both humeri are incomplete. Surface damage limits cranial detail and posterior skull bones remain unfused. Near-completeness does not mean every bone is intact, and a collection-level soft-tissue label does not establish preservation of the entire membrane in this specimen.

## Rostral deflection, nasal process and short wing elements support comparison {#s-b1101d3c0f}
The revision describes roughly 20° rostral deflection, a forward-inclined descending nasal process and a tall piriform orbit, with metacarpal one about 40% the length of metacarpal four and wing phalanx four about 20% of phalanx one. The broken crest still appears trapezoidal, matching the corollatus combination. Larger crest size or a longer posterior spine does not establish a separate species, and proportions retain preservation and measurement limits.

## Reassessment of an incomplete humerus changes the reported proportion {#s-3b49ab067b}
The original description gives a humerus only about 55% of femoral length. The 2023 authors attribute that value to an incomplete right humerus and identify damaged ends of the left humerus, giving a comparison near 80%. This illustrates how damage can create an anomalous proportion. The reassessment is cited without independent measurement or image inspection here; the older anomaly does not establish distinctive flight ability.

## More extensive fusion indicates relative maturity {#s-18670be8ba}
Fused humeral epiphyses, carpals, first wing-phalanx extensor tendon process and tibiotarsus contrast with still-unfused posterior skull bones; damage prevents assessment of scapula/coracoid fusion. The authors infer an advanced subadult with a specimen wingspan estimate near 1.6 m. Within-group fusion comparison does not establish exact age, sex or maximum adult size.
