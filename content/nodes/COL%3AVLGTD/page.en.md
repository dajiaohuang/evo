## Pectis elongata {#s-cd854f61b8}
## Oil-dotted leaves and small radiate heads

The historical *Flora of Panama* account describes an upright herb 5–100 cm tall, usually annual but sometimes apparently perennial. Its stem is round or slightly angled, often purplish, and usually branches above. Narrow, linear to oblanceolate leaves have bristles along the base and scattered round oil glands beneath.

Small radiate heads occur singly or in cymose clusters, on short to elongated stalks. Each narrow, cylindrical-to-bell-shaped involucre has five bracts. Five yellow ray florets have 3–7 mm corollas that may become reddish; the 5–9 central disc florets have 2–4 mm, two-lipped corollas. Black dry fruits are 2–3 mm long and bear short stiff hairs. Their pappus has 2–20 rough, bristly awns, each 2.5–4 mm long. This is a regional historical description, not a global account.
