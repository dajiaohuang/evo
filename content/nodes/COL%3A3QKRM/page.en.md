## *Jamesbrittenia burkeana* {#s-e1b9da4358}
The account describes a subshrub with stems 0.3–1.5 m long, erect or straggling. Leaves usually have one to three pairs of prominent teeth and a folded, sickle-shaped blade; the upper surface is gland-dotted, while the lower bears glandular bristly hairs and shiny glands. Racemes have few flowers. Corolla lobes are dark and thick-textured, with pale, thin margins; the thick tissue is only proposed to be scent-producing tissue.

The diagnostic account notes that lower-stem leaves can instead be jaggedly toothed, lobed, and flat. It contrasts *J. accrescens* by its usually many-flowered racemes and different leaf and corolla glands; the two are partly sympatric and may hybridize where they meet. It also contrasts the white, thin-textured corolla of *J. albobadia*. No collector reported scent for *J. burkeana*; possible night scent is presented as speculation, not observation.

The account records rocky grassy slopes in open woodland or scrub, and scrub along forest margins. This is the cited habitat wording, not a complete range statement.
