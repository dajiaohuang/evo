## Names and material in the 2022 study {#s-5d25f87514}
The study separates West Antarctic Peninsula (WAP), Falklands/Malvinas (FAL), Kerguelen (KER) and South Georgia (SG) gentoo lineages. Methods report newly sequenced high-coverage genomes for FAL, KER and SG. Pinned COL26.8 retains one accepted P. papua page; four research lineages do not create four catalogue species. The full study combines 27 genomic entries and fossil information; this is not a sample of 27 individuals of this species.

## Effective-size inference across glacial history {#s-caa9ed9e56}
The four curves differ: KER expands early in the Last Glacial Period and declines later; WAP and SG fall and then recover; FAL is grouped with declines across roughly the last 250,000 years. These differences require regional reading rather than selecting one curve for the entire accepted species. These curves infer long-term effective size from genomic heterozygosity and depend on mutation rates and time calibration. They are not individual censuses and cannot reliably recover changes in the most recent years.

## Near-UV observations in captive gentoo penguins {#s-b80e39e853}
At SEALIFE Kelly Tarlton’s Aquarium in Auckland, the authors projected a 365 nm torch onto enclosure snow. At least five captive P. papua appeared to follow the spot, without similar interest when the torch was off; the authors interpreted this as near-UV perception. Captive conditions and the observed count remain explicit. This does not measure wild visual thresholds, prey-capture performance or differences among the four regions, nor directly test every penguin species.

## Diet samples from an unusually warm winter in 2009 {#s-b45d18a226}
Xavier et al. studied non-breeding Gentoo penguins at Bird Island, South Georgia, from June to September 2009, when local ocean conditions were unusually warm. Stomach samples were obtained from 55 birds; 43 with food residue were used in diet comparisons. Males ate more fish and females more crustaceans in this sample, with the amphipod Themisto gaudichaudii prominent in both diets. The authors also found individual differences in feeding and isotope-based habitat use. These observations describe one colony in one unusual winter, not a fixed species-wide diet.
