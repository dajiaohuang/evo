## A glaucous shrub with finely divided leaves {#s-f525e09b00}
*Notobubon capense* is a robust, few-branched shrub reaching 3 m. Its pale, waxy-looking leaves are divided three times; the ultimate segments are often split into three narrow, nearly cylindrical lobes, and their veins are not visible. Yellow flowers form large, round compound umbels. The account records “> 40” for these umbels, but this excerpt does not specify what was counted. The mostly rounded fruits are slightly flattened from above, have faint ribs and narrow wings at the margins, and contain visible oil ducts; additional ducts lie in the ribs, where they are inconspicuous.

## Regional forms and close comparisons {#s-861b5ca7cd}
The narrow, nearly needle-like leaf segments help separate *N. capense* from *N. tenuifolium*. Against *N. ferulaceum*, the account emphasizes a stout, short flower stalk, no more than two small secondary umbels, a very large primary umbel with many rays, and the waxy appearance of the whole plant. Its fruits are usually smaller, rounder, and more narrowly winged. In the Kamiesberg, some plants have a longer-looking stalk, fewer rays in the primary umbel, and longer, thicker leaf segments. That form was once named *Peucedanum kamiesbergense* as a separate species, but intermediate specimens from other Northern Cape localities led the cited account to interpret it as an extreme northern form of *N. capense*. The typical form and Kamiesberg form are distinguished from *N. ferulaceum* and *N. pearsonii* by small, rounded fruits with narrow marginal wings and a combination of oil ducts below the rib vascular bundles and broad ducts on the commissural face.
