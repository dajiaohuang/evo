## Posture experiments on two adult cadavers {#s-65accf94c8}
Chang and Ting (2017) used two fresh-frozen adult Phoenicopterus ruber cadavers from Birmingham Zoo, fully thawed for kinematic analysis and dissection. Clamps fixed the distal tibiotarsus of one leg while the body was tilted forwards and backwards to observe hip and knee angles. Passive support here concerns joints under a fixed support arrangement; it does not show a freely unsupported cadaver balancing itself.

## Gravity can contribute to support without active contraction {#s-d0b605d004}
In the simulated one-legged pose, body weight maintained a proximal-joint configuration resembling a resting live bird. Hip and knee angles remained largely fixed while the tibiotarsus was tilted through about 119° in the sagittal plane. Manipulation found resistance to knee extension but not flexion, supporting a proposed unidirectional passive stay. The 119° describes the experimental tilt range, not wild movements or every individual’s anatomical range.

## Foot position determines whether support engages {#s-41ff29f2fe}
The authors identify three conditions: the centre of mass lies anterior to the knee; hip and knee motion limits oppose gravitational moments; and the foot lies medial to the hip in the frontal plane. With the leg vertical, approximating a two-legged pose, the experimental configuration became unstable. Support therefore depends on geometry rather than a permanently locked knee, and the cadaver result does not imply that live birds cannot stand on two legs. Contributions of particular bony and cartilaginous structures remain to be resolved.

## The energy-saving explanation still needs direct measurement {#s-6138d038be}
Cadavers show that some supporting configurations can persist without active muscle contraction. The paper’s live balance experiments used juvenile P. chilensis, not live muscle recordings from these adult P. ruber. The authors propose reduced muscular energy expenditure during one-legged standing, without directly measuring metabolism or muscle activity. Heat retention and fatigue avoidance are other discussed explanations, not comprehensively excluded by this experiment.
