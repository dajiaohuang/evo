## Accepted name and placement {#s-598672c983}
COL26.8 accepts Hynobius kunibiki Sugawara, Iwata, Yamashita & Nagano, 2021 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Caudata (order) → Hynobiidae (family) → Hynobius (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## A dated male holotype and specimen-level measurements {#s-2461a94e3c}
The 2021 description designated a male from Nishinomura, Matsue, Shimane, collected on 20 December 2019 at 100 m, and two paratypes: a female from the same site and date and a male from Unnan at 90 m in January 2021. The holotype measured SVL 60.0 mm, tail 49.7 mm, head length 13.6 mm and head width 10.4 mm, with four fingers, five toes, no interdigital webbing and thirteen costal grooves. These are one specimen’s dimensions. The printed measurement list repeats RUEL with different values; that ambiguous abbreviation is not silently reassigned to eyelid width or used as a resolved trait.

## Yellow tail edges and variable markings in sampled adults {#s-b24cfb57d2}
The diagnosis reported mean SVL 58.9 mm in males and 58.2 mm in females, with yellow stripes on both dorsal and ventral tail edges and a fifth hind toe. Both-sex tail stripes and fifth toes occurred in all sampled animals in the comparison, not a guarantee for every future individual. Dorsal colour varied from yellowish-brown to blackish-brown; distinct black dorsal spots or white lateral spots were usually absent but sometimes present. Preserved dorsums tended to fade to dark grey. Frequencies retain sex and study scope, and live colours remain distinct from faded preserved colours.

## Still-water breeding, coiled sacs and early larval balancers {#s-5dc0d801d8}
The species account described breeding in still waters at forest edges in mixed Castanopsis and Quercus woodland. Adults came to ponds between December and March; the account mentioned attachment to fallen branches or leaves in still water. Egg sacs were coil-shaped. Early larvae had one pair of balancers and black dots along the lateral tail; claws were absent from finger and toe tips. These observations describe reported sites and stages, not clutch size, incubation duration, breeding at all months or survival rates. The text does not justify converting adult attachment wording into a quantified egg-substrate experiment.

## Shimane localities and a disputed isolated record {#s-f1d4c97eb9}
The 2021 distribution account listed Oda, Yasugi, Matsue, Izumo and Unnan municipalities in Shimane Prefecture. The discussion treated a previously sampled Iinan haplotype assignment cautiously: the locality was isolated, voucher details were unavailable, and the authors’ Iinan samples did not support occurrence of the new species there. Thus the listed municipalities and uncertain Iinan lead are not merged into an occupied continuous range. Their proposed northeastern restriction is a study interpretation, not a verified present-day census or physiological altitude limit.

## The Izumo lineage and marker-bounded taxonomic evidence {#s-f68bc69e89}
Cytochrome-b Bayesian and maximum-likelihood trees did not group the former Izumo lineage and the other sampled H. utsunomiyaorum as a single clade; the Izumo lineage grouped with H. akiensis. The authors combined this with morphological differences to describe H. kunibiki. They discussed earlier SNP-based lumping and cited allozyme evidence, retaining disagreement among evidence sets. A mitochondrial tree is not a complete nuclear genealogy or a measured reproductive-isolation test, and the authors’ separation does not automatically certify every historically assigned population.

## A call for conservation reassessment in 2021 {#s-17fccf1ca7}
The authors called for reassessment of H. kunibiki conservation status and national or regional management against overcollection. They linked the concern to release of detailed locality information and cited overcollection of H. utsunomiyaorum; that cited event is not transferred as a measured kunibiki harvest rate. They did not provide a current Red List category or quantified extinction probability in this paragraph. The management request remains a dated recommendation rather than proof that regulations or monitoring were subsequently implemented.
