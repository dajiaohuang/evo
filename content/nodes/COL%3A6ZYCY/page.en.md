## Strumaria unguiculata: tepal claws and leaf form {#s-856b0b9c94}
A 2012 regional account describes a bulbous geophyte reaching 35 cm. It records two leaves, rarely three, usually dry at flowering, suberect, broadly elliptic, abruptly narrowed toward the base, hairless and pale green. The 7–24 star-shaped flowers are borne on long, spreading pedicels; they are white with wine-red stripes on the reverse, strongly scented, and have tepals clawed at the base.

A 1994 systematic account identifies the distinctly clawed tepals as diagnostic. It describes them as widening abruptly above the claw, leaving characteristic “windows” between neighbouring tepals at the flower base. It also describes the leaves as usually 30–60 mm wide, thin-textured, and somewhat wavy at the edges or folded lengthwise. The excerpt names the closest ally as “5. karoopoortensis” (as rendered there), describing it as an isolated population in the more arid Tanqua Karoo to the southeast.
