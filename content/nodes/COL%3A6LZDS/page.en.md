## A short release experiment at four seagrass sites {#s-1e7a6fdfa3}
The accepted name and taxonomic placement follow Catalogue of Life COL26.8 (ChecklistBank dataset 316115; source checklist dataset 1107).

Hair and colleagues released hatchery-cultured Holothuria scabra juveniles into 100 m² seagrass sea pens near Kavieng, Papua New Guinea, on 22 March, 15 May and 15 July 2014. At the first recovery, about two months after release, overall survival was 93.5% at Limanak 1, 84.6% at Limanak 2 and 52.7% at Enuk; no juveniles were found at Ungakum. These site totals combine free release and one-day and seven-day cage treatments.

## What this study does not establish {#s-f2754a6db8}
The short-term recovery results do not establish natural survival, long-term population recruitment, species-wide habitat suitability or regional wild population status.

The release study does not document diagnostic anatomy or variation in the species' morphology.

Juvenile survival after release is not a full life-cycle, reproduction or lifespan account.

The study does not analyze phylogeny or evolutionary history.

Four experimental release sites do not establish the species' geographic range.

The study does not assess fossil occurrences or geological age.

The release experiment is not a wild population assessment or conservation-status evaluation.
