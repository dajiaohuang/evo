## A name fixed to COL26.8 {#s-2d79a4bdda}
Catalogue of Life release COL26.8 records Ardenna pacifica (J. F. Gmelin, 1789) as the accepted species name (published 2026-08-20). This card preserves that version's identity; the name entry alone does not describe anatomy or ecology.

## Separate total birds from complete sets {#s-32fd96814b}
The morphology record covers 5 birds: 2 females, 2 males and 1 of unrecorded sex; 4 have complete measurement sets. Historical row means are 30.7 mm for beak length from nostril to tip, 46.9 mm for tarsus, 296.8 mm for unflattened wing, 130.4 mm for tail and 61.4 for hand-wing index. Since only 4 sets are complete, not every bird contributes to every field. Inference=NO does not replace each measurement's sample denominator. The hand-wing index is Kipp's distance divided by wing length ×100, not flight speed; beak length alone gives neither bite force nor prey-handling ability.

## Mass comes from a separate compilation {#s-dbf78434ac}
The compiled species-average mass is 388 g, with Dunning as its source, and incorporates both sexes. The 5 morphology birds are not a verified count of the individuals behind that average. The original book, weighed birds, localities and dates were not examined, so sex-specific, seasonal or regional mass ranges remain unsupported. Mass and beak length do not establish feeding mechanics.

## Aquatic animal niche and trophic level {#s-7030e941b1}
The Aquatic predator niche category means at least 60% of food resources come from vertebrate and invertebrate animals in aquatic systems. The Carnivore trophic-level category means at least 70% of resources come from live invertebrate or vertebrate animals. These are separate thresholds, not measured proportions of this species' diet or shares of fish, crustaceans or molluscs. The underlying diet papers were not read, so prey lists, seasonal diet, dive depth, capture behavior and prey-handling ability remain unsupported.

## Separate marine food niche from lifestyle {#s-1386a1e17a}
The source defines Primary.Lifestyle=Generalist as time spread across lifestyle classes with no single dominant category. This historical label is not a separate behavioral observation and gives no precise aerial feeding action, dive depth or time budget. Generalist does not mean omnivory. Marine habitat includes pelagic and near-coastal settings; density code 3 means an open environment, including open water, not seabird abundance. Migration=3 means most individuals undertake long-distance migration. The row gives no seasons, routes or distances and does not show that every individual migrates. The hand-wing index gives neither flight speed nor migration mileage. Historical map fields remain uninterpreted and do not establish current range-wide distribution.

## Evidence limits {#s-972419536d}
This account uses 1 of 7 selected historical AVONET v7 rows, same-name eBird data, an older Puffinus BirdTree link, relevant definitions and COL ar/pu name shards. Seven catalogue synonyms provide name correspondences but do not prove that historical and current species boundaries match. The morphology total, complete sets and Dunning mass have separate denominators. Original specimens, Dunning's book, the full workbook and a fresh full reading of the methods paper were not examined. Food niche, trophic level, lifestyle, habitat density and migration remain separate fields; their combination is not given an invented explanation. The sources do not provide measured diet, seed or prey lists, handling behavior, plumage, calls, breeding, current range or migration routes. This is not a complete natural history.
