## A transcriptomic diversity ranking rather than whole-genome diversity {#s-9f85bfe9e9}
Bolitoglossa stuarti had the highest estimate among the species assessed using heterozygous transcriptomic loci in the 2023 study. Only one individual per species was sequenced; annotated transcripts were used, and only loci with heterozygosity contributed to the estimator. Transcriptomic libraries had higher average coverage than ddRAD libraries across the dataset, but those library ranges are not independently assigned to stuarti. The ranking does not establish that this species has the highest diversity at all genomic sites or across all populations. The authors found no significant correlation between the two locus-type diversity estimates, so the transcriptomic ranking cannot be transferred to ddRAD or treated as direct evidence of climatic resilience.

## One sequenced individual and a separate DNA-content estimate {#s-ed9a8a8a56}
The study extracted liver DNA from one Bolitoglossa stuarti specimen as part of a sixteen-species, three-genus comparison, targeting ten gigabases of short-read data per species. Repeat analysis randomly sampled merged reads at roughly 0.1-fold coverage. Table 1 gives a genome C-value estimate of 87.2 picograms, based on blood-smear densitometry and species means from earlier genome-size work. Picograms of DNA are not gigabases, body mass or a complete genome assembly; the sequenced individual and the species-mean DNA-content estimate have different evidence roles. The sample does not determine variation among sexes, populations or ages.

## Detected repeat abundance is a method-dependent lower estimate {#s-d8a2cccd87}
Table 1 reports 56.4% detectable repeat abundance for Bolitoglossa stuarti, including uncategorized repeats. Table 2 reports LTRs at 17.06%, and Table 3 separates ERV at 7.174% from Ty3/Gypsy at 9.886%. These are percentages of sampled bases identified by the study's repeat library, not percentages of animals, newly inserted elements or measured gene expression. Mutation can degrade repeats beyond recognition; the authors regard short-read masking as likely underestimating true repetitive content. Rounded table categories need not sum exactly, and uncategorized repeats should not be silently assigned to a known TE superfamily.

## Repeat divergence suggests relative expansion histories {#s-b8c013f6fb}
The authors place this species among six Bolitoglossa with broad ERV divergence peaks at Kimura two-parameter distances of 20–26. Ty3/Gypsy peaks in this group generally occur around 23–28; platydactyla instead has a reported bimodal pattern around 18 and 28. These distances are differences between reads and repeat consensus sequences, not millions of years. No conspicuous ongoing expansion was evident in the larger-genome profiles, which is not proof that all transposition has ceased.
