## Distinguish the measured length {#s-fe50cdbd46}
Recorded dimensions are reported maximum total length (TL) 1900 mm. SVL runs from snout to vent and differs from total length including the tail. Compiled maxima are reported material, not theoretical growth limits or typical individual size. Sex-specific values may come from different localities or studies and alone cannot quantify population sexual dimorphism.

## How to read reproductive records {#s-9424872e10}
the reproductive-mode field uses the original label oviparous; consolidated mean offspring or eggs per litter/clutch 11.65; egg length 70 mm. Clutch/litter summaries can use midpoints of reported means or, when means are unavailable, extrema; they are not necessarily arithmetic averages of observed clutches. Fractions do not describe non-integer young in a litter. The lowest/highest fields are defined as endpoints of reported means rather than guaranteed smallest/largest individual clutches. Reproductive and sex-determination labels do not establish fetal nutrition or uniformity across all populations.

## Diet in the Abanda cave and forest samples {#s-252dc8b088}
A Gabon study treated dwarf crocodiles from the Abanda cave system and surrounding forest as Osteolaemus tetraspis. Cave animals ate mainly cave crickets and bats; forest animals ate mainly freshwater crustaceans and insects, and cave prey was not found in the forest animals examined. Juvenile cave crocodiles had higher body condition than forest juveniles. The adult difference was not statistically significant, and the authors noted that small sample size may have limited that comparison.

Continue with Osteolaemus osborni (COL 8SBC9) to compare the cave study with a separate shared-site genomic sample while retaining both sample boundaries. The Crocodylia group guide provides the wider reading route; the neighboring page retains its own sources and limits.

## A multilocus hypothesis of geographic lineages {#s-c0d3b7c97a}
A multilocus analysis of African dwarf crocodiles from known localities supported three deeply divergent geographic lineages in the Congo Basin, Ogooué Basin and West Africa. The type specimen of O. tetraspis came from the Ogooué River basin in Gabon, and the study emphasized that lineage boundaries require multiple kinds of evidence. The cave-ecology study used the name O. tetraspis; this guide neither raises the Abanda cave population to a separate species nor treats it as representing the full range and ecology of the COL accepted species.

## Cave samples and taxonomic scope {#s-3376899e60}
The cave-ecology comparison uses samples from Abanda and surrounding forest. Its results do not define the full range or ecology of the accepted COL26.8 concept.
