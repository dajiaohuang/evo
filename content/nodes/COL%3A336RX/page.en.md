## Cymbopappus hilliardiae {#s-960c829f43}
## Narrow leaves and white-pink ray florets

This rounded shrub grows up to 0.5 m. Its narrow alternate leaves are often divided into three parts above the middle, though simple and more divided leaves can occur on one plant. Young leaves look grey from a loose woolly covering that later thins; small glands may be hidden in grooves and wrinkles. Solitary flower heads are carried on long stalks, with white-pink ray florets and yellow disc florets often tipped reddish. The cypselae are oblong, ribbed and glandular, with a short white cup-shaped pappus. The source records flowering from March to May and rocky slopes at 1,000–1,500 m in Sub-Escarpment Grassland.
