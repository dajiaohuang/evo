## The 2014 anatomical account and 2020 name correction {#s-b2714d12a0}
The 2014 paper describes Ikrandraco avatar anatomy. A 2020 correction explains that the electronic article did not include the required ZooBank registration evidence, so the genus- and species-group names were unavailable from that work. The correction supplies the registration and restates the type material and diagnosis. The anatomical findings and the correction’s nomenclatural ruling answer different questions and are cited separately.

## The two specimens preserve different parts of the body {#s-85a4bc8894}
Holotype IVPP V18199 from Lamadong, Jianchang, preserves cranial and mandibular material, part of the neck, sternum, ribs, both wings and foot material. Referred specimen IVPP V18406 from Sihedang, Lingyuan, principally preserves the head and anterior cervical vertebrae. Both are housed at the Institute of Vertebrate Paleontology and Paleoanthropology in Beijing. The second does not supply the same limb data as the first, so they cannot be combined into one wholly measured ideal individual.

## Lateral flattening and unknown horizons limit comparison {#s-20d7b921ed}
Both fossils are laterally flattened. Fracturing displaced the cranial tip and some wing phalanges of the holotype; parts of the referred specimen’s cortical bone are missing. Sites are about 20–25 km apart and exact horizons are unknown. The paper’s approximately 120 Ma Jiufotang context does not establish simultaneous deaths. The authors attribute some differences in cranial and jaw height to compression; size differences alone do not establish distinct species, sexes or growth stages.

## A low elongated head and deep mandibular crest {#s-0528b771bf}
The skull is low and elongated without a median premaxillary crest, while the ventral dentary bears a thin, deep blade-shaped crest. Both specimens preserve its posterior hook-like process, interpreted by the authors as genuine rather than an artifact, with greatest depth near the ninth and tenth lower alveoli. A horny covering is possible without a directly preserved complete contour or color; two individuals do not establish a single function for the structure.

## Small teeth support comparisons without recording a diet {#s-a0b0d6f461}
The paper records at least twenty-one teeth per upper-jaw side and nineteen in the lower jaw, with small oval alveoli more closely spaced anteriorly. Crowns are smaller than those of Guidraco and Liaoningopterus discussed from the same formation, and differ from typical triangular, compressed istiodactylid teeth. Dentition informs prey-capture hypotheses without stomach contents or direct feeding observations; small teeth do not establish a unique prey species.

## Associated wings and foramina provide skeletal evidence {#s-a1017e967c}
The holotype’s wings are closely associated despite incomplete articulation. The humerus has a warped deltopectoral crest, carpals are fused, and wing phalanges two and three have pneumatic foramina. Involvement of an air-sac system in skeletal pneumatization is the authors’ interpretation. Some forearm elements are incomplete and hindlimb material consists only of incomplete metatarsals; these do not directly restore complete span, membrane or leg function, and bone openings do not preserve whole soft-tissue air sacs.

## Fusion supports adult interpretation while measurements remain individual {#s-a44a0a3665}
The authors infer adulthood from fused extensor tendon processes of the holotype’s first wing phalanges. Table 1 gives skull length as 286.5 mm and humerus length as 78.7 mm; the referred skull is recorded as greater than 268.3 mm, retaining a lower-bound measurement. Table 2 compares humerus/skull proportions across specimens rather than establishing an invariant species ratio or flight-performance measure. Adult interpretation supplies no exact age, sex or maximum adult size.

## Temporary skimming and a throat pouch are feeding hypotheses {#s-97638a8f12}
The authors propose low flight over water, with brief mandibular immersion after detecting prey, potentially aided by the low head and thin dentary crest. The posterior hook could anchor extensible skin in a throat-pouch-like structure. These are functional interpretations without a preserved complete pouch in this species or recorded skimming behavior. The paper notes the absence of extensive modern skimmer adaptations; the proposal is neither continuous skimming nor a validated drag model.

## The tree is a result from the 2014 matrix {#s-73524141ab}
The 2014 study analyzes a modified morphological matrix with unordered, equally weighted characters. It recovers eighteen equally parsimonious trees and a strict consensus, placing Ikrandraco within Pteranodontoidea, outside but close to Anhangueria. That placement is the result of the study’s full character matrix; a single jaw or crest feature cannot stand in for it. The PBDB parent path below serves as a separate database classification, not as part of this phylogenetic analysis.

## Which fossil records this name represents {#s-8373aae624}
The Paleobiology Database (PBDB) accepts Ikrandraco avatar under the recorded parent Ikrandraco. The selected database set contains two species-level occurrences from Lamadong and Sihedang, linked to two collections and one reference identifier. These counts describe records, not specimens or individual animals.

## Begin with named collections and strata {#s-21e5eaba30}
Lamadong, Jianchang and Sihedang, Lingyuan are both recorded in Liaoning, China (region code CN), in the Jiufotang unit. Each collection carries a PBDB age bracket of 121.4–113.2 Ma, with Aptian as the early interval. These two localities are selected records, not a complete map of the species’ distribution.

## Age bounds of these records {#s-b50f8d27cc}
The selected records share an outer database age bracket of 121.4–113.2 Ma (Ma means millions of years ago). The broad span does not indicate continuous presence through every part of that interval, directly date either specimen or establish the species’ origin or extinction.

## Read preservation setting separately from living habitat {#s-8aed91e896}
PBDB labels both collections “lacustrine indet.” for environment and “body, adpression, soft parts” for preservation mode. These collection-level labels do not determine Ikrandraco’s habitat or diet and do not describe each fossil individually.

## Follow collection records back to references {#s-2e8c21b474}
The collection records cite Wang et al. (2014), database reference ref:59181. The anatomical account and 2020 nomenclatural correction are also linked separately because one describes the fossils and the other establishes name availability.

## Evidence limits {#s-cb40ed5821}
The anatomical account and name correction provide selected descriptions and analyses, not a specimen-by-specimen redescription. Skimming, a horny covering, a throat pouch and air-sac function remain hypotheses. One caption gives IVPP V18046 where the account identifies the referred specimen as IVPP V18406; that numbering difference remains unresolved here. The database retains its own accepted name, collections and broad ages, separate from the anatomical interpretations.
