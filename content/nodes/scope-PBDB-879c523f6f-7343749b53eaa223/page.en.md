## Research overview {#s-42900e131d}
Helicolocellus cantori is known from goblet-shaped fossils with nested square grids. Researchers use that preserved framework to test an organic-skeleton body plan and a possible placement among crown sponges; the placement comes from a morphology matrix.

## What this evidence can establish {#s-ab25d2f94d}
The holotype and referred specimens preserve the gridded framework. Its material composition, physiology and exact position near Hexactinellida remain interpretations.

## A grid in a goblet-shaped fossil {#s-fdab1ca600}
The fossil has a goblet-shaped body. Its skeletal framework contains nested square grids.

The organic skeleton follows a helical arrangement.

## Inferences from the preserved framework {#s-644f90d617}
The fossils are centimetre-scale and come from the marine Shibantan setting. Suspension feeding and sessile life are inferred from the sponge-body interpretation and morphology; they do not directly record physiology.
