## Thirteen evidence cases on mollusc and brachiopod body plans {#s-9de6119b45}
Follow fossils, experiments and genomes while keeping observed structures, proposed homologies, evolutionary trees and scientific names distinct.

## A fossil outline is mollusc-like, but not a crown diagnosis {#s-a1516d15e8}
More than 35 White Sea specimens preserve bilateral organization and a dorsal cover. They lack a radula and the diagnostic combination used to identify crown molluscs, so the fossils support a body-plan comparison without fixing their position among living molluscs.

## Repeated tooth rows make a radula hypothesis testable {#s-cde2412f41}
Across 189 Odontogriphus specimens, paired teeth sit on a membrane and are replaced toward the rear beside a broad foot and mantle groove. A radula is a toothed feeding ribbon in living molluscs; the repeated arrangement offers a specific feature to test against that interpretation.

## One articulated body carries shell and sclerites {#s-9c8970c9d0}
Orthrozanclus combines an anterior shell with three zones of overlapping sclerites, small hard plates, and long marginal spines. Their arrangement on one articulated fossil is direct evidence; how the parts relate to other molluscan shell structures is a separate homology question.

## Shell layers are not all nacre {#s-d8280cffd0}
Pojetaia shell sections preserve a layered inner fabric, compared in the study with foliated aragonite. The evidence does not establish undisputed nacre in Cambrian molluscs, so the shell microstructure should not be assigned a familiar material name without qualification.

## Nectocaris keeps a cephalopod hypothesis open {#s-5816764d91}
Ninety newly prepared specimens show paired eyes, fins, two tentacles and a funnel-like structure. The combination makes the proposed cephalopod connection testable, but these structures alone do not settle how Nectocaris belongs in the molluscan tree.

## One phylogenomic matrix recovers Aculifera {#s-d4a46f63fd}
In the Kocot et al. analyses, chitons group with sampled aplacophorans, while gastropods and bivalves form another supported clade. This is a result from one phylogenomic matrix—an analysis combining many genetic markers—not a final answer independent of taxon sampling and method.

## New transcriptomes bring Monoplacophora into the comparison {#s-0c522537d1}
Fifteen new transcriptomes expanded the sample to include every major living molluscan class. In the principal analyses, Monoplacophora groups with Cephalopoda; this placement belongs to that sampled matrix and its methods.

## Embryos connect Nodal signaling with shell chirality {#s-232d50f832}
In Lottia and Biomphalaria embryos, Nodal and Pitx expression occurs on opposite sides. Inhibition disrupts the pathway output and shell coiling, linking developmental signaling to chirality without making the two species’ shell forms identical.

## An octopus genome reveals routes to novelty {#s-05cc878960}
One Octopus bimaculoides genome and transcriptomes from twelve tissues reveal targeted gene-family expansions and extensive rearrangement across the genome. These findings show genomic change in the sampled animal; they do not reduce cephalopod novelty to one mechanism.

## Two isolated sclerite types support a testable Micrina model {#s-80f885cb40}
Micrina mitral and sellate sclerites have complementary shapes and muscle scars. Researchers use them to reconstruct a sessile animal with two valves, but no fossil preserves the proposed valves joined together; the assembly remains a model, not a directly observed body.

## Kutorgina preserves soft anatomy within paired valves {#s-10d054b13d}
Kutorgina chengjiangensis preserves an early spirolophe lophophore, digestive tract and pedicle together with its paired valves. The lophophore is the tentacle-bearing feeding organ; its form and the attachment stalk provide clues to function, but not an observation of the animal feeding or attaching.

## Three data layers separate shared and lineage-specific shell proteins {#s-412b56abba}
The Lingula genome, RNA-expression profiles across developmental stages and a shell proteome—the proteins detected in shell material—can be compared as separate data layers. Together they distinguish shared components from proteins associated with phosphate shells in particular lineages; one living brachiopod does not represent every fossil shell.

## Yuganotheca combines valves, tube and lophophore {#s-615d2f8fca}
Yuganotheca preserves paired agglutinated valves, a horseshoe-shaped lophophore, a two-part tube and a long pedicle. Agglutinated valves are assembled from bound particles. The combination resists a simple ladder of body plans: several structures must be compared together, and no one feature alone establishes direct ancestry.
