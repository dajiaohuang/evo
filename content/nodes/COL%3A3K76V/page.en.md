## Helichrysum refractum {#s-2a24a4a2b4}
## Reflexed leaves and clustered heads at branch tips

Hilliard's *Flora of Southern Africa* account describes a much-branched perennial that sprawls or forms a rounded bush or mat. Older stems become bare, while young branches are gray-woolly and closely leafy. Narrow leaves, 4–10 by about 1 mm, spread and later bend backward; their edges roll under strongly and the lower surface is densely woolly.

About 10–15 small, bell-shaped heads form dense clusters at branch tips, soon overtopped by new growth. The bracts are arranged in five rows and soon fall; outer bracts are webbed to adjacent heads, while inner bracts are pale golden-brown with blunt, irregular edges. The account records 15–23 flowers; ovaries bear paired mucilage-producing hairs, though achenes were not seen. Many rough pappus bristles cohere lightly at the base. The source habitat fields list shrub communities and inland sand dunes.
