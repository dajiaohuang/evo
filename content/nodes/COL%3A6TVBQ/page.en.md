## Form and flowers {#s-cf1fb02737}
The 2013 regional account describes a corm-bearing geophyte up to 300 mm tall, with softly fibrous corm coverings. Its several leaves are spreading, delicate, pale green, and V-shaped in cross-section. It bears 2–5 yellow flowers, and the scape is approximately as long as the pedicels. The style branches are long and narrow, and the slender ovary widens upwards. There is one leaf-like, spreading bract for each flower.

These traits come from a regional South African account published in 2013; the excerpt gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency.

## Ovary and species comparisons {#s-5d694f487e}
A 2014 revision describes an unilocular ovary with ovules attached to its inner wall, distinguishing *P. scullyi* from the three-locular ovaries of *P. aquatica* and *P. alba*. It recounts that Nel, under the name *Ianthe*, placed the species in the Aquaticae group based on an absence of fibrous corm coverings and disc-like remains of old corms; the 2013 account separately describes the coverings as softly fibrous. The revision considers *P. scullyi* more closely allied to *P. maximiliani*, with which it shares the single-chambered ovary, thin-textured leaves, and broad flower-cluster bracts. *P. maximiliani*, found in mountains immediately east of the Olifants River Valley, consistently has two flowers per cluster and an elongated, sharply three-cornered ovary 13–30 mm long. In *P. scullyi*, each cluster has 2 to as many as 4 or 5 flowers, and the ovary is consistently shorter, at 3.5–10.0 mm.

These diagnostic comparisons come from a 2014 taxonomic revision of *Pauridia* in southern Africa; the excerpt gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency.
