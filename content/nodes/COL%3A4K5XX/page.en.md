## A skeletal holotype and two captive-associated paratypes {#s-94a7faab58}
The 2002 description designates INPA 3929 as holotype: a complete adult skeleton and skull of unknown sex, found on the forest floor and collected by M. G. M. van Roosmalen in November 1998. Apparent natural death is the authors’ interpretation; no independent cause-of-death examination is reported there. Two male paratypes were obtained as juveniles at Santa Cruz on the Rio Mariepauá in 1996, then kept in Manaus. They died in April and August 2001 and were registered as INPA 4029 and 4033, respectively. Acquisition dates, death dates and specimen identifiers are distinct, not three independent population surveys.

## A combination of white ear tufts and terminal tail hair {#s-2c8d4beef1}
The original diagnosis compares a combination of grayish-black crown, trunk and outer limbs; dark-orange sideburns, underside and inner limbs; and white ear tufts, hands, feet and terminal tail tuft with neighboring titi forms. The tail is described as mainly black with a white terminal tuft, not wholly white. Figure 27 shows the appearance of paratype INPA 4033, whereas figure 28 shows the skull and mandible of INPA 3929. Appearance photographs and the skeletal holotype are different records. Descriptions from limited material do not define invariant coloration across ages and localities.

## 64 millimetres belongs to one skull {#s-af710ed45f}
Table 3 on page 26 reports cranial and dental measurements of holotype INPA 3929: greatest skull length 64 mm, condylobasal length 53 mm and zygomatic breadth 40 mm. The caption identifies an adult of unknown sex. These use different landmarks and cannot be added into a head length, or treated as species-wide size ranges or sexual differences. Greatest skull length and condylobasal length each appear twice in the table but still refer to one specimen, not extra independent individuals. Live body mass is not estimated from this skull here.

## Named observations and an unconfirmed extension {#s-917f0f72b9}
The 2002 type locality is the edge of Nova Olinda on the west bank of the lower Rio Aripuanã, about 41 km southwest of Novo Aripuanã. The authors also list Monte Alegre, Novo Oriente (Capimtuba), and seringal São Luis on the east bank of the Rio Madeira south of Manicoré as wild-observation localities. Their suggestion that an unidentified gray titi at Pimenta Bueno could be this species explicitly needs confirmation. Listed sites and an unconfirmed extension are not a continuously surveyed complete range. Some printed coordinate seconds exceed the usual 0–59 range; they are neither guessed into corrected coordinates nor mapped here.

## Disturbed-forest observations are not a diet list {#s-26956feb0d}
The authors report bernhardi groups in naturally or human-disturbed forest, including secondary forest after windthrow, liana forest on abandoned terra preta sites, secondary vegetation around fields, and managed riverbank forests called seringais. No group total, observation effort or habitat-use proportion is supplied in that passage, so it cannot quantify preference or show that primary forest is never used. The subsequent fruit-tree list describes potential forest resources rather than itemized feeding observations. Morning pair-calling remarks are not a population-density estimate corrected for detection probability.

## Distribution records {#s-666290cc9d}
The supplement records Prince Bernhard's titi at Humaitá in Amazonas and Ji-Paraná in Rondônia. These are regional survey occurrences and do not by themselves define the complete range or population status.

## Evidence limits {#s-ca0166ed8f}
The 2002 description rests on one adult skeleton and two captive paratypes; Pimenta Bueno remains a tentative locality extension. Reported coordinate seconds outside the usual range are left unresolved, so no corrected point is inferred. Potential food trees are not feeding records, and historical sites do not establish current occupancy.
