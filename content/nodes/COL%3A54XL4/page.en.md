## One accepted name, two records {#s-c7d7c8c865}
The pinned Catalogue of Life release COL26.8 accepts Tarentola fascicularis in Phyllodactylidae, the leaf-toed gecko family. ReptTraits v1-2 supplies the size, habitat and reproductive labels here, under the workbook’s Reptile Database 2023 names and trait-specific sources. Its one-row-per-species summary combines cited reports; it does not establish that every population shares every recorded trait. A separate historical archive supplies the life-history figures later on this page.

## The lengths stop at the vent {#s-b88a4efbc4}
The largest reported snout–vent length is 86 mm, and the largest female value is 53 mm. Snout–vent length (SVL) runs from the nose to the vent and leaves out the tail. Both figures are compiled maxima, not typical sizes or limits on growth. They may combine measurements from separate studies or localities, so their difference alone does not describe size variation between the sexes in a population.

## Broad labels for habitat, diet and timing {#s-dd18604b3b}
The workbook records a saxicolous, or rock-associated, microhabitat, a carnivorous diet and nocturnal activity. These are broad categories, not a complete habitat description or a fixed daily schedule. “Carnivorous” does not name the prey. Reports may differ among populations, seasons and life stages.

## What the clutch fields mean {#s-0b7b56315c}
The source labels the reproductive mode oviparous, meaning egg-laying. It reports a consolidated figure of 2 offspring or eggs per litter or clutch, with both the lowest and highest reported clutch or litter means also listed as 2. These values summarize source reports: the consolidated figure may be a midpoint of reported means, or of reported extremes when means are missing. The low and high fields are endpoints of reported means, not necessarily the smallest and largest individual clutches. The labels do not show embryo nutrition or guarantee that all populations reproduce alike.

## A historical record under the same name {#s-0f2a118a09}
The August 2015 life-history archive includes a record called Tarentola fascicularis. Five fields are used here, each with support from cited species-level rows matching the name and family. This traceable name match does not erase possible inheritance from older species or subspecies groupings, nor prove that the archived concept is identical to the accepted COL26.8 taxon.

## Adult and hatchling mass are separate summaries {#s-9485da13a9}
The historical archive summarizes adult body mass as 6.54 g and mass at birth or hatching as 0.19 g. The adult value may combine sexes or adults of unknown sex; the other describes a different life stage. Neither is a range of individual masses or a growth series following the same animals.

## Egg number and incubation entry {#s-c9e5811ea3}
The archive lists 2 offspring or eggs per litter or clutch and 70 days from egg laying to hatching. The combined count field can describe live-born young or eggs; it should not override the separate source’s oviparous label. The timing figure covers incubation from laying to hatching, not an entire reproductive cycle.

## Maximum longevity is not a typical lifespan {#s-3370165453}
The archive’s maximum-longevity value is 14 years, selected as the largest record in its collected material under the source method. It does not tell us the usual lifespan, mean survival in the wild, or a limit reached by every individual; this page has not independently confirmed a current record.

## Following the source labels {#s-df5e987b57}
The same-name historical rows cite Meiri (2010), Meiri, Brown and Sibly (2011), the Reptile Incubation book and IUCN (2011), among others. The archive keeps row numbers and citations by field. A label may refer to a paper, book or database; row counts are not animal counts, and repeated citations do not amount to independent experiments.

## Evidence limits {#s-963ae27687}
ReptTraits compresses reports into one row per species under its 2023 name framework. Sex, age, locality and population differences may therefore be hidden. The reported lengths are maxima from cited material, not biological limits, and reproductive summaries are not fixed counts in every clutch. This page does not update conservation, venom, range or population trends; absent fields remain unknown. Citation labels preserve the source chain, but the project has not independently reviewed every underlying work.

The 2015 archive reports medians or the mean of the two middle values, except that maximum longevity uses the largest record found. These are not direct measurements of one animal, ranges of variation, average wild lifespans or current surveys. Historical species and subspecies may share inherited records. A same-name, same-family match supports the comparison, but does not prove equivalence with COL26.8 or remove all inherited components. The archive does not consistently identify wild versus captive conditions, and the underlying publications have not been reviewed one by one. Diet, behavior, present range and conservation need other sources.
