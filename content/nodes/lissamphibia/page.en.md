## A small jaw enters a large origin debate {#reading-introduction}
Funcusvermis jaw material adds a Triassic comparison to the debate over living amphibian origins. Anatomical observations and different character matrices lead into competing relationship hypotheses. Reading those results together means distinguishing preserved structures from the trees inferred from sampled characters.

## A jaw feature tests caecilian origins {#jaw-origin-test}
At PFV 456 in Arizona, isolated jaws, vertebrae and femora represent at least 76 individuals that the authors infer belong to the same taxon, *Funcusvermis gilmorei*; they are not one articulated skeleton. The fossils preserve parallel labial and lingual tooth rows at the mandibular symphysis. From comparative anatomy, the authors interpret the lingual row as belonging to an adsymphyseal bone co-ossified lingually to the dentary, rather than a coronoid. In their modified 63-terminal-taxon matrix, *Funcusvermis* was scored for 29 characters among 355 morphological characters. The analyses place it as the earliest-diverging gymnophionomorph; this interpretation does not make it a direct ancestor.

## Different matrices recover different origins {#origin-trees}
Origin trees vary with fossil sampling and character coding. Schoch's 2019 analysis used 33 taxa and 108 characters, including Triadobatrachus, Karaurus and Eocaecilia as representative stem taxa; it recovered an Amphibamiformes–Lissamphibia clade but left several relationships unresolved. Marjanović and Laurin's 2019 revision reported 4,200 misscored matrix cells and added 56 terminal taxa to the original 102. In their constrained comparison, the lepospondyl hypothesis was nine steps shorter than the temnospondyl hypothesis, while most bootstrap values were low. The later Funcusvermis study analysed a modified matrix of 63 terminal taxa and 355 characters, with Funcusvermis scored for 29; all analyses recovered monophyletic Lissamphibia nested within amphibamiform dissorophoids. That sampled result does not settle every competing origin hypothesis.
