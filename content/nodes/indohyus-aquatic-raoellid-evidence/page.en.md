## Indohyus: skulls, dense bones and isotopes {#s-566948d7a7}
Separate Indohyus skulls preserve a cetacean-like involucrum, while other limb bones and a multi-tooth isotope sample add independent evidence. Together they support aquatic wading and a raoellid sister placement to Cetacea in the cited analysis, not membership in crown whales or direct ancestry.

## Several records, not one skeleton {#s-0f6e1041bc}
Skulls RR 207 and RR 208 preserve the involucrum, a thickened bone around the middle ear. Separate specimens RR 157 and RR 42 supply dense long-bone sections, and stable-isotope measurements come from several teeth. These observations converge, but the material is a disarticulated bone-bed sample rather than one associated skeleton.

## From anatomy to a way of life {#s-f101e2e446}
Bone density, tooth isotopes and skull anatomy support the cited study’s interpretation of aquatic wading. Its phylogenetic analysis places Raoellidae as sister to Cetacea. Both are inferences from sampled evidence: Indohyus is not a crown cetacean, and neither the matrix nor the anatomy demonstrates that it directly became a whale.
