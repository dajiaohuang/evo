## Crocodile-line and bird-line archosaurs: eleven evidence cases {#s-85cb0614a2}
Compare crocodile-line and bird-line records while keeping fossils, experiments, trees, clocks and living-name catalogues distinct.

## A crocodile-line body can mimic a theropod {#s-c3ece226cb}
AMNH FR 30587 preserves a slender, toothless suchian, a crocodile-line archosaur. Its body proportions resemble those of ornithomimid dinosaurs, but the resemblance is convergence: similar form evolved on separate branches. It does not establish an immediate shared ancestor or identical ecology.

## Carnufex: a partial fossil, a bounded predator claim {#s-dec8ecf90d}
NCSM 21558 is a partial holotype of a large Carnian crocodylomorph, a member of the crocodile-related archosaur line. Its proposed size and high predator tier are inferred from proxies and the fossil assemblage around it; the specimen does not preserve a feeding event. The predator interpretation is therefore bounded by indirect evidence.

## Junggarsuchus: skull and trunk tell different things {#s-5b134894c3}
Junggarsuchus holotype IVPP V14010 combines a consolidated skull resembling that of crocodylians with a slender, land-adapted postcranial skeleton. Those parts form one anatomical mosaic. Functional claims about skull mechanics or fast running, and its exact tree position, remain interpretations based on anatomy and character analysis.

## A dated model tests gharial relationships {#s-94f43106d0}
One tip-dated model—a time-tree method that assigns ages to sampled fossils at the tree tips—combines fossil ages and morphology with molecular data from living crocodilians. It groups Gavialis, the gharial, with Tomistoma, the false gharial, and places the sampled thoracosaurs outside the crocodilian crown group, defined by the last common ancestor of living crocodilians and all its descendants. Other analyses recover different relationships, so the result is a model test rather than a settled genealogy.

## Archaeopteryx shows mixed anatomical signals {#s-a142be3c23}
The Thermopolis specimen WDC CSG 100 preserves theropod-like features in the feet and palate alongside avialan, or bird-line, anatomy. This combination supports close evolutionary affinity between birds and theropod dinosaurs. It does not make this individual the uniquely first bird or a directly observed ancestor.

## Pigment structures constrain a feather-colour map {#s-722a3468ef}
Microscopy of BMNHC PH828 identified melanosomes, pigment-associated structures whose shapes can be compared with reference samples. A statistical analysis used them to reconstruct likely plumage regions. Preservation quality and the use of a tail from another specimen constrain how completely the colour map represents Anchiornis.

## Feathers cover four limb surfaces in one fossil {#s-f5e6eb841a}
Microraptor specimen IVPP V13352 preserves long pennaceous, vane-bearing feathers on the forelimbs, hindlimbs and tail. The fossil documents where feathers occur on this individual. It does not preserve a flight path or directly show how the animal moved through the air.

## A wind tunnel tests one reconstructed glide {#s-5aa7012eaa}
Researchers measured forces on a scaled Microraptor model and found stable, high-drag gliding across the configurations they tested. The result shows that this reconstructed geometry can glide under those conditions. Scaling, body posture, model shape and fossil distortion bound the experiment; it is not a recording of extinct behaviour.

## A tightly dated fossil, two crown-bird placements {#s-991fa7740f}
CT imaging of Asteriornis holotype NHMM 2013 008 supplies skull anatomy for testing its position near Galloanserae, the group that includes waterfowl and landfowl. Parsimony and tip-dated Bayesian analyses place it on different nearby branches. Its tight age is valuable, but does not by itself make it the globally oldest crown bird or settle whether it falls within the crown group of living birds and their descendants.

## Neoavian genomes expose gene-tree conflict {#s-aa76dbadfc}
A whole-genome analysis sampled 48 representatives across Neoaves, the large living-bird group, and recovered major groupings. Individual gene trees still conflict over some branches, so the result is a topology for a selected extant sample, not a complete species tree or a record of fossil ancestry.

## A modern-bird time tree rests on calibrations {#s-eef1c183d3}
A fossil-calibrated analysis combines clock-like exons, broader RAG (recombination-activating gene) sampling and fossil constraints to infer a Cretaceous root and radiation around the Cretaceous–Palaeogene boundary. Its dates follow the chosen calibrations, topology and priors. COL26.8’s 11,071 accepted living species describe the package’s nomenclatural coverage, not fossil completeness, direct ancestry or agreement on one crown-bird date.
