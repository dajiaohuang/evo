## Ecological evidence {#s-36b4f96f33}
At Loango National Park in Gabon, a study followed one group of 10 western lowland gorillas from January 2018 to July 2020, recording 5,896 observation hours over 652 days. Scan-based estimates for the two complete years assigned about 31% of feeding time to fruit, 21% to herbs, 31% to tree foods, 10% to nuts and 0% to insects. These are local estimates for one group.

## Ecological evidence {#s-9e2081ba93}
Across 31 monthly observations of the same Loango group, fruit availability was positively correlated with the proportion of feeding scans spent eating fruit (Pearson r = 0.623, p < 0.001). This describes covariation at one site; it does not establish causation or a species-wide seasonal pattern.

## Ecological evidence {#s-2f4b549d4f}
Two field observations at Mbeli Bai recorded adult female western gorillas using detached plant material to assist movement. On 9 October 2004, Leah used a branch to probe water depth and steady herself; on 21 November, Efi used a shrub trunk to stabilize food processing and then as a short bridge over swampy ground. Two cases cannot estimate frequency or how the behavior was acquired.

## Life history {#s-8ec9d9fb29}
At Mbeli Bai, long-term records for western lowland gorillas gave a mean female age at first parturition of 12.2 ± 0.6 years (7 females) and a mean interbirth interval of 5.4 ± 0.9 years (54 intervals; the analysis included intervals whose offspring survived to age three). These estimates describe one study population and defined subsets, not fixed values for all populations.
