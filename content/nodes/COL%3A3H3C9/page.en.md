## Feeding scans in the Atananga group at Loango {#s-36b4f96f33}
At Loango National Park, Gabon, one habituated Atananga group of 10 western lowland gorillas was followed from January 2018 to July 2020. The team collected 5,896 observation hours over 652 days. Across the two complete years, mean feeding time was 31% fruit, 21% herbs, 31% tree foods, 10% nuts, and 0% insects. These scan-based estimates describe one group at one site, not a species-wide diet.

## Fruit availability and feeding observations {#s-9e2081ba93}
Across 31 monthly observations at Loango, fruit availability and the Atananga group's proportion of feeding scans spent eating fruit were positively correlated (Pearson r = 0.623, t = 4.209, df = 28, p < 0.001). This is an association in one group at one site; it does not establish causation or a species-wide seasonal pattern.

## Two observations of tool-assisted support {#s-2f4b549d4f}
At Mbeli Bai in the Republic of the Congo, 2 wild adult female western gorillas from different groups used detached plant material for support in swamp water. On 9 October 2004, Leah used a branch to probe depth and steady herself while crossing an elephant pool. On 21 November 2004, Efi used a shrub trunk to stabilize food processing and then as a short bridge across swampy ground. These 2 observations do not estimate how common the behavior is or how it was learned.

## First births and birth intervals at Mbeli Bai {#s-8ec9d9fb29}
At Mbeli Bai, the monitored western lowland gorilla population (Gorilla gorilla gorilla) had a mean female age at first parturition of 12.2 ± 0.6 years (n = 7). Mean interbirth interval was 5.4 ± 0.9 years (n = 54 intervals; the analysis included intervals whose offspring survived to age 3), and the rate of offspring surviving to age 3 was 0.121 per adult female-year. These are estimates from that monitored population and study definitions, not species-wide values.

## Evidence limits {#s-10d30061e5}
This introduction brings together selected studies of western lowland gorillas; it is not a full ecological or life-history review of Western gorilla. Key results come from local samples at Loango and Mbeli Bai. Observed behavior is not a prevalence estimate, and demographic values should not be transferred uncritically to other subspecies or sites.

## Facial asymmetry in a comparative skull sample {#facial-asymmetry}
A 3D geometric-morphometric comparison examined 114 crania from 3 Gorilla subspecies. Facial fluctuating asymmetry, the random departure from bilateral symmetry, explained 6% of total facial-shape variation in the western lowland gorilla sample. This is an archival-skull comparison for one subspecies sample, not a species-wide estimate or a result about other anatomical regions.

## Demographic changes inferred from western lowland genomes {#demographic-model}
A diffusion-approximation model of the genome-wide site-frequency spectrum from 14 western lowland gorilla genomes and 4,554,752 SNPs inferred an ancestral effective-population-size expansion of about 1.4-fold near 970 ka, followed by a 5.6-fold contraction near 23 ka. This is a model-based estimate for the sampled western-lowland lineage, not a current census or an estimate for every Gorilla subspecies. The samples were mainly blood from wild-caught zoo animals, with some origin uncertainty; the causes of the inferred changes remain unknown.
