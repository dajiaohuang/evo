## Year-round captive breeding with possible female misidentification {#s-afdc049dc3}
Frogs maintained as betsileanus bred in every month and contributed 86.7% of all recorded egg masses in the eleven-species study. Most eggs were laid on land beneath refugia. This pooled share is not a fecundity rate per female or a natural abundance estimate. The authors explicitly warned that some captive females could have been katae, because separation relied on femoral gland size and call and the founders lacked genetic verification. The breeding record remains assigned to the maintained label, not a certified species-pure colony or equivalent evidence of year-round katae reproduction.

## Long development and explicit exclusions from the summary {#s-68bc5b6e7a}
Most retained betsileanus tadpoles completed metamorphosis in less than four months, while some required nearly 300 days. The authors excluded tadpoles from a pilot captive-diet study in which several took more than a year, and also excluded ten infertile masses below eight eggs from clutch summaries because they might be remnants of earlier spawning. Those exclusions delimit the reported distributions; they do not prove that longer development or smaller apparent counts are biologically impossible. The tadpoles received external food, and the observed timings are conditional on captive treatment and uncertain founder identity, not a species-wide field schedule.
