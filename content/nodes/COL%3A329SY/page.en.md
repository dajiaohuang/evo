## Annual stems on a perennial creeper {#s-dfe4d446c4}
The 1962 southern African account calls *Cucumis kalahariensis* a perennial, dioecious creeper: the plant persists from year to year, while its above-ground stems are annual. The stems branch, may reach 3 m and can root where they touch the ground. Fibrous roots reach 50–60 cm; toward their base they swell into cylindrical, spindle-shaped or sausage-shaped tubers 5–20 cm long and 2–3 cm across, with a thin light-brown covering and soft, white tissue inside. The stiff grey-green leaves are 5–15 × 3–9 cm and deeply divided into 3–7 lobes. Male and female flowers are borne on separate plants; both are yellow, with male flowers solitary or occasionally grouped and female flowers solitary. The oblong fruit is 3–5.5 × 2–3 cm, greenish white with irregular brown-purple stripes and soft spines. The white seeds are 6–7 mm long. These details come from a regional account; it gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency.

## The account notes edible tuberous roots {#s-8411a7eda4}
The same 1962 treatment singles out its edible tuberous roots, separate male and female flowers, banded and spiny fruits, and seeds described as large for the genus. “Edible” is the source’s description of the roots; the account does not identify who eats them or give a cultivation context.

## Deep Kalahari sand {#s-acb0e00e9f}
The account records deep sandy soils in the arid Kalahari as habitat. That phrase describes a setting, not individual collection sites or the species’ full range.
