## Davilla elliptica A.St.-Hil. {#s-bc5c9e382e}
## Hairy stems and leathery foliage

Davilla elliptica has stems with either long hairs or silky hairs. Its leathery leaves range from becoming nearly hairless to woolly, shaggy or silky; the petiole is crenate, and the secondary veins either arch toward the margin or reach it. Flowers may be terminal or lateral. Their stamens project beyond the other floral parts; the flower surface ranges from nearly hairless to woolly or silky. The filaments are cylindrical, the petals notched, and the inner sepals overlap. The fruit has two carpels and one or two seeds with an aril that covers part of each seed.
