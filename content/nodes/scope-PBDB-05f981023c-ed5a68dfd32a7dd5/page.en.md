## Overview {#s-9b619dae71}
Two isolated Garbani Channel ankle bones, UCMP 197509 and UCMP 197517, are attributed to Purgatorius through comparative analyses. Their joint surfaces support a mobile ankle and possible foot inversion, but they are not associated with teeth and do not establish a named dental species, crown-Primate membership or direct ancestry.

## Diet {#s-72d051f8a4}
Diet cannot be tested from the isolated ankle bones.

## Habitat {#s-b7ac4cb1ed}
The Garbani Channel setting identifies the fossil locality; it does not by itself establish the animal’s habitat.

## Locomotion {#s-e22d71953e}
Ankle mobility and an inverted-foot capability are inferred from tarsal form.

## Body size {#s-657eafa2e2}
Small-bodied; body mass is estimated by comparison rather than measured.

## Ecological role {#s-5cec1d02c5}
An arboreal locomotor interpretation is possible, but diet remains unknown.

## Preserved features {#s-2c0b488bad}
Mobile upper ankle joint

## Preserved features {#s-a514692053}
Foot inversion inferred from tarsal anatomy

## Preserved features {#s-b8d3f4b968}
Isolated astragalus and calcaneus attributed by comparison

## Evidence and scope {#s-6f60673868}
UCMP 197509 and UCMP 197517 are isolated tarsals attributed through comparative regressions and matrices. The 65.2–64.4 Ma interval is the bounded late-Puercan sample context, not a genus-wide range, crown-Primate age or direct date on either bone.

## Two bones, not a skeleton {#s-c35e14538c}
The Garbani Channel sample consists of an astragalus and a calcaneus attributed to Purgatorius. Neither bone is associated with teeth, so the evidence is narrower than a complete species account.

## What the ankle can suggest {#s-9e6f0a5228}
Measurements and joint surfaces place the bones toward the mobile end of the comparison sample. That anatomy supports possible foot inversion and branch use, but it does not directly show climbing or the animal’s daily routine.

## Attribution is not ancestry {#s-fbc10abd35}
Comparative regressions and phylogenetic matrices support the attribution to Purgatorius. They do not identify a dental species, establish membership in crown Primates or demonstrate a direct ancestor.

## atlas-profile / readerLimitations {#s-60aaa38519}
The 65.2–64.4 Ma interval is the late-Puercan sample context, not a direct date on either bone or a genus-wide range. Tarsal attribution and arboreal movement are comparative interpretations; the sample does not resolve diet, population ecology or a direct place in primate ancestry.
