## Two isolated tarsals, a limited attribution {#s-9b619dae71}
Two isolated Garbani Channel tarsals attributed to Purgatorius support ankle-mobility interpretations but do not establish a tooth-based species or crown-Primate membership.

## Diet {#s-72d051f8a4}
Diet cannot be tested from the isolated ankle bones.

## Habitat {#s-b7ac4cb1ed}
The Garbani Channel setting identifies the fossil locality; it does not by itself establish the animal’s habitat.

## Locomotion {#s-e22d71953e}
Ankle mobility and an inverted-foot capability are inferred from tarsal form.

## Body size {#s-657eafa2e2}
Small-bodied; body mass is estimated by comparison rather than measured.

## Ecological role {#s-5cec1d02c5}
An arboreal locomotor interpretation is possible, but diet remains unknown.

## 65.2–64.4 Ma: the bounded sample context {#s-6f60673868}
UCMP 197509 and UCMP 197517 are isolated tarsals; 65.2–64.4 Ma is sample context, not a genus range or crown-Primate age.

## Two bones, with no associated teeth {#s-c35e14538c}
The Garbani Channel sample consists of an astragalus and a calcaneus attributed to *Purgatorius*. Neither bone is associated with teeth, so the evidence is narrower than a complete species account.

## Mobility and inversion are comparative interpretations {#s-9e6f0a5228}
Measurements and joint surfaces place the bones toward the mobile end of the comparison sample. That anatomy supports possible foot inversion and branch use, but it does not directly show climbing or the animal’s daily routine.

## Attribution to Purgatorius is not ancestry {#s-fbc10abd35}
Comparative regressions and phylogenetic matrices support attribution to *Purgatorius*. They do not identify a dental species, establish membership in crown Primates or demonstrate a direct ancestor.

## What the Garbani Channel sample cannot establish {#s-60aaa38519}
The 65.2–64.4 Ma interval is the late-Puercan sample context, not a direct date on either bone or a genus-wide range. Tarsal attribution and arboreal movement are comparative interpretations; the sample does not resolve diet, population ecology or a direct place in primate ancestry.
