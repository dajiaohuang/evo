## Helichrysum argyrosphaerum {#s-6713bf53cd}
## Woolly leaves and pink-tipped yellow florets

The SANBI accounts describe a prostrate or decumbent herb with spatulate to oblanceolate leaves, about 25 by 7 mm, and thin gray wool on both surfaces. Solitary, nearly round heads sit at branch tips. Their flowers are yellow with pink tips; the silvery bracts become more pink toward the center and fade as they mature. Flowering is recorded from May to October and again in January.

The source habitat note describes hot, dry, sandy places and says the plant can become a weed. Another bound note reports that it has been regarded as good forage, while also recording blindness and encephalopathy in sheep and cattle after large intakes. These are source-reported observations, not a quantified toxicity threshold.
