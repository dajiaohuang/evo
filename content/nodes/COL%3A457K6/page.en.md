## Lanceolate leaves and terminal flower clusters {#s-743a85b214}
The Brazilian Flora 2020 v393.147 field records monopodial growth and no hairs on the stem. Leaves are lanceolate, with a raised upper-surface midrib and a glabrous underside. Bracts or small scale-like cataphylls below leaf nodes are absent. The inflorescence branchlets are compressed, with rounded bracteoles beneath the flowers. Two to four inflorescences are listed per axillary shoot, in terminal or subterminal positions; their form is described as spike-like or alternate pyramidal. The regional dataset field gives no frequencies for these alternatives.

## Partly fused sepals and an enlarging fruit calyx {#s-bbbaf6b5e2}
The anther's two pollen sacs are equal; floral-disc hairs are limited to the staminal ring. The flower cup (hypanthium) is obconic, or inversely cone-shaped, and described as silky-haired or tomentose (covered with a dense mat of hairs). Sepals are partly fused, the ovary has two chambers, and the calyx opens partly by tearing. Its lobes enlarge on the globose fruit. The field lists these alternative hair coverings without frequencies.
