## Historical local survey records are distinct from a current provincial census {#s-28c2ecbc52}
Table 2 of the 2026 paper attributes 13 records to source-labelled Pristimantis nigrogriseus in Fátima parish, Pastaza, Ecuador, during September 2013–September 2018. Counts are cumulative records, not an independently certified number of distinct marked animals, population density or a current abundance estimate. Surveys combine daytime/night-time visual and photographic encounters, ten 8 × 8 m quadrats, four 100 × 20 m transects and fifty pitfall traps across six habitats. The site description’s 900–1,100 m gradient and later 2025 weather-summary temperature are not this species’ measured elevation limits or thermal tolerance. Province-wide occurrence and all source identifications have not been independently certified.

## Coverage and community turnover do not prove a species trend {#s-2d411113a0}
Reported global sample coverage is 97.4%, with habitat values 93.8–100%; this is not 97.4% of all provincial species discovered or proof that no rare species remain. The Methods’ sentence describing the next-record probability at high coverage reverses the usual observed-sample interpretation, and its effort wording does not resolve all per-habitat totals. Jaccard clustering and year-five diversity changes describe mixed herpetofaunal records, not a measured decline or recovery of this species. Drought, disease, disturbance tolerance and physiological sensitivity are proposed or cited explanations rather than newly tested causes. Raw yearly/habitat matrices and detection models have not been independently reconstructed.

## Species-labelled rows conflict with a genus-only identification statement {#s-992b00f140}
Methods state that Pristimantis individuals were conservatively identified only to genus, without molecular analyses. Table 2 nevertheless prints Pristimantis nigrogriseus and other named Pristimantis species alongside five separate Pristimantis sp. rows. This conflict remains unresolved: the named row is presented as an attributed source record, not independent certification of species identity. The five unnamed rows are not assigned to this species or pooled into its 13 records. Original diagnostic photographs, vouchers or author clarification are needed before treating the names as verified occurrence evidence.
