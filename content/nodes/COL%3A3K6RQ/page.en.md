## Helichrysum griseum {#s-a89f543c5b}
## Large woolly leaves and sharply spreading bracts

Hilliard's *Flora of Southern Africa* account describes a perennial herb with a stout woody base and a brown, silky-woolly crown. Flowering stems reach about 700 mm and may grow singly or in tufts. There are often only three or four ascending leaves, up to 200 by 80 mm: lower leaves narrow at the base, while upper leaves have broad, heart-shaped bases that clasp the stem. The upper surface begins cobwebby and becomes harshly hairy; the lower is gray-white and felted.

Many small bell-shaped heads form dense or loose corymb-like clusters. Their four close rows of bracts have woolly bases and glossy pink, buff or pale tips that spread sharply outward. The account counts 25–31 flowers; the smooth achenes are about 1 mm long. Numerous rough pappus bristles equal the corolla and lightly fuse at the base. The source places it in grassland up to about 1,200 m and distinguishes it from *H. pilosellum* by the very acute, spreading bracts.
