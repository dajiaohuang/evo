## Tadpole images in a cross-species study {#s-b54528e159}
The 2024 paired-stage shape study includes this species. Table S1 lists the tadpole source as “Photo, literature” (photographs and published images) and gives voucher LGE 21732. A dash means the table lists no voucher; it does not show that no specimen existed. Across the study, 112 tadpole photographs or drawings represent 83 species, one or two images per species, at Gosner stages 26–41. One person digitized 32 landmarks; the researchers digitally corrected tail curvature caused by fixation and analyzed mean shapes for each species. This row gives no exact stage for each specimen. The images are not new observations of living tadpoles or an independent species diagnosis.

## How the adult measurements were assembled {#s-d753c6fd84}
The adult-source field is “ImageJ”, meaning measurements were taken from images; the voucher is FML2907-3 and the sex field is “?” (unspecified). The study selected ten linear measurements, mostly from male dorsal or side-view images and earlier descriptions. It used principal component analysis (PCA) to fill some missing values, although Table S1 does not flag every imputed cell. Log-shape ratios divide linear measurements other than snout–vent length by their geometric mean to remove overall size effects. These image- and literature-based proportions do not establish live performance, internal anatomy, a population sex ratio or a new sex-specific size range.

## Habitat labels differ between tadpoles and adults {#s-46312195e6}
The study codes the larval guild as LE (lentic, or still-water associated) and the adult ecomorph as SEMAQ (semi-aquatic). It keeps the two life stages separate: the adult label cannot automatically be applied to tadpoles, or vice versa. Other study labels such as lotic, torrential, saxicolous and terrestrial describe broad reported associations, not measured flow tolerance or a complete geographic range. An endotrophic tadpole category is distinct from direct development and does not erase a larval stage. The authors compiled these functional assignments from species accounts rather than experimentally measuring each species’ feeding, movement or habitat choice.

## What the paired-stage comparison can show {#s-2dac335171}
Across the sampled species, differences in larval and adult shape were weakly but significantly associated (Mantel r = 0.16, p = 0.003); disparity patterns also varied among families. The authors interpret the broader results as support for adaptive decoupling. They do not show zero association, genetic independence, experimental selection or this species’ own developmental trajectory. Phylogenetic analyses include 74 of the 83 sampled species; ancestral shapes and timing are model reconstructions from living species, not newly measured fossil morphology. For adult shape, K = 0.44 with p = 0.001 indicates weaker resemblance among close relatives than the Brownian-motion reference, while still detecting phylogenetic structure. Thus the paper’s phrase “not phylogenetically structured” cannot mean that the test was nonsignificant. Supplement S3 repeats family sample totals of 112 for both stages, so adult individual denominators and imputed cells remain unverified without the raw matrices.
