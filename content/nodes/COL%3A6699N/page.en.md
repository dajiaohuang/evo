## Accepted name and placement {#s-da260197c4}

The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## A reported distance to velocicantus {#s-fc4559ff7f}
The2020 study’s Table2 reports 10.0% uncorrected p-distance and 10.8% Kimura2-parameter distance between the Allobates flaviventris label and velocicantus, based on a678-bp16S fragment. These metrics are distinct and do not give divergence time or a whole-species identity certificate. The table is a sampled comparison; original accessions, alignments and per-donor TableS1 entries remain independently unreviewed.

## Dark dorsal marks in the published comparison {#s-29d6410b0b}
The velocicantus description places flaviventris among compared Allobates with large dark dorsal patches or rhomboid,diamond,hourglass ortriangular marks. It contrasts this group with the focal species’ lack of large dark marks; it does not assign one particular shape to every listed species. This is the2020 author comparison, not a newly inspected photograph series, a full diagnosis or an invariant pattern in all life stages.

## Two reported note arrangements {#s-3b7adadc31}
The2020 comparison summarizes flaviventris as having continuously emitted notes or trills of unpulsed notes, with 2–10 notes per trill in the cited earlier study. The number is a reported comparison, not newly measured sampling or a refreshed full-species range. It remains distinct from velocicantus trills of66–138 two-pulse notes.
