## Type mountain and the broader regional interpretation {#s-0ad29d25ba}
Male holotype CIB 98008, formerly CIB 580768, came from Jizu Mountain, Binchuan, Yunnan, at 1920 m. The monograph examined additional adults from Yumen, Yanbian, Sichuan, at about 1730 m. Its distribution summary placed the species in hilly areas along northern Yunnan and southernmost Sichuan at 1700–2800 m. This wider interpretation includes revised historical identifications; it is not a survey proving every locality or elevation is occupied.

## Five examined Sichuan adults {#s-c410941ec5}
The examined diagnosis reported two adult males at 34.4–36.3 mm snout–vent length and three females at 39.4–46.2 mm. These are measurements of the Yumen sample rather than all historically listed types. It described no vomerine ridges or teeth, rudimentary basal toe webbing with narrow lateral fringes, and brown nuptial spines on the dorsal bases of fingers I and II in breeding males, with a subgular vocal sac. The small sample does not establish population-wide size limits or statistical sexual size differences.

## Historical minor records proposed for reassignment {#s-30cc4b95cd}
The remarks traced the original subspecies designation and subsequent recognition as a species. They assigned the Yanbian population formerly recorded as B. minor to B. binchuanensis and proposed revising historical B. minor records from Wuding and Qiaojia in Yunnan and Huili, Jiulong and Muli in Sichuan on biogeographic grounds. These proposals retain the monograph’s attribution and rationale. They do not certify new molecular evidence for every reassigned historical voucher.

## A character cited in the gutu comparison {#s-bfb9ad1a0b}
The 2025 gutu description lists Boulenophrys binchuanensis as without a vomerine ridge. This is an attributed comparator statement within that paper’s taxonomic scope, not a new measurement series for this species. The gutu adult sample spans 34.4–44.7 mm in seven males and 36.2–52.8 mm in eight females; those numbers belong to gutu, not to this comparator.
