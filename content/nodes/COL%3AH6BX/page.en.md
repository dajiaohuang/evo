## An erect herb with red flowers and yellow corona horns {#s-836b273825}
The Flora of Panama describes an erect herb 60–80 cm tall, sometimes reaching 2 m, usually with a single stem but sometimes several. Stems are green, glabrous or slightly pubescent when young. Opposite leaves are lanceolate to linear-lanceolate, attenuate-acuminate, with an acute or attenuate base; blades are 5–16 × 1–4 cm, thin and glabrous to glabrate, on 1–2 cm petioles. Few-flowered axillary inflorescences have 1–2 cm pedicels with fine hairs. In mature flowers the green, five-lobed calyx is hidden by the reflexed petals; its lobes are 2–3 × 0.8–1 mm. The corolla is red to orange-red, with ovate to oblong lobes 4–7 × 2–3 mm. Erect, broadly ovate corona segments are 4–5 mm long, rounded or obtuse at the apex, and exceeded by conspicuous bright-yellow inner horns. Erect, slender, fusiform follicles are 8–10 cm long and 1–1.5 cm in diameter. Seeds are about 6 mm long, narrowly winged, with a white, silky coma. The historical flora calls it a widely distributed weed, ranging from Florida to South America and the West Indies, and reports it from all provinces of Panama. This does not establish current distribution, native versus introduced status, or survey completeness.
