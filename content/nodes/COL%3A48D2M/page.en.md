## What does Ochna barbertonensis look like? {#s-616af15eb7}
This woody subshrub grows from a thick horizontal rhizome about 2 cm across, sending up several annual shoots to about 20 cm. Gray, hairless branches may peel in thin papery strips; younger leafy twigs are brown and finely hairy. Narrow elliptic leaves are 21–47 mm long and 6–18 mm wide, green when mature and reddish brown when young. Their toothed edges have small dark points; the midrib and 10–14 pairs of side veins are prominent. Terminal flower clusters arise on leafless shoots, bear two to nine flowers, and measure 1–3.5 cm long. Yellow petals are 6–10.5 mm long; flowers have more than ten stamens and five carpels. Immature black fruits are about 6 × 8 mm.

## How is Ochna barbertonensis distinguished from O. confusa? {#s-4e2e0f8040}
The source compares it with O. confusa by its dwarf woody habit, narrow elliptic leaves and toothed margins. It distinguishes O. barbertonensis by pores at the anther tips, anthers nearly twice the filament length, and five separate, recurved stigma lobes. It also has a thick horizontal woody rhizome with spaced slender annual stems, rather than O. confusa’s large lignotuber and sturdier branching.

## Where does Ochna barbertonensis grow? {#s-28628291c2}
The source places it in short Barberton Montane Grassland on reddish clay loams.
