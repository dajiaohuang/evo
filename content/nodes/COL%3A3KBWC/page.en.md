## Heliophila scandens {#s-643220d9ad}
## Glabrous climbing stems bear terminal racemes and net-veined pods

The South African flora account describes a hairless, straggling woody climber up to 3 m tall. Its fleshy, stalked leaves are 4–8 cm long and 0.7–3 cm wide, lance-shaped to broadly elliptic, with acute or blunt tips. They have tiny stipules; their margins often roll under in dried specimens.

The leafless racemes are short and corymb-like in flower, then looser in fruit. Petals are 8.5–14 mm long and 3–8 mm wide; the flowers have two ovules. The elliptic to lance-shaped fruits are 2.5–4 cm long, sometimes oblique, with flat valves bearing one main nerve and a net of smaller veins. Seeds are nearly circular with a narrow rim. The account says flowering occurs mostly in June and July; one specimen carried flowers and fruit in mid-September.

The habitat source says the species is apparently confined to scrub and forest margins on coastal sand dunes.
