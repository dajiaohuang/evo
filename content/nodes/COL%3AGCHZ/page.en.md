## Two EltonTraits field groups {#s-79419ab098}
EltonTraits 1.0 links one historical same-name, same-family record for Ardea insignis and supplies two ecological field groups: diet and foraging position. Its classification predates COL26.8; the name link helps locate the entry but does not settle later splits or synonymies.

## Relative weights of food categories {#s-fd6c03243d}
The EltonTraits profile assigns 70% relative weight to fish, 10% to invertebrates, 10% to mammals and birds, and 10% to reptiles and amphibians. Code B indicates reasonable confidence in the source, with possible uncertainty in the proportions. These weights standardize verbal descriptions; they are not direct measurements of food consumed, and a zero category would not prove that a food is never eaten.

## Water-surface and ground positions {#s-dcb3141ff0}
The species-level foraging entry assigns 50% to the water surface or just below it and 50% to the ground. These weights estimate relative use of feeding positions, not nest sites, complete habitat use or a whole-day time budget. They do not provide water depth, flight height or habitat area.

## A shared source label {#s-3fde563108}
Both field groups carry the Ref_1 label in the EltonTraits archive. It connects the diet and foraging summaries to their recorded source trail; it does not make either estimate a direct observation.

## Reading the estimates {#s-4b6e03cd4f}
EltonTraits converts descriptions from earlier sources into broad food categories and relative weights; these are not direct measurements of intake, energy or observation time. Code B retains uncertainty about the proportions. The same-name association does not establish equivalent historical and current species concepts, and the entries do not describe local or seasonal variation, present distribution, population trends or conservation status.

The AVONET entry classifies habitat as freshwater wetlands such as lakes, marshes or reedbeds, trophic niche as aquatic animals, and primary lifestyle as terrestrial. Its dominant-niche categories use a threshold of at least 60% of food resources. That threshold defines a category; it is not a separate measurement of this bird’s diet, and the labels do not replace local habitat or behavioral observations.
