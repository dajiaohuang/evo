## What does the regional account describe about this perennial? {#s-ca5b98ba76}
The account describes a slender, erect perennial 30–50 cm tall, growing from a woody base. Most leaves are near the base; the oblong blades have coarse, uneven lobes that do not reach the midrib. Their surfaces bear appressed star-shaped hairs that may thin with age. Flowers, yellow to orange-red, occur singly or in pairs in erect, terminal, leafless cymes on a much-branched inflorescence.

## How does the revision compare its leaves and flowers with Hermannia paucifolia? {#s-ba64d1b371}
A 1980 revision distinguishes H. macra from H. paucifolia by leaves and flower size. Most dried H. macra flowers are up to 6 mm long, compared with up to 10 mm in H. paucifolia. H. macra has oblong leaves with coarse, uneven lobes that rarely approach the midrib; H. paucifolia has ovate leaves, usually lobed to the midrib, making many lower leaves deeply divided into finger-like parts. The revision describes both as 30–50 cm plants with several stems from a woody base, mostly basal leaves and nearly leafless flowering branches. It says the persistent, fused styles of H. macra look somewhat club-shaped and may explain the unpublished name “H. rhopalostylys.” When published in 1919, the name “macra,” or “Meagre Hermannia,” was chosen instead.

## What habitats does the regional account report? {#s-6c6756d0d7}
The account reports sandy flats and dry riverbeds. It gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency.
