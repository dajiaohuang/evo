## Two new specimens beyond the giant forelimbs {#s-c0519fe576}
The 2014 redescription retains holotype MPC-D 100/18, formerly ZPal MgD-I/6, collected at Altan Uul III in Mongolia in 1965. It consists mainly of shoulder girdles, approximately 2.4 m forelimbs, and vertebral, rib and gastral fragments. Referred MPC-D 100/127, from Bugiin Tsav in 2009, is nearly complete but lacks middle dorsals, many caudals and the right forelimb. MPC-D 100/128, from Altan Uul IV in 2006, preserves post-cervical vertebrae, ilia, partial ischia and a left hindlimb. The latter is not another complete whole skeleton, and three specimens are not three population estimates.

## A composite is not one complete individual {#s-035b93e0b9}
Figure 1 combines MPC-D 100/127 with proportionally enlarged material of MPC-D 100/128. The paper describes the latter as about 74% of the former’s size and the former’s left forelimb as about 6% longer than the holotype’s. These are comparisons among specimens, not one individual’s growth series. Extended Data figure 5 also links a right second-toe phalanx collected in 2009 with a matching impression in matrix attached to a poached phalanx, providing evidence for association of recovered material. The contact surfaces and complete custody history were not independently reexamined here.

## A broad beak and deep lower jaw {#s-2707c8b595}
The skull of MPC-D 100/127 measures 1024 mm from premaxilla to occipital condyle, with an anteriorly expanded spatulate beak and a deep toothless lower jaw, while the cranium is low and narrow overall. Anterior surface pitting supports the authors’ inference of a keratinous beak covering, not preservation of the complete soft covering. Small adductor chambers support a weak-bite interpretation; comparison of the sclerotic ring with skull length supports possible diurnality. No live bite force or daily activity was measured. Morphology, soft-tissue reconstruction and functional inference remain separate; a duck-like outline does not establish the same feeding mechanism as living ducks.

## Tall spines, pneumatic spaces and a fused tail tip {#s-8fa118fa5b}
Dorsal neural spines rise posteriorly; the last dorsal spine is approximately 8.5 times its centrum’s height. Pneumatic spaces and support structures do not directly preserve the complete living back outline. The authors proposed ligamentous support of the abdomen and also possible display. At least two terminal vertebrae are fused into a pygostyle-like structure, documenting a bony tail ending. A feather fan remains a comparative possibility; the paper reports no feather impressions of this species. Tall spines, possible back soft tissues, tail plumage and preserved bones are different evidence levels.

## Large-size reconstruction and a slow-movement interpretation {#s-3b188904a7}
For MPC-D 100/127, the paper reports a body length of about 11 m and an estimated mass of 6358 kg. Mass follows a cited estimation method, not direct weighing of the living animal or a species average. Robust hindlimbs, a femur longer than the tibia, a short metatarsus and broad blunt pedal unguals differ from the usual cursorial ornithomimosaur form and support the authors’ slow-movement interpretation. No speed in metres per second was measured. Blunt toe tips may have limited sinking on wet substrates, but substrate bearing capacity, foot soft tissues and step-by-step behavior were not directly measured.

## What gastroliths and fish remains support {#s-6d8fc9c06e}
Excavation within the ribs and gastralia of MPC-D 100/127 yielded more than 1400 gastroliths approximately 8–87 mm across, with fish vertebrae and scales found in situ among them. Extended Data figure 7 shows the association; some fragments in the photograph carry question marks and cannot automatically become identified fish or extra independent feeding events. Gastroliths, toothless beaked jaws and morphology interpreted as herbivory-related combine with fish remains to support omnivory. They do not determine dietary proportions, daily stone load, how fish were obtained or invariant diets at every age.

## Soft-plant cropping and suction remain interpretations {#s-01d83ef33c}
The 2014 authors linked the broad beak, deep oral cavity and a possible large tongue to hypotheses of disturbing and drawing in organic matter underwater, or cropping low soft vegetation or aquatic herbs. Long forearms and compact manual claws were also discussed as possible digging and plant-gathering tools. Tongue anatomy, suction, actual claw use and plant species were not directly preserved or experimentally established as confirmed behavior. Nemegt river deposits and fish remains provide environmental context, without making this animal obligatorily aquatic, a sustained underwater swimmer or an exclusively bottom-feeding species.

## Separate the 2014 tree from the current catalogue {#s-d458982fd0}
The study analyzed 568 morphological characters and 96 taxa, obtaining a strict consensus of six most parsimonious trees. Deinocheirus and Garudimimus were sister taxa, joining Beishanlong in the authors’ Deinocheiridae discussion. This is a result under that matrix and coding, not evidence that sister taxa were each other’s ancestors. Current PBDB storage puts Beishanlong on another path and includes additional family entries not redescribed from equivalent material here. Catalogue paths are retained rather than reorganized from one 2014 analysis. Figure 4 explicitly presents a hypothetical fleshed-out, feathered reconstruction; outline, colors and plumage add no fossil specimen.
