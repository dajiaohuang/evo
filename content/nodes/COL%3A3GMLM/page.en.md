## What does the regional account describe about the dwarf shrublet? {#s-e005384016}
The account describes a densely tufted, branched undershrub 5–15 cm tall, arising from woody underground stolons. Stems may be simple or branched, finely hairy or becoming nearly hairless, leafy throughout or bare and scarred near the base. Crowded alternate leaves are usually 9–10 by 1.5–2.5 mm, linear-lanceolate, pointed and narrowed at the base; they begin with fine hairs beneath and later become hairless except for ciliate edges. Flowers are solitary or rarely paired in leaf axils. Oblong-lanceolate bracteoles are about 3 by 1.25 mm and finely hairy on the back. The pale cream calyx tube is about 5 mm long, constricted to 0.75 mm at the middle and covered in appressed hairs. Eight or ten yellow, fleshy, elliptic petals may be present or absent, sometimes joined in pairs; each is about 0.75 by 0.5 mm. There are eight or ten anthers, each under 0.5 mm. The ovary is 1.25 by 0.5 mm; the style is 2.25 mm, with a head-shaped stigma. The dark brown fruit, 2.5 by 1.5 mm, remains enclosed at the base of the persistent calyx.

## How does the revision distinguish its flowers from related species? {#s-71f08485c0}
A 1986 revision describes a dwarf, densely tufted shrublet with abundant flowers in October. Flowers grow singly in leaf axils along nearly the full length of each branchlet, unlike G. baurii and G. fastigiata, which bear flowers only in upper leaf axils. In G. baurii flowers are always paired; G. fastigiata usually has two to several per axil, and new sterile shoots soon overtop its flowering tips. These are distinctions reported in that regional revision.

## What sites does the regional account report? {#s-401dcd4647}
The 1986 account reports the species as common on bare sandstone on parts of Mawahqua Mountain. The authors also found it in short turf on steep slopes below the Cave Sandstone cliffs on Bamboo Mountain’s north face, about 30 km west of Mawahqua, and on similar ground below dolerite cliffs about 50 km to the west-northwest. These are locality observations in the regional source, not a current survey.
