## Two forest bats behind a species description {#preview}
A Central African bat described from two museum specimens is distinguished by its large skull and short free tail tip. The original description places the female holotype in montane forest in the eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo and the male paratype in Rwanda.

## Two forest bats behind a species description {#field-study}
The 2019 description names the bat Parahypsugo macrocephalus. Its holotype is an adult female skin, skull, and postcranial skeleton collected in Kabogo Forest, 4 km southwest of Talama, at 1,950 m in the Democratic Republic of the Congo on 17 February 2007. The adult male paratype came from Nyungwe Forest Reserve in Rwanda in March 1990. The paper therefore grounds its description in two specimens from two montane forest localities, not a broad survey of the species’ ecology.

The diagnosis reports a total length of 82–93 mm, a forearm of 37.5–37.9 mm, and body mass of 9.8–12.0 g for the specimens, and identifies it as the largest Parahypsugo by skull measurements in the comparison. The skull is broad in front; the first upper incisor has a smaller second cusp, and the free tail tip is only 0.6–2.0 mm. A 2021 revision based on genetic and morphological sampling of more than 400 African pipistrelle-like bats lists Parahypsugo macrocephalus as (Nycticeinops macrocephalus) in its current-taxonomy table; the table note says this parenthesized generic placement needs genetic confirmation. This published, qualified name bridge does not update the pinned COL26.8 classification.
