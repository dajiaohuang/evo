## Euryops abrotanifolius {#s-ab47ad9c9f}
## A persistent tip on the dry fruit identifies the species

Manning and Goldblatt's account describes a densely leafy shrub up to 1 m tall. Its ascending leaves are divided into narrow linear to thread-like lobes, though some may be entire; leaves measure 60–90 mm. Yellow radiate heads are solitary at branch tips on stalks woolly at the base.

The glabrous dry fruits have 10 ribs and a distinct apical appendage formed by the persistent stylophore, the base of the style. Nordenstam notes that this feature is visible even in young florets and is the most reliable recognition character. Similar but smaller fruits occur in *E. lasiocladus*, which has lateral flower stalks and belongs to another section; other species can look similar until floral structures, especially fruits and the pappus (bristles or scales attached to a dry fruit), are examined.

Vegetative form varies, especially leaf shape and size and stalk length. Heads usually have about 13 involucral bracts, sometimes more. Ray florets may have red or coppery undersides in different parts of the range, but the colour is not consistent within populations or combined with other traits; Nordenstam regarded it as taxonomically unimportant. The cited flora records sandstone slopes as habitat.
