## Highest ddRAD estimate within a constrained comparative sample {#s-1a0151a096}
The 2023 study ranked Bolitoglossa yucatana highest for genetic diversity estimated from its ddRAD loci among the sampled bolitoglossine species. The study deliberately sequenced one individual per species and estimated diversity only at loci containing at least one heterozygous site, using the median across loci. Low coverage could miss one allele and rare variants, and the authors considered ddRAD diversity likely underestimated. This ranking is therefore specific to the sampled individual, loci and comparison set. It is not a population-wide allele-frequency estimate, a ranking across all salamanders, a measured adaptation rate or evidence of present demographic security. The pooled ddRAD range is not reported as a numerical value for yucatanaing the appendix.

## One sequenced individual and a separate DNA-content estimate {#s-ea6f31c945}
The study extracted liver DNA from one Bolitoglossa yucatana specimen as part of a sixteen-species, three-genus comparison, targeting ten gigabases of short-read data per species. Repeat analysis randomly sampled merged reads at roughly 0.1-fold coverage. Table 1 gives a genome C-value estimate of 60.1 picograms, based on blood-smear densitometry and species means from earlier genome-size work. Picograms of DNA are not gigabases, body mass or a complete genome assembly; the sequenced individual and the species-mean DNA-content estimate have different evidence roles. The sample does not determine variation among sexes, populations or ages.

## Detected repeat abundance is a method-dependent lower estimate {#s-88e2585942}
Table 1 reports 49.7% detectable repeat abundance for Bolitoglossa yucatana, including uncategorized repeats. Table 2 reports LTRs at 14.77%, and Table 3 separates ERV at 8.43% from Ty3/Gypsy at 6.331%. These are percentages of sampled bases identified by the study's repeat library, not percentages of animals, newly inserted elements or measured gene expression. Mutation can degrade repeats beyond recognition; the authors regard short-read masking as likely underestimating true repetitive content. Rounded table categories need not sum exactly, and uncategorized repeats should not be silently assigned to a known TE superfamily.

## Repeat divergence suggests relative expansion histories {#s-074fe565e6}
The authors place this species among six Bolitoglossa with broad ERV divergence peaks at Kimura two-parameter distances of 20–26. Ty3/Gypsy peaks in this group generally occur around 23–28; platydactyla instead has a reported bimodal pattern around 18 and 28. These distances are differences between reads and repeat consensus sequences, not millions of years. No conspicuous ongoing expansion was evident in the larger-genome profiles, which is not proof that all transposition has ceased.
