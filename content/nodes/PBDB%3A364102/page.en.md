## One subfamily, different skeletons and ecological questions {#s-3eaa9508c2}
In the working catalogue, Halszkaraptorinae is a subfamily within Dromaeosauridae, connecting Halszkaraptor, Natovenator, Hulsanpes and Mahakala. Their grouping in cited relationship studies and the fixed database does not assign identical bodies or lifestyles to every member. Evidence for necks, snouts, ribcages and feet is uneven: some specimens preserve many body regions, others only limited foot bones and braincase material. ## Relationship hypotheses change with sampling and characters {#s-4694c068a0}
The 2011 Mahakala analysis places it near the base of Dromaeosauridae. The 2018 Hulsanpes redescription groups Hulsanpes and Mahakala as sister taxa alongside Halszkaraptor. The 2019/2020 debate supports a relationship with Unenlagiinae but differs over ancestral retentions and novel traits. Adding Natovenator in 2022 leaves its internal relations with Mahakala and Hulsanpes unresolved. Different sampling and character coding prevent these dated trees from becoming one uniquely supported diagram; the PBDB parent path remains a separate recorded classification.

## Aquatic and cursorial interpretations use different body regions {#s-9a9107eec4}
The Halszkaraptor debate concerns snout canals, tooth-replacement patterns, neck anatomy and forelimbs. Brownstein (2019) questions specialized semiaquatic predation; Cau (2020) replies character by character and supports amphibious adaptations. The 2020 correction changes caption abbreviations and panel letters without resolving ecology. Backward-directed dorsal ribs in Natovenator add ribcage comparisons supporting the 2022 authors’ semiaquatic interpretation, while aquatic locomotion remains unknown. Metatarsal anatomy in Hulsanpes and Mahakala is interpreted in 2018 as potentially reducing relative torsion and supporting some cursorial adaptation. These named studies offer functional hypotheses, not a uniform subfamily diet, swimming mechanism or running speed. Mahakala’s 82/52-mm metatarsal discrepancy cannot supply precise locomotor parameters.
