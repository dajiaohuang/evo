## Hessea pilosula: leaves, flowers and comparison {#s-386a4da692}
A 2013 regional account describes a bulbous geophyte 70–170 mm tall. It records two leaves, dry at flowering, spreading, narrowly strap-shaped and slightly channelled. The leaves are smooth or bear short, spreading hairs on both surfaces or only the underside. The flowers are star-shaped and pink, with a white or darker-pink centre, in a dense, widely spreading head. The stamens are consistently shorter than the tepals.

A 1994 systematic account distinguishes *Hessea pilosula* by its deep-seated bulb with a long, slender neck; narrow, usually somewhat hairy leaves 1–5 mm wide; stamens less than 3 mm shorter than the tepals; and a perigone tube shorter than 1 mm or absent. It identifies *H. incana* as the closest allied species and the only other one with short-hairy leaves. The account describes *H. pilosula* as less robust in vegetative and floral form and flowering one to two months earlier than *H. incana*; it refers readers to that species for detailed differences.
