## Three tested routes to living amphibians {#s-fcab893932}
These matrix studies test temnospondyl, lepospondyl, and stereospondyl-linked routes to living amphibians. Their outcomes depend on sampled taxa and analytical choices; none alone establishes a settled sequence of direct ancestors.

## Dissorophoids near the living branch {#s-56588fa32f}
In Schoch's dissorophoid matrix, sampled living amphibians fall within Amphibamiformes near Gerobatrachus. Support is low, and other matrices recover alternatives, so the result remains a tested topology rather than a settled ancestor tree.

## A revised matrix favors a lepospondyl route {#s-b20fe71b97}
Marjanović and Laurin corrected and expanded their matrix. Under their analyses, the lepospondyl route became the shorter tree, but bootstrap support was generally low and other matrices disagreed.

## Broader caecilian sampling changes one route {#s-7291373f7d}
One analysis of Chinlestegophis linked caecilians with stereospondyls. With broader sampling that includes Funcusvermis, another analysis instead recovered a dissorophoid stem for caecilians. The contrast shows how added fossils and matrix choices can change the tested relationship.
