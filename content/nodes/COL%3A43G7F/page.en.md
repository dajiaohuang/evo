## Stem surface {#s-8dfd51fdfd}
The Brazilian Flora 2020 record describes the stem as pubescent or strigose: covered with short soft hairs, or with stiff, straight hairs pressed close to the surface. It records the hairs as pointing forward, alongside simple hairs and thread-like, unbranched bristles. Branched and glandular hairs, prickles, and short lateral shoots (brachyblasts) are recorded as absent.

## Leaves and inflorescences {#s-3b89016c2e}
Stipules, small leaf-like structures at leaf bases, are persistent. The record marks extrafloral nectaries (nectar-secreting glands outside flowers) and projections between pairs of pinnae (the leaf’s primary side divisions) as absent. The inflorescence, or flower cluster, is globose; its floral bracts (small leaf-like structures) are longer than the flowers before they open (anthesis).

## Flowers {#s-e8744317a7}
The perianth (calyx and corolla together) is recorded as having four parts. The membranous calyx, the outer sepal whorl, is marked as lacking lobes. Corolla (petal-whorl) lobes have one to three veins and are hairy. The androecium (the stamens collectively) is isostemonous, meaning the stamens equal the petals in number; the filaments are pink.

## Habitat category {#s-18a50f39e7}
The regional dataset labels the habitat “Terrícola,” Portuguese for terrestrial. This category does not provide a collection locality or establish the species’ full range.
