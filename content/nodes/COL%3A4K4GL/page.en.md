## What distinguishes its hairy leaves and mauve corolla? {#s-c74a749121}
Plectranthus oribiensis is described as an erect herb or soft shrub up to 1.5 m tall, with tuberous roots and densely hairy stems. Its broad, heart-based leaves are densely woolly, especially underneath. The mauve corolla is hairy and gland-dotted on the back.

## Which landscape settings are named? {#s-882d32a2d5}
The habitat field names forest margins and wooded kloofs—steep-sided ravines—for Plectranthus oribiensis. It provides no coordinates or complete range.

## What comparison and collection history does the account give? {#s-f0db75d81b}
The regional account says H. B. Nicholson first collected the species in 1971 and 1972. It distinguishes the corolla from P. fruticosus by the absence of purple spots on the upper lip, and from P. fruticosus and P. graliatus by its gland-dotted, villous surface and cordate-based leaves whose undersides are densely woolly-haired with colorless gland-dots. The 1985 account does not provide a population survey.
