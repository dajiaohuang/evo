COL26.8 accepts Galago gallarum Thomas, 1901 as species ID 3F2D4 under genus ID 4LR5. The country list is coarse and is not a continuous range map. The Chinese display name is editorial.

## Natural history {#s-46ce25337f}
Butynski and de Jong’s 2004 natural-history account combines a literature review with their preliminary observations. It describes G. gallarum as a nocturnal, arboreal primate of comparatively dry habitats and reports it occurring with G. senegalensis in Meru National Park. The authors distinguish the species in habitat, appearance and loud advertisement calls; this account is a historical synthesis, not a range-wide standardized survey.

## Survey records {#s-8564b4ebdc}
A 2004 nine-day survey of Meru National Park and Kora National Reserve recorded G. gallarum at about 0.7 individuals per survey hour in Commiphora–Acacia woodland and bushland north of the Tana River, at 300–800 m elevation. It was sympatric with G. senegalensis in parts of Meru, and the team did not observe G. gallarum south of the river. These are encounter results from that short Kenya survey, not density or population estimates.

## North coast survey {#s-ba6a26b126}
During a rapid assessment on Kenya’s north coast in May 2011, de Jong and Butynski surveyed 114.3 hours over 1,630 km. At Kiunga they reported at least two G. gallarum in Acacia–Commiphora woodland and heard no loud calls. They considered two shy, unidentified galagos at Agarbul likely to be G. gallarum, but did not treat those as confirmed identifications. The report notes that large areas west and south of the Tana River remained unsurveyed, so its suggested boundary is provisional.
