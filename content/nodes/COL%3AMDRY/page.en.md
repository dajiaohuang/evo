## A sympatric sequenced specimen distinct from chinanteca {#s-90182deabd}
The study sequenced rufescens specimen IBH 22536 from the chinanteca type locality and included an earlier GenBank comparison from MVZ 194254. Its reported GTR distances between rufescens and chinanteca were 0.08 for 16S and 0.21 for cytochrome b. The authors treated their local co-occurrence as additional support for recognizing chinanteca. Co-occurrence and mitochondrial divergence are retained as different evidence; they do not by themselves demonstrate mating incompatibility or molecularly verify every historical rufescens population.

## Overlapping external traits in the study samples {#s-5136241f69}
The authors described strong external resemblance to occidentalis and nympha and noted variation in traits such as presence of maxillary teeth within the broader complex. In the female three-component discriminant analysis, 19 of 36 assigned rufescens individuals were classified elsewhere: one as chinanteca, eight as nympha and ten as occidentalis. These analytical classifications are not formal taxonomic reassignment of those vouchers. The result retains sample and model scope rather than implying that external morphology is always sufficient or always useless for identification.

## Two mitochondrial markers and one nuclear locus define this study {#s-cfc4157032}
The 2012 study combined fieldwork from 2005–2011 with museum tissues, reporting ninety-six individuals from fifty-seven localities across its studied Nanotriton sample. Alignments were 522 bases for 16S, 772 for cytochrome b and 481 for nuclear POMC. POMC alleles were computationally phased; two distinct alleles from one animal are not two independent animals. Separate mitochondrial and nuclear gene trees generally supported four named lineages, but neither an individual locus nor this historical sample certifies every contemporary population or catalogue concept. Contact-zone sampling found no cross-species POMC-allele pairs or mitochondrial–POMC assignment discordance; this is no detected hybrid signature, not proof that hybridization is impossible.

## Internal geographic lineages do not mirror one simple isthmus split {#s-4cb5d2d86a}
The mitochondrial analysis recovered divergent Los Tuxtlas, Mexican/Central American and northwestern Guatemala lineages under the source’s rufescens concept. Samples on opposite sides of the Isthmus of Tehuantepec were not reciprocally monophyletic, and the POMC analysis also separated Los Tuxtlas early from other samples. AMOVA supported differences among populations within regional groups, but not an excess among the three regional groups over its null expectation (p = 0.60). These are sampled genetic patterns, not proof that geography has no effect or a complete current population partition. The source separately reassigned eastern Guatemala, Belize and Honduras samples to nympha.

## Two rate assumptions produce conditional divergence estimates {#s-fb8227f048}
For sampled populations spanning the isthmus, the Results estimated divergence around 3.3 million years with a slower cytochrome-b substitution rate and 1.9 million years with a faster rate. The Discussion instead prints 3.2 for the slower-rate estimate. This source discrepancy is retained without choosing an exact corrected value. The authors lack suitable direct fossil calibrations for the group and compare estimates to a proposed Pliocene seaway. These are rate-dependent lineage estimates, not a measured age of the living species, proof that the seaway caused divergence or an independently verified chronology.

## An unresolved origin and Los Tuxtlas hypothesis remain distinct {#s-c201dd9870}
Although the geographic model placed the ancestral point east of the isthmus, range-centre, northern Oaxaca and Los Tuxtlas alternatives could not be rejected. Long-standing Los Tuxtlas separation and its cited maxillary teeth motivated a suggestion that those populations might warrant a distinct species. The study did not formally establish that new taxon or settle the ancestral location. The variable tooth character and historical name reassignment mean that a familiar rufescens label cannot alone transfer all regional measurements. Such hypotheses remain source-attributed and do not replace the pinned accepted-species hierarchy.

## One sequenced individual and a separate DNA-content estimate {#s-2b434409b3}
The study extracted liver DNA from one Bolitoglossa rufescens specimen as part of a sixteen-species, three-genus comparison, targeting ten gigabases of short-read data per species. Repeat analysis randomly sampled merged reads at roughly 0.1-fold coverage. Table 1 gives a genome C-value estimate of 39.5 picograms, based on blood-smear densitometry and species means from earlier genome-size work. Picograms of DNA are not gigabases, body mass or a complete genome assembly; the sequenced individual and the species-mean DNA-content estimate have different evidence roles. The sample does not determine variation among sexes, populations or ages.

## Detected repeat abundance is a method-dependent lower estimate {#s-8dc6367839}
Table 1 reports 59.1% detectable repeat abundance for Bolitoglossa rufescens, including uncategorized repeats. Table 2 reports LTRs at 17.49%, and Table 3 separates ERV at 7.604% from Ty3/Gypsy at 9.885%. These are percentages of sampled bases identified by the study's repeat library, not percentages of animals, newly inserted elements or measured gene expression. Mutation can degrade repeats beyond recognition; the authors regard short-read masking as likely underestimating true repetitive content. Rounded table categories need not sum exactly, and uncategorized repeats should not be silently assigned to a known TE superfamily.

## Repeat divergence suggests relative expansion histories {#s-1f7157413b}
The study places this species among four Bolitoglossa with ERV peaks at Kimura distances of 11–13, lower than the main peaks in the six larger-genome Bolitoglossa. Their Gypsy/Ty3 profiles have lower, broader peaks extending to smaller distances; rufescens has a reported additional low-distance peak. These are relative sequence-divergence histories, not absolute dates or direct observed insertion rates. The study could not distinguish element deletion from more effective silencing.
