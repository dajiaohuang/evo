COL26.8 lists Brachycephalus ibitinga Condez, Monteiro, Malagoli, Trevine, Schunck, Garcia & Haddad, 2021 as an accepted species, with the recorded path Anura (order) → Brachycephalidae (family) → Brachycephalus (genus). ## Accepted name and biological study {#s-73a1da2703}

## Bone plates beneath the skin {#s-8a23b13918}
Condez and colleagues described Brachycephalus ibitinga as a hyperossified pumpkin toadlet: ornamented bones occur in the skull and along the vertebral column. Two large paravertebral plates on either side of the spine form a dorsal bone shield; the parotic plates extend across the squamosal, pro-otics and auditory capsule. In the adult male holotype (CFBH 44449; snout–vent length 12.5 mm), these structures are part of a diagnosis based on multiple characters, not a stand-alone measure of the whole species.

## Size differences in the type series {#s-acce4d435a}
The reported type series includes 10 adult males, 15 adult females and six juveniles. Within that sample, females averaged 14.6 ± 1.0 mm snout–vent length and males 12.2 ± 0.4 mm; the authors report a significant sex difference. Those means describe the measured adults in this study, not a universal size limit for every population or age class.

## Calls from leaf-litter forest {#s-16026095da}
The authors describe this species as ground-dwelling in preserved Atlantic Forest remnants, where individuals were found under tangled roots, fallen trunks, bromeliads and deep leaf litter. Calling activity was diurnal and concentrated on cloudy or rainy days at about 20°C. The field account is from sampled forest sites in São Paulo; the paper notes that males were rarely exposed while calling, which made natural-history observations difficult.

## A repeated, pulsed call {#s-6914576868}
The advertisement call is one note repeated in sequence, usually with 8–12 pulses per note. The detailed description uses 48 calls from two recorded males: 25 calls from the type locality and 23 from Santo André. Their notes lasted 0.18–0.25 s (mean 0.22 s), at about 1.85 notes per second. The authors also recorded two aggressive-call types, but said their social context needs further study.

## Short introduction {#s-8e79a9f982}
In Brachycephalus ibitinga, a dorsal shield of ornamented bone plates and a pulsed call distinguish the studied species; the field observations come from São Paulo forest remnants.
