## Name and classification in COL26.8 {#s-4297e5a4c3}
The 20 August 2026 COL26.8 release accepts Boulenophrys dongguanensis (J. Wang & Y. Y. Wang in J. Wang et al., 2019) as a species. Its recorded placement is Anura (order) → Megophryidae (family) → Boulenophrys (genus). This identifies the catalogue name and classification; biological observations below retain the scope of their own sources.

## Dongguan localities and corrected administrative wording {#s-a502583c06}
The monograph listed male holotype (the name-bearing specimen designated in the original description) SYS a001973 from Yinping Mountain, Xiegang, Dongguan, at 132 m, and summarized known records near the type locality at 100–300 m. It explicitly corrected the earlier account’s administrative wording: Xiegang and Qingxi are towns in Dongguan, not counties. These are dated type-locality and cited geographic records, without proof of absence from other sites or physiological elevation limits.

## A corrected ten-male type series {#s-021c930950}
The monograph reported ten adult males with snout–vent length (measured from the tip of the snout to the vent)s of 30.2–39.3 mm. It corrected the original description’s inconsistent counts to nine male paratype (an additional specimen in the original type series)s plus the holotype. Selected features included a visible tympanum (the visible membrane of the ear), unnotched tongue, strong vomerine ridges (ridges on bones in the roof of the mouth) with teeth, granular dorsal skin and rudimentary toe webbing without lateral fringes. These measurements concern the corrected male sample; female dimensions and population-wide limits are not supplied by this paragraph.

## A character cited in the gutu comparison {#s-d2eae0a325}
The 2025 gutu description lists Boulenophrys dongguanensis as with vomerine teeth (teeth on bones in the roof of the mouth). That statement belongs to the paper’s comparison; it is not a new measurement series for this species. The gutu adult sample spans 34.4–44.7 mm in seven males and 36.2–52.8 mm in eight females; those numbers belong to gutu, not to this comparator. This single comparison does not show how often the character occurs across the species or provide a complete diagnosis, diet or developmental account.
