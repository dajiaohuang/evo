## Overview {#s-f2672cd9e4}
The Kenya Coast Dwarf Galago is one of several similar-looking, nocturnal eastern African galagos distinguished in part by their calls. After reviewing names and vocal evidence, researchers reassigned some Kenyan coastal records published as “Zanzibar galagos” to this species. Older ecological studies therefore need to be read alongside the later name clarification.

## Social behavior and organization {#s-fe6961b91f}
Harcourt and Nash (1986) used mark–recapture and radio-tracking at two Kenyan coastal forests to study social organization. Their paper used the older name *Galago zanzibaricus*; a later taxonomic review reassigned the Diani and Gedi coastal studies to *G. cocos*. The study described generally non-overlapping adult male ranges shared with one or two females and their offspring, with sleeping groups and radio-tracking documenting local associations.

## Distribution records {#s-2995026c2e}
A 2006 taxonomic and distribution review summarized the species as occurring in coastal forests of Kenya and northeastern Tanzania. That account clarifies some older name usage, but it is not a complete modern distribution survey and does not make historical localities evidence of continuous present-day occupancy.

## Evidence limits {#s-72f94dca96}
The main field behavior evidence comes from two Kenyan forests in the 1980s and was published under a name later revised. Those observations do not establish the species-wide social system, current population size or a complete range.
