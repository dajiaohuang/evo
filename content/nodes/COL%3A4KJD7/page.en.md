## Large leaves, pink-backed flowers, and winged seeds {#s-13f0ce25d0}
The Flora of Panama account describes *Plumeria rubra* as a tree with corky stems, prominent leaf scars, and softly hairy young growth. Leaves reach 40 × 15 cm; their shape is described as obovate, broadest toward the tip, or possibly oblong-elliptic, a long oval. They are mostly hairless above; hairs beneath, especially near the midrib, are marked uncertain. Flower clusters are mostly at shoot tips. The white flowers are flushed pink on their undersides and sometimes have a yellow throat. The fused-petal tube, the joined lower part of the petals, is about 14–18 mm long; broad corolla lobes, the separate flower-petal segments, are 30–45 × 15–25 mm. Flattened follicles, dry fruits that split along one side, measure 20–30 × 2–3 cm; the seed itself is 2.8–3 cm and its wing 3–4 cm. The historical distribution field lists southern Mexico, Central America, and the West Indies, and separately says the plant is widely cultivated in tropical regions of both hemispheres; it does not label the listed places as native occurrences.
