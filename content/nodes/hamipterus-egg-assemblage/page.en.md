## Hamipterus eggs record one place across several layers {#s-fe5e7811c3}
At the Shengjinkou Formation in Xinjiang, one sandstone block measuring 3.28 square metres exposes 215 Hamipterus eggs; the study estimates that roughly 300 may occur in the block. Across the broader sample, 16 eggs contain embryonic bones, and egg-bearing deposits occur at at least four stratigraphic levels.

Repeated layers and embryos make this a rare record of pterosaur reproduction, but the fossils do not directly show why the eggs accumulated there. Storm transport, colonial nesting and site fidelity are interpretations. Claims about hatchling flight or parental care also depend on assumptions about embryonic development and ossification.
