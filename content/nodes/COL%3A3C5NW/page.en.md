## A regional description with eight-ribbed berries {#s-a2d6beac96}
An undated historical Flora of Panama account describes the plant as a shrub 3–5 m high, matching a separate habit field that says “shrubs.” Young twig tips bear very short hairs that the source calls “dibrachiate” but does not define; the twigs soon become nearly hairless. The leathery, hairless leaves are elliptical or elliptical-oblong, 7–13 cm long and 4–5 cm wide, with acute bases and bluntly tapering tips. The midrib is sunken above and prominent below; about 12 pairs of lateral veins are faint above and slightly raised beneath. The petiole is 8–10 mm long. A short raceme, a flower axis bearing stalked flowers, has 2–8 flowers, is no longer than the petiole and bears sparse silky hairs; pedicels are 4–8 mm long. Flowers have four parts: the two outer sepals are round and 2.5–3 mm long, the inner pair 1–1.5 mm; obovate petals are 3–4 mm. The outside of the inverted-cone ovary bears hairs that become sparse; it has two chambers, each with 12–18 ovules attached to a structure the field calls “peltate.” Berries are obovate-oblong, distinctly eight-ribbed and up to 1.5 cm long. The source does not identify the material's life status or give a sampling frame or observation date.

## A historical regional distribution statement {#s-d7191de9d5}
The Flora of Panama distribution field lists the Lesser Antilles—Guadeloupe, Martinique and Dominica—and Panama. This is the historical regional statement in that source version, limited to the places literally listed; it does not establish a current range, a global inventory or whether records are native, introduced, cultivated or wild.
