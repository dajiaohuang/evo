## Did familiar callers change the monkeys' alarm responses? {#s-bf703372cf}
Researchers played recordings of familiar and unfamiliar male alarm callers that signaled leopard or eagle threats to wild Diana monkeys at two sites: Taï Forest, Côte d’Ivoire (20 monkeys, August–December 2013), and Tiwai Island, Sierra Leone (23 monkeys, January–March 2014). Response acoustics varied with caller familiarity at both sites, but the familiar-versus-unfamiliar pattern differed between them. Familiarity affected responses to leopard alarms at Taï, but not at Tiwai. These playback results concern the tested calls and groups; they do not measure actual predation risk or map a complete social network.
