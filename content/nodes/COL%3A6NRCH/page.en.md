## Limits of these trait data {#s-a22ce05fd4}
This account paraphrases the same-name ReptTraits v1-2 entry, retaining its 2023 taxonomic framework and sources for individual traits. One row per species compresses variation in sex, age, locality and populations; matching name and family do not ensure equivalent later concepts. Reported maximum length is not an absolute species limit, and reproductive means are not fixed counts in every clutch. Many mass entries are estimated from length equations and are not presented here as measured mass. Juvenile-length headers and definitions differ and are also omitted from reader conclusions. Conservation, venom, current distribution and population trends are not updated here; unrecorded fields remain gaps. Dataset citation labels preserve the source chain and do not imply that this project independently reread every original work.

## Different stages in the mass fields {#s-43df67b202}
Historical summary values are adult body mass 9.87 g; mass at birth or hatching 0.2 g. Adult mass can combine males, females and adults of unspecified sex; birth/hatching mass describes a different life stage. These are not a longitudinal series of the same individuals or a species-wide range of individual masses.

## Distinguish the measured length {#s-00ee21b7d4}
Recorded dimensions are reported maximum snout–vent length (SVL) 114 mm; female maximum snout–vent length (SVL) 77.6 mm. SVL runs from snout to vent and differs from total length including the tail. Compiled maxima are reported material, not theoretical growth limits or typical individual size. Sex-specific values may come from different localities or studies and alone cannot quantify population sexual dimorphism.

## Reproduction and developmental timing {#s-8dfef94b80}
Available compilation fields are offspring per litter or eggs per clutch 5.8 offspring/eggs; oviposition to hatching 48 d; d denotes days. The litter/clutch field combines offspring or egg counts, and a fractional summary does not mean a single event produced a fractional individual. Timing fields refer to different events and cannot be exchanged for one uniform reproductive cycle.

## A longest record differs from usual lifespan {#s-fdefbb038a}
The archive’s maximum_longevity_y value is 12 years, taking the largest record in the collected material under the source method. It is not typical lifespan, mean wild lifespan, a survival ceiling for every individual or a current repeatedly attainable record.

## How to read reproductive records {#s-9b716601f9}
the reproductive-mode field uses the original label oviparous; sex determination is recorded as GSD (ZW/ZZ); consolidated mean offspring or eggs per litter/clutch 8.4; lowest reported clutch/litter mean 1; highest reported clutch/litter mean 21; consolidated litters/clutches per year 1. Clutch/litter summaries can use midpoints of reported means or, when means are unavailable, extrema; they are not necessarily arithmetic averages of observed clutches. Fractions do not describe non-integer young in a litter. The lowest/highest fields are defined as endpoints of reported means rather than guaranteed smallest/largest individual clutches. Reproductive and sex-determination labels do not establish fetal nutrition or uniformity across all populations.

## MDHC 176–178: vertebral samples {#s-6ac5802268}
Tschopp (2016) examined MDHC 176, 177, 178 as part of a museum skeletal comparison. Vertebral structures were inspected under a light microscope. The material comes from the MDHC collection at the University of Turin; no additional animals were collected for this study.

## What the vertebrae show {#s-cd3868b039}
The centrum-to-prezygapophysis connection occasionally forms a distinct crest, without a consistent positional pattern. A midline notch in the interprezygapophyseal lamina was observed at presacral positions 13–19; the author discusses incomplete neural-arch fusion as a possible explanation.

## Evidence limits {#s-dfb6c8ac88}
These are observations of the listed museum specimens, not population estimates or a complete species account. Serial variation, skeletal maturity and sample size limit generalization. Distribution, behaviour, conservation status and a validated species diagnosis are not established by this source.
