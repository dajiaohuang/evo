## Workshop vocabulary and later species naming {#s-c06f7ebf79}
The underlying workshop used an opportunity sample of 13 volunteer native isiZulu speakers, aged18–55, from five Zululand areas: ten participants on28November and three on1December2016 at Tembe Elephant Park. Participants viewed frog photographs and discussed names collectively, resolving disagreements by discussion. The six broad names elicited during the workshop did not individually distinguish all58 species. Species-level names were assigned afterwards with Bongani Mkhize, combining this vocabulary with earlier published names and naming guidelines. These group discussions do not supply independent per-person frequencies, dialect-wide agreement, or a test of recognition accuracy for Sclerophrys pusilla.

## What a local naming category can establish {#s-6c307f3687}
The study distinguishes broad folk categories from the scientific species labels used in the compilation. Several folk categories span multiple scientific genera or families; their correspondence was assessed within this regional set, not across all African amphibians. Names referring to habitat, appearance, locality or calls are linguistic choices in this naming exercise. Their wording alone is not a new measurement of habitat preference, coloration, acoustic behavior or geographic restriction for Sclerophrys pusilla. The2019 table is also not a current occupancy survey or an exhaustive present-day Zululand checklist. No independent linguistic validation or current community-usage survey is claimed here.
