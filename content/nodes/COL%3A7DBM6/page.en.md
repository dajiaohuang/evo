## Comparative results retain their measurement limits {#s-cfca0285b6}
T. kweichowensis differed from several members of the Tylototriton subgenus but did not occupy a separate morphospace supporting a separate subgenus. Its cranial centroid size resembled Okinawa E. andersoni and exceeded Amami E. andersoni and many congeners; Chelotriton was not larger than kweichowensis in the reported comparison. Centroid size is a landmark measure, not body mass. Historical Qiantriton proposals are discussed as background rather than adopted as a new revision.
