## Name and classification in COL26.8 {#s-5756676aaa}
COL26.8 lists Brachycephalus verrucosus Ribeiro, Firkowski, Bornschein & Pie, 2015 as a species in Anura, family Brachycephalidae, genus Brachycephalus. This is the classification in the release dated 20 August 2026; it does not reconcile names in other taxonomic or fossil databases.

## Two different kinds of biological record {#s-565ebc9c82}
The 2025 comparative study reports specimen measurements, colour observations, call data and a mapped conservation assessment. A separate morphology v3 table contains one coded measurement row. Their samples and methods differ; neither source alone gives a complete account of the species.

## The morphology table contains one coded row {#s-1e632e13d9}
The morphology v3 table places this name in Anura, family Brachycephaloidea, unlike the Brachycephalidae placement in COL26.8; the table's classification does not resolve concept identity. Row 864 lists SVL 11.35, HL 3.72 and HW 4.38, with additional values in ED, IOD, IND, EN, TL and THL. The source reader leaves sex unspecified. One table row is not necessarily one unique animal, and the absolute units are unconfirmed, so these values cannot be treated as comparable dimensions or a sex-based diagnosis. Blank cells do not show that a structure is absent.

## The study measured different numbers for each sex {#s-ad9def26f9}
In comparative Table 2 of the 2025 study, male snout–vent length (SVL) is 9.3–11.7 mm (N = 20), and female SVL is 10.4–13.2 mm (N = 9). The authors describe dorsal and lateral skin as densely rough, each with n = 29. Counts belong to individual variables and cannot be pooled into one sample size. The ranges describe examined animals, not species-wide maxima or a statistical test of sexual dimorphism. The authors note that smooth skin may include small, inconspicuous warts; externally absent digits alone do not establish that bones are absent.

## Colour changes after preservation {#s-4fecb3e544}
The authors describe live animals as pale green, with an orange-yellow head, dorsal midline and belly. After preservation, these bright areas appear cream against darker tissue. These are qualitative comparisons, not standardized colour-chart readings. Colour and specimen counts vary by trait, and similar colours occur in other species, so hue alone is not a complete identification rule. Preserved appearance cannot stand in for live colour.

## The recorded calls contain notes and pulses {#s-b83aef0ecf}
The note-centred comparison records note groups as present (N = 11), attenuated notes as present (N = 11), a maximum of 2 notes per group (N = 11), and up to 8 pulses per note (N = 11). Here N counts animals with one or more calls recorded and analysed, not the total notes or complete calls. A call sequence, an isolated note, a note group and a pulse are different units. These values describe the examined recordings, not fixed limits for every population; earlier call-centred descriptions may use different units.

## The mapped assessment proposes least concern {#s-dd5230a148}
Table 7 reports an observed elevation range of 455–945 metres above sea level, not a physiological altitude tolerance. It gives an extent of occurrence (EOO) of 4 square kilometres, lower-bound grid area of occupancy (AOO) 4, upper-bound AOO ---, and 1 threat-defined location. The dash means the mapped estimate is unavailable, not that occupied area is zero. The study proposes Least Concern (LC) in its assessment; this is a dated research result, not a current official Red List check. Records, potential-habitat polygons, convex-hull EOO and two-kilometre grid AOO have different meanings. One location is not one specimen, and modeled management or recovery scenarios are not measured intervention outcomes or a population census.

## Limits of these records {#s-2d83ebfc80}
The 2025 paper summarizes selected specimens, analysed calls and a mapped assessment; its original specimens, recordings and supplementary GIS have not been independently reanalysed here. The linked morphology row has unconfirmed absolute units and does not identify a unique specimen. Neither table counts nor map estimates establish a current population census.
