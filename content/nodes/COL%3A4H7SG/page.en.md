## Accepted name and placement {#s-a168eb2258}
COL26.8 accepts Phyllomedusa sauvagii Boulenger, 1882 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Phyllomedusidae (family) → Phyllomedusa (genus). This name and placement follow the COL26.8 release of 20 August 2026; the same spelling in another checklist or fossil database may refer to a different taxonomic concept.

COL26.8 records the accepted name and its hierarchical placement. Those catalogue fields establish taxonomic identity within that release; they do not by themselves establish this taxon’s anatomy, ecology, life history, or distribution.

## A source-coded development mode and oviposition environment {#s-0bc0aa2f88}
The Atlantic Forest trait compilation codes Phyllomedusa sauvagii as having larval development and its oviposition as terrestrial. A larval-development code does not specify larval duration, all larval habitats or the degree of dependence on standing water; those require additional evidence. These are literature-compiled categories, not newly observed spawning or a developmental experiment for this species. Exact correspondence between the source taxonomy and the current accepted concept remains uncertified.

## Juvenile habitat and egg-site categories have defined scope {#s-a90b6af03b}
The same row codes juvenile habitat as aquatic and marks arboreal eggs attached to plants among the seven oviposition-site categories. The positive category summarizes a broad trait; it does not establish a specific nest architecture, substrate species or oviposition height. A juvenile-habitat code is not automatically an adult habitat preference. The compiled record does not newly establish clutch size, egg dimensions, parental attendance, spawning season or offspring survival.

## Compiled lengths retain inference and measurement limits {#s-10073373f7}
The row explicitly marks head width and tibia length as inferred, not an observed measurement from a new specimen. The article fills missing traits with a phylogenetic maximum-likelihood procedure; across the full pool, 139 head widths and 146 tibia lengths were inferred. Body length, head width and tibia length are retained numerically in the row-level evidence snapshot. The Methods use means when available and otherwise maxima, while the workbook length headers do not explicitly state units and do not consistently identify sex or stage. These cells are therefore not presented as new diagnostic millimetre measurements, a sex-specific range or measured species-wide variation. Upstream reference entries are preserved, but their original measurements have not been independently checked.
