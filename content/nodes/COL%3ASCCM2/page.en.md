## Accepted name and classification {#s-213d4ce389}
In the 20 August 2026 COL26.8 release, Pristimantis anaiae Ortega, Brito-M. & Ron, 2022 is an accepted species. Its recorded classification path is Anura (order) → Strabomantidae (family) → Pristimantis (genus).

## Reading the source records {#s-e67ac912f0}
The catalogue name is accompanied by selected observations or compiled values from named sources. Each result remains tied to the source concept and any stated sample, place, period, and method; together they do not make a complete account of the species.

## Six males and an unknown female size {#s-a834b2bce4}
The anaiae description reported adult male SVL 13.08–20.63 mm, average 17.46 mm, from six males; female size was unknown. The diagnosis included no tympanic membrane despite a small visible annulus, no vocal slits or nuptial pads, an eyelid conical tubercle and basal toe webbing. Membrane absence is not proof of deafness, and absent slits are not a measured inability to vocalize. The group’s pooled female ranges cannot be assigned to anaiae. These are diagnostic-sample observations rather than population-wide size limits or an independently complete identification key.

## Zarentza forest records and nighttime perches {#s-875ecc2f01}
The study knew anaiae around Zarentza Community in Llanganates National Park, Pastaza, at 1,350–1,419 m. Individuals were collected at night on leaves or branches 100–250 cm above ground in primary and secondary forest. The broader 1,300–3,600 m elevation assigned to the Eastern Montane Forest ecosystem is not the species’ measured range. Perch heights are encounter records, not a complete vertical niche, maximum climbing capacity or breeding substrate. Presence in secondary forest does not quantify demographic performance relative to intact forest.

## A dated Data Deficient proposal and land-cover proxy {#s-c2baa53a74}
The authors recommended Data Deficient because population size and full distribution were poorly known. Their 2018 land-cover map indicated agricultural or settlement deforestation over 10% of the area within five kilometres of known sites. This buffered land-cover statistic is not a measured 10% loss of anaiae individuals, complete occupied area or current assessment. The suggestion of adaptability from secondary-forest occurrence and likely additional localities remained interpretations. Neither a dated recommendation nor road distance certifies present population security.
