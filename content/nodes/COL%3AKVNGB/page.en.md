## Foraging among four species {#brief}
In a Florida Keys field study, four Sparisoma species differed in how they took food from reef surfaces: three mainly tore algae, while S. viride more often scraped or excavated. These observations concern named Caribbean species and sampled sites, not every member of the genus in every habitat.

## Tearing, scraping and excavating {#feeding-bites}
Adam and colleagues observed nine Caribbean parrotfish species off Key Largo, Florida, during summers 2013 and 2014; four belonged to Sparisoma. Individual-bite observations grouped S. aurofrenatum, S. chrysopterum and S. rubripinne among fishes that mainly tore algae from the reef. S. viride grouped with fishes that scraped and excavated epilithic algal matrix (a mat of algae on the rock surface), endolithic algae (growing within the reef material) and crustose coralline algae (CCA). A tearing bite means algae are pulled from the surface; scraping or excavating also contacts or removes material from the reef framework. This account is limited to the observed species, sizes, sites and periods.
