## Searsia montana {#s-5766ccc1e0}
Two regional descriptions emphasize different leaflet counts and warn that isolated specimens can be difficult to identify from leaf form alone.

## Leaflet counts vary across the cited accounts {#s-09e8950025}
The 1997 account describes a slender deciduous shrub or small tree, single- or multistemmed and up to 2.25 m. It records leaves as consistently 4–7-foliolate, with sessile, generally toothed leaflets from elliptic to narrowly elliptic-obovate; terminal leaflets measure (27-)44(-68) × (8-)12(-16) mm. The drupe is circular and obloid. A 1993 diagnostic excerpt separately discusses 5(-7)-foliolate plants, while noting that entire or toothed, three- and five-foliolate leaves can occur on the same plant, often on separate branches. It says this makes isolated herbarium sheets difficult to identify. Habit, underside venation and membranous leaflets are emphasized; trifoliolate specimens may be confused with *R. transvaalensis*, *R. grandidens*, *R. dentata* or, more rarely, *R. pyroides* var. *gracilis*. A habitat entry says “scrub forest margins.” The excerpts are regional accounts, not a quantified survey of variation.
