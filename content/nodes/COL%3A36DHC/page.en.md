## Diosma arenicola {#s-372dc59f26}
Regional accounts distinguish *Diosma arenicola* from similar species by its crisped-ciliate leaves and bracteoles, crowded sessile flowers and limestone-sand habitat.

## Crisped leaf margins and crowded sessile flowers {#s-f2036c186e}
The 1979 diagnostic account describes lanceolate leaves with a straight sharp tip, crisped-ciliate margins and a midrib that is often sparsely hairy. Linear bracteoles are about 4 mm long, crisped-ciliate and pubescent along the midrib; flowers are crowded and sessile. A 2012 account notes alternate leaves on mature plants and long-crisped cilia on bracts and sepals. It distinguishes *D. awilana* by broader, more recurved leaf tips, shorter and less hairy bracteoles, and longer fruit horns; it calls *D. subulata* the closest species but describes it as more glabrous, with short-ciliate bracts, bracteoles and calyx lobes, and reduced racemes rather than crowded sessile flowers. The recorded habitat is calcareous sand on limestone. These are comparisons in regional accounts, not a measured survey of variation.
