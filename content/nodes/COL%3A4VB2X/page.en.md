## Whorled upper leaves and a grooved corona appendage {#s-52afbe3e2e}
The regional account describes sturdy, unbranched stems 60–90 cm tall. At least the upper leaves are in whorls of three or inserted irregularly; blades are oblong-triangular, 29–34 × 4–15 mm, on 1–3 mm petioles. Each stem bears 3–8 inflorescences with 4–7 flowers; peduncles are 0–10 mm and pedicels 5–10 mm. Calyx segments are 4 × 1.5 mm. Green corolla lobes with purple veins are 8–10 × 5.5 mm and replicate; they are glabrous dorsally and ventrally except for a basal pubescent patch, with glabrous margins. The corona is about 5 mm long and exceeds the gynostegium; its colour is unknown. Each lobe has an obtriangular base that narrows abruptly into an erect, narrowly oblong appendage about 3 mm long. A transverse dorsal ridge marks the junction; the appendage has a longitudinal ventral groove and a bilobed tip. Triangular anther appendages are about 1.5 mm long and lie against the side and top of the gynostegial head. The corpusculum is 0.4–0.52 mm, without flanges; pollinia attach subapically and are reniform, 0.95–1.03 mm long, with the germination zone occupying the outer half. Fruit is unknown. The habitat entry places it in grassland near streams and on forest margins. These regional descriptions do not establish developmental or population-level variation.
