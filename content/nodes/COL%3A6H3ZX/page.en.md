## Leaves, flowers and coiled pods {#s-f3e22dea12}
The historical Flora of Panama account describes small trees or shrubs, usually leafless when flowering. Twigs dry yellow and carry spines up to 0.8 cm long with appressed hairs. Terminal leaflets are ovate-lanceolate or rhombic-ovate, 8–30 × 4–15 cm, with long tapering tips and wedge-shaped to squared-off bases; they are papery and sparsely hairy to nearly hairless. Petioles are 10–15 cm long and sometimes spiny; narrow, lance-shaped stipules reach 2 cm. Flower clusters are about 15 cm long and densely woolly near the top. The bell-shaped calyx is about 15 mm long; the fleshy, hairless standard (banner petal) is narrow-oblong, about 60 × 12 mm. The ovary is sickle-shaped, about 20 × 1.5 mm, densely covered with soft appressed hairs; the curved style is about 17 mm. Mature pods are partly woody, coiled and twisted, with an irregularly wavy, scalloped lower seam. Seeds are uniformly scarlet and soft, becoming wrinkled when dry. This historical regional description gives no sampling frame or observation date and is not a current or global account.

## A historical range statement {#s-56f6966b72}
The same Flora of Panama source places the species in Central America from Honduras to Panama. This is a historical regional statement, not a current range or global inventory; the source does not classify these records as native, introduced, cultivated, escaped or wild.
