## Overview {#s-846b15045f}
COL26.8 accepts RYYR as Cebus aequatorialis J. A. Allen, 1914. MDD v2.5 lists C. aequatorialis as a synonym of C. albifrons. This page follows the pinned COL identity; the 2012 field study used the combination C. albifrons aequatorialis.

## Distribution records {#s-3cc567f545}
Jack and Campos combined on-foot searches with interviews at 11 forested sites in western Ecuador. They confirmed records at seven, including four new localities. They could not complete censuses in two large, remote national protected areas, so those results were presence/absence only. Density estimates at five intensively censused private reserves ranged from 2 to 22 animals per km²; the 22/km² estimate at the 138-ha Jauneche fragment was a clear outlier.

## Ecological evidence {#s-0c6c3c78cb}
At Cerro Blanco, the researchers estimated home ranges of 507 and 561 ha for two groups. Across study sites, groups were usually multi-male and multi-female, averaging 13.9 individuals (range 5–20). At Cerro Blanco, the monkeys used mature forest near streams most strongly but also used degraded forest frequently. These are local results from 2002–2007, not a current range-wide population assessment.

## Evidence limits {#s-898eedbad2}
A source-linked regional study guide. Locality, count and group data come from 2002–2007 surveys in western Ecuador and do not provide a current complete range, range-wide abundance or conservation assessment.
