## Flowers and leaves {#s-f4a3812d2c}
The 2012 regional account describes an acaulescent, corm-bearing geophyte and records its size as 10–15 mm. Its stems reach ground level; the leaves are firm, upright, linear, and softly hairy. Several flowers form short, upright spikes. The flowers are radially symmetrical and purple with a red centre; their lower margins are often white. The perianth tube is elongated, the anthers upright, the inner bracts forked at the tip, and the ovary smooth.

These traits come from a regional South African account published in 2012; the excerpt gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency.

## Radial flowers and comparison with Babiana sambucina {#s-d28e55e0de}
A 2020 diagnostic account recognizes *B. radiata* by its radially symmetrical pink-purple flower, shallow bowl-shaped tepals, and short style whose branches extend between the lower parts of the anthers. The stem is subterranean, with only the spike above ground. Its linear leaves are 2–4(–7) mm wide and usually have fine, long hairs. Large, soft, pale-green floral bracts, with the inner bracts forked at the tip, and a smooth ovary place it in section Teretifolieae. The account suggests it may be most closely related to the widespread *B. sambucina*.

*B. sambucina* has broader, noticeably pleated leaves, usually with short hairs or a rough surface, and bilaterally symmetrical flowers, usually blue to violet. The lower lateral tepals have bold central white blotches outlined in darker blue or, rarely, red-purple. The account describes the radial flower of *B. radiata* as an independent development within the section.

These diagnostic details come from a regional South African account published in 2020; the excerpt gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency.
