## What photographs and bone sections show {#s-9a600bd6bc}
Photographs in the comparative study show preserved skull fragments, while a reconstruction shades the areas that remain. Thin sections from wing bones help estimate growth stage. Wingspan values are estimates that fill missing bones by comparison with other skeletons. Each method answers a different question: a complete drawing does not mean the skull is complete, near cessation of growth does not establish the largest adult size, and an estimated wingspan does not reveal diet or flight performance.

## Taxonomic names, definitions and dates {#s-0235394063}
The PBDB supplementary directory currently places Haliskia and Ferrodraco within Anhangueridae, with Ferrodraco nested in Tropeognathinae. The 2019 Ferrodraco description uses Ornithocheiridae, while the 2024 Haliskia study discusses analyses with different matrices and clade definitions. These records document distinct classification choices; they do not combine into one uncontested family tree.

## Ferrodraco and Haliskia: two Australian holotypes {#s-c75302dcf6}
Two species illustrate how the evidence differs: Ferrodraco lentoni is represented here by AODF 876, and Haliskia peterseni by KK F1426. ## Limits of the selected Anhangueridae records {#s-71c26c361e}
This is a selected path through two holotype studies and comparative material, not a complete family review. Published studies use different specimens and taxonomic definitions, and the selected species pages do not form a complete descendant catalogue. Occurrences, collections, and species are different counting units. Database age bounds do not give exact origin or extinction times, and the record set does not supply a shared ecological account for Anhangueridae.
