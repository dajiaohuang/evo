## Overview {#s-671a13fee7}
COL26.8 accepts Mirza zaza Kappeler & Roos in Kappeler et al., 2005 as species ID 43MKK under genus ID 5TQ2; MDD v2.5 supplies the display name Northern Giant Mouse Lemur. The field studies below concern nests and density in Sahamalaza forests in north-western Madagascar.

## Nest-group records {#s-478c51672c}
At Ankarafa Forest, researchers radio-tagged five males and three females and followed them for 2.5 months in the dry season. Up to four animals shared one to three group-exclusive nests for periods of up to 50 days. Nest trees were tall and large, with canopy cover and many lianas. The short, small-sample study documents nest sharing at this site; its authors called for observation across a full season.

## Local density estimates {#s-aeb49f3a76}
A 2019–2022 transect study covered 107 km across Anabohazo and fragmented Ankarafa forests and recorded 237 M. zaza detections; some animals may have been seen on multiple walks. The pooled estimate was 0.87 individuals/ha (95% CI 0.70–1.07). Estimated density did not differ significantly among the four surveyed forest-core and forest-edge areas. This is a local estimate, not a range-wide population count or a test of long-term fragmentation effects.

## Evidence limits {#s-f5a3c848e6}
Nest observations cover eight radio-tagged animals over 2.5 dry-season months at one forest. The density paper samples two forests and four local core/edge areas; repeat sightings were possible. Neither source supports a current range-wide population estimate or a general claim that fragmentation has no effect. The Chinese display name is editorial.
