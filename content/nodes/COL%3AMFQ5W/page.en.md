## Accepted name and placement {#s-ff0049a620}
COL26.8 accepts Chiropterotriton perotensis Parra-Olea, García-Castillo, Rovito, Maisano, Hanken & Wake, 2020 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Caudata (order) → Plethodontidae (family) → Chiropterotriton (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## Twenty types and a female locality with kilometre-scale uncertainty {#s-bcd14195a9}
Female holotype MVZ 200693 was collected at Valle Alegre on 26 August 1982 at 3020 m. Its coordinate estimate had a maximum error distance of 1.142 km. Nineteen paratypes comprised twelve males and seven females. The diagnosis gave twelve adult males mean SVL 29.7 mm, range 26.5–32.8 mm, and eight adult females mean 31.7 mm, range 27.4–34.3 mm. The point is not presented as more precise than its recorded uncertainty, and the adult sample does not establish species-wide size limits.

## Short limbs and relatively small hands and feet {#s-13c443eb0a}
The diagnosis described a stout body, short snout, small eyes and small hands and feet with little or no webbing. Adpressed limbs never overlapped in the measured sample: male limb interval averaged 2.5 costal folds and female interval 3.3. Male maxillary teeth averaged 7.2, range 2–17, and females 27.9, range 19–36. The male mental gland was oval but not especially prominent. These are sampled anatomical summaries, not a measured reproductive role, universal tooth number or locomotor-performance experiment.

## Live colours from referred specimens, not the type series {#s-6682c11df7}
No live-colour notes were available for the type series. Referred specimens were dark, sometimes with reddish-brown or obscure brown-grey dorsal stripes; adults could be nearly black, while juveniles had less distinct bands. The preserved holotype was uniformly dark brown with a paler venter and blackish-brown tail. The provenance and life stage of these colour observations remain separate; live type colours are not invented, and colour variation alone does not certify every historical identification.

## Cofre de Perote from forest to the summit {#s-8ce30a1aea}
The account reported pine-and-fir forest and tree-line-to-summit localities on Cofre de Perote, Veracruz, at 2950–4015 m. Specimens occurred under terrestrial objects and were active on road banks and boulders at night; several other named salamanders occurred sympatrically. These are locality and encounter observations, not a critical cold threshold, proof of exclusive nocturnality, evidence of road dependence or an experimentally demonstrated partition of resources among species.
