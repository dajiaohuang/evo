## Stem surface {#s-5dfc85e859}
The Brazilian Flora 2020 record describes a strigose stem surface, with stiff hairs lying close to the surface and pointing forward. Simple hairs are present. Unbranched bristles are absent, while branched bristles bear small barbs (they are barbellate). Glandular hairs, prickles, and short lateral shoots (brachyblasts) are absent.

## Leaves and inflorescences {#s-b163e5ac8e}
Stipules, small structures at leaf bases, are caducous and fall early. Extrafloral nectaries (nectar-secreting glands outside flowers) and projections between pairs of pinnae (the leaf’s primary side divisions) are absent. The inflorescence, or flower cluster, is globose; floral bracts (small leaf-like structures) are shorter than the flowers before they open (anthesis).

## Flowers {#s-096e784e20}
The perianth (calyx and corolla together) is recorded as having four parts. The membranous calyx, the outer sepal whorl, has pappus-like lobes resembling a tuft of fine bristles or scales. Corolla (petal-whorl) lobes have one to three veins and a silky covering of hairs. The androecium (the stamens collectively) is diplostemonous, with two stamen whorls; the filaments are pink.

## Habitat category {#s-2102f4c1f6}
The regional dataset labels the habitat “Terrícola,” Portuguese for terrestrial. This category does not give a collection locality or establish the species’ full range.
