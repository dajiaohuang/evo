## Aloe kamnelii: long racemes and toothed leaves {#s-3cf5d91f35}
A South African regional account describes a succulent perennial shrub reported as up to 1.5 m long, branching from the base and rarely solitary, with persistent dry leaves. The glossy, grass-green leaves are distinctly lineate on both surfaces and linear-lanceolate, 30–40 cm long. Their margins are reddish brown and cartilaginous, with dark-brown teeth. Simple, loose, ascending racemes are cylindrical and taper toward the tip, 80–100 cm long. The flowers are yellow, green-tipped in bud and 45 mm long; the stamens and style project slightly beyond them.

These characters and measurements come from a regional South African flora account cited to a 2012 publication. The excerpt gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency and does not establish variation across the full range. It does not independently classify the material as native, naturalised, cultivated or domesticated.

The account records shale outcrops in succulent karoo as habitat.
