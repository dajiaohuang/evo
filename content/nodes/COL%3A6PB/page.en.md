## Measured records and where to start {#s-2028e25b32}
The available AVONET v7 records include measurements for 36 Ardeidae species. For examples, start with Agamia agami, Ardea cinerea, Ardea cocoi, Ardea goliath and Ardea melanocephala. Navigation follows pinned COL26.8, while biological values retain the source's historical names and sampling scope.

## Differences between records {#s-5f0c4ba39b}
Across these records, compiled mean mass ranges from 123 g for Zebrilus undulatus to 4468 g for Ardea goliath. Recorded habitat categories include Forest, Wetland, Coastal and Riverine; trophic niches include Aquatic predator and Omnivore. These examples help compare records but do not define traits shared by the family or list every descendant.

## Species pages with historical life-history fields {#s-01c8bb7041}
Thirty-one historical life-history accounts are available; examples include Ardea alba, Ardea cinerea, Ardea cocoi, Ardea goliath and Ardea herodias. Frequently represented fields are adult body mass (31 entries), offspring per litter or eggs per clutch (31), time from oviposition to hatching (31), and age at flight capability (22; some sources describe leaving the nest). These counts describe the records summarized here, not all accepted species. Compare values only after checking each field's event, unit and source.

## Ecology records with two or more field groups {#s-9133ee9b85}
Forty-four historical entries have at least two usable ecological field groups. Examples are Tigrisoma mexicanum, Tigrisoma lineatum, Tigriornis leucolopha, Cochlearius cochlearius and Zonerodius heliosylus. Food-category weights and foraging strata are available for 44 entries each; mammalian activity-period data are available for none. Check source scope and distinguish coarse weights from categorical or binary codes before comparing records. These counts do not cover every accepted descendant or describe family-wide ecology.

## Ecology records with a single field group {#s-4666282962}
Two historical entries have exactly one usable ecological field group: Tigrisoma fasciatum and Zebrilus undulatus. Neither has food-category weights or mammalian activity-period data; both have a foraging-stratum record. That narrow coverage is not a family-wide ecological pattern or a complete list of accepted descendants.

## Evidence limits {#s-36756e98d6}
The measurement summaries cover records with a one-to-one historical crosswalk and matching catalogue name and family, not every accepted descendant. Mass endpoints compare compiled species means; they are not individual minimum and maximum measurements. Each species page carries its own taxonomic and sampling limits.
The 31 life-history accounts have at least three source-supported focal fields. They retain limits from compiled medians, maximum-lifespan records and historical taxonomy. A family-level route does not imply shared reproduction or life history across descendants; other sources are needed for a fuller account and current status.
The ecological summaries cover unique historical same-name, same-family records: 44 with at least two usable field groups and two with exactly one. They do not make descendant food types, estimated weights or activity patterns traits of the whole family. Semiquantitative conversion, historical taxonomy and possible congeneric borrowing in ABC fields remain relevant to each record.

## What is readable and what remains uninterpreted {#s-817bb5395c}
The seven limited-ecology records each have a habitat, feeding-niche, primary-lifestyle and migration category. Interpret them using the original definitions, and distinguish categorical labels and historical maps from individual observations. Shared labels do not establish a lifestyle for every descendant. Beak, wing, tarsus, tail and mass fields are not interpreted here and cannot support a family-wide size comparison.

## Limits of these historical data {#s-2b1683d476}
These limited historical ecology records do not cover every accepted descendant. COL26.8 navigation, same-name and same-family associations, and two one-to-one name crosswalks do not erase later differences in species concepts. Coarse categories are not independent behavioral observations, and historical maps are not current occupancy or conservation assessments. The linked morphology and mass sources are not interpreted here as direct measurements of the species.
