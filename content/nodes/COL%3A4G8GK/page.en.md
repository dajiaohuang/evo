## A cordate leaf with side-specific vein counts {#s-c29105e77f}
The accepted name follows Catalogue of Life COL26.8 usage 4G8GK and a matched World Flora Online usage wfo-0000269059. The biological details below come from the Brazilian Flora 2020 regional morphology field.

COL26.8 accepted species ID 4G8GK and exact name/authorship map through exactly one accepted WFO usage wfo-0000269059; the Brazilian Flora source record carries the same WFO ID.

The flora field for Philodendron toshibae combines a cordate leaf base with several side-specific vein counts and a spadix whose female and male zones differ in shape. It also records a mature cream berry and a subglobose seed.

## Morphology and flowers {#s-1a59834c0d}
The source records clasping roots, an erect stem, and a pale-green prophyll that is not persistent. The inconspicuous leaf sheath contrasts with a smooth, cylindrical petiole that is slightly rough near its tip, convex above and rounded below. The ovate blade has an acuminate tip and cordate base, with entire margins. The field lists 8–9 primary lateral veins on both sides, then separately records 2 acroscopic and 1 basiscopic vein; it also describes the basiscopic side as rounded and slightly asymmetric. The axillary, sympodial inflorescence is one unit. Its short subcylindrical peduncle expands near the spathe. The slightly constricted spathe has a cream-and-carmine outer tube, a greenish-cream inner tube, and an inner blade listed as light green or cream. The spadix is recorded as subsessile or stipitate (on a short stalk), with a subcylindrical female zone, a sterile-male zone broader at its base, and a cylindrical fertile-male zone with an obtuse tip.

The field gives 3 or 5 short stamens and short staminodes. The gynoecium and ovary are subcylindrical and narrowed at the base. It lists 3 or 4 locules, 15 hemianatropous (partly inverted) ovules, and axial placentation. Hairs are recorded on the funicle, the stalk of an ovule; the stigma is discoid. The mature berry is cylindrical and cream-coloured, and the seed subglobose.
