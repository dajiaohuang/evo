## Ecological evidence {#s-d781ca14d7}
During follows of a wild western-chimpanzee community at Bossou, Guinea, in 2012–2013, mature forest was the most selected habitat overall. In noncultivated habitat, feeding records occurred more often than expected beyond 200 m from fields and less often than expected in the 0–100 m and 101–200 m bands. The authors suggested that field-associated risk may influence feeding locations. These results describe one site and community.

## Life history {#s-dfadbc5e29}
Among 36 known-age wild females at Gombe National Park, mean age at sexual maturity was 11.5 years (range 8.5–13.9), and mean age at first birth was 14.9 years (11.1–22.1). First-birth estimates came from a smaller, censored subset and may be biased low because non-dispersing females were overrepresented. These are Gombe population estimates, not fixed species-wide constants.

## Development {#s-6cd34eaa9a}
A cross-sectional video sample from three neighboring western-chimpanzee communities at Taï National Park covered 70 individuals, 135 videos, and 1,460 stick-use events. For stick insertion, the model predicted that the multi-digit grip became predominant at about 5.2 years and had a 98% predicted probability of use by age 15. Observations above age 45 came from only three females in one group. This age pattern is specific to the sampled stick task and does not establish a universal developmental trajectory or cognitive cause.

## Gestation and first reproduction {#s-7edf8fcc67}
Ernest’s compilation reports averages of 7.36 months for active gestation and 131.87 months for female age at first reproduction. Gestation covers active fetal development and excludes delayed implantation. First reproduction is the age at which a female can breed and conceive; when a source gives first-birth age, the method subtracts gestation. It is distinct from sexual maturity and first birth. These cross-source summaries are not timetables for every individual.

## From newborn offspring to weaning {#s-20cf559df6}
The compiled averages are 1669.92 g for newborn mass, 36.54 months to milk independence, and 8500 g at milk independence. Newborn mass refers to one offspring; the definition excludes records explicitly measured more than 12 hours after birth. Weaning means independence from milk, with age measured from birth and mass referring to that transition. Wild, captive and locality conditions are not distinguished consistently, so these values do not provide a growth curve or account of parental care.

## Litter size and frequency {#s-b0b5ed9a46}
The compiled averages are 1.1 offspring born per litter and 0.2 litters per female per year. Litter size counts born offspring, excluding estimates based only on embryos or reproductive scars; the fraction is an average, not a literal litter. Litter frequency counts births per female over 12 months. Captive studies designed to maximize production were excluded where possible, but the remaining records are not thereby all wild. Multiplying the two averages does not give an individual female’s annual reproductive success.

The Ernest 2003 table records a maximum lifespan of 726 months, retaining the longest available male or female record when a longer value is found.
