## Elegia decipiens {#s-21eb347c8f}
## Small flower clusters gathered almost like a head

The regional flora describes Elegia decipiens as a perennial that can produce new shoots again and has female and male flowers on separate plants; it forms tufts and reaches 70 cm. Its stems have one or two nodes (points where leaves or branches attach to the stem), and its leaf sheaths (the basal parts of leaves around a stem) fall away. Each spikelet (small flower cluster) bears one or two female flowers, and the clusters gather into a group shaped almost like a head. The account lists sandstone slopes as habitat.
