## Mammals before K–Pg: an early branch and a later boundary {#s-0833368525}
A character analysis tests the early synapsid–sauropsid split; boundary geochemistry and radiometric ages test the K–Pg impact and turnover. These records place a later crisis beside an older branch without turning the boundary into a mammal-origin date.

## Skull openings distinguish branches, not a precise date {#s-e7f117e5ed}
The cited character analysis uses diagnostic skull openings to distinguish early synapsid and sauropsid branches. Early members are rare, several Paleozoic placements vary among analyses, and the divergence date depends on tree calibration. This is a branching result—not a named fossil ancestor or a directly measured split.

## Mesozoic mammal diversity {#s-a9f3dee429}
Small body size does not mean ecological uniformity: Mesozoic mammals include climbers, diggers, swimmers and gliders.

## The impact boundary records selective survival {#s-cdf3712b16}
Boundary clay, ejecta and high-precision dates link the K–Pg impact to abrupt turnover at 66.043 ± 0.043 Ma, within about 32,000 years. The event record also shows why this is not a complete account of what followed: survival and recovery differed among groups and regions, and the boundary itself does not explain later mammal diversification.
