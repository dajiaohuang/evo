## Manicaria saccifera: enormous leaves and clustered fruit {#s-f9aeb20e15}
The historical Flora of Panama account describes a tree up to 10 m tall, usually growing in colonies. Trunks reach 30 cm in diameter; two trunks often join at the base, lean, and have aerial roots there. Leaves also lean and may eventually droop or hang; they reach 10 m long and 2 m wide. Their leathery, glabrous blades range from nearly entire to very irregularly pinnate. Pinnae or segments are 2 cm or more wide, with a strong midrib and indistinct parallel veins; they are about 1 m long, little narrowed, and attached obliquely at the base. The petiole is at least 1 m long, soft and light, not woody.

The spadix has a simple continuing axis and simple rachillae 25–30 cm long. The account describes single or one-seeded fruit as essentially globular and tapered toward the base, 4–5 cm in diameter. Twin or triplet fruits measure 7–8 cm along the long axis. They are brown when dry and covered with angular tubercles.

This is a morphology description in a historical regional Flora of Panama source. It gives no population sampling frame or observation date and is not a current or global account.

The Flora of Panama account records wet places on or near shores and in swamps on Panama’s Atlantic side, often in dense groves. It says the species is “probably widely distributed” in the Republic and that a plant in Central America and the southern West Indies is “apparently” this species.

This is a historical regional statement from the Flora of Panama source version retrieved in 2026. Its geographic detail is limited to the places named in the excerpt; it is not a current range or global inventory, and the source’s “probably” and “apparently” qualifications are retained.
