## Haemanthus sanguineus: form and variation {#s-90f218bf67}
A 2012 regional account describes a bulbous geophyte 5–30 cm in size. It records two leaves that are dry at flowering, prostrate, often broader than long, leathery and often edged in red. The flowers are crowded into a dense head and are red or pink; the 5–11 bracts are leathery and red or pink.

A 1984 diagnostic account describes an unmarked peduncle, often compressed and furrowed. Its 5–11 thin-textured spathe valves are erect to slightly spreading; the lateral valves are keeled and sickle-shaped. The perianth segments spread slightly from the base. Leaves are prostrate or pressed to the ground, leathery, mostly rough and dark green on the upper surface. Their cartilaginous margins are described as “red to opaque” and as smooth or toothed. The bulb is laterally flattened, with overlapping, two-ranked tunics.

The same account reports field observations of extreme variation in form. It describes the current circumscription as including several ecological races centred mostly in the mountains and valleys of the Worcester district. It says that variation in floral and vegetative size, together with subtle population differences in flower colour, makes subdivision into infraspecific taxa difficult.
