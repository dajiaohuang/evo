## Accepted name in COL26.8 {#s-32378716bb}
The COL26.8 release dated 20 August 2026 lists Cardioglossa gracilis Boulenger, 1900 as an accepted species in Anura, Arthroleptidae and Cardioglossa. ## A Congo voucher is not an LMR record {#s-fa668f8401}
The 2017 study account includes Republic of Congo museum voucher USNM 584072. After a specimen judged to be misidentified was reassigned, no Cardioglossa observation remained in the account for LMR. LMR is the Loubomo–Mougagara Road corridor in Nyanga, Gabon; it is distinct from the Republic of Congo, which is also distinct from the Democratic Republic of Congo. A museum reference specimen is not automatically a record from LMR. A survey non-detection describes the places and seasons sampled, not regional absence or the full species range. The original vouchers, present-day occurrences and identification have not been independently checked here.

## Barcode groups support an identification within the reference library {#s-32399ca434}
In the study, the specimen fell within one COI BIN assignment and one ABGD group. Its 16S sequence was nearly identical to references labelled gracilis (EF640994, EF621773 and FJ151065). Together these comparisons support the authors’ identification against their reference library. They do not independently authenticate every reference voucher or establish that Cardioglossa is absent from the whole road corridor.

## Two mitochondrial markers and an uneven reference set limit inference {#s-3ff1cc9b04}
The identification workflow combines COI barcode groups, comparison of the mitochondrial 16S marker, and morphology and type-locality context. COI and 16S are both mitochondrial markers, so they are not independent nuclear confirmation and do not test reproductive isolation. The authors used neighbor joining to cluster similar sequences and explicitly limited its use for inferring evolutionary history. Most species in the study were represented by material from only one or two localities, and some GenBank vouchers could not be checked morphologically. Reference labels and missing sequences therefore remain limitations. Fieldwork took place in May–June 2012 and March–April 2013; the GenBank and BOLD comparison used a 2016 source-library snapshot, not a current database audit.

## Library totals describe the study, not this species or a population {#s-1f1a722188}
Across its multispecies reference library, the study reports 540 sequenced specimens, 524 COI sequences and 528 16S sequences, assigned to 94 BINs and 85 ABGD groups. These are library-wide totals, not counts for Cardioglossa gracilis. The methods list 80 LMR specimens from the first season and 97 from the second, while Table 1 lists 79 and 97 sequenced or retained entries. The methods also report 275 Republic of Congo reference specimens, whereas the Table 1 locality rows sum to 274. The source does not explain these collection-versus-analysis differences, so they remain unresolved. The group totals are not a verified census of currently accepted species. Raw sequence traces, BOLD assignments and GenBank accessions were not reanalysed here.
