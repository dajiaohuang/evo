## Dense stem hairs and large, woolly follicles {#s-d9fb6d6bfd}
The Flora of Panama describes a vine with cylindrical stems densely covered by two kinds of hairs: short, sometimes bulbous hairs overtopped by stiff, brown, spreading, multicellular hairs 3–5 mm long. Elliptic leaves are 10–15 × 7–10 cm, discolorous and pubescent on both surfaces; their base is obtuse, truncate or shallowly cordate. Petioles are 3–6 cm long. Umbel-like inflorescences are pubescent like the stems and about 10-flowered, although usually only two flowers are open at once; peduncles are about 2 cm and pedicels about 1.5 cm. Flowers are greenish or purplish brown. Lanceolate calyx lobes are 5–6 × 2–3 mm, densely brown-pubescent outside and glabrous inside.

The corolla is rotate to shallowly bell-shaped and 2–3 cm across. Its broadly ovate lobes are 6–9 mm long and wide, with inconspicuous short hairs on both surfaces and longer hairs mostly in a narrow band at the base. The throat ring is a low, thin tissue ridge about 0.1 mm high, densely but minutely white-pubescent. The fleshy, five-lobed corona has its lobes joined by a narrow ring; the thickened tip of each lobe is broadly kidney-shaped, about 1 × 1.5 mm. The gynostegium is about 2 mm high. The broad pentagonal stigma head is slightly depressed and covered by a prominent, thick white membrane. Subovoid follicles are about 20 cm long and 9 cm in diameter; the woody exocarp has a low, short dorsal ridge. The historical flora records the species only from Panama; this source slice does not establish a current or complete global range.
