## Teeth and ankle bones from early Eocene Dormaal {#s-68aaecd147}
At Dormaal in Belgium, early Eocene fossils dated here to 56–55.8 million years ago document teeth and ankle bones assigned to Dormaalocyon. The sample includes an extensive record of deciduous and permanent teeth, two named astragali and a calcaneum. These materials support a morphology-based placement among basal carnivoraforms, a broader group than living cats, dogs and their kin. That placement does not make Dormaalocyon a member of crown Carnivora, a direct ancestor or the first carnivoraform worldwide.
