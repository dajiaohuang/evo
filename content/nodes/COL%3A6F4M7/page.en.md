## Only two negative swabs at El Coni {#s-3353465823}
The discussion reports that both Eleutherodactylus cystignathoides samples from El Coni tested negative for Bd, while explicitly stating that only two were collected. They were sampled with salamanders and frogs in a cave survey. Two negative swabs do not establish immunity, absence of the pathogen at the site, species-wide freedom from infection or a reservoir role. Finding the frogs in a cave does not show obligate cave dwelling or a breeding site.

## Collection and chemical interpretation limits {#s-2e57beb2be}
The frogs were sampled in Austin, Texas, in 2024; dorsal and ventral skin were processed separately after benzocaine euthanasia. UHPLC-HESI-MS/MS is more sensitive than the GC-MS pipeline but gives less precise retention-time and structural annotations. Standards were unavailable for most frog alkaloids, so the reported signals do not yield absolute quantities, and the two pipelines are not directly comparable. Full-skin GC-MS results were not mass-corrected; co-eluting compounds with the same base peak may not be distinguishable. The authors did not repeatedly sample each species across seasons and a broad range. Raw chromatograms, vouchers and supplementary individual records were not independently reanalyzed here.

## Passive accumulation remains a mechanistic hypothesis {#s-5056cd1c10}
The paper proposes a sequence in which dietary toxin exposure and some resistance may be followed by slower elimination and temporary retention, then by derived transport or storage associated with sequestration. For this species, a skin profile alone does not establish dietary origin, elimination rate, a binding mechanism or transport specifically to skin. Detection in skin cannot distinguish passive retention from specialized sequestration. The proposed ancestral state and evolutionary sequence need broader sampling and tissue or time-series experiments; they are not direct observations of ancestors or a reconstructed molecular pathway in these sampled frogs.

## Species-specific detections and their evidence limits {#s-b87c0c7dd5}
Under stringent LC-MS criteria, no lipophilic frog alkaloids were reported in the three animals. A broader classifier identified 55 alkaloid-pathway metabolites, 40 treated as matching features found in Silverstoneia flotator. Computational annotations and cross-sample feature matches are not independent structural confirmation, proof of dietary origin or a positive defense assay; some features may come from frog or symbiont metabolism.
