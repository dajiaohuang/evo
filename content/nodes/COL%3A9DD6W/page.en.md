## Stem surface {#s-b63c26db46}
The Brazilian Flora 2020 record describes the stem as pubescent or tomentose—covered with short soft hairs or a dense, matted coat. The hairs point forward and lie close to the surface. Simple hairs and thread-like unbranched bristles are listed; branched and glandular hairs, prickles, and short lateral shoots (brachyblasts) are absent. The field does not say whether the two surface descriptions refer to different specimens.

## Leaves and inflorescences {#s-fb1c4e7516}
Stipules, small structures at leaf bases, are persistent. Extrafloral nectaries (nectar-secreting glands outside flowers) and projections between pairs of pinnae (the leaf’s primary side divisions) are absent. The inflorescence, or flower cluster, is globose; its floral bracts (small leaf-like structures) are longer than the flowers before they open (anthesis).

## Flowers {#s-2b880ae54d}
The perianth (calyx and corolla together) is recorded as having four parts. The calyx, the outer sepal whorl, is paleaceous, or thin and papery; its lobes are pappus-like, resembling a tuft of fine bristles or scales. Corolla (petal-whorl) lobes have one to three veins and are pubescent, covered with short soft hairs. The androecium (the stamens collectively) is isostemonous, with stamens equal in number to the petals; the filaments are pink.

## Habitat category {#s-541994a08f}
The regional dataset labels the habitat “Terrícola,” Portuguese for terrestrial. This category does not provide a collection locality or the species’ full range.
