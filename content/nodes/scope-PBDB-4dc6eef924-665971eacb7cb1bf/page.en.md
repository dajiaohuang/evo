## atlas-profile / overview {#s-857a0c19a3}
Researchers attributed isolated calcanei (heel bones) and tali (ankle bones) from Shanghuang fissure fillings and the Yuanqu Basin in China to *Eosimias*. They scored ankle features in a comparative matrix and recovered anthropoid affinity, but the bones are not associated with teeth, so both their taxonomic link and tree position are inferences. The broad 45–40.5 Ma interval describes these locality samples, not the full range of the genus.

## atlas-profile / evidenceSummary {#s-67695c46a0}
The study reports isolated heel and ankle bones from Shanghuang and Yuanqu and tests their attribution with selected anatomical characters. They are not associated with teeth or a complete foot. The 45–40.5 Ma envelope is broad locality context, not a genus-wide range; placement near anthropoids depends on the comparative matrix.

## Ankle bones from two Chinese localities {#s-ddfc0088b0}
The study describes isolated calcanei and tali from fissure fillings at Shanghuang and from the Yuanqu Basin in China. These are pieces of the ankle, not a complete foot attached to a skull or teeth. Researchers attributed them to *Eosimias* by comparing their shape with known anatomy, so the two localities and the isolated bones matter when interpreting the result.

## Testing an anthropoid relationship {#s-a5bbb9e378}
The authors scored ankle features in a comparative matrix to test whether the fossils support an anthropoid relationship. The bones preserve a mixture of characters, and their placement depends on which features and fossil comparisons enter the analysis. Anthropoid affinity is therefore a result of this comparison, not a label directly visible on one bone.

## What the ankle bones cannot show {#s-abc9b16901}
The tarsals are not associated with the teeth used to define *Eosimias*. They do not reveal the animal's full range of movement, diet or habitual habitat. Attributing the bones to the genus and placing that genus on a tree are separate inferences; neither makes these ankle bones a complete picture of the animal.

## atlas-profile / readerLimitations {#s-28a38899d8}
This page describes isolated ankle bones and how researchers compared them with other fossils. Because they are not associated with teeth or a complete foot, they cannot establish the animal's full anatomy, habitual movement, ecology or direct ancestry.
