## Eosimias: isolated ankle bones and inferred affinity {#s-857a0c19a3}
The study attributes isolated calcanei and tali from Shanghuang fissure fillings and the Yuanqu Basin to Eosimias. A comparative matrix supports anthropoid affinity, but the ankle bones are not associated with teeth, so their attribution and tree position remain inferences. The 45–40.5 Ma interval describes these localities, not the genus's full range.

## Evidence from isolated tarsals and a comparative matrix {#s-67695c46a0}
The study tests selected ankle characters from Shanghuang and Yuanqu. The fossils are not a complete foot and are not associated with teeth used to define Eosimias. The 45–40.5 Ma interval is locality context; anthropoid placement depends on the comparative matrix.

## Calcanei and tali from Shanghuang and Yuanqu {#s-ddfc0088b0}
The study describes isolated heel bones (calcanei) and ankle bones (tali) from Shanghuang fissure fillings and the Yuanqu Basin in China. These are not a complete foot attached to teeth or a skull. Attribution to Eosimias rests on anatomical comparison, so both the isolated material and its two localities matter.

## Anthropoid affinity from a character matrix {#s-a5bbb9e378}
The authors scored ankle features in a comparative matrix. The fossils preserve a mixture of characters, and the placement depends on the characters and fossil comparisons included. Anthropoid affinity is the analysis result, not a label directly visible on one bone.

## Separate inferences, incomplete anatomy {#s-abc9b16901}
The tarsals are not associated with the teeth used to define Eosimias. They do not show the animal's full movement, diet or habitual habitat. Attributing them to the genus and placing that genus on a tree are separate inferences; neither makes the ankle bones a complete anatomical record.

## Limits of the ankle-bone evidence {#s-28a38899d8}
The account covers isolated ankle bones and their comparison with other fossils. Without associated teeth or a complete foot, these specimens cannot establish full anatomy, habitual movement, ecology or direct ancestry.
