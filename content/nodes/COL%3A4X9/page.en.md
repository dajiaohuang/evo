## Historical PanTHERIA entries {#s-98c4619c2b}
PanTHERIA 1.0 groups historical records under Cercopithecoidea, with examples such as Cercocebus galeritus, Cercopithecus ascanius, C. cephus, C. erythrogaster, and C. erythrotis. The entries keep their MSW05 names and notes about variable-specific conditions and missing fields. The local selection contains 36 usable accounts; that number describes this archive’s coverage, not the accepted-species count. Navigation follows the pinned COL26.8 route.

## Adult-mass medians in the selection {#s-a829eef600}
Among the available sex-adjusted historical adult-mass medians, Miopithecus talapoin is at 1,248.86 g and Theropithecus gelada at 15,964.11 g. These values come from separate source entries and mark endpoints in this selected set. They are neither individual extremes nor a complete account of body-size variation in Cercopithecoidea.

## What the life-history archive records {#s-0e5d7fff96}
The August 2015 Amniote archive provides 110 historical accounts on this route. Adult mass appears in all 110; offspring per litter or eggs per clutch and maximum-lifespan records appear in 103 each; the source-defined female-maturity field—age at first conception—appears in 94. Examples include Allenopithecus nigroviridis and several Cercocebus species. These field counts describe the archive selection, not the living superfamily’s membership or a shared reproductive pattern.

## How EltonTraits encodes feeding and activity {#s-cf016cabc0}
EltonTraits 1.0 includes 121 historical entries on this route. Food-category weights are available for 98; foraging strata and activity periods for 121. Examples include Allenopithecus nigroviridis and Macaca species. Food weights standardize published descriptions, while strata and activity use categorical or binary codes. They are not direct measurements of every animal’s diet or daily time budget.

## Limits across these separate datasets {#s-85fbc7a46e}
PanTHERIA, the Amniote archive, EltonTraits, and Ernest’s compilation are separate historical datasets with different name crosswalks, field definitions, inclusion rules, and source conditions. None covers every COL26.8 descendant, and an older name match does not establish equivalence with a current species concept. For the 2015 Amniote archive, non-longevity values are medians or interpolated medians; maximum longevity is the longest recorded value, not an average. EltonTraits weights and codes summarize literature categories, and some mammalian ABC entries may infer from a congener.

## A smaller PanTHERIA selection {#s-b21f40f9ab}
A separate two-account selection links Cercopithecus sclateri and Presbytis frontata. Their MSW05 names and field notes remain attached to their own historical entries. The pair is a selected reading set, not a summary of all cercopithecoid diversity.

## Two adult-mass summaries {#s-6345a40313}
The sex-adjusted historical medians are 3,067.29 g for Cercopithecus sclateri and 6,096.6 g for Presbytis frontata. Each value summarizes its own historical entry. The pair is not a comparison of individual extremes or a complete body-size range for the superfamily.

## How the two records were selected {#s-02b1a628bb}
This small PanTHERIA route includes historical entries with at least one focal field, linked through matching name and family plus a one-to-one historical crosswalk. It does not cover all COL26.8 descendants, and the name matching does not prove current species-concept equivalence. Present distributions and conservation status need other evidence.

## Life-history examples in Ernest’s compilation {#s-574cf38182}
Ernest’s 2003 compilation contributes 30 historical life-history entries linked to Cercopithecoidea. Its examples include Allenopithecus nigroviridis, Cercocebus galeritus, and three Cercopithecus accounts. The links show where the selected records sit in the pinned catalogue route; they are not a complete accepted-species list.

## Gestation, first reproduction, and maximum life {#s-265a52ee70}
The compilation records gestation in 20 accounts, female age at first reproduction in 17, and maximum lifespan in 10. First reproduction is not the same event as first birth or sexual maturity. Maximum lifespan is a longest-record measure rather than an average. Source conditions vary among fields, and missing entries are not filled from relatives.

## Interpreting Ernest’s historical values {#s-888c57fba3}
Species links use matching names and families plus a one-to-one MSW93/MSW05 crosswalk; this does not establish equivalence with current species concepts. Except for maximum lifespan, values generally average literature entries equally, regardless of sample size. Inputs may be published means, individual values, or range midpoints, and underlying studies may be reused. This route does not interpret adult mass, and wild, captive, and locality conditions were not established for every source.
