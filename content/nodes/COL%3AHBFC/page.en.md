## Asparagus hirsutus: a hairy foxtail form {#s-f490672c11}
A detailed South African account describes a compact, evergreen, very hairy shrublet of foxtail form, olive to grey-green and 300–500 mm tall. It has a woody rhizome, cataphylls, roots 0.1–5.5 mm in diameter, and a few lateral ellipsoid tubers measuring 36–40 × 14–21 mm. The pale, faintly ribbed stems are 1.2–5.0 mm thick; older specimens may shed their outer skin in patches. Straight, slender, hairy spines 1.2–6.0 mm long occur only below branch axils on the main stem. Grey leaf scales with dark blotches measure 1.0–3.2 × 1.0–1.5 mm; they become smaller toward the tips and eventually disappear. One to five hairy branches, 117–134 mm long, rise in clusters; branchlets are absent. Cladodes are club-shaped to linear, olive green and slightly curved, with 6–10 per bundle (up to 36 in terminal bundles), six or seven angles and simple white hairs about 0.2 mm long along the ridges. Their dimensions are printed as “(1.5-)4.0-6.4 (-10) x 0.5 mm long”; the excerpt does not clearly label the dimensions, so that notation is retained as given. A tiny pointed tip is reported as 0.5 mm long. Flowers are solitary in a cladode bundle; no scent was detected. The hairy flower stalk is 0.5–2.0 mm long and jointed in its upper half. There are six tepals: three larger ones, 2.1–2.5 × 1.0–1.5 mm, and three smaller ones, 2.0–2.1 × 0.9–1.0 mm; they are white with a brownish midline, a hair ridge, acute tips and sometimes toothed margins. The six stamens have orange anthers; their filaments are 2.6–3.5 mm long, joined for 1.5 mm and free for 1.1–2.0 mm. The style is 1 mm long. The three-chambered ovary measures 1.0–1.5 × 1 mm. The berry is 5–7 mm across, red when ripe, with the perianth remaining attached.

The habitat account describes rocky hillsides among small rocks, usually in full sun and sometimes in sparse shade. It places the plants on medium- to coarse-grained, titanite-bearing tonalite and in vegetation type SVcb 28, Sekhukhune Mountain Bushveld.
