## What does the account describe for Euphorbia arida? {#s-b6b8bfc7cc}
This is a dwarf succulent, about 50 mm tall and 45–50 mm wide, often with a tuberous base and a nearly cylindrical main stem. Its branches reach 25–37 mm long and 8–10 mm wide, then eventually fall; the small leaves are also deciduous. Solitary cyathia, the cup-like flower units of Euphorbia, sit at branch tips. Their stalks persist over several seasons, wither, and become spine-like, 5–13 mm long. The dull olive-green glands have two to four processes. The styles join almost halfway into a 27–30 mm column.

## Where does the habitat record place it? {#s-d6a3ca18ef}
The source lists dry grassland and dwarf Karroid shrubland, including stony ground, riverbanks, limestone outcrops, and slopes.
