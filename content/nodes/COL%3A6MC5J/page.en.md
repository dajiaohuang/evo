## A two-season invasive-population diet study {#s-fcd93b11d0}
Researchers sampled tigerinus at three Andaman sites from February to July 2017 across agriculture, plantation, logged forest and less-disturbed forest plots. Sampling covered subadults and adults, not newly metamorphosed frogs; stomachs were flushed after evening capture and recaptures were marked. Pooled comparator identities were limited to Limnonectes and Fejervarya genera because taxonomy was unresolved. Those pooled prey or diet results are not assigned here to individual accepted species. The sites had established invasions, so the sample does not describe initial colonization or the full native-range diet.

## Prey-number and prey-volume proportions differ {#s-94fb6617f8}
The tigerinus sample contained 687 recovered prey items across twenty-nine taxonomic categories. Vertebrates accounted for 2.62% by number but 58.03% of estimated prey volume, demonstrating why the two metrics must be distinguished. Coleoptera and Orthoptera were prominent by the study’s relative-importance index. Volumes were modelled from prey dimensions; fragmented items lacking measurements received an order-level median volume. These are local sampled diet estimates, not daily energy intake, a constant vertebrate percentage throughout the species or a direct measure of prey-population losses.

## Consumed vertebrates establish predation records rather than demographic impact {#s-6a8bcad8b0}
Recovered prey included amphibians, reptiles and a rodent, with several Andaman-endemic prey labels. The results documented four Limnonectes and two frogs identified as Microhyla chakrapani, among other vertebrates. Limnonectes identity stayed genus-level, and the Microhyla spelling requires reconciliation with the pinned catalogue before a canonical prey-species assignment. These records demonstrate sampled consumption, not a quantified decline, extinction probability or stable trophic position. Poultry and stream-fish accounts discussed elsewhere were attributed reports rather than these recovered stomach observations.

## High overlap is potential competition under limited resources {#s-bc902a7ef4}
The results gave tigerinus–Limnonectes dietary overlap Ojk = 0.87, while the discussion described 86%; this small reporting difference is retained. Overlap with Fejervarya was not significant in the null-model comparison. The authors proposed possible competition when prey is limited but did not measure reductions in growth, survival or population size caused by the invader. Newly metamorphosed tigerinus were not sampled, so suggested juvenile competition is extrapolation rather than observed juvenile diet. Overlap does not by itself prove competition or ecological equivalence.

## Electivity and an inferred ambush strategy have sampling limits {#s-11fd74aefa}
Prey electivity compared consumed terrestrial invertebrates with pitfall catches, excluding flying evasive insects and vertebrates from availability estimates. Tigerinus electivity analyses were restricted to agriculture and plantations and adequately sampled prey categories. The authors inferred mostly ambush foraging from evasive prey in the diet, rather than tracking every capture sequence. Positive associations of predator size and prey volume do not establish active choice for all prey types, and pitfalls can overrepresent social insects. The inferred strategy is not a measured fixed behavioural repertoire across habitats.

## Historical introduction pathways and prospective impact {#s-f2698bb9cd}
The 2018 paper cited early-2000s introduction to the Andamans and expansion from 2009, with fish-culture contaminants and deliberate releases proposed as pathways. These dated attributed histories are not verified current island occupancy or a new reconstruction of every transfer. Its recovered diet supports concern about predation, while demographic effects and trophic-level consequences were not evaluated. Predictions for Madagascar were extrapolations from the Andaman sample, not Malagasy stomach analyses. A reported pathway, confirmed consumption and measured population-level impact represent different evidence levels.
