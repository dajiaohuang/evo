## atlas-profile / overview {#s-089597603a}
No single Yuganotheca fossil shows the whole animal. The reconstruction brings together 710 fossils referred to Yuganotheca elegans from the Yu’anshan Member at Chengjiang: paired valves built from grains, a lophophore, a two-part tube and a pedicle. That combination is useful for comparing forms near the brachiopod–phoronid stem, but it does not settle the homology of each structure or identify an ancestor.

## atlas-profile / ecology / diet {#s-dbbdf7a0e9}
The preserved lophophore supports a suspension-feeding interpretation; feeding itself is not observed.

## atlas-profile / ecology / habitat {#s-6288c8beaa}
The fossils come from the marine Chengjiang depositional setting.

## atlas-profile / ecology / locomotion {#s-69ad44f628}
The pedicle suggests attachment, but the fossils do not record movement.

## atlas-profile / ecology / bodySize {#s-a04066df9f}
The reconstructed tubular body is measured on the millimetre scale.

## atlas-profile / ecology / guild {#s-a45af89934}
Suspension feeding is inferred from the reconstructed anatomy, not established for every fossil.

## atlas-profile / traits {#s-44fcd66fb4}
Paired grain-built valves surround a chamber for the lophophore.

## atlas-profile / traits {#s-3e1ed2b528}
The tube is divided into two parts by a median collar.

## atlas-profile / traits {#s-62a8074ba6}
A long pedicle is preserved; its internal space is interpreted as coelomic.

## atlas-profile / evidenceSummary {#s-d0be70fae8}
Read together, the Chengjiang fossils supply the anatomy and formal stratigraphic context, but not a complete body in one specimen. Whether the valves and tube match structures in other groups, and where Yuganotheca belongs near the brachiopod–phoronid stem, depend on comparative interpretation and the tree tested. The sample is not a global range or proof of direct ancestry.

## atlas-profile / readerLimitations {#s-21ba2f2e58}
The body plan is reconstructed across referred fossils rather than from one complete specimen. Suspension feeding and attachment are functional interpretations; structural homology and the precise brachiopod–phoronid stem position remain interpretive, not evidence of direct ancestry.
