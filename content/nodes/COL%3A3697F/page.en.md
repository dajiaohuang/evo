## Reading an older ecology record {#s-b4bcad3d9f}
COL26.8 accepts Dinopium benghalense in Picidae. EltonTraits 1.0 contains a same-name, same-family entry following an earlier taxonomy. This page reads its diet and foraging-position groups with their source labels; the match does not settle later splits or synonymies.

## Diet-weight estimate {#s-58feca0b8e}
The nonzero weights are 80% invertebrates, 10% fruit, and 10% nectar, pollen and plant exudates. Code A marks high compiler confidence in the source and diet entry. The proportions standardize verbal descriptions rather than measure intake; zero weight elsewhere would not prove a food is never eaten, and broad labels do not imply exclusive diets.

## Feeding positions across ground and vegetation {#s-bc57287c02}
The species-level entry divides foraging weights among four positions: 30% in understory, edges or shrubs below 2 m; 30% in trees or tall bushes from 2 m to below the canopy; 30% in or just above the canopy; and 10% on the ground. These are relative feeding positions, not nest sites, complete habitat use or a daily time budget. The source's water, understory, mid/high, canopy and aerial classes are not elevation bands and cannot give flight height or habitat area.

## Provenance of the selected groups {#s-9c3923d102}
Both selected groups cite Ref_7, the Handbook of the Birds of the World, volume 7. Other records can use different sources for diet, strata and activity, and bird and mammal tables may reuse numbers for different works. The archive retains full citation strings by file, row, group and table. The Handbook was not independently reread here.

## Limits of the historical record {#s-2ff582e9cc}
EltonTraits is a historical same-name, same-family ecology comparison, not a complete species account. Matching names do not establish equivalence between the older record and COL26.8. Diet and foraging weights are standardized readings of published descriptions rather than direct measurements of intake, energy or time. The selected bird fields are species-level, although the Handbook was not independently reviewed here. Individual, seasonal and local variation, current distribution, population and conservation require other evidence.
