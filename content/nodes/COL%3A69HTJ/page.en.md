## The amended diagnosis belongs to buckleyi sensu stricto {#s-3e545e9572}
The amended account gives seventeen males at 26.1–32.5 mm snout–vent length and thirteen females at 24.2–39.8 mm. Although females are described as larger overall, the sampled ranges overlap and some females fall below the male minimum. Green dorsal surfaces, a pale lip and flank line, green bones and a reticulated grey-white iris with brown horizontal stripe form part of the combination. Adult males have humeral spines, vocal slits and sacs, and conspicuous dorsal spicules reported absent in females. These traits describe the restricted examined concept, not every historical population once labelled buckleyi or a fixed sex-based size cutoff.

## Colombian and Peruvian occurrence remained uncertain {#s-92590834e9}
The revision restricted confirmed buckleyi sensu stricto records to northern and central Ecuadorian Andean ranges, with the distribution account giving 2,677–3,416 m and the neotype at Cuicocha. It explicitly regarded occurrence in Colombia and Peru as uncertain and reassigned Guarumales material to marcoreyesi. Separately, an analysed historical Papallacta recording was labelled 2,660 m, slightly below the distribution paragraph’s lower bound; these source scopes remain distinct. Forest, shrub, páramo and pasture records do not demonstrate complete habitat occupancy or an experimental disturbance threshold. Neither old country lists nor records reassigned to a new species are silently retained as confirmed current buckleyi records.

## Only specifically attributed recordings support this account {#s-f7528588b5}
The authors distinguished three historical call descriptions and considered only the Oyacachi description attributable to buckleyi sensu stricto. Their analysis used the Oyacachi male recorded in 2012 and a Papallacta recording from 1975, both labelled 10 °C. Regular calls were single pulsed notes, mean duration 0.261 s, mean dominant frequency 2.9 kHz and call rate 1.4–2.8 per minute; mean pulse count was 17.9. These measured summaries do not apply to recordings now assigned to elisae or marcoreyesi. Multiple calls and pulses within a recording are repeated measurements, not additional independent frogs or populations.

## A five-call sequence does not establish its social trigger {#s-08657ec40c}
Male MZUTI 0763 also produced a sequence of five consecutive calls with regular-call structure but much shorter intervals, 0.7–0.9 s. The authors proposed nearby females or competing males as likely triggers. That is an interpretation of a calling sequence, not a controlled test distinguishing mate attraction from aggression. The shortened intervals do not turn the sequence into a new two-note call type or a species-wide maximum calling rate. Calls from elisae interacting laboratory males remain a different individual, species and setting.

## A source-bound comparison with the La Enramada types {#s-360c253003}
The 2023 paper cites low or absent ulnar/tarsal folds in the camposi comparison, and a sloping lateral snout, more prominent supratympanic fold, absent outer tarsal fold and brown iris stripe in the ericsmithi comparison for Centrolene buckleyi. The fixed catalogue page uses Centrolene buckleyi while retaining the original source spelling. These selected comparator traits are not new measurements of a population of this species. They contrast it with the new type material but do not establish a complete identification key, universal trait frequency, diet or reproductive chronology.
