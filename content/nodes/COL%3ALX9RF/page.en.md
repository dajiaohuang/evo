## Accepted name and placement {#s-fa9d3228e3}
COL26.8 accepts Boulenophrys yaoshanensis Qi, Mo, Lyu, Wang & Wang, 2021 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Megophryidae (family) → Boulenophrys (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## Corrected type coordinates and discrepant elevations {#s-d5e3a4bb5a}
The monograph listed male holotype SYS a002189 from Dayaoshan Nature Reserve, Jinxiu, Guangxi. It explicitly attributed a correction to Qi and colleagues (2022), replacing the originally reported coordinates and approximately 845 m elevation with 24.1602° N, 110.2304° E and approximately 1200 m. Its distribution summary included Jinxiu, Mengshan and Guiping in the Dayao Mountains at 1200–1300 m. Separately listed paratypes included a Xianglu Mountain female at approximately 1305 m and a male from west of Jinxiu at approximately 1115 m. These specimen elevations remain separate from the narrower summary; no unreported correction or continuous occupancy is inferred.

## Eight examined types and male finger pads {#s-8a803abd64}
The examined type material comprised six adult males with snout–vent lengths of 32.5–42.6 mm and two adult females at 46.6–47.4 mm. The diagnosis described weak vomerine ridges without teeth and rudimentary basal toe webbing without lateral fringes. Breeding males had fine, dense black spines on the dorsal bases of finger I and II nuptial pads and a subgular vocal sac. These are sample measurements and anatomical characters; they do not establish a breeding season or population-wide size limits.

## A character cited in the gutu comparison {#s-35821a6a05}
The 2025 gutu description lists Boulenophrys yaoshanensis as without lateral toe fringes. This is an attributed comparator statement within that paper’s taxonomic scope, not a new measurement series for this species. The gutu adult sample spans 34.4–44.7 mm in seven males and 36.2–52.8 mm in eight females; those numbers belong to gutu, not to this comparator. The selected character does not establish population-wide frequency, complete diagnosis, diet or developmental biology.
