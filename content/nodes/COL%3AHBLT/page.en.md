## Asparagus stipulaceus: brush-like form {#s-999524de1d}
A South African regional account describes an erect, brush-like shrublet up to 50 cm tall. Its stems are finely ribbed and hairy, with spines in groups of three. The cylindrical cladodes also occur three per bundle. One or two flowers sit on a disc at the shoot tip, with the tepals and filaments spreading outward.

These characters and measurements come from a South African regional flora account cited to a 2012 publication. The excerpt gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency and does not establish variation across the full range. It does not independently classify the material as native, naturalised, cultivated or domesticated.

The account records coastal dunes as habitat.
