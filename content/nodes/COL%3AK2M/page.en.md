## Compare food, feeding positions and activity periods {#s-e8c3083154}
Fifteen historical ecological records are represented, including Microgale longicaudata, Microgale thomasi, Microgale soricoides, Microgale pusilla and Microgale principula. Food-category weights appear in 14 records, foraging strata in all 15, and mammalian activity periods in 12. These are different kinds of fields: standardized food estimates, stratum categories and activity codes. The counts describe the available records, not all accepted species or a single ecology shared by the group.

## Compare food, feeding positions and activity periods {#s-3feef28549}
Three additional historical entries are represented: Microgale taiva, Microgale fotsifotsy and Microgale nasoloi. None has a usable food-weight or activity-period field; all three have a foraging-stratum category. The absence of the other fields is a gap in these records, not a finding that the species lack those traits.

## Evidence limits {#s-d39c56befd}
The first set contains historical same-name, same-family entries with at least two usable ecological field groups; it is not a complete catalogue of accepted descendants. A food estimate, activity code or stratum assignment belongs to its source record and does not establish a shared trait across Oryzorictinae. Standardization, historical taxonomy and possible congeneric borrowing in ABC fields retain their record-specific limits.
The second set contains three same-name, same-family entries with only one usable ecological field group: foraging stratum. Missing food and activity values are not evidence that those traits are absent. These records likewise do not represent all accepted descendants or establish group-wide ecology.

## Compare body size and life history {#s-5854cde1a9}
The consolidated adult-mass values range from 3.4 g for Microgale pusilla to 40 g for Microgale dryas. These are endpoints of historical medians adjusted for sex, not individual extremes. Fetal development, weaning, activity and group fields describe other parts of life history; body-mass differences alone do not establish a shared lifestyle among descendants.

## Limits of these historical data {#s-444728d3fe}
The PanTHERIA accounts summarized here have at least one focal field, matching names and family placement, and a one-to-one historical correspondence. They do not cover every accepted descendant. Comparison endpoints are consolidated historical values rather than individual extremes, and source conditions can differ by field. These data do not establish current species concepts, distribution or conservation.
