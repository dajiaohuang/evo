PBDB 存档字段 scm 原文（未翻译或解释）：
Black limestone (“Fischschiefer”) of the lower Cretaceous of Karst, Gorizia in Veneto Friuli. The black limestones of Mzrlek were originally classified as Lower Cretaceous by Kner (1863), also Stache (1905) located it the 'lower Karst chalk'. Later the layers have been considered as lowermost Upper Cretaceous (Cenomanian). According to the geological map of Yugoslavia, the limestone is Aptian in age. Pleničar and Buser (1967: 148) write that flat limestones with fish remains appear in the lower part of the Cretaceous strata, so they could be classified as Albian. Pleničar (2009: 266) writes that the remains of fish were found in the middle part of the Albian dark gray flat argillaceous limestone. But, in the opinion of the Italian geologists, the black fossiliferous limestone is considered to be Hauterivian up to Barremian age (dalla Vecchia et al., 2008). The fish Amiopsis prisca, from Mrzlek, is also found in Barremian strata in Italy (dalla Vecchia et al., 2007). Križnar (2014) argued that the fish-bearing beds at Mrzlek are Albian-Cenomanian in age, and many fish genera are shared with the Komen beds in the Albian-Cenomanian Povir Formation (Jurkovšek et al., 2016). Platy limestones are a facies that occur throughout the succession, but the large majority of historical fish collections come from the Cenomanian part (Cavin et al., 2000).
