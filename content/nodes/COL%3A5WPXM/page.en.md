## Sex-specific measurements retain variable-specific sample counts {#s-e5a774e642}
In the 2025 comparative table, male snout–vent length for Brachycephalus tridactylus is 10.7–14.2 (N = 37) mm and female length is 14.4–15.7 (N = 5) mm, with N retained separately for each sex. Dorsal skin is smooth (n = 42) and lateral skin moderately rough (n = 42). N refers to animals observed for each variable, so the size, skin and colour denominators cannot be pooled into extra specimens. These examined ranges are not whole-species maxima, a statistical proof of population sexual dimorphism or a growth series. Smooth skin in this study can include small, inconspicuous warts; external digit absence does not alone establish skeletal absence.

## Live and preserved colours are separate evidence {#s-2377e1cda1}
Orange-green-dotted, darker orange and brown-backed patterns occur, with pale lateral dots or patches. These alternatives caution against one fixed colour diagnosis. These are the authors’ comparative observations rather than a standardized colour-chart measurement. Colour and specimen counts vary by trait; similar colours recur among species, so a single hue is not a complete identification rule. Preserved appearance should not be substituted for live appearance.
