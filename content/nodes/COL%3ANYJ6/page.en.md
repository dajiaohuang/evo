## *Bursera simaruba* {#s-d870c360e0}
The source describes trees 5–20 m tall with shiny copper-red bark that peels in thin sheets to reveal a smooth bright-green layer. Leaves are odd-pinnate, 21–35 cm long, with 5–7(–9) leaflets; leaflet shape, hairiness, and texture vary with position or age in the account. Male and female inflorescences are separately described, and flowers differ in part number between the sexes. This is a summary of the cited description.

The account describes fruits as nearly spherical, slightly three-angled, green to bright pink before ripening reddish brown; they split into three valves. The hard, angled seeds are usually one per fruit, with two also recorded. These details and ranges are those of the source description.

The source describes a broad Caribbean-region range, from coastal northeastern Mexico and southern Florida through the West Indies and Central America to Panama, Colombia, and Venezuela. In Panama it reports flowering from mid-March to mid-June and fruit collections in all months except September. These are records in that source, not a completeness estimate.
