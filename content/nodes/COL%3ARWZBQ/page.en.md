## Name and classification in COL26.8 {#s-8e9af18f33}
COL26.8 (Catalogue of Life dataset 316115, released 20 August 2026) records *Phantasmarana apuana* as an accepted species. Its recorded hierarchy is Anura (order) → Hylodidae (family) → Phantasmarana (genus). This is the classification in that release; the name and placement alone do not describe the organism’s biology.

## A larval image record with explicit provenance {#s-a4aec02dd5}
The 2024 paired-stage shape study includes Phantasmarana apuana. Table S1 records its tadpole source as “Photo, literature”, meaning photographs together with literature images, and retains the voucher field “MTR 10828”. A dash denotes no voucher listed in this table, not proof that no specimen ever existed. Across the study, 112 tadpole photographs/drawings represent 83 species, with one or two images per species and Gosner stages 26–41. Thirty-two landmarks were digitized by one person, tail curvature was digitally corrected for fixation artifacts, and species mean shapes were used. This row does not specify each specimen’s exact stage, and the images are not new live developmental trajectories or an independently checked species diagnosis.

## Adult measurements preserve source type and sex limits {#s-6df69154d2}
For Phantasmarana apuana, the adult-source field is “Literature”: measurements taken from literature text or tables. Its voucher field is “-” and sex field “M” (male). ## Larval guild and adult ecomorph are separate source categories {#s-a98ece79b9}
The row codes Phantasmarana apuana as larval LO (lotic) and adult TOR (torrential). The two stages are retained separately; the adult code cannot be assigned automatically to tadpoles, or the reverse. Lentic/lotic and torrential labels summarize broad habitat associations rather than quantified flow tolerance, while saxicolous or terrestrial codes do not define an entire geographic range. An endotrophic tadpole category is distinct from direct development and does not remove the larval stage. The authors compiled functional assignments from species accounts rather than experimentally measuring every species’ feeding, hydrodynamics, locomotion or habitat choice.

## Paired-stage comparisons do not establish complete developmental independence {#s-4b53de461a}
Across the sampled species, larval–adult shape dissimilarities show a weak but significant Mantel association (r 0.16, p 0.003), while disparity correlations differ among families. The authors interpret the overall pattern as support for adaptive decoupling; it does not demonstrate zero correlation, genetic independence, an experimentally selected trait or this species’ developmental trajectory. Phylogenetic analyses use 74 of the 83 sampled species, and ancestral shape/time patterns are model reconstructions from extant data rather than newly measured fossil morphology. Adult K 0.44 with p 0.001 describes less close-relative similarity than the reference expectation; the paper’s “not phylogenetically structured” wording cannot mean that the test is nonsignificant. Supplement S3 repeats family N values summing to 112 for both stages, so adult individual denominators and imputed cells remain uncertified without raw matrices.
