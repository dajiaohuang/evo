## Perissodactyls across a changing 56-million-year record {#s-5d8fd709b5}
From PETM-era first samples to a few living branches, the fossil record tracks changing perissodactyl diversity; it does not reveal the exact origin or a single cause for later turnover.

## Earliest sampled fossils appear near the PETM {#s-43fbd2c40e}
The earliest sampled perissodactyl fossils occur during the Paleocene–Eocene Thermal Maximum (PETM) on northern landmasses. They do not reveal the exact place or time when the group originated.

## Different browsing records in the Eocene {#s-04809e8e2e}
Eocene brontotheres diversified in North America and Asia. A separate western European sample of Palaeotherium medium supports leaf browsing; it does not establish the diet of every lineage.

## Cooling, turnover and a gap in the record {#s-5da496a811}
Marine records resolve stepwise ice growth. The Hampshire mammal sequence distinguishes faunal turnover, a gap in the record and later appearances of immigrant taxa. Together, these records do not prove a single cause for changing perissodactyl diversity.

## Living branches and extinct diversity {#s-990e69f001}
Living horses, tapirs and rhinoceroses represent only a small part of the diversity known from perissodactyl fossils. Coelodonta species also show that extinct rhinoceros ecology was not uniform.
