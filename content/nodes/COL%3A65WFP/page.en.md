## Aloe monotropa: one-sided flowers {#s-a760a61494}
A South African regional account describes a succulent herb that is stemless or has a short stem, up to 300 mm tall excluding the inflorescence; the stem usually lies along the ground. About 20 leaves spread from each rosette and measure 340–400 × 45–60 mm. The raceme bears flowers along one side, and the perianth opening turns upward. Flowers are old-rose pink, rarely yellow.

A diagnostic account places the species in section *Saponariae* and says its distinctive growth form and one-sided flowers distinguish it from the other species in that group. Its flowers are described as similar to those of *A. secundiflora* Engler, reported from Kenya and Tanzania, but the constriction above the ovary is cited as linking *A. monotropa* with *Saponariae*.
