## Name and placement in COL26.8 {#s-f850ea614b}
In its 20 August 2026 release, COL26.8 lists Brachycephalus albolineatus Bornschein, Ribeiro, Blackburn, Stanley & Pie, 2016 as a species in Anura, family Brachycephalidae, genus Brachycephalus.

## What the 2025 study covers {#s-122394459c}
A 2025 comparative study reports sampled morphology and colour, advertisement-call comparisons, and a mapped conservation assessment. The observations below come from particular study material and tables, and sample counts vary by trait. They describe what that study examined and analysed, not every population or long-term change.

## A separate morphology table {#s-3797698598}
The linked Amphibian morphology v3 table has one male row and nine trait columns with recorded values. Its units are not confirmed, so the numbers cannot be read as absolute measurements. The table’s own placement fields list Anura, Brachycephaloidea and Brachycephalus for the same name; equivalence with the COL26.8 species concept remains unresolved. Treat it as a separate comparison record.

## Size and skin use different sample counts {#s-da9b82e5f5}
In the 2025 comparison, males measured 8.9–11.4 mm in snout–vent length (SVL, from snout to vent; N = 27), and females 9.7–12.7 mm (N = 2). The authors describe dorsal skin as smooth (n = 29) and the sides as densely rough (n = 29). Each N is the number examined for that variable; counts from size, skin and colour observations cannot be added to infer extra animals. These sampled ranges are not species-wide maxima, a statistical demonstration of population-level sexual dimorphism, or a growth series. The paper notes that “smooth” skin may still bear small, inconspicuous warts. The absence of visible digits alone does not establish that digit bones are absent.

## Colour changes with specimen condition {#s-93506b0023}
The authors describe live animals as green with orange patches underneath; some have a pale line along the back. Preserved specimens show a different dark-and-cream pattern. These are comparative descriptions, not readings from a standardized colour chart, and the number of specimens varies by colour trait. Similar colours occur in other species, so hue alone is not a complete identification rule. Preserved colour should not be used as a stand-in for live colour.

## Call descriptions count different sound units {#s-16db6bac0b}
For 20 animals with recorded and analysed calls, the note-based table marks note groups present and attenuated notes absent. It records at most 2 notes per group and 3 pulses per note (N = 20 for each comparison). A pulse is part of a note; notes may be grouped within a call, while a call sequence is a larger unit. The table’s N counts animals, not a universal number of notes or complete calls. These presence/absence statements and maxima describe the recordings examined, not fixed limits for every population. Earlier studies centred on calls may count different units.

## The mapped assessment is not a population census {#s-144d2af6ef}
Table 7 reports field elevations of 500–835 m above sea level; that is an observed range, not a physiological altitude limit. It gives an extent of occurrence (EOO) of 450.3 km², a lower-bound grid area of occupancy (AOO) of 16 and an upper-bound AOO of 220, with 6 threat-defined locations. A dash means a mapped estimate was unavailable, not that no area is occupied. The authors propose VU B1ab(iii)+B2ab(iii) in this assessment; it is a dated study result, not a newly checked official Red List category. Collection records, potential-habitat polygons, a convex-hull EOO and AOO from 2-km grid cells describe different things. The location count is not a specimen count, and suggested management or recovery scenarios are not measured intervention results or a current census.
