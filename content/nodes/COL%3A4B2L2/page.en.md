## Othonna cyclophylla {#s-972179fed4}
## Felted branch tips carry small yellow heads

Manning and Bruyns's 2013 account describes a laxly branched shrublet up to 1 m tall. Its slender, smooth stems are bare except at the tips, and young branchlets are felted. Leaves usually cluster at branch ends, though vigorous shoots may bear scattered leaves. Short-petioled, oblanceolate blades are usually 20–30 by 3–10 mm, with three to five coarse teeth on each side; leaf axils are felted.

At branch tips, flower heads form subumbellate clusters 25–102 mm across. Each small head is disciform and yellow, carried on a short stalk about 5 mm long. The narrow bell-shaped involucre (ring of bracts around the head) is 3–4 mm in diameter and has five bracts, each 4–7 by about 2 mm. The hairless dry fruits are about 2 mm long; the pappus (bristle crown at the fruit tip) is about 6 mm long. Granitic hills are recorded as habitat.
