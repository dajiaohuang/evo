## Eggs and attending males in artificial cavities {#s-4b6a498c55}
Frogs identified as Platypelis barbouri bred exclusively in water-filled holes in the reported captive study, using bamboo and film canisters among the offered structures. Eggs were laid in the water, and males stayed with eggs and larvae through metamorphosis. Artificial cavity use documents behaviour under the enclosure choices; it does not establish that only those materials are used in nature or quantify wild cavity availability. The authors interpreted attendance as egg guarding but did not test additional parental feeding or experimentally measure the protective effect of male presence. The founders were not genetically verified.

## A seventeen-day observed minimum to metamorphosis {#s-67c881e900}
Barbouri larvae were classified as nonfeeding endotrophic tadpoles and developed in the adult enclosures without external larval food. Completed metamorphosis occurred as early as seventeen days after egg laying, the shortest interval cited for this species in the study. This is an observed minimum under captive conditions, not a typical field duration or guaranteed survival time. The study’s interpretation that rapid development could reduce drying risk was not a desiccation experiment for barbouri. Morphology-only founder assignment and selective retention of clutches remain limits on the species concept and survival inference.
