## Dallasaurus and the family-level limb comparison {#s-85845cceed}
A 144-character analysis places plesiopedal Dallasaurus and flipper-limbed hydropedal forms within Mosasauridae; limb grade alone does not define the family or show ancestry.

## atlas-profile / ecology / diet {#s-90e845ff8b}
Mosasaurids were marine carnivores, but their diets were not uniform. Carbon-isotope measurements from tooth enamel in five Maastrichtian taxa support resource partitioning among that sample. The small sample does not establish one family-wide menu or fully separate the effects of prey, diving and habitat.

## atlas-profile / ecology / habitat {#s-b97adca9e0}
The sampled animals lived in marine settings. Isotope comparisons suggest that foraging differed among the five Maastrichtian taxa, but the signal does not identify a single habitat for every mosasaurid. The Texas and Maastricht localities in this profile are examples, not a map of the family’s full distribution.

## atlas-profile / ecology / locomotion {#s-a35b169d3b}
The cited analysis compares plesiopedal limbs with the more flipper-shaped hydropedal condition. Its character coding places both within Mosasauridae and supports separate acquisitions of hydropedal limbs in the sampled tree. This is a phylogenetic result, not a simple march from walking to swimming.

## atlas-profile / ecology / bodySize {#s-7d258f42af}
In the sampled comparison, smaller forms tend to have plesiopedal limbs and larger forms tend to be hydropedal. These are adaptive grades in the study sample, not a measured size range for every member of Mosasauridae.

## atlas-profile / ecology / guild {#s-cb8d7d968b}
