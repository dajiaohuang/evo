## Form and leaves {#s-8d061cebdb}
The regional account describes a cormous geophyte 3–10 cm tall, with woody, concentric corm tunics. Leaves range from lance-shaped to ovate and are often prostrate. Flowers are small and white, with spreading tepals. The account says buds often abort and are replaced by cormlets, and that capsules are not produced.

## Stem forms and reproduction described in a second account {#s-3a71a92695}
A separate SANBI account says the species is unusual in its genus for its several flat, more-or-less sword-shaped leaves, usually lying on or arching toward the ground, its several small sheathing stem leaves, and two kinds of stems. Some stems produce no flowers and develop a cormlet in the axil of each flower bract. Others bear small white flowers but no capsules; a cormlet later develops where each aborted bloom was. The account says most populations contain both kinds of plants, that the species does not reproduce by seed, and that reproduction is exclusively through small axillary cormlets.

The same account says *Geissorhiza bolusii* is probably most closely related to *G. ovalifolia*, which grows in similar habitats and also has small white flowers. *G. ovalifolia* usually has no stem leaf or bract, or only one, and produces one or a few cormlets in its leaf axils. The account describes fruit development as apparently fairly rapid and notes immature capsules in some collections where fading flowers remain at spike tips. These capsule observations sit alongside its statements that capsules are not produced and that reproduction is seedless; the records do not reconcile the difference.

## Habitat {#s-2616f38b23}
