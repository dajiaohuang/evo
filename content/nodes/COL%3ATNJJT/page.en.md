## Accepted name and placement {#s-4854716513}
COL26.8 accepts Tylototriton ngoclinhensis Phung, Pham, Nguyen, Ninh, Nguyen, Bernardes, Le, Ziegler & Nguyen, 2023 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Caudata (order) → Salamandridae (family) → Tylototriton (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

Begin with the accepted record and its parent genus, then use the family and order pages to locate related catalogue entries. This page supplies a taxonomic orientation. ## Ngoc Linh records with a reserve-boundary wording difference {#s-77082ce82a}
The 2023 distribution paragraph reported the species only from Ngoc Linh Nature Reserve, Kon Tum, while the discussion said individuals were found both inside and outside the reserve. These statements retain their different scopes rather than restricting every record to the protected boundary. The discussion anticipated other Kon Tum Plateau localities, which remain predictions. Neither paragraph supplies complete contemporary occupancy or proof that other highland forests lack the species.

## Daytime forest-floor and shelter collections {#s-c6848f487c}
All study specimens were collected by day on the forest floor, beneath rotten trees or moss, near a small rocky stream. The type-locality surroundings were primary montane evergreen broadleaf forest at 1800–2300 m. Daytime collection and stream proximity do not establish diurnal surface activity, stream breeding or physiological elevation limits.

## Six types with different collection dates {#s-aacb0c2096}
Male holotype IEBR A.5130 was collected on 22 May 2022 in montane evergreen forest of Ngoc Linh, Dak Glei, Kon Tum. Five paratypes comprised two males and three females; female IEBR A.5135 was separately dated 20 May. The material paragraph printed the holotype elevation as 1.854 m, while the ecological paragraph gave surrounding forest at 1800–2300 m and the discussion said above 1800 m. The punctuation difference remains explicit; the original 1.854 notation is not presented as a literal lowland altitude or silently substituted with a separately verified point elevation.

## Sexed size ranges and a ridged holotype {#s-613f9d2baf}
The diagnosis reported male SVL 60.8–66.5 mm and tail length 57.6–61.8 mm, and female SVL 72.5–75.6 mm with tail 62.9–67.9 mm. The holotype itself measured SVL 66.5 mm and tail 59.5 mm. It had enlarged backward-projecting parotoids, a high segmented glandular vertebral ridge and fourteen isolated rib nodules on each side. Adpressed limbs overlapped and digits lacked webbing. These are sampled anatomy and sizes, not universal adult boundaries, a measured climbing-performance result or proof that the ridges have a particular defensive function.

## Live orange markings and a small-sample sex comparison {#s-8730efa653}
Live ground colour was black with orange on parts of the head, rib nodules, vertebral ridge, cloacal periphery and ventral tail edge; finger and toe tips and elbows were orange-brown. Preservation changed the background to blackish-brown and faded orange to pale yellow. The authors described males as probably smaller than females but explicitly requested confirmation because of the small sample. Male inner cloacal papillae and a longer slit were contrasted with the female short slit lacking papillae. These observations remain sample-specific, not a quantified mating mechanism or universally established size dimorphism.

## A dated southernmost-record claim and geographic distance {#s-a3b6333e99}
The authors called this the first Central Highlands crocodile-newt record and the southernmost known genus record at publication. They estimated approximately 370 air kilometres to the nearest T. notialis population in Laos and predicted other Kon Tum Plateau sites. Air distance is not travelled dispersal distance, and a geographical gap does not establish a measured reproductive barrier or species age. The regional climate values and Ngoc Linh summit height in the discussion are environmental context rather than critical thermal limits or the elevation of every collected newt.

## A provisional Endangered recommendation from 2023 {#s-ba28a6ad31}
The paper proposed Endangered under B1ab(i,iii), estimating extent of occurrence below 500 km² and citing habitat degradation from forest-product exploitation, tourism and agriculture, plus potential collector interest in the colourful animal. These are attributed threats and a dated proposal; EOO is not area of occupancy or a population census. The paragraph cited trade and Vietnamese regulations as of its publication. That historical legal statement is not an independently verified current regulatory determination, and occurrence in a reserve does not prove every population is protected or secure.

## ND2 separation does not fully resolve the nearest sampled relationships {#s-8a4d57ff45}
The 2023 study used mitochondrial ND2 and an alignment of 1,035 positions, comparing the Kon Tum population with thirty-three examined Tylototriton species. Reported uncorrected distances span 6.77% to panhai and 12.36% to ngarsuensis. These are marker-specific sample comparisons, not a universal species threshold or nuclear-genomic isolation test. The population clusters with panhai and vietnamensis in clade B, but the authors explicitly report nonsignificant support, printing 0.83 and 60%; later calling panhai a sister species does not resolve that weak placement. The 2025 corrigendum changes three deposited-sequence accession identifiers, not an independently reanalysed tree or newly measured distance. The reader links the correction separately and retains the original study’s uncertainty.

## The published corrigendum replaces three accession identifiers {#s-63961bcaa1}
The 2 December 2025 corrigendum to the 2023 original description, DOI 10.3897/zookeys.1168.96091, states that its Table 1 listed three new-species GenBank accessions incorrectly. LC575221, LC575222 and LC575223 are replaced respectively by LC757221, LC757222 and LC757223. The corrected notice has its own DOI, 10.3897/zookeys.1261.175115. Old values are retained here only to identify the superseded publication error, not as current sequence bindings for this species. This notice does not add specimens, certify contemporary catalogue identity, independently verify downloaded sequence contents or report re-estimated distances and node supports. It corrects the identifiers; the original morphology, field observations and qualified phylogeny retain their own evidence scope.

## A new mitogenome is distinct from the corrected original ND2 labels {#s-a992eb4b17}
One specimen collected at Ngoc Linh Mountain, reported as the type locality in Quang Ngai Province, Vietnam, supplied liver DNA for Illumina sequencing and MitoZ assembly and annotation. The reported circular mitogenome is 16,260 base pairs, deposited under PX673616, with a 735-base-pair control region. This new assembly accession is distinct from the LC757221–LC757223 ND2 labels corrected in the original-description corrigendum. The study does not add a population sample or independent verification of the downloaded accession records here, and its administrative locality wording is retained as recorded rather than used to rewrite earlier locality evidence.

## A conventional mitochondrial gene complement in one assembly {#s-ba7ee7b2ec}
Both new assemblies were annotated as containing thirteen protein-coding genes, twenty-two transfer RNAs and two ribosomal RNAs: thirty-seven genes, plus a noncoding control region. Most genes lie on one strand; ND6 and eight tRNAs lie on the opposite strand. The source predicts tRNA secondary structures computationally, including loss of the D-arm in tRNA-Ser2. These are assembly annotations and predicted RNA structures, not experimentally measured gene expression, metabolic performance or a whole nuclear genome. The biological consequences of small sequence and gene-length differences remain unresolved.

## Mitochondrial grouping depends on the sampled comparison {#s-fe8ecc3856}
In the thirteen-protein-coding-gene analysis, ngoclinhensis and vietnamensis were described as early-diverging branches within Yaotriton. The separate 16S–ND2 comparison grouped specimen TY000002 with earlier ngoclinhensis ND2 samples IEBR A.5130 and A.5131. This supports the authors’ identification within their marker sample; it is not a divergence date, an assertion that living species are ancestral, or a fully sampled species tree. The paper discusses re-identifying another accession, KR733683, from wenxianensis to maolanensis; that is a source-proposed reassignment of a different taxon and does not authorize changing ngoclinhensis evidence or the pinned catalogue.

## Assembly resources support future assays rather than validated monitoring {#s-3bdbd0f6d1}
The authors propose using mitochondrial regions such as ND2 or COX1 to develop species-specific environmental-DNA assays. They did not validate detection sensitivity, cross-species specificity or field occupancy estimates for these two species. One individual per species cannot characterize geographic haplotype variation, heteroplasmy or population diversity. Short-read assembly could include nuclear mitochondrial pseudogenes; the paper recommends further read-coverage and PCR–Sanger checks, especially for repetitive and control regions. Genetic resources therefore supplement future conservation work without establishing current threat categories, complete ranges or successful interventions.
