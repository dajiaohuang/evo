## The captured thermal evidence {#s-a5f758c45c}
In the archived AmphiTherm dataset, the source classification associates the name Rana with 25 species concepts and 521 estimate rows. Recorded metrics: CTmin, 84 rows across 10 source concepts; LT50_cold, 4 rows across 1 source concept; CTmax, 323 rows across 23 source concepts; Tpref, 107 rows across 7 source concepts; LT50_hot, 3 rows across 1 source concept. CTmax and CTmin are the upper and lower critical thermal limits; LT50_hot and LT50_cold are temperatures associated with 50% mortality in hot and cold assays; Tpref is preferred temperature. These metrics describe different experiments and cannot be merged into one tolerance range. Overlapping metric counts do not sum to distinct species or animals. Source life-stage codes are adults, larvae, juveniles, metamorphs. This dated material describes study coverage, not the physiology of every current member; the source's 2018 classification and present group boundaries remain distinct.

## Two cold-acclimated species with unequal hypoxia exposure {#s-8f36cbff13}
The study compares six controls and six experimental frogs per species after cold acclimation. Experimental amurensis shared one 10.3L container and spent ten days near 0.1mg/L after a seven-day oxygen decline. Dybowskii occupied separate 0.63L containers and reached a distress-defined endpoint near 0.9–1.1mg/L much earlier. These different designs do not isolate a matched species effect or give wild lethal thresholds. New amurensis brain data are combined with earlier heart/liver data; the dybowskii three-organ measurements are new here.

## Concentration patterns constrain the proposed pathway {#s-8990bb8180}
Lactate accumulation was greater in dybowskii despite shorter exposure; succinate similarity holds for liver and brain, not heart. Glycerol was present and increased in dybowskii but remained below hypoxic amurensis. The proposed alanine/glycerol/2,3-butanediol diversions may help explain lower lactate, yet tissue NMR concentrations do not measure flux or protection. Unknown reactions, alternative amino-acid transport and genomic or enzymatic confirmation remain unresolved. No uniform Rana tolerance trait follows from two sampled species.

## Selected reference assemblies and their scope {#s-80075f69a3}
The comparative anuran genome study lists two assemblies for the selected Rana pages: Rana temporaria (GCA_009801035.1), 4.11 Gbp; and Rana kukunoris (GCA_036250615.1), 4.83 Gbp. Their contig N50 values are 6,264.8 Kbp and 1,704.3 Kbp, respectively. Gbp and Kbp mean billion and thousand base pairs. Contig N50 measures assembly continuity: at least half of the assembled bases lie in contigs this long or longer. These are assembly statistics from the study's NCBI snapshot dated 29 October 2024, not direct cellular DNA measurements or a fixed genome size for the genus. The named assemblies are linked through the recorded nomenclatural matches; those links do not independently verify specimen identity.

## Repeat-data identifiers require reconciliation {#s-9b3fd24eb6}
Main Table 1 and Supplementary Table S1 do not agree on accessions for any of the 59 rows with an identical species name among 61 assemblies; two other rows also differ in spelling or name combination. The linked species accounts give the specific conflicts. The supplementary transposable-element (TE) counts and accession-linked clutch or larval records therefore cannot be assigned to accepted genus members from these tables. The study's five BUSCO (Benchmarking Universal Single-Copy Orthologs) percentages have no component labels in the inspected main table, so the measures cannot be identified.

## Comparative models and proposed mechanisms {#s-de4e316d3d}
The authors compared 61 assemblies with phylogenetic models, multiple-testing correction and contig N50 as a covariate. RepeatMasker matches, occupied length and genome proportions are distinct measures. Sequence divergence from a repeat consensus is a proxy for the age or activity of a repeat copy, not a direct observation of current transposition. Similarity to a putatively full-length Ty3 open reading frame (ORF, a DNA sequence that could encode a protein) does not show that the protein is expressed. Developmental constraints are interpretations of across-species associations, not experimental evidence of a causal effect in each genus member. The paper gives conflicting statements about TE proportions, ORF totals and GC-content associations; GC content is the share of DNA bases that are guanine or cytosine.
