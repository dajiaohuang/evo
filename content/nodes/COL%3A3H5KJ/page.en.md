## Leaves, flower clusters and ovary details {#s-eaaec5eb21}
A Brazilian Flora 2020 v393.147 field lists petiolate leaves whose blades may be elliptic, broadly elliptic, oblanceolate or obovate. Their tips are acute or cuspidate, their bases rounded or wedge-shaped, and their margins entire; three basal acrodromous veins run from near the base toward the tip. The inflorescence is a terminal corymb-like thyrse with leafy bracts. Flowers may be four- or five-parted and stalked. The hypanthium ranges from tubular to narrowly bell-shaped; inner calyx lobes are blunt or rounded, while the outer lobes are tiny and toothed or absent. Petals are recorded as white, pink or white with a pink mark, obovate and with asymmetric tips. Stamens are similar in size and form. Their slightly sickle-shaped, awl-like anthers have rough pollen sacs and terminal pores on the ventral side; the connective extends below the pollen sacs and carries linear, awl-like basal appendages. The ovary is attached to the hypanthium at its base, oblong and ribbed, with a toothed, ciliate tip; the source records “4–5 loculicida” without expanding that term. The fruit-type label is velatídio and is described as ribbed; seeds are obpyramidal. The field’s alternatives are not a measure of population variation or a complete diagnosis.

## A single habitat label {#s-bff433abee}
A separate field classifies the recorded habitat as “terrícola,” or terrestrial. It does not provide a collection locality, survey date or range coverage, or specify whether the material was wild, cultivated, managed or fossil. This label gives no fuller account of the species’ ecology.
