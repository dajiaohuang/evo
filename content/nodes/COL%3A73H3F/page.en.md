## Name and classification {#s-be85c4df32}
COL26.8 lists Microhyla fodiens as a species. Its recorded classification path is Anura (order) → Microhylidae (family) → Microhyla (genus). This entry reflects the catalogue release dated 20 August 2026.

## How to read the evidence below {#s-82cb2687ec}
The catalogue entry identifies the taxon and its recorded placement. The observations and measurements below come from separate sources, so each remains tied to the material and limits stated with it.

## Adult and subadult sizes retain a holotype discrepancy {#s-dd455cab6b}
The diagnosis gives two adult males at 20.8–29.12 mm snout–vent length. The holotype description instead prints 20.1 mm, while its measurement list gives 20.8 mm; the referred male is separately reported at 29.1 mm. These source values are not silently reconciled. Five subadult paratypes measure 12.6–17.9 mm and are not an adult female sample. The stout frog has a broad flattened head, small finger I, no terminal discs or median digit grooves, short limbs, basal toe webbing and a greatly enlarged shovel-shaped outer metatarsal tubercle. The diagnostic combination does not establish an adult female range.

## Live markings differ with individual and developmental stage {#s-19a3d5b3db}
The live holotype has a beige background with dark brown hourglass-like dorsal marking, black inguinal spots extending onto the thighs, barred hind limbs, grey mottling on throat and chest, a whitish belly and pinkish limbs below. Its iris is gold-reticulated with bluish sclera. Subadult throats are off-white without the adult mottling, while the larger referred male has a darker throat and additional axillary spots. These are described specimen and stage differences, not a fixed female colour or a measured developmental colour trajectory. Preservative fading is not interpreted as natural seasonal change.

## Sheltered nighttime encounters support only an apparent burrowing habit {#s-93500a94b2}
The holotype came from Kan Pauk near a pagoda at 232 m; the natural-history account describes a dry, sparsely vegetated village setting around a permanent, likely artificial livestock pond. Collections occurred at 19:00–21:00. Subadults hid in bank cracks and the holotype in a stone-fence crevice; the authors consequently call the frog an apparent good burrower. No digging trial, burrowing rate or physiological drought tolerance was measured. During the hot, dry survey animals were inactive in shelters and no calls were recorded. Nearby Shinma Taung’s 514 m summit is not the frog’s collection elevation, and regional climate averages are not body-temperature limits.

## Kan Pauk is confirmed; wider arid-valley occurrence is anticipated {#s-d0da575c4b}
The 2019 account confirms only Kan Pauk in Yesagyo Township, Magway, at roughly 230 m, distinct from the holotype’s precise 232 m label. Searches of paddy fields and large water bodies within two kilometres found no additional site, but this local non-detection is not proof of regional absence. Occurrence elsewhere in the Irrawaddy–Chindwin interfluve is anticipated rather than confirmed. The historical rubra record from Moulmein, now Mawlamyine in Mon State, is expressly considered taxonomically unresolved and possibly a different species, not included as a confirmed fodiens locality.

## Mitochondrial evidence supports a distinct examined lineage {#s-ace8558c46}
The 2,481-base mitochondrial 12S–16S genealogy places the sampled frog in the achatina group, with morphological differences supporting its description. The taxonomy paragraph gives divergence of at least 8.8% from sequenced congeners, whereas the diagnosis reports 9.1–12.4%; these differently stated summaries remain separate. They are not nuclear-genomic proof of reproductive isolation or a universal species threshold. Old rubra labels need voucher-level reassessment and are not automatically transferred on geographic grounds.

## Missing biological information underlies the dated Data Deficient proposal {#s-9394230f49}
The authors explicitly report unknown diet, eggs and larvae and no recorded calling during their survey. Missing recordings do not prove that the species is mute, and unknown larvae do not support direct development. With range and population trends unresolved, the 2019 paper proposes Data Deficient using cited IUCN category guidance. This is an author proposal, not live verification of a current Red List assessment. The single known locality is an evidence limitation, not by itself a quantified population decline, extinction probability or proof that all suitable habitats were surveyed.
