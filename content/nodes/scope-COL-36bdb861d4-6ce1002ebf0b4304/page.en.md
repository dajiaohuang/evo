## Lion-tailed macaque: local observations and sampled lineages {#s-a9fcc9060e}
COL26.8 accepts Macaca silenus (Linnaeus, 1758). Field observations describe one troop in a 2016 dry season; a separate version-one 2024 preprint reports four groups, while genetic studies use mitochondrial samples from defined Western Ghats sites. These sources do not form a complete annual or range-wide account.

## atlas-profile / ecology / diet {#s-72c4c3b9f1}
At Puthuthottam, one troop ate plant parts from 19 species and other foods during a 2016 dry-season study. Plant feeding was greater in forest interior than at the edge; invertebrate feeding was greater in an open patch than in the interior.

## atlas-profile / ecology / habitat {#s-6ab4494894}
Over 480 follow-hours in one habituated troop covered forest interior, edge, an open patch and a settlement from February to May 2016. Active foraging was higher in the open patch and edge, and lower in the settlement than in the interior.

## Evidence from different groups and markers {#s-53660e8b50}
One troop was followed at Puthuthottam in a dry season. A separate version-one 2024 bioRxiv preprint reports four groups but no sampling year. Mitochondrial lineage and HVR-I studies address sampled genetic variation. Together these sources do not provide a range-wide or annual ecological synthesis.

## One field troop, separate genetic samples {#s-9a39688794}
Feeding and activity were recorded from one Puthuthottam troop in 2016; mitochondrial studies sampled animals or fragments at Western Ghats sites. A separate seed-dispersal result remains a version-one preprint. Each line of evidence has its own sample and scope.

## Wild samples from 15 Western Ghats sites {#s-c0db3bd0c1}
A 2010–2012 genetic study sampled wild animals at 15 sites in Karnataka, Kerala and Tamil Nadu, with captive blood samples identified separately. Those sites describe the study's sampling frame, not the full present-day range.

## Activity in one Puthuthottam troop {#s-74592b3bef}
From February to May 2016, researchers followed one habituated troop for more than 480 hours across forest interior, edge, an open patch and a settlement. Active foraging was higher in the open patch and edge, and lower in the settlement than in the interior. The comparison covers one troop and one dry season.

## Foods recorded in the 2016 troop {#s-99d08ed3db}
The troop ate parts from 19 plant species as well as non-plant foods. In the habitat comparisons, invertebrate feeding was more frequent in the open patch than in forest interior, while plant feeding was greater in the interior than at the edge. These observations are not a complete species diet.

## A four-group result from a version-one preprint {#s-ebe027c0a3}
A version-one 2024 bioRxiv preprint reports 375.9 hours of focal observations across four groups in a 92-hectare Puduthottam fragment. Subadults consumed a greater variety of native and non-native fruits than adults and dispersed fewer Ficus seeds than adult females. The sampling year is not reported.

## A fossil-calibrated mitochondrial estimate {#s-e1a4cf3b6d}
A Western Ghats study distinguished sampled mitochondrial lineages north and south of the Palghat Gap. A fossil-calibrated model using 893 mitochondrial bases estimated their mean most-recent common ancestor at 2.11 million years ago. This is not a nuclear species tree or a direct fossil date for the species' origin.

## HVR-I diversity in eight Anamalai fragments {#s-a6193aa7e0}
A separate study compared HVR-I mitochondrial samples from eight Anamalai forest fragments with nearby continuous-forest samples and found lower diversity in fragments. This describes the sampled marker and sites; it does not measure population viability or extinction risk.

## What these studies leave unresolved {#s-1f2d77fc61}
The selected sources do not synthesize body size, life history, complete distribution, annual ecology or current formal conservation status. Feeding observations come from one troop in one dry season; the seed-dispersal findings remain a preprint.

## Limits of the selected evidence {#s-f10544d7a4}
The selected studies cover specific Western Ghats genetic samples and local observations, not a complete distribution, annual ecology, life history or current conservation assessment. Feeding observations come from one troop in one dry season; seed-dispersal results remain a preprint.
