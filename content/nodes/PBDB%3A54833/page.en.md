## Several kinds of evidence, several kinds of inference {#s-5e24e21147}
Tyrannosaurus rex is known from a large specimen sample, but no single fossil contains the whole account. A Hell Creek stratigraphic census tracks occurrences; a growth series compares skull changes across specimens; bite traces and coprolite contents preserve evidence of feeding on bone. Each line answers a different question about presence, development or behaviour.

The observations do not automatically yield population density, adult mass or bite performance. Those quantities depend on sampling and models, while tooth marks and coprolites support a narrower conclusion: bone was consumed. Keeping the sample, trace and model separate makes the animal’s history easier to read without turning one estimate into a universal fact.
