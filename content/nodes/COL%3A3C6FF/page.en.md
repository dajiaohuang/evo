## Two veins and alternatives in fruit shape {#s-e8934d3b66}
The Brazilian Flora 2020 v393.147 field describes elliptic, papery leaves with wedge-shaped bases and tips that taper to a point. Their margins roll under and lack a hard, cartilage-like edge. The upper midrib, or main leaf vein, is grooved, with two veins near the leaf edge. Flowers grow in compact clusters called fascicles. Their supporting bracts fall away, while smaller, inconspicuous bracteoles beside flowers persist. The flower cup is smooth but bears hairs; its calyx is open in bud, with four sepals and four petals. The fruit is round or ellipsoid and smooth, with persistent inconspicuous bracteoles. Seed number and embryo are marked unknown.

## A terrestrial label without range details {#s-dd8cd93577}
The habitat field records “terrícola,” meaning terrestrial. It gives no locality, survey date or range coverage and does not identify wild, cultivated, managed or fossil material. This is a habitat category, not a distribution account.
