## Name and this data account {#s-23335747a6}
COL26.8 accepts Bitis heraldica in Viperidae. Its ReptTraits v1-2 entry follows the Reptile Database 2023 name framework and retains citations for individual traits. The species row summarizes compiled records, not one uniform condition across populations.

## Distinguish the measured length {#s-d78286bc37}
The reported maximum total length (TL), including the tail, is 410 mm. Snout–vent length (SVL) ends at the vent and is a different measurement. This compiled maximum describes source records, not typical size or an absolute growth limit. Values for different sexes may come from separate studies or places and do not establish size differences within a population.

## How to read reproductive records {#s-fa241629b7}
The source labels reproductive mode as viviparous. This category does not establish fetal nutrition or show that reproduction is uniform among populations. No supported clutch or litter count is included here.

## Evidence limits {#s-c6189d86ca}
This supplemental account uses limited trait material and does not count as complete morphology or ecology. This account paraphrases the same-name ReptTraits v1-2 entry, retaining its 2023 taxonomic framework and sources for individual traits. One row per species compresses variation in sex, age, locality and populations; matching name and family do not ensure equivalent later concepts. Reported maximum length is not an absolute species limit, and reproductive means are not fixed counts in every clutch. Many mass entries are estimated from length equations and are not presented here as measured mass. Juvenile-length headers and definitions differ and are also omitted from reader conclusions. Conservation, venom, current distribution and population trends are not updated here; unrecorded fields remain gaps. Dataset citation labels preserve the source chain and do not imply that this project independently reread every original work.

## Traits not interpreted here {#s-40ef22ed33}
The sourced records cover total length and reproductive mode. No supported species value is given for snout–vent length, sex-specific length, clutch or litter counts, egg dimensions, microhabitat, diet, activity time, foraging mode, or sex-determination mechanism. Blank fields and related species cannot fill these gaps.
