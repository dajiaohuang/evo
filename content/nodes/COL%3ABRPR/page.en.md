## What leaf and flower traits identify Algernonia leandrii? {#s-706981e26d}
The brown branches are hairless and exude latex. Alternate simple leaves have cylindrical stalks, triangular glandular stipules with toothed edges, and papery, elliptic-oblong, obovate, or lance-shaped blades. Bases are wedge-shaped or blunt, tips have a small point, and margins are broad-toothed and rolled under; rounded glands occur at the base, with net-like tertiary veins. Terminal or axillary spikes have overlapping bracts at the base. Male flowers have a single perianth whorl, linear bracts, three to five toothed sepals, and two globose anthers. Female bracts are oval with blunt tips and scalloped edges; the calyx has five or six oval, entire sepals with glands. The ovary lacks horns. Fruits are capsules that split along their chambers; seeds are globose.
