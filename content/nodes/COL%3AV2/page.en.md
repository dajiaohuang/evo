## What the Aves measurement route contains {#s-98432a2fc3}
This Aves page follows the pinned COL26.8 class into an AVONET v7 measurement sample. The sample uses historical same-name, same-family records with one-to-one crosswalks and direct morphological measurements. Compare beak, wing and tarsus averages with each species’ sample size; this route presents research material, not a complete account of every accepted bird.

## How the beak, wing and mass fields differ {#s-4002e2807f}
Beak length, width and depth use specified anatomical landmarks. Wing length runs from the carpal joint to the longest primary feather tip on an unflattened wing. These are source species averages, not individual extremes. Body mass has separate weighing or literature provenance and cannot inherit the morphology sample count. A missing morphology value does not imply that ecological information is also missing.

## Selected AVONET ecology records {#s-3c22ef7bc8}
The selected AVONET records include a forest-associated Accipiter nisus, penguin Aptenodytes forsteri and marine-bird Phoebastria immutabilis. Their habitat, trophic niche, lifestyle and migration fields are dataset categories. Geographic summaries describe historical breeding and resident ranges, not non-breeding distribution or current occupancy; missing categories remain unrecorded.

## Life-history fields need their own events and units {#s-48d97392b0}
The selected life-history records include adult body mass, offspring or eggs per clutch, time from oviposition to hatching, and age at flight capability. Some source records instead describe age at leaving the nest, so those events should not be treated as interchangeable. This route is limited to the batch’s unique historical same-name, same-family entries with at least three source-supported focal fields. Accipiter chionogaster, A. erythronemius, A. nisus, A. ovampensis and A. rufiventris provide entry points. Check each field’s unit and provenance before comparing values.

## Diet and foraging fields in selected archive records {#s-d7bf719ec5}
The selected EltonTraits records for Struthio camelus, Rhea americana, R. pennata, Casuarius casuarius and C. bennetti include food-category weights and foraging strata. This route draws on unique historical same-name, same-family entries with at least two usable ecological field groups. Food values are coarse weights; foraging fields use the source’s categories or binary codes. These are historical archive fields, not direct observations of a particular meal or activity.

## A separate EltonTraits subset for tinamous and chachalacas {#s-5ebfc5ec3d}
A separate EltonTraits subset links records for Crypturellus erythropus, Tinamotis ingoufi, Ortalis garrula, O. erythroptera and O. motmot with diet and foraging fields. It uses unique historical same-name, same-family entries with exactly one usable ecological field group; the selected records span diet and foraging fields, while each entry meets the one-group filter. The archive’s food weights and stratum categories are historical coded data, not complete species accounts or direct observations of a particular animal. Keep this subset distinct from the other EltonTraits route.

## How the Aves datasets differ {#s-b30397e8ca}
Historical names and one-to-one crosswalks do not guarantee current species-concept equivalence. These compilations are not current population or conservation assessments, and no one dataset describes biology shared by all birds.

## Selected birds in the AVONET ecology-only route {#s-60900ec887}
This AVONET route records habitat, trophic niche, primary lifestyle and migration categories for selected species. Entry points include Aviceda madagascariensis, Buteo albigula, Circaetus beaudouini, Henicopernis infuscatus and Nisaetus bartelsi. These are selected historical records, not complete ecology accounts for all birds.

## Ecology categories and withheld measurements {#s-66af9e28be}
Habitat, trophic niche, primary lifestyle and migration are recorded as dataset-defined categories; they are not independent observations of behavior. Historical range maps do not show current occupancy. The ecology-only route does not interpret beak, wing, tarsus, tail or body-mass fields, so it cannot support a body-size comparison.

## Scope of the AVONET ecology-only route {#s-ebafbecdb7}
This AVONET ecology-only route follows selected historical same-name, same-family records under the pinned COL26.8 hierarchy.

## A navigation route from Archaeopteryx to living birds {#fossil-anthology}
This Aves route pairs a rounded 150 Ma fossil anchor from referred Archaeopteryx material in the Late Jurassic Bavarian lithographic limestones with living birds at the present. Archaeopteryx is outside crown Neornithes, so this is a browsing anthology, not a continuous ancestor chain or a date for the origin of crown birds.
