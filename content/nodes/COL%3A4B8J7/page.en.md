## Evidence limits {#s-d00bbb5680}
AVONET v7 summarizes historical samples and species-level values. Morphology is averaged across the listed birds; it is neither an identification diagnosis nor a range of individual measurements. Body mass comes from a separate source and does not use the morphology sample count. Habitat, diet and migration are broad categories, while mapped geography covers historical breeding and resident ranges. Matching names across BirdLife, eBird, BirdTree and COL26.8 supports a crosswalk but does not settle species boundaries after splits or mergers. Reproduction, present populations and conservation require other evidence.
The archive supplies one supported ecological field group; the other diet, activity and foraging-stratum fields remain without an interpretable value here. EltonTraits 1.0 compiles historical foraging descriptions under earlier names and family assignments. A same-name, same-family match supports comparison with the COL26.8 record but does not prove that the historical and current species concepts are identical. Food and bird foraging-stratum percentages are standardized relative weights from descriptions, not measurements of intake, stomach contents, energy use or time budgets. Bird A/B diet codes retain uncertainty in source and relative proportions; mammal ABC food or activity fields may draw on a congener judged ecologically similar. Genus- or family-level typical values, non-species-level strata and records affected by taxonomic splits do not support species-level conclusions. These data describe the listed fields, not complete ecology, current distribution, population or conservation status.
