## Accepted name and classification {#s-72a8700f7b}
COL26.8 accepts *Discoglossus pictus* Otth, 1837 as a species. Its recorded classification places it in the genus *Discoglossus*, family Alytidae and order Anura. This name and placement follow the release dated 20 August 2026.

## What these records describe {#s-4dd7e8b05c}
This page combines older trait compilations, image-based tadpole records, two thermal-test estimates and a comparative genome study. The sources cover different measurements and historical name concepts. Matching a name to a source is a route to that record, not proof that every study used the same species concept as COL26.8.

## Foraging categories in AmphiBIO {#s-3fc23d77eb}
In AmphiBIO’s Frost 2011 classification, fossorial (burrowing), terrestrial and aquatic foraging categories are all marked for *Discoglossus pictus*. These broad labels do not describe seasonal or life-stage changes. A later TetrapodTraits revision reports correcting some amphibian microhabitat entries carried forward from older compilations; the available comparison does not show whether these categories changed. They remain historical AmphiBIO records.

## Adult food categories {#s-cc8aa43e2a}
The same AmphiBIO record lists arthropods and vertebrates as adult food categories; its vertebrate category includes cannibalism. The dataset compiled reports based on expert judgment, observation or stomach contents. These categories are not a complete menu or measured diet proportions.

## Activity recorded by day and night {#s-d61bb5e83e}
AmphiBIO marks both daytime and night-time activity. Those are broad categories from the compilation, not a continuous record of when an individual is active.

## Activity in wet and warm conditions {#s-7ff5af7f1d}
AmphiBIO records “wet” and “warm” seasonal categories. The dataset compares conditions during activity with the local annual climate, so these labels do not correspond to the same calendar months everywhere.

## Maximum adult size in an older compilation {#s-81507bb477}
AmphiBIO lists a maximum adult body mass of 22.05 g and maximum body length of 80 mm. For frogs, its length measure is snout–vent length; for salamanders and caecilians it is total length. These are literature-derived maxima, not averages or measurements of every frog. A later TetrapodTraits revision reports corrections to some older amphibian body-mass entries, but the available record does not say whether the 22.05 g value was affected.

## Life-history values gathered from literature {#s-ed2ab75939}
The Frost 2011 AmphiBIO entry lists sexual maturity at 4 years, 20–1,500 offspring or eggs per clutch, up to 1 reproductive event per year, and offspring-or-egg sizes of 1–1.5 mm. These are compiled literature values, not measurements from one population. The row does not identify sex, population, date or wild-versus-captive conditions for each value, and the size field may refer to an offspring or an egg.

## A recorded larval stage {#s-3d1e34d7fa}
AmphiBIO flags a larval stage for *D. pictus*. This records a larval phase but does not describe parental care, breeding location or seasonal timing.

TadpoleTraits contains no literature-derived rows and one image-derived row for this source name. Its stage is recorded as 28, with the label “Judging based on the picture.” That is an image-based stage judgment, not a directly observed developmental series. Row counts are not counts of unique animals, and sample sizes from separate studies are not added together.

## Proportions measured from the image record {#s-c2974e6416}
TadpoleTraits row 395, judged to be stage 28 from the picture, reports body length/total length = 0.349572, maximum body height/total length = 0.239928 and maximum tail height/total length = 0.226233. These are dimensionless proportions from an image, which may be a drawing or photograph, not measurements of a representative wild population. Fields inconsistent with their same-row numerator and total length are excluded.

## One upper thermal-limit estimate {#s-38de66f374}
AmphiTherm row 2037 (ID 2241; Gutierrez-Pesquera_et_al_2016) reports a larval CTmax of 39.1 °C, with no response to prodding as the endpoint. The record gives 20 °C acclimation, water as the test medium and a 0.25 °C/min heating or cooling rate. Its n_trait field is 16 and should not be added across repeated cohorts. Sex is unknown and origin is wild. Chemical, hormone, infection and pathogen fields are NA (unreported, not confirmed absent). The reported uncertainty, 0.73 °C, is a standard deviation rather than a temperature range. This is one compiled assay estimate, not a species-wide tolerance boundary or environmental limit; the study is not independently reanalysed here.

## A reference genome assembly {#s-f795e990e5}
A comparative study published online in December 2025 and in a 2026 volume lists assembly GCA_033576555.1 for *Discoglossus pictus*. Its assembled genome-size summary is 3.87 Gbp (billion base pairs), and its contig N50 is 3,043.6 Kbp (thousand base pairs): at that length, at least half the assembly is contained in contigs this long or longer. These values describe that assembly, not DNA content in every cell, a fixed genome size for the species or ploidy. The authors selected NCBI reference assemblies on 29 October 2024. The original sequence data and assembly quality were not independently reassessed here. A separate table conflict described below prevents treating the accession-to-species link as settled.

## What the assembly table does and does not show {#s-6bc6f50269}
Table 1 marks short-read sequencing, long-read sequencing and 3D-structure data for this entry. An unmarked cell does not prove that a technology was never used. A BUSCO caption names compleasm 0.2.7 and tetrapoda_odb12, but the inspected table does not label the components of its five-value score vector; those percentages cannot be assigned to assumed completeness categories. The study removed three assemblies with contig N50 below 1 kb and retained 61. Passing that continuity filter alone does not certify completeness, recovery of repeats or a species-wide biological measurement.

## Repeat annotations are computational matches {#s-e051c6a776}
The authors built species-specific repeat libraries with RepeatModeler2, classified unknown elements with RFSB, added vertebrate repeats and then used RepeatMasker. The paper calls its sequence matches “insertions,” but a match alone does not show that an element is complete, expressed or currently mobile. A superfamily label can combine distinct families. Counts, total occupied length and genome proportion are different quantities, and overlapping hierarchy categories cannot be added as if independent. This page reports the study’s annotation; it does not add a new repeat annotation or expression experiment for *D. pictus*.

## A cross-species association is not a species mechanism {#s-f3f10bdae0}
The authors compared 61 assemblies with models adjusted for shared ancestry, using a previously pruned tree, contig-N50 covariates and multiple-comparison correction. Across that sample, sequences classified as recently active and associated with Ty3/DIRS1 correlate with genome size. “Recent activity” is inferred from sequence divergence; it is not direct observation of current transposition in *D. pictus*. Length and open-reading-frame similarity filters do not demonstrate protein expression. The proposed developmental constraints are comparative hypotheses, not proof that transposable elements cause this species’ larval duration or clutch size.

## Conflicting assembly labels limit species-level claims {#s-56fa12e49f}
Main Table 1 links *Discoglossus pictus* to GCA_033576555.1, but Supplementary Table S1 row 20 assigns the same species name to GCA_027410445.1. Main Table 1 instead lists that accession under *Ranitomeya imitator*. The tables therefore do not establish which accession belongs to *D. pictus*. Supplementary repeat counts and proportions remain conflicting source values, not verified traits of the accepted species. Tables S2–S3 contain 55 clutch and 39 larval-period rows by accession, but their headers give no individual provenance or units; the accession conflict prevents assigning those life-history values to this species.

## Scope of the available evidence {#s-4cb37e8c9b}
