## Accepted name and placement {#s-0aa8417b54}
COL26.8 accepts Ptychadena delphina Goutte, Reyes-Velasco, Freilich, Kassie & Boissinot, 2021 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Ptychadenidae (family) → Ptychadena (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## The usual forest records and an unresolved outlier {#s-626a9e1130}
Most records were from mid-elevation forest west of the Great Rift Valley and north of the Geba River, at 1612–2064 m. Two historical Asgori individuals at 2370 m clustered with this species genetically, but possible introgression and failure to confirm a population left their interpretation unresolved. That outlier is not merged into an unqualified normal range.

## Calling at rain-filled sites {#s-4c79dcd192}
Males called at night in flooded grassland ponds, puddles and rain-filled road holes, sometimes jointly with P. doro. These observations record calling-site use and shared presence, without establishing hybridization, identical egg sites or complete reproductive timing.

## A geographically sampled mitochondrial and nuclear dataset {#s-55f1930459}
The 2023 Ethiopian Highlands study lists 15 mitochondrial samples and 9 nuclear-SNP samples for Ptychadena delphina in Table 1, with mitochondrial nucleotide diversity π=0.24%. Samples across the twelve-species study were collected in 2011–2019; these are analysed specimens, not a population census. All assembled mitochondrial genomes had the usual two rRNAs, thirteen protein-coding genes and twenty-two tRNAs, but no species had a completely resolved control region because repeats could not be confidently assembled from short reads. The phylogenetic alignment used selected genes (13,937 bp across 303 samples), rather than an alignment of complete control regions.

## A study-defined highland clade and regional distribution {#s-4317c9ecf1}
The paper places Ptychadena delphina in its P. neumanni clade and reports it from the western side of the Great Rift Valley. These distributions combine the authors' geographic sampling and earlier range work; they are not independently updated complete range polygons. The twelve-species highland clade is a subset of Ptychadena. Its mitochondrial-clock analysis estimated a common ancestor around 11.7 million years ago, with a reported interval of 8.827–15.494 million years. The east-of-rift ancestral reconstruction had a model probability of 69%; neither that estimate nor inferred crossings is a directly observed migration event or a nuclear-genome divergence date for this species.

## A dated reassessment argument, with remaining demographic gaps {#s-aaa157fefc}
The 2023 discussion calls for conservation assessment or reassessment using revised species concepts and genetic and geographic evidence. Earlier names and broad distributions can combine lineages that later work separated. The present genomic sample does not itself measure current abundance, fragmentation, decline or reproductive success in Ptychadena delphina. Any Red List category mentioned in this paper belongs to its dated discussion, not a verified current official listing. Regional land-use and climate concerns support further investigation but do not quantify this species' current threat level.

## Its position differs between mitochondrial tree analyses {#s-978bb524a4}
The all-sample maximum-likelihood tree placed delphina beside harenna + neumanni with weak bootstrap support (48). The time-calibrated Bayesian tree placed it beside doro, with the pair sister to harenna + neumanni (reported posterior probability 1). These are different analyses of mitochondrial information, not independent nuclear confirmation of one topology. The paper itself identifies this exception to otherwise broadly concordant species relationships.
