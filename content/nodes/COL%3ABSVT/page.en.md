## What the Turkish study says about jerboa burrows {#preview}
A Turkish study reports that Allactaga euphratica used summer and reproductive burrows and produced two or three litters per year in the observed study context. The abstract does not provide enough detail here to extend those observations to all populations. The study’s use of this name has not been reconciled with the pinned COL26.8 concept; a matching name alone does not establish concept equivalence.

## What the Turkish study says about jerboa burrows {#field-study}
Çolak and Yiğit’s study compares three jerboas in Turkey. The abstract states that A. euphratica and A. williamsi had two burrow categories—reproductive and summer burrows—whereas A. elater had four, including winter and temporary burrows. It reports two or three litters per year for A. euphratica, but the accessible abstract does not give a species-specific breeding season for it.

The same abstract reports mean litter size of 4.8 for A. euphratica, with observed litters ranging from four to eight. It also says all three species generally fed on plant material and that the study found no agricultural pest status. These are study summaries rather than a complete species profile: capture locations, observation periods, and methods need the full paper before the figures can be interpreted more broadly.

The study’s use of A. euphratica has not been reconciled with the pinned COL26.8 concept. The matching name alone does not establish equivalent taxon concepts.
