## Pendulous pink flowers and close-veined bracts {#s-c2a2d5acfa}
A regional flora account describes Dierama cooperi as a corm-bearing herb (a corm is a swollen underground storage stem) reaching 2 m, growing alone, in small tufts or in colonies. Its pendulous spikes carry relatively small, usually bright pink flowers, sometimes white. The closely spaced, parallel veins in its bracts (modified, leaf-like structures associated with flowers) make the bract base look nearly solid, a useful distinction from D. robustum, which has more widely spaced veins and larger flowers. The account suggests the two may hybridise in northern Lesotho and the neighbouring Free State.

## Recorded in mesic grassland {#s-76af207bae}
The cited regional account records mesic grassland. It gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency, so this is a habitat entry rather than a complete ecological account.
