## atlas-profile / overview {#s-07af159e73}
Sea urchins and sand dollars belong to Echinoidea, but its deep history is not read from one clock. Darriwilian plates assigned to the total group show echinoid relatives in the Ordovician; a phylogenomic analysis instead estimates the crown group’s origin near the Carboniferous–Permian boundary. The fossil records an occurrence, while the model estimates a divergence under its assumptions.

## atlas-profile / ecology / diet {#s-2238af8ef9}
Living echinoids include omnivores and detritivores. Those present-day feeding modes do not yet resolve the diet of the class ancestor.

## atlas-profile / ecology / habitat {#s-21e4d8ce1d}
The class occupies marine seafloors from shallow settings to deep water; the cited global record does not reduce that span to one habitat.

## atlas-profile / ecology / locomotion {#s-c4595581ea}
Some species live on the sediment surface and others within it. Neither epifaunal nor infaunal life describes the entire class.

## atlas-profile / ecology / bodySize {#s-c0ca279a52}
The cited global occurrence analysis does not estimate a class-wide body-size range.

## atlas-profile / ecology / guild {#s-2089ddba7b}
Echinoidea spans several feeding and living modes, so the evidence supports no single guild assignment for the class.

## atlas-profile / traits {#s-f3d0722798}
Living crown echinoids bear spines and tube feet, structures that help describe the body plan of familiar sea urchins and sand dollars.

## atlas-profile / traits {#s-3346aedfae}
Ordovician bothriocidaroid plates document an early part of the echinoid total-group record, without by themselves dating the crown group’s origin.

## atlas-profile / evidenceSummary {#s-2966b42d52}
The Ordovician bothriocidaroid plates and the modelled crown-group date are different kinds of evidence, not competing measurements of the same event. Living species vary in diet and position on the seafloor, and the global occurrence study supplies no general body-size range. These records therefore do not support one ecological profile for all echinoids.

## atlas-profile / readerLimitations {#s-f868eb99a6}
Darriwilian plates document stem-group material, while the crown-group date comes from a separate phylogenomic model. Living species occupy varied depths and feeding modes; these samples support no single ancestral ecology or class-wide body-size envelope.
