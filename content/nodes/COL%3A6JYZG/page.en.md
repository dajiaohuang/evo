## Form and flowers {#s-1a60659a90}
The regional account describes a delicate, cormous geophyte up to 12 cm tall, with woody, overlapping corm tunics. The stem has short hairs below and is rarely branched. Leaves are linear and sickle-shaped, with narrow wings on the margins and central vein. Flowers occur in spikes of one or two; they are white, with veins tinged blue below and the outer tepals flushed blue on the outside. Stamens are symmetrical, with unequal filaments.

## Comparison with *Geissorhiza aspera* {#s-643f32b16c}
The SANBI diagnostic account describes *G. demissa* as closely allied to the common, widespread *Geissorhiza aspera* and smaller, rarely reaching 120 mm tall. It notes flat leaves with only lightly thickened margins and main vein, a short-haired stem, and unequal stamens in both species, but smaller flowers in *G. demissa*: its tepals are 7–10 mm long and anthers 2–3 mm.

The account describes *G. aspera* as variable: flowers are typically dark blue to violet, while northern populations from the Cederberg and Gifberg have white or white-and-blue flowers. It is typically larger in all respects and almost always branched, with leaf blades 2–7 mm wide, spikes bearing 2–7 flowers, and stems short-haired up to the base of the spike, including in those northern white-flowered populations. By contrast, *G. demissa* stems are unbranched and nearly hairless below the spike, and its leaves are never more than 1 mm wide. The flowers are similar in shape, but those of *G. aspera* are larger and nodding, with horizontally oriented stamens, tepals 11–15 × 4–6 mm, and anthers 2–5 mm long.
