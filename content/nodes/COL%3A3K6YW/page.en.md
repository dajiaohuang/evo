## Helichrysum mollifolium {#s-c7552ddaad}
## Soft leaves and narrow underground tubers

Hilliard's *Flora of Southern Africa* account describes a perennial with narrow, spindle-shaped root tubers and slender rhizomes. A solitary flowering stem, 350–600 mm tall, rises from a small woody stock. Most leaves form a basal group of four to six; their soft, broadly elliptic to ovate blades have long wiry stalks, blunt to slightly pointed tips, and long hairs above. The lower surface has five prominent veins, hairs concentrated on the veins, and shining yellow glands elsewhere.

Many small, bell-shaped heads form loose or compact corymb-like panicles. Outer bracts are golden-brown and inner ones yellow. The account records 19–30 florets; ovaries are smooth, though achenes were not seen. Pappus bristles are rough and strongly cohere at the base. The source places the plant in moist grassy hollows, streamside flats and damp forest margins at about 2,100–2,450 m, and distinguishes it from *H. miconiifolium* and *H. nudifolium* by stem bracts and leaf form.
