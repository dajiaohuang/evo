## A name fixed to COL26.8 {#s-af805b5879}
Catalogue of Life release COL26.8 records Pternistis atrifrons (Conover, 1930) as the accepted species name (published 2026-08-20). This card preserves that version's identity; the name entry alone does not describe anatomy or ecology.

## Measured samples and inference limits {#s-f034afe7e3}
The row has four morphology birds (three female, one male); none has a complete measurement set. Inference=YES flags nostril-to-tip beak length, beak width/depth, tarsus, Kipp’s distance, first secondary and hand-wing index as inferred from castaneicollis. They cannot be treated as direct measurements of these four atrifrons birds. Wing 173.8 mm, culmen 27.5 mm and tail 49.2 mm are not listed among this row’s inferred fields; they remain reported row values without individual specimen or range-wide bounds.

## Separate provenance for body mass {#s-2a975c318f}
Mass 791.53 g has DataFromSplit provenance, an inherited value after a taxonomic split rather than independent weighing of the four morphology birds. The source distinguishes this from Dunning’s compiled means. Its equality with castaneicollis at 791.53 g does not establish identical individual masses.

## Ground feeding, habitat and sedentary category {#s-161e307e3c}
Habitat is Forest; density code 1 means dense habitats, not birds per unit area. Omnivore niche means use of multiple resources without one dominant niche. Trophic level is separately recorded as Omnivore; niche and level are different fields whose labels are not substituted for each other. Terrestrial means mainly feeding while walking or hopping on the ground; Migration=1 is sedentary. Neither supplies individual routes, flight capacity or annual diet percentages.

## Evidence limits {#s-22283a1199}
This is a historical AVONET v7 guide using read fields, two name crosswalks, relevant Metadata definitions and local COL name shards. Morphology and mass denominators remain separate, and historical categories are not guaranteed to equal catalogue concepts. The whole workbook, individual raw measurements, Dunning’s book and a new full methods-paper reading were not read. Breeding, plumage identification, population movements, current range and conservation status still need independent sources; atrifrons mass is inherited after a split and several morphometric fields come from castaneicollis; concept differences and inference scope remain unresolved, without a complete directly measured size account.
