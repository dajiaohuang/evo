## One sequenced individual and a separate DNA-content estimate {#s-288eca3f71}
The study extracted liver DNA from one Chiropterotriton dimidiatus specimen as part of a sixteen-species, three-genus comparison, targeting ten gigabases of short-read data per species. Repeat analysis randomly sampled merged reads at roughly 0.1-fold coverage. Table 1 gives a genome C-value estimate of 30.3 picograms, based on blood-smear densitometry and species means from earlier genome-size work. Picograms of DNA are not gigabases, body mass or a complete genome assembly; the sequenced individual and the species-mean DNA-content estimate have different evidence roles. The sample does not determine variation among sexes, populations or ages.

## Detected repeat abundance is a method-dependent lower estimate {#s-a087ba6ef2}
Table 1 reports 62.9% detectable repeat abundance for Chiropterotriton dimidiatus, including uncategorized repeats. Table 2 reports LTRs at 15.81%, and Table 3 separates ERV at 3.329% from Ty3/Gypsy at 12.478%. These are percentages of sampled bases identified by the study's repeat library, not percentages of animals, newly inserted elements or measured gene expression. Mutation can degrade repeats beyond recognition; the authors regard short-read masking as likely underestimating true repetitive content. Rounded table categories need not sum exactly, and uncategorized repeats should not be silently assigned to a known TE superfamily.

## Repeat divergence suggests relative expansion histories {#s-0a807c450b}
Dimidiatus lacked or nearly lacked the older high-distance ERV peak found in priscus and the larger Bolitoglossa. A lower ERV peak occurred below distance four, while the principal Gypsy/Ty3 peak was around thirteen. Several repeat superfamilies included reads below distance one, which the authors interpret as evidence of recent or ongoing expansion. This indirect divergence evidence is not a direct transposition experiment, and neutral mutation rates were insufficient to date events absolutely.
