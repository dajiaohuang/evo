## The name and this data account {#s-d12aa364f1}
COL26.8 accepts Nestor meridionalis within Strigopidae. AVONET v7 records measurements under the HBW–BirdLife 2020 name: 6 individuals were measured, and 4 have a complete set of measurements. Sample sizes can still differ among traits.

## Measured beak dimensions {#s-27d04de12e}
Recorded mean beak measurements are 44.5 mm from the anterior nostril edge to the tip, 14.4 mm in width and 35.8 mm in depth at that edge. These describe separate dimensions at standardized landmarks, not field-identification cutoffs.

## Wing, tarsus and mass {#s-b7679982f7}
Mean unflattened wing length from the carpal joint to the longest primary is 284.3 mm; mean tarsus length is 38.2 mm. Compiled mean mass is 428.01 g, with source category Dunning. Mass comes from separate species-average information; this does not mean every morphological specimen was weighed.

## Habitat, feeding and lifestyle {#s-3ba0746e6f}
The dataset assigns habitat Forest, trophic niche Omnivore and lifestyle Insessorial. These are dominant categories for cross-species comparisons; they do not imply exclusive use of one habitat or food type.

## Movement and geographical summary {#s-3a5499c840}
The migration field classifies this record as sedentary. Historical breeding and resident maps span latitude endpoints -47.29° to -35.2°. This excludes a complete account of non-breeding movements and is not a current-position or global occupancy estimate. Geography and morphological measurements come from different compilation steps.

## Evidence limits {#s-4fd05b3420}
AVONET v7 summarizes historical samples and species-level values. Morphology is averaged across the listed birds; it is neither an identification diagnosis nor a range of individual measurements. Body mass comes from a separate source and does not use the morphology sample count. Habitat, diet and migration are broad categories, while mapped geography covers historical breeding and resident ranges. Matching names across BirdLife, eBird, BirdTree and COL26.8 supports a crosswalk but does not settle species boundaries after splits or mergers. Reproduction, present populations and conservation require other evidence.
