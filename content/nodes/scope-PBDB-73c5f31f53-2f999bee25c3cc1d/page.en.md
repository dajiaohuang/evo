## What the material shows {#s-6361de635e}
Micro-CT reconstructions of the Chengjiang fossil show short, pincer-like great appendages, overlapping exite flaps and a head interpreted as seven segments. Researchers use these features to test a stem-chelicerate placement; segment count and anatomical homologies are comparative interpretations.

## Feeding {#s-3cf661d8b7}
The pincers are preserved, but the fossils do not establish what Urokodia ate.

## Setting {#s-fc0d122562}
The fossils come from the marine Chengjiang setting. Depositional context does not by itself show how the animal lived.

## Movement {#s-04799fa1e2}
The available anatomy does not measure walking or swimming performance.

## Size {#s-86884cc177}
The material preserves a small, articulated arthropod; no precise body-size estimate is provided.

## Ecological role {#s-44c34794f9}
Its ecological role remains unresolved.

## Preserved features {#s-266962b7ca}
The short great appendages end in pincer-like structures.

## Preserved features {#s-413652b05c}
Overlapping exite flaps are preserved along the appendages.

## Preserved features {#s-aab9ad0bf3}
A seven-segmented head is an interpretation of the preserved anatomy.

## How to read the evidence {#s-25f29f871a}
Tomography documents the structures; chelicera and book-gill homologies and an upper stem-chelicerate position come from comparative analyses. These findings do not identify a direct ancestor of living chelicerates.
