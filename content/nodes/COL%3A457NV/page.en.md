## Woolly leaf undersides and mixed flower-cluster positions {#s-8c510eb0d7}
The Brazilian Flora 2020 v393.147 field allows either monopodial or sympodial growth and records simple stem hairs. Leaves are elliptic, ovate, or round, with an upper-surface midrib that may be channelled or fully flattened; the underside is tomentose, with a dense mat of hairs. Bracts or small scale-like cataphylls below leaf nodes are deciduous. Rounded inflorescence branchlets have bracteoles beneath the flowers that fall away. Two to four inflorescences are listed per axillary shoot, in axillary or subterminal positions with mixed terminal placement, and in an opposite pyramidal arrangement. The field presents alternatives without frequencies.

## A hairless flower cup and several recorded fruit forms {#s-33c3f01206}
The anther's two pollen sacs are equal. Hairs on the floral disc are reported at the style base or only on the staminal ring. The flower cup (hypanthium) is top-shaped (turbinate) or urn-shaped (urceolate), and glabrous. Sepals are partly fused; the ovary is recorded as having three or more than three chambers. The calyx opens by tearing, with partial tearing also listed. Fruit may be globose, ellipsoid, or cylindrical; its calyx lobes may remain attached as they wither (marcescent) or fall away. These alternatives come from one regional dataset field without frequencies.
