## What leaf, flower and fruit traits are described? {#s-062ee5e60b}
This small tree or scrambler grows to 5 m. Its branchlets are hairless. The leathery leaves are 40–80 by 20–50 mm, usually shiny and hairless above, with blunt to slightly pointed tips and blunt to rounded bases. Their margins are nearly entire; petioles reach 15 mm, and the 2–3 mm linear stipules fall away. Leaf-opposed inflorescences usually have one flower, sometimes three. Sepals are 15 mm long, greenish and hairy outside, pinkish inside. Pink petals are 13–15 mm long, with a basal nectar-producing region about 2.5 mm long. The androgynophore is hairless below and densely hairy above. The ovary is densely hairy and four-lobed; the style is 10 mm long and hairless, with a broad stigma. The four-lobed fruit is glossy, somewhat fleshy and brown to nearly black, becoming mostly hairless.

## What uncertainty does the account note about the type specimen? {#s-11993150d6}
The 1984 account compares it with G. occidentalis, noting that its leaves are larger, thicker and shinier above, with entire or nearly entire margins. The holotype in Berlin was destroyed, and no further material from that type gathering was located; the author judged from the written description that it most likely fits the current species concept.

## Where is it reported? {#s-af5925c68a}
The regional account reports it as a scrambler in coastal bush. It gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency.
