## Branched flower clusters and five or more seeds {#s-4e36c3310e}
The Brazilian Flora 2020 v393.147 field describes elliptic or narrowly elliptic, papery leaves with rounded, nearly heart-shaped or blunt bases. Their tips may have a short point, taper to a point or extend like a tail. The flat margins lack a hard, cartilage-like edge. The upper midrib, or main leaf vein, is described as flat or raised, with one vein near the leaf edge. The source lists the inflorescence as a dichasium (a branching cluster with paired side axes) or a thyrse (a branched flower cluster). Supporting bracts and the smaller bracteoles beside flowers fall away. The flower cup is smooth but bears hairs; its calyx is open in bud, with four sepals and four petals. The fruit is round and smooth, with bracteoles that fall away. Five or more seeds are recorded, with fused cotyledons (the seed leaves) in the embryo.

## A terrestrial category beside seed counts {#s-ae84649303}
The habitat field records “terrícola,” meaning terrestrial. It gives no locality, survey date or range coverage and does not specify wild, cultivated, managed or fossil material. The category is separate from the seed-count field.
