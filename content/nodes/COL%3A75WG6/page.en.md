## What leaf, flower and fruit traits does the historical account describe? {#s-28567239ac}
The Flora of Panama account describes a herb or subshrub up to 2 m tall, with a slender greyish stem bearing short star-shaped hairs and sometimes longer simple hairs. Petioles reach 4.5 cm; the stipules are awl-shaped. The ovate to lance-shaped blades reach 8 by 3.5 cm, with rounded to heart-shaped bases, pointed tips and coarsely scalloped-toothed edges. They have three, sometimes five, main veins. The upper surface is sparsely hairy; the lower is more hairy with appressed star-shaped hairs. Solitary flowers in the upper leaf axils form long, leafy, branched panicles. Their slender stalks are 2–3.5 cm and jointed above the middle. Six to eight narrow epicalyx bracts are 4–8 mm long; the hairy calyx is 3.5–5 mm. White petals are 4–8 mm long. The staminal tube is about half the corolla length, and the style is slightly longer. Each fruit segment (mericarp) is about 2.5 mm, three-sided in cross-section, obovoid, rounded and without a tip; it has a faint ridge on the back but is otherwise nearly smooth and hairless. The kidney-shaped seeds are about 2 mm with fine hairs.

## What range and settings does the historical account report? {#s-cc8fcda668}
The historical account reports this species only from Panama and Colombia, along forest edges and roadsides. This is the source’s stated range, not a current survey or complete global inventory.
