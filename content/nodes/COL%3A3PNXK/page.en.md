## Life history {#s-bd7f199cf0}
At Maromizaha Forest in Madagascar, researchers followed eight wild young indris from eight family groups across four age classes: 0–3, 4–7, 10–15, and 16–20 months. Maternal carrying was common in the youngest class and declined as locomotion increased. Play and grooming with non-maternal group members were first recorded at 128 days; singing appeared only in the oldest class. These observations describe this small sample, not species-wide development.

## Ecological evidence {#s-914a9f20a3}
Leaves were the most consumed food in all four sampled infant age classes. Fruit made up 15.14% of feeding time in the 0–3-month class and 27.09% in the 4–7-month class. Across the study, the authors recorded foods from 18 plant families and found that mother–offspring co-feeding declined with infant age. These are feeding observations from young indris at one forest, not a complete species diet.
