## Relative weights of food categories {#s-297077effe}
The archive’s nonzero food weights are invertebrates 60%; mammals and birds 40%. Source code A indicates high compiler confidence in the source and diet information. These percentages standardize verbal descriptions rather than measure diet composition; zero does not prove an item is never eaten. Categories such as Nect, Seed and PlantO are broader than their everyday names and cannot imply exclusively nectar, seeds or plants.

## Foraging strata differ from whole-day activity {#s-0600295d9f}
Foraging-stratum weights flagged as species-level in the archive are ground 70%; trees or high bushes from 2 m upward but below the canopy 20%; understory, edges or shrubs below 2 m 10%. They estimate relative use of feeding positions, not nest sites, all habitat or whole-day time budgets. Surface-water, understory, mid/high, canopy and aerial categories have different source definitions; they are not elevational ranges and cannot yield flight heights or habitat area.

## Life-history evidence limits {#s-9917fc6c4e}
EltonTraits 1.0 compiles historical foraging descriptions under earlier names and family assignments. A same-name, same-family match supports comparison with the COL26.8 record but does not prove that the historical and current species concepts are identical. Food and bird foraging-stratum percentages are standardized relative weights from descriptions, not measurements of intake, stomach contents, energy use or time budgets. Bird A/B diet codes retain uncertainty in source and relative proportions; mammal ABC food or activity fields may draw on a congener judged ecologically similar. Genus- or family-level typical values, non-species-level strata and records affected by taxonomic splits do not support species-level conclusions. These data describe the listed fields, not complete ecology, current distribution, population or conservation status.
