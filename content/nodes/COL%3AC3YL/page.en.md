## Aloe pretoriensis: a tall flower stalk {#s-6a7bedc0a0}
A South African regional account describes a stemmed succulent herb with a stem up to 1 m high and an inflorescence 2.0–3.5 m high. The perianth has no basal swelling; the outer segments are joined through their basal two-thirds. Pedicels are 12–40 mm long, and the flowers range from pale to bright pink.

A diagnostic account says the tall inflorescence and often short, upright stem make *A. pretoriensis* difficult to confuse with *A. suprafoliata* or *A. thorncroftii*.

An earlier account says the plant lacks the shrubby or tree-like form of Berger’s Arborescentes but shares inflorescence and flower characters with *A. pluridens* and *A. arborescens*. The stem is rarely branched and seldom exceeds “a foot or two” in height; it is usually covered with dried or charred old leaf bases. Its leaves are stiffer than those of *A. pluridens* and *A. arborescens* and are not sickle-shaped. The reddish leaf-edge spines are more robust and pungent than theirs. The tall, branched inflorescence is contrasted with the unbranched inflorescence of *A. lineata*, a species with other stated differences. The account reports that *A. pretoriensis* was often mistaken for *A. lineata*.
