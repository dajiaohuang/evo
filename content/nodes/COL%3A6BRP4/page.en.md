## COL26.8 name and the ReptTraits row {#s-a5b2751d48}
COL26.8 records Cuora trifasciata as an accepted species. The ReptTraits v1-2 row gives its Family field as Geoemydidae under the Reptile Database 2023 name framework. This association uses an exact accepted-name match and matching family; it does not establish that historical and current taxonomic concepts are identical.

## Length values in the archived records {#s-b37fd1db3f}
The row reports a maximum straight carapace length (SCL) of 250 mm. ReptTraits attributes this field to Parham et al. 2001, Buskirk et al. 2005.

## Habitat use and activity labels {#s-ea710df197}
The microhabitat field classifies the recorded position as tree-dwelling (Arboreal)/rock-dwelling (Saxicolous)/ground-dwelling (Terrestrial) [source label: Arboreal/Saxicolous/Terrestrial]. It is a habitat-use category, not a full description of every setting used. ReptTraits attributes this field to Stark et al. 2018. The diet field classifies the entry as animal-eating (Carnivorous); this category does not identify particular prey or foods. ReptTraits attributes this field to Parham et al. 2001, Buskirk et al. 2005. The active-time field classifies activity as active across day and night (Cathemeral); it is the dataset's timing category. ReptTraits attributes this field to Stark et al. 2018. The foraging-mode field records ACT (active foraging). ReptTraits attributes this field to Parham et al. 2001, Buskirk et al. 2005.

## Reproductive fields in ReptTraits {#s-d2899ccbb4}
The compiled mean for offspring per litter or eggs per clutch is 2. It is a mean summary, not the count from one litter or clutch. ReptTraits attributes this field to Iverson et al. 1993. The reproductive-mode field records egg-laying (oviparous); this is the source's category label. ReptTraits attributes this field to Zimin et al. 2022. The sex-determining-mechanism field records TSD. ReptTraits attributes this field to Nemesházi & Bókony 2023.

## Reading this compiled trait record {#s-d09ca76608}
ReptTraits compiles separately cited fields into a species-level row. Its values may come from different source materials, localities, or study samples and should not be treated as observations from one animal.
