## Leaves, flowers and fruit {#s-eec6086008}
The Brazilian Flora 2020 entry describes alternate, compound leaves with 7–9 leaflets arranged along a central axis and one at its tip. The papery leaflets are elliptic to oval and densely hairy underneath; their bases are blunt and their tips taper to a point. The terminal leaflet is 1–1.5 times longer than the side leaflets. The trunk has brown heartwood and cream sapwood; the bark has lenticels, small openings, and the branches are hairy. Loose, brown, hairy flower clusters (thyrses) grow at shoot tips or in leaf axils and may be straight or curved. The flowers are bilaterally symmetrical and lack petals; they have five sepals and two stamens with long yellow anthers that release pollen through pores. The fruit is smooth and hairless, does not split open at maturity, and ranges from spherical to elliptic; it is green when unripe and black, gray or brown when ripe. Each fruit has one elliptic seed with a pulpy inner layer. This is one regional flora description, not a complete or global account.
