## The source concept {#s-cccb12fd29}
AmphiBIO v1 lists Thoropa taophora as row Anf1416, classified in Thoropa, Cycloramphidae and Anura. The compilation follows the species concepts in Amphibian Species of the World (Frost 2011). This page describes the deposited source record; its correspondence to a current COL26.8 species concept has not been established. The source row also notes identification as T. miliaris in Giaretta and Facure (2004); this is retained as a historical source label, not treated here as a current taxonomic equivalence.

## Foraging strata {#s-bc28ea91b2}
The row marks terrestrial and aquatic foraging. These broad categories do not describe seasonal or life-stage variation in detail. A later TetrapodTraits revision reports corrections to some amphibian body-mass and microhabitat entries carried over from earlier compilations. This row has not been checked against those revisions; verify the historical categories before reuse. No replacement values are inferred, and the notice does not imply that every original entry is wrong.

## Adult food categories {#s-0113f7423c}
AmphiBIO lists arthropods among the adult food categories. These categories compile literature reports based on expert assessments, observations or stomach contents; they are not a complete diet or measured diet proportions.

## Seasonal activity {#s-fb971f3a82}
The row places activity in wet and warm conditions. In this dataset, seasonal categories compare activity conditions with the local annual climate; they do not identify fixed calendar months worldwide.

## Compiled adult dimensions {#s-1adea3dc69}
AmphiBIO reports a maximum adult body length of 102.1 mm for Thoropa taophora. In the compilation, body length means snout–vent length for Anura and total length for Caudata and Gymnophiona. This is a compiled maximum, not a measurement of every individual.

## Compiled life history {#s-593afe5f95}
The row reports minimum and maximum clutch counts of 345 and 1241 offspring or eggs and minimum and maximum offspring or egg sizes of 1.8 and 2.6 mm. These literature-derived extrema and counts do not separately identify sex, population, date or wild versus captive conditions. Size values may refer to offspring or eggs.
