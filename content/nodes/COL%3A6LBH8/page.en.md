## Distribution and habitat {#s-7e5b86196f}
MDD currently displays Northern Bamboo Lemur, while 1990s papers often used Hapalemur griseus occidentalis or “western gentle lemur.” Curtis and colleagues reported a Tsiombikibo Forest sighting roughly 200 km south and 200 km north of previously reported populations. They called for further surveys rather than treating the sighting as a range census.

## Ecological evidence {#s-9440c875b9}
A 2008 short report documented the lemur at six locations in the agricultural landscape of Fampotobe on the Masoala Peninsula. The author intermittently followed one group of three at Tsarasoa for 11 days, totaling 45.9 hours. In that report, feeding occupied 37% of observation time; 89% of feeding observations involved young pseudo-petioles of the planted native bamboo Cathariostachys madagascariensis. These are observations of one group in small forest fragments, not species-wide dietary proportions.

## Different stages in the mass fields {#s-452a6be91c}
Historical summary values are adult body mass 1202.71 g; mass at birth or hatching 45.2 g. Adult mass can combine males, females and adults of unspecified sex; birth/hatching mass describes a different life stage. These are not a longitudinal series of the same individuals or a species-wide range of individual masses.

## Evidence limits {#s-7fd38b7f8f}
Older records used a subspecies name, and the behavioral data come from a short study of one three-animal group. Neither locality records nor sample proportions establish the current range, abundance or species-wide diet. These pages are.
The 2015 archive compiles historical species- and subspecies-level life-history records: non-longevity values are medians or interpolated medians, while maximum_longevity_y is the largest compiled record. Historical taxonomic inheritance means a same-name match does not establish equivalence to the pinned COL26.8 concept; these records do not yield a uniform wild-only or captive-only estimate. EltonTraits 1.0 converts literature descriptions into diet weights and categorical activity/foraging fields; mammalian ABC fields may draw on congeners, so they are not direct species-level observations in every field. Neither archive establishes current distribution or conservation status.
