## How do the male and female flower clusters of Bernardia flexuosa differ? {#s-49f89d7472}
This species is recorded as dioecious, with male and female flowers on separate plants. The leaves are leathery, toothed, and hairy beneath, with immersed glands. Male flowers form spikes bearing 5–20 small cymes, each subtended by a cup-shaped bract; their stalks exceed 3 cm, and the flowers have seven to ten stamens. Female flowers occur singly or in rounded clusters of one to three, usually without a stalk; their styles are undivided. The fruit is sessile.
