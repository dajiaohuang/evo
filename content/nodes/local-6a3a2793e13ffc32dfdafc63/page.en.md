## The deposited caecilian record {#s-dbb2259d5a}
CaecilianTraits version 4 lists Ichthyophis beddomei in the genus Ichthyophis and family Ichthyophiidae. It keeps individual, population and species records separate. This source concept has not been reconciled with a current Catalogue of Life (COL26.8) species concept.

## Measurement material {#s-a12b6ca5f6}
The compilation lists 15 measurement records for this source concept, all at individual level and none at population or species level. These row counts are not estimates of population size or proof of 15 distinct specimens. Each record retains its specimen identifier, sex code and reference. Body-size comparisons also need to distinguish fresh, preserved and summary measurements.

## Compiled habitat categories {#s-b31cacd22e}
The compilation labels the broad habitat “Terrestrial” and “Freshwater (inland waters),” and the microhabitat “Fossorial.” These are source categories: they do not show how often each setting is used or describe the species’ complete geographic range.

## Compiled reproduction category {#s-98728c2b1b}
The compilation classifies the species as oviparous, meaning that it lays eggs. This category does not tell us about parental care, when reproduction occurs or how it varies among populations.

## Compiled anatomical counts {#s-063a1ac2e6}
The species-level record gives 272.375 for dorsal annuli (body rings), 273.75 for ventral annuli, 275.166666666667 for primary folds, 6.14285714285714 for post-vent annuli, and 104.25 vertebrae. ## Water-dependent larval development {#s-9ae95d1290}
The compilation marks a water-dependent larval stage as present. This is the dataset category described in its accompanying data paper; it does not mean adults always live in water. The reproductive metadata and original reference are needed to understand the observation’s scope and qualifications.

## Compiled clutch counts {#s-f7041a9b94}
The source species record lists a minimum clutch size of 6, a maximum of 29 and a mean of 15. These values come from cited reproductive accounts and describe the compiled source material, not universal limits or annual reproductive output. The associated reproductive metadata remains part of their interpretation.
