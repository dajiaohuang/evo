## Stem surface {#s-fc6224916d}
The Brazilian Flora 2020 record describes the stem as hirsute, meaning covered with coarse hairs that spread outward from the surface. Simple hairs are present, while branched and unbranched bristles, glandular hairs, prickles, and short lateral shoots (brachyblasts) are recorded as absent.

## Leaves and inflorescences {#s-8ca45a26f9}
Stipules, small structures at leaf bases, are persistent. Extrafloral nectaries (nectar-secreting glands outside flowers) and projections between pairs of pinnae (the leaf’s primary side divisions) are absent. The inflorescence, or flower cluster, is globose; floral bracts (small leaf-like structures) are shorter than the flowers before they open (anthesis).

## Flowers {#s-75ee1e6a53}
The perianth (calyx and corolla together) is recorded as having four parts. The membranous calyx, the outer sepal whorl, is marked as lacking lobes. Corolla (petal-whorl) lobes have one to three veins and are described as pubescent, with short soft hairs, or tomentose, with dense matted hairs; the source row does not choose between these descriptions. The androecium (the stamens collectively) is diplostemonous, with two stamen whorls; the filaments are pink.

## Habitat category {#s-2a1620dcaa}
The regional dataset labels the habitat “Terrícola,” Portuguese for terrestrial. This category does not specify a collection locality or the species’ full range.
