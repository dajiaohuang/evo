## What does Phyllopodium cordatum look like? {#s-a2d3308cd3}
Phyllopodium cordatum is described as a hairy annual up to 30 cm tall, with ovate to elliptic leaves mostly near the base. Pale white or cream flowers begin in small heads, then the flower stalk grows into a long, leafless axis bearing the cluster.

## How does it vary and compare with similar species? {#s-4f8e36baed}
The source says leaves are usually limited to the lower stem, though they may extend farther up the flowering stalk. Hairy leaves and bracts on both surfaces, pale unpatterned flowers, and cream-colored seeds help separate it from P. heterophyllum. It can also resemble P. mimetes, which likewise has basal leaves and flower heads on long stalks; the account refers to that species’ entry for the distinguishing traits.

## What habitat and elevation are reported? {#s-e98d66de20}
The account records sandy or stony places up to 750 m elevation. It gives no complete range.
