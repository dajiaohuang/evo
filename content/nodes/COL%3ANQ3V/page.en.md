## Bulbine torsiva: spiralled leaves and afternoon flowers {#s-a58d46aec9}
A South African regional account describes deciduous herbs 6–11 cm tall, arising from dorsiventrally flattened, pyramidal tubers 1–1.5 cm high and 2–3 cm wide, with spreading horizontal roots. There are 2–3 upright, light-green leaves, subtended by up to 5 tight, papery, white-spotted sheaths 4 mm–1.5 cm long and 2–4 mm wide. The nearly round leaves form tight spirals, are acute at the tip, 5–8 cm long when flattened and 1.5–2 mm in diameter. Their margins may have a few hairs below.

The erect inflorescence is solitary. Its peduncle averages 5 cm long, reaches 15 cm in robust plants after unseasonal late rains, and is about 1.5 mm thick. Racemes are 2.5–6 cm long and bear 6–12 flowers. The narrowly triangular, membranous bracts are 0.8–1 mm long; the 8 mm pedicels bend downward at 45°, and the flowers droop and open only after midday. Tepals are very pale yellow with a green to orange central vein. Outer tepals are elliptic, slightly pointed and 5–7 × 1.3–2 mm; inner tepals are elliptic, slightly pointed and 5–7 × 2.5–4 mm.

The stout, bearded filaments are dimorphic and about 5–6 mm long. The outer filaments curve toward the tepals and have a single hair cushion directed toward the anther; the laterally flattened inner filaments project forward and have a double cushion. The hairs are finely club-shaped with bulbous tips, 1.8 mm long. Oblong anthers are 0.8 mm long, with brownish pollen. The ovary is globose to oblong, dark green to brown and 1.5 mm long. The yellow style is very slender and 4 mm long; the stigma is very small and green. The capsule is subglobose to oblong, about 2 mm long. The black, pyramidal seeds have wavy ridges and a granular surface and are 1–1.5 mm long.

The diagnostic account says *B. torsiva* resembles *B. quartzicola* but has up to 4 or 5 basal leaf sheaths and distinctly spiralled leaves, rather than leaves that simply curl toward the tip. Its flowers droop at about 45° and open only after midday.
