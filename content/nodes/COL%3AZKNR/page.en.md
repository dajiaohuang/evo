## Bite force in sampled Cuban crocodiles {#s-aa7e49f1dc}
How was bite force measured for Crocodylus rhombifer in a standardized adult sample? This guide follows the accepted COL26.8 record ZKNR and reads the study at its stated sample and geographic scale. In Erickson et al.'s 2012 comparison, 3 sexually mature Cuban crocodiles were tested, with a mean sample body mass of about 52 kg. At the prominent molariform tooth near the back of the upper jaw, the study reported a taxon-representative bite force of 2,107 N; the sampled values ranged from 1,392–3,127 N. Researchers used the highest of 3–5 bites per animal. Across species, body mass was the main predictor of bite force, while rostral proportion explained little additional variation. This is a standardized measurement from a limited institutional sample, not a field-performance constant or population range (Erickson et al., 2012: Methods; Table 1).

Continue with Crocodylus siamensis (COL ZKNS) to compare the same experimental measure while keeping sample size and body mass in view. The Crocodylia group guide provides the wider reading route; the neighboring page retains its own sources and limits.
