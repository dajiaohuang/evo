## The described fruiting bodies {#s-05e3e4731f}
The paper describes the apothecia (visible fruiting bodies) as disk-like, scattered or grouped, short-stalked, and orange-grey to brownish-grey when fresh. Fresh apothecia measured 2.3–4.2 mm across (mean 3.0 ± 0.6 mm; n=15). For dry apothecia, the authors report 1.2–2.0 (–2.5) × 0.47–0.72 mm, with mean dimensions of 1.6 ± 0.3 × 0.58 ± 0.09 mm (n=18). These measurements describe the studied collections; developmental stages and broader within-species variation were not assessed.

## Growing on decayed wood {#s-751568ac29}
The authors describe *T. yuxiensis* as saprobic, meaning it obtains nutrients from dead organic matter, and report it on decayed wood. The type was collected from soft, decayed wood whose source was not identified in a managed plantation; the paratype came from decayed wood, also of unknown source. The study does not establish host identity, interaction mechanisms, seasonality, or population ecology.

## A relationship in one DNA-based tree {#s-19e3da81d1}
In the study's phylogeny combining two DNA regions, LSU and ITS, *T. yuxiensis* was placed as sister to *T. aseptata* among the sampled taxa. A sister relationship means their branches share the closest branch point in that sampled tree. It had 100% maximum-likelihood bootstrap support (the branch appeared in every resampled data set) and a Bayesian posterior probability of 1.0; the paper also reports Φw=1.0 for the pair. These results are specific to the study's sampled species and loci. Broader phylogenomic evidence, alternative trees, and divergence times were not reviewed, and the genus placement remains provisional because the type species lacks genetic data.

## Two documented localities {#s-954c7d657f}
The taxonomic treatment lists the type specimen (the name-bearing reference) and one paratype (another specimen in the original type series) from Xinping County, Yuxi City, Yunnan, China. The type was collected at 2090 m on 2022-06-05; the paratype was collected at 2340 m on 2022-06-06. These are the study's documented localities, not an established global range; no exhaustive occurrence search was assessed.
