## What can a tooth whorl reveal? {#s-30cfda2cf4}
Qianodus duplicis is a late Aeronian stem chondrichthyan known from 23 isolated tooth whorls, including IVPP V26641. The fossils preserve dentition and histology at about 439 Ma, but no associated jaw or articulated body.

## atlas-profile / evidenceSummary {#s-3fe084a507}
IVPP V26641 and the referred whorls directly preserve tooth form and histology at roughly 439 Ma. No associated body anatomy establishes its full jaw structure, crown-group membership or global range.

## New teeth in offset lingual rows {#s-f0b29fabb3}
The whorls preserve teeth that were retained rather than shed, with new teeth added in offset rows on the lingual side. Histology was examined in named material including IVPP V26641, so the study traces tooth development without a complete jaw or body.

## atlas-profile / readerLimitations / en {#s-f272e79f63}
The 23 isolated whorls come from the late Aeronian Rongxi Formation at Leijiatun. This records a depositional sample, not a known living habitat; the material does not establish a feeding event, locomotion, body size or ecological role.
