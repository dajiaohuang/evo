## A raised midrib and several flower-cluster forms {#s-d4d772f425}
The Brazilian Flora 2020 v393.147 field records monopodial growth, with simple hairs on the stem. Leaves are oblong and hairless below; the upper midrib is raised along its length. Bracts or cataphylls (small scale-like leaves) beneath leaf nodes are absent. These are regional field states, not a complete account of variation.

## Terminal clusters and a top-shaped flower cup {#s-2956726e19}
Small flowering branches are rounded, and the flower bracteoles may fall away or be linear. Per axillary shoot, the field lists three, four, or more than four inflorescences. They are terminal and whorled; their form is spike-like or pyramidal, with alternate arrangement. The two pollen sacs in each anther are equal. The floral disc is hairless; the flower cup is inverted-cone-shaped and densely woolly. Sepals are separate, the ovary has two chambers, and the calyx opens without tearing. In fruit, the calyx lobes wither but remain attached (marcescent), and the fruit is globose.

## A terrestrial habitat label {#s-5e7ccd968e}
The habitat field records “Terrícola,” meaning terrestrial. It supplies no locality, survey date, range coverage, or life-status information.
