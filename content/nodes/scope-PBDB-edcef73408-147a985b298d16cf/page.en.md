## Research overview {#s-2e9e569df6}
The holotype is a right upper third molar (M3), IVPP V20115; other teeth and ankle bones are referred to the same Middle Eocene sample from the lower Irdin Manha Formation. Researchers use these pieces to compare mimotonid anatomy and relationships.

## What this evidence can establish {#s-5b714ed83d}
The named teeth and tarsals preserve the anatomy and document an Erlian Basin occurrence. The estimated mass, inferred herbivory and locomotion, and proposed relationship to lagomorphs all come from comparisons; they do not make Mimolagus a direct ancestor.

## Teeth and ankle bones in comparison {#s-bec441c4ef}
The holotype is the right upper third molar IVPP V20115. Other teeth and tarsals have also been referred to the species.

Dental and tarsal characters were included in comparative analyses of *Mimolagus*.

## Diet, movement and mass are comparisons {#s-2a4627056b}
Herbivory is inferred from dental form, with no direct food remains assigned. Tarsal comparisons inform locomotor interpretation; comparative regressions estimate a mass of about 4.5 kg rather than measure it. The lower Irdin Manha depositional setting does not resolve habitual microhabitat, and the proposed large herbivorous duplicidentate guild is an ecomorphological inference.
