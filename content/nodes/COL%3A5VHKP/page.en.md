## Form described in the flora record {#s-3ba3588238}
The Brazilian Flora 2020 field describes a creeping stem, with fibers at the base and entire cataphylls (scale leaves) and prophylls (the first leaves on a shoot) at the stem tip. Petioles (leaf stalks) are smooth and recorded as flattened or channelled, with sharp margins above and rounded margins below. Leaf blades are entire (undivided, with unbroken margins), flat or bullate (with blister-like raised areas), and ovate; the tip is acuminate or acute, and the base rounded or truncate. Glandular dots are absent. Primary veins are obtuse on both sides; secondary veins are prominent above, and a collecting vein (running along the leaf margin) extends over the full upper edge.

The inflorescence is larger than half the leaf size; the field does not characterize the peduncle (inflorescence stalk). The spathe (floral bract) is flat and elliptic, with an acute angle to the peduncle. A stipe (supporting stalk) is recorded as shorter than the spadix (fleshy flower-bearing axis) or absent. The berries are green.

The regional dataset row reports Coelho and colleagues' 2020 Anthurium treatment as its citation.

This is one regional morphology field, not a global description; it does not establish sampling coverage, population variation, or diagnostic scope.

The field does not say whether the observations concern wild, cultivated, managed, or fossil material; none is inferred.
