## Which record these facts describe {#s-159186624a}
The pinned COL26.8 checklist accepts Liolaemus irregularis in the family Liolaemidae. The traits below come from its ReptTraits v1-2 row, which follows the Reptile Database's 2023 name framework and cites sources by trait. That row describes the compilation, not every population as if it were identical.

## What the length numbers measure {#s-385875293a}
The row reports a maximum snout–vent length (SVL) of 90 mm and a female maximum of 72.2 mm. SVL measures from the snout to the vent and excludes the tail. These compiled maxima are neither typical sizes nor biological ceilings. The two sex-specific values may come from different studies or places; by themselves they do not measure how much males and females differ within one population.

## Habitat labels, food, and time of activity {#s-b8d83eeece}
The microhabitat field combines Saxicolous, meaning associated with rocky surfaces, and Terrestrial, a ground-associated category. The same row classifies its diet as omnivorous, or including plant and animal material, and its activity as diurnal, or active by day. These labels are not a detailed habitat or prey account, and the one-row summary does not show how traits vary among places, seasons, or life stages.

## What the litter figure means {#s-b5cab07a8b}
The source labels the reproductive mode viviparous, or live-bearing. It gives a consolidated litter-or-clutch mean of 5.38, with reported-mean endpoints of 4 and 8. ReptTraits may calculate a summary from the midpoint of published means, or use the midpoint of reported extremes when means are missing; 5.38 is therefore not a count from one observed litter. The low and high fields mark endpoints of reported means, not guaranteed smallest and largest individual litters. These labels do not tell us how embryos are nourished or establish that every population shares the same reproductive pattern.

## Limits of this account {#s-c3925a9ad7}
This page paraphrases the same-name ReptTraits v1-2 row and keeps its 2023 name framework and trait-specific citations. A single row compresses differences in sex, age, locality, and population; the same name and family do not prove that a historical record matches every later taxonomic concept. A reported maximum is not an absolute size limit, and a reproductive mean is not a fixed litter size. ReptTraits also includes mass estimates derived from length equations; they are not presented here as measured mass. Juvenile-length fields have differing headers and definitions, so they are not used as conclusions. This page does not update conservation status, venom, current distribution, or population trends. The citation labels retain the dataset's source chain; they do not mean this project independently rechecked every original study.
