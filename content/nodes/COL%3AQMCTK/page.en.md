## Accepted name and placement {#s-991ec5d22e}
COL26.8 accepts Mantidactylus tavaratra Rabibisoa, Welt & Raxworthy, 2023 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Mantellidae (family) → Mantidactylus (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## The source disagrees about tibiotarsal reach {#s-c19da25097}
Males are reported at 35–52 mm and females 42–63 mm snout–vent length. The brown back lacks black granules, a sharp transition separates dark flank from pale ventral surface, and thin pale inguinal and lip stripes are present. Toe IV has 1–1.5 outer free phalanges, toe II none externally, and maximum disc width/base width is at most 1.8. The diagnosis places tibiotarsal reach between eye and nostril, but comparisons with femoralis and danieli say beyond the nostril. This disagreement is not silently resolved into a definitive identification character.

## Adults and juveniles differ in reported encounter timing {#s-18b594d9f7}
The account covers rainforest and savannah waters, including streams, rivers, ponds and shallow marshes, with waterfalls excepted. Encounters span 09:30–23:00: adults were mainly nocturnal and juveniles seen during the day. Night perches included rocks and leaves, rarely branches, with incidental aerial-root and Pandanus records. Females appeared more often on rocks than leaves, whereas males showed no stated contrast; male heights were 5–100 cm and female heights 5–150 cm. These encounter tendencies do not experimentally establish preference, breeding substrate or universal avoidance of waterfalls.

## The northern distribution summary spans a broad elevation interval {#s-bd690e3d32}
The revision gives northern Madagascar and an elevation range of 80–2,450 m for tavaratra. These are reported occurrence bounds, not physiological tolerance limits or evidence that every elevation within the interval is occupied. The breadth of the interval does not determine abundance, habitat continuity or current conservation status. Older mocquardi-labelled records require voucher-level reassignment rather than geographic pooling.

## Specific former mocquardi vouchers support the revised attribution {#s-e54c8bb84f}
The source associates tavaratra with earlier aff. mocquardi material from Marojejy. Its molecular group includes AY324819 / FGMV 2002.824 from Manongarivo and AY324820 / ZSM 643/2001 from Tsaratanana, formerly cf. mocquardi. The comments also track FGMV 2002.824 through later unnamed-candidate usage and mention FGMV 2001.114. These links concern particular vouchers; they do not reassign all mocquardi or unnamed-candidate records. Mitochondrial clustering is not a completed population-genomic audit or certification of every modern catalogue concept.

## High-elevation February larvae do not establish a thermal trigger {#s-b6565856f9}
The reproductive discussion reports tavaratra tadpoles at Gosner stage 36 in late February in Tsaratanana, around 2,300 m and 8 °C. Temperature-related triggering and rapid high-elevation metamorphosis are proposed explanations, not experimentally isolated mechanisms or measured critical temperatures. This single seasonal and locality context does not establish year-round reproduction, a species-wide breeding calendar or larval growth rate. Background seasonal observations for other Ochthomantis populations are not transferred to every tavaratra population, and egg-placement accounts for other species remain separate.
