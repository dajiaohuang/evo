## Octopoma tanquanum {#s-587f15c34d}
## Fine teeth line the keels of its spreading leaves

Klak’s 2010 description portrays a densely branched dwarf succulent shrublet about 80 mm tall. Woody stems branch from the base and reach 5 mm thick; their short internodes are ochre to grey.

The leaves are joined at the base and spread outward. They are somewhat triangular in cross-section, pointed without an extra apical tooth, and have finely toothed keels. Each flower grows singly; small bract-like leaves called bracteoles embrace its base. Flowers reach 27 mm across and have cream petals and thread-like sterile stamens (staminodes). The inner petals are shorter than the outer ones; the staminodes form a cylinder around the stamens. Most floral parts are smooth, though inner parts may have a few short bumps called papillae.

The six-chambered fruit has a funnel-shaped base, a convex top, large closing bodies and broad ridges (keels) that diverge upward without awns. The pale ochre seeds are D-shaped, about 0.9 mm long, nearly smooth and lack a crest. The species is reported to prefer flat or gently sloping, slightly gravelly, loamy ground.
