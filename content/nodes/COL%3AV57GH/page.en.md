## Name and classification in COL26.8 {#s-91c2c3e730}
The 20 August 2026 COL26.8 release accepts Boulenophrys xiangnanensis (Lyu, Zeng & Wang in Lyu, Li, Zheng, Zhao, Liu, Guo & Wang, 2020) as a species. Its recorded placement is Anura (order) → Megophryidae (family) → Boulenophrys (genus). This identifies the catalogue name and classification; biological observations below retain the scope of their own sources.

## Hunan–Guangxi records with a conflicting remarks label {#s-4ba009674f}
Male holotype (the name-bearing specimen designated in the original description) SYS a002875 came from Yangming Mountain, Shuangpai, Hunan, at about 1360 m. The distribution paragraph placed multiple localities along the Hunan–Guangxi border at 500–1600 m, including Jiuyi, Yangming, Mao’er, Haiyang, Dupangling and Xiling. In contrast, one remarks sentence referred to the Hunan–Jiangxi border. That wording conflicts with the named distribution and examined-locality context and is retained as an unresolved source discrepancy rather than adding Jiangxi to the range.

## Sample sizes and correction of a nuptial-pad typo {#s-7bd780dbaf}
The diagnosis reported 25 adult males at 33.3–42.1 mm snout–vent length (measured from the tip of the snout to the vent) and one adult female at 44.4 mm. Males had a subgular vocal sac (a throat pouch used when calling) but lacked nuptial pads or spines. The remarks explicitly attributed the original description’s assertion of male finger pads to a typographical error and corrected it to absence. This is the monograph’s attributed correction; the female value remains a single measurement, and pad absence does not determine breeding timing.

## Conspecificity and geographic-isolation hypotheses {#s-7006b329c9}
The remarks reported earlier possible cryptic-species interpretations for Guanyang, Shuangpai and Fuchuan populations labelled M. sp1, M. sp2 and M. sp3. The monograph found no distinguishing morphology and treated them as B. xiangnanensis. It described relatively large differences among populations in three mitochondrial segments and proposed geographic isolation and a wider distribution as possible explanations. These are attributed delimitation and causal hypotheses, not independent tests of reproductive compatibility or proven causes of genetic divergence.

## A character cited in the gutu comparison {#s-862f1bfd54}
The 2025 gutu description lists Boulenophrys xiangnanensis as with wide lateral toe fringes. That statement belongs to the paper’s comparison; it is not a new measurement series for this species. The gutu adult sample spans 34.4–44.7 mm in seven males and 36.2–52.8 mm in eight females; those numbers belong to gutu, not to this comparator. This single comparison does not show how often the character occurs across the species or provide a complete diagnosis, diet or developmental account.
