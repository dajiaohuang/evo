## Seven evidence boundaries from fins to limbs {#s-87f2453b6b}
Read tracks, whole bodies, internal fin bones, digits and joint models as different kinds of evidence. The named fossils do not form a direct chain of ancestors.

## A mosaic of changes, not a march {#s-1ba237acb8}
One synthesis separates the appearance of digits, changes in movement and the ability to live fully on land. Its broad time window organizes evidence from different places and animals; it is not one event or an ancestor-to-descendant sequence.

## Tracks without a named body {#s-f3c3058088}
Eifelian surfaces at Zachełmie preserve quadrupedal tracks and impressions of digits. Tracks constrain how an animal moved, but they cannot identify a named body-fossil species, provide its full anatomy or prove that it was an ancestor of later tetrapods.

## One animal with fish and tetrapod-like features {#s-addc0b2f5f}
Tiktaalik combines scales, fin rays and fish-like jaws with a mobile neck, a modified ear region and strong ribs. Its anatomy and setting make shallow-water use a testable hypothesis; they do not record it walking on land.

## A wrist-like joint inside a fin {#s-edc864348b}
Tiktaalik’s pectoral fin has a robust internal skeleton and wrist-like joints that could transmit load. Fin rays remain, however, and the fossil preserves neither free digits nor tracks made by this animal.

## Digit-like bones beneath fin rays {#s-a8c5eeb884}
CT images of MHNM 06-2067 reveal branched wrist bones and rows at the end of the fin that researchers interpret as digits or possible digits. Fin rays, called lepidotrichia, are still present, so the appendage remains a fin.

## Eight digits do not prove walking on land {#s-a9a7abae44}
The described Acanthostega forelimb has eight digits; Ichthyostega has seven and Tulerpeton six. These fossils show that early tetrapods did not begin with a fixed five-digit pattern. Digit number alone does not tell us whether an animal walked on land.

## A joint model tests one animal’s movement {#s-f681b4cd18}
A three-dimensional reconstruction of Ichthyostega’s joints found that they lacked rotations needed for a typical side-to-side walking sequence. The model suggests restricted mobility or a specialization of this animal, not a universal gait for early tetrapods.
