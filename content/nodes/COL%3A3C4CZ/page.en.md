## A cartilage-like edge and two fruit forms {#s-e04c3e06e4}
The Brazilian Flora 2020 v393.147 field describes elliptic or obovate leaves. Their bases are listed as acute, gradually narrowed or blunt; their tips taper to a point or extend like a tail. The texture is papery or leathery. The margin is flat with a cartilage-like edge, and the raised midrib on the upper surface is accompanied by two veins near the leaf edge. Flowers grow in compact clusters called fascicles. The bracts and smaller bracteoles beside the flowers fall away. The flower cup is smooth and hairless; its calyx is open in bud, with four sepals and four petals. Fruit is listed as round or ellipsoid, with a smooth surface; its bracteoles fall away. One seed is recorded, with fused cotyledons (the seed leaves) in the embryo. The source lists alternatives without quantifying their distribution.

## A terrestrial label without range coverage {#s-2978544fb9}
The habitat field says “terrícola,” meaning terrestrial. It gives no locality, survey date or range coverage and does not identify wild, cultivated, managed or fossil material. This label is not a range statement.
