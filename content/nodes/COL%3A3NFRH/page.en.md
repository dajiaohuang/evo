## Evolutionary evidence {#s-5f1b376e2d}
A Y-chromosome study sampled 26 captive male gibbons from ten species, including three agile gibbons. Its tree, built from seven concatenated amplicons, left *Hylobates agilis*, the silvery gibbon, and Kloss’s gibbon in an unresolved three-way split. The authors reported weak support and too little Y-chromosome variation to resolve those branches. This paternal-marker result is not a complete species tree.

## Evolutionary evidence {#s-82d56c1ea3}
A separate gibbon opsin study collected blood from 37 agile gibbons at zoos in Indonesia and Thailand and from animals kept by local residents in Kalimantan; two samples were excluded from opsin genotyping. In male H. agilis samples, detected L/M opsin gene-conversion tracts were mostly in introns. The study analyzed gene sequences, not color-vision behavior, and does not establish species-wide prevalence.

## Evidence limits {#s-e72e17a25b}
The two studies provide limited Y-chromosome-marker and opsin-sequence evidence; together they do not establish complete phylogeny, sensory ecology or field ecology.
The 2015 archive compiles historical species- and subspecies-level life-history records: non-longevity values are medians or interpolated medians, while maximum_longevity_y is the largest compiled record.
