## Fossil evidence {#s-4d037a03d1}
At Tam Pà Ling cave in northern Laos, researchers attribute the partial frontal bone TPL 6 and proximal tibial fragment TPL 7 to *Homo sapiens*. A modeled cave chronology places TPL 6 at 70 ± 3 thousand years and TPL 7 at 77 ± 9 thousand years. These are estimates for the sediment sequence, not direct dates on the bones; TPL 7 lies 30 cm below the deepest sediment sample and may be older than the model indicates.

## Morphology {#s-65853b8950}
TPL 6 is a partial left frontal bone with a modest superciliary arch and no supraorbital torus of the kind described for archaic humans; the authors’ shape comparison places it among more gracile frontal forms. TPL 7 is a proximal left tibial fragment whose taphonomic alteration prevents accurate osteometric analysis. These two specimens from one site do not describe morphological variation across Homo sapiens.

## Ecological evidence {#s-dc4d3d0245}
At Ilsenhöhle in Ranis, Germany, researchers analyzed collagen stable isotopes from 10 human remains alongside 52 animal remains. The results support a dietary signal dominated by large terrestrial mammals for the sampled people, associated with a cold steppe–tundra setting about 45,000 years ago. This inference comes from archaeological samples at one cave and its stratigraphic context; the isotope results do not show that every animal taxon was hunted or eaten at the site, and they do not describe a general Homo sapiens diet.
