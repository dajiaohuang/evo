## Two hagfish-like fossils preserve different clues {#reading-introduction}
Two Carboniferous Myxinikela fossils preserve body outlines and soft-tissue traces, but differ in the structures they retain. Comparing their anatomy and preservation leads to a cautious stem-hagfish interpretation. A separate phylogenetic study tests relationships; the fossils do not establish modern hagfish habitat or behaviour.

## Myxinikela: two fossils and a disputed anatomy {#s-fb03201c56}
The holotype FMNH PF15373 (73.3 mm) and paratype FMNH PF8472 (62.5 mm) of *Myxinikela siroka* come from the Moscovian Francis Creek Shale at former Pit 11, Illinois; the recorded age interval is about 310–307 Ma. Their body outlines and soft-tissue traces combine general cyclostome features with traits interpreted as hagfish specializations. The revision compares two specimens, not a complete animal population.

Preservation complicates the comparison: the specimens differ in what they retain, and the paratype does not show definitive cartilage. A 2020 revision therefore treats several structures as equivocal and places *Myxinikela* on the hagfish stem or, more cautiously, in the possible hagfish total group. This fossil is not a record of crown Myxini.

A separate 2019 morphological analysis resolved *Myxinikela* as a stem myxinoid and recovered hagfishes and lampreys together as Cyclostomata. In that study, the branchial apparatus of *Myxinikela* lies within the cranial region, unlike the posteriorly placed branchial series reported for the Cretaceous crown hagfish *Tethymyxine*; the authors also treated slime-gland evidence in *Myxinikela* as preservation-sensitive. These are character-based placements, not proof that either fossil is a direct ancestor. The two Carboniferous specimens do not establish deep-water habitat, slime production or the timing of crown-hagfish origins.
