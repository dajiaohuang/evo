## Zantedeschia jucunda: spotted leaves and yellow spathes {#s-072714b478}
A 1997 regional account describes Zantedeschia jucunda as a perennial herb up to 0.6 m high. Its triangular-hastate leaves measure 170–300 × 50–150 mm and are densely marked with long, white, translucent spots. The spathe is golden yellow.

A 1973 diagnostic account places Z. jucunda closest to Z. pentlandii and Z. albomaculata. Compared with Z. pentlandii, it has triangular-hastate leaves with many spots, upward-spreading basal lobes and an acute tip. Its spathe is more cup-shaped and deeper yellow inside and out, slightly rugose inside, and has a more spreading limb that tapers to a subulate tip. Z. pentlandii is described with spotless or rarely spotted oblong-lanceolate leaves, short rounded basal lobes, a regularly funnel-shaped spathe, and an erect, lighter-colored limb that is greenish outside and ends abruptly in a subulate tip.

Compared with the eastern Transvaal form of Z. albomaculata subsp. albomaculata, Z. jucunda is described as having consistently brilliant-yellow spathes; the other form may have white, cream or pale-yellow spathes, with occasional coral-pink variation.
