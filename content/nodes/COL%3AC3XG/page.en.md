## Aloe pearsonii: recurved leaves in vertical rows {#s-6db7dfec26}
A South African regional account describes a large, much-branched shrub 1–2 m tall. Its upright stem carries leaves along most of its length. The dull blue-green leaves often turn reddish, especially during drought; they are nearly triangular, arranged in orderly vertical rows and curve backward. Branched, head-shaped panicles bear flowers ranging from red to orange-red or yellow.

A comparative account says the leaves are smaller than those of other species in section *Aloe*, except *A. arenicola*. It contrasts *A. arenicola*—with decumbent stems, incurved leaves, white spots and cartilaginous margins—with the rigidly upright stems, reflexed unspotted leaves and non-cartilaginous margins of *A. pearsonii*. *A. dabenorisana* also has reflexed leaves, but is described as larger-leaved and pendent rather than upright, with anthers and style that are not or hardly exserted. The account calls *A. pearsonii* one of the slowest-growing aloes and estimates that some large Richtersveld shrubs may be several hundred years old.
