## Accepted name and placement {#s-01fa98740d}
COL26.8 accepts Bolitoglossa muisca López-Perilla, Fernández-Roldán, Meza-Joya & Medina-Rangel, 2023 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Caudata (order) → Plethodontidae (family) → Bolitoglossa (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## Type material and a larger measured sample {#s-d03ec039e8}
The 2023 description designated a female holotype and eight paratypes (three females and five males). Its comparative diagnosis reports snout–vent lengths of 33.0–72.1 mm across 22 measured B. muisca specimens; this measured sample is distinct from the nine-specimen type series. The authors described a broad head, mostly smooth skin, moderate webbing and a short, robust tail. The reported range is a study measurement, not a universal size boundary.

## Cloud-forest records in Cundinamarca {#s-e407c1dfd5}
The study recorded B. muisca from small cloud-forest remnants on the western slopes of Colombia’s Cordillera Oriental, in Bojacá, Granada, San Antonio del Tequendama, Silvania and Soacha municipalities. Collected specimens came from 2390–2700 m elevation. These records describe the study’s geographic sample; they do not establish physiological altitude limits or complete present-day occupancy.

## Different resting and active sites {#s-d7b01f33c5}
At Bojacá, salamanders were regularly observed at night around fern bases and leaves; at San Antonio del Tequendama, most were active at night on small shrub branches, usually away from streams. A few daytime observations found inactive individuals inside bromeliads below 2 m height. Handled individuals released a sticky whitish secretion, which the authors interpreted as a possible predator defence; its effectiveness was not demonstrated by these observations.

## Nine types and a separate referred-material count conflict {#s-0bd566c353}
Female holotype IAvH-Am-17413 was collected at Finca La Esmeralda, Bojacá, on 4 February 2021 at 2630 m. Eight paratypes comprised three females and five males from Bojacá and Soacha. The referred-material heading stated 36 specimens, including seven females and 29 juveniles or subadults, while the range ICN 58245–58268 was described as a batch of 23. That inclusive range comprises 24 numbers, so range, batch count and aggregate total are retained as unresolved rather than silently harmonized. The type series and larger comparative samples remain different denominators.

## One female holotype with an incomplete tail {#s-8a48c61b88}
The holotype description gave SVL 61.3 mm, head width/SVL 0.16, 33 maxillary teeth, 25 vomerine teeth and thirteen costal grooves. It printed tail/SVL 0.93 while explicitly saying the tail tip was missing and the apparent abruptly widened, rounded tail ending was artefactual. That ratio is not a complete-tail limit or proof of a natural rounded terminal shape. The same paragraph includes a male premaxillary-tooth statement within the female description and ambiguous digit-order expressions; neither is assigned uncritically as a measured female trait or repaired into an inferred ranking.

## A day-after-capture photograph and five months in ethanol {#s-b2fc587787}
Live holotype colour was described from a photograph taken the day after capture: brown dorsal surfaces with salmon-coloured speckling, thin irregular white markings, and a light sky-blue iris with reddish reticulations. After approximately five months in 70% ethanol, stripes and ventral surfaces were smoke-grey and the iris amber with orange-rufous reticulations. Other specimens varied in orange-rufous or salmon background and brown markings. These dated preservation conditions are explicit; preserved iris colour does not replace life colour, and no single pattern is an invariant identity test.

## A sampled sister lineage and a corrected museum number {#s-4171a9f2fd}
The study analysed a 2070-bp concatenated matrix for 67 ingroup terminals, using 16S, cyt b and RAG1 with different sequence-sample counts. The new-species clade had ultrafast bootstrap 100 and Bayesian posterior probability 1.0; its sister relationship to sampled B. adspersa from Bogotá and surroundings had support 98 and 0.99. These are distinct support measures, not percentages of reproductive isolation. The authors corrected an El Soche GenBank voucher formerly labelled MVZ 167947 to MVZ 167997 from the museum catalogue. Pairwise mitochondrial distances and delimitation results support the attributed study hypothesis; they do not certify all contemporary concepts or universal species thresholds.

## Estimated extent of occurrence and a protected type locality {#s-cfc0593449}
The authors calculated extent of occurrence as 102 km² from known localities using GeoCAT. They placed the type locality within the Cerro Manjui–Salto del Tequendama regional protected area and described deforestation, logging and forest clearing as habitat threats. They proposed Endangered under the criterion printed as B1b(iii) and called for monitoring. Extent of occurrence is not occupied area, and protected-area placement does not demonstrate population security. The criterion and proposal are retained as the authors’ 2023 statement, not silently amended or represented as an independently verified current Red List assessment.
