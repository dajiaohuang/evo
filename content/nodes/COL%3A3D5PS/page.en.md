## Euryops petraeus {#s-bb86c9f40a}
## Short, half-round leaves and heads in the upper axils

Manning's 2013 account describes a much-branched dwarf shrub 0.1–0.8 m tall, with young branch tips sparsely woolly. Leaves are crowded, spreading, oblong and almost round in cross-section, only 4–7 mm long. Their surfaces are rough, and the margins have tiny hairs and teeth.

Solitary yellow radiate heads grow in the upper leaf axils on short stalks, 2–8 mm long. The involucre is bell-shaped with five or six bracts; how far the bracts are joined is not recorded. The dry fruits are hairless and wrinkled.

Nordenstam compares *E. petraeus* with *E. annae*: *E. petraeus* has ciliate leaf margins, shorter, more clearly half-round leaves and shorter flower stalks. He reports seeing both species together on Compassberg, distinct and without intermediate forms. The cited flora records cliffs and rocky faces as habitat.
