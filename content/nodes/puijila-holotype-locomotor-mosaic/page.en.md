## Puijila combines terrestrial limbs with aquatic clues {#s-4c504e1bf8}
Holotype NUFV 405 preserves about 65% of one skeleton from an early Miocene lake deposit in the Haughton Formation of Devon Island, Canada. Its skull, teeth, vertebrae and substantial fore- and hind limbs let researchers compare features associated with land movement and swimming.

Robust limbs and other anatomical traits have been used to infer a semi-aquatic animal and a freshwater setting. Those are functional and environmental interpretations, not witnessed behaviour. The published placement among pinnipedimorphs also does not show that Puijila was a direct ancestor of later seals and sea lions.
