## A name fixed to COL26.8 {#s-a8e4c4b78b}
Catalogue of Life release COL26.8 records Hydrobates monteiroi (Bolton, A. L. Smith, Gómez-Díaz, Friesen, Medeiros, Bried, Roscales & Furness, 2008) as the accepted species name (published 2026-08-20). This card preserves that version's identity; the name entry alone does not describe anatomy or ecology.

## The scope of one measured bird {#s-b27a47c89a}
The morphology record covers one bird: 0 females, 1 male and none of unrecorded sex; that bird has a complete measurement set. Historical row means are 9.7 mm for beak length from nostril to tip, 22.2 mm for tarsus, 153 mm for unflattened wing, 91 mm for tail and 43.8 for hand-wing index. A single bird cannot establish sex differences or a population distribution. Inference=NO does not replace the sampling denominator for each field. The hand-wing index is Kipp's distance divided by wing length ×100, not flight speed; beak length alone gives neither bite force nor prey-handling ability.

## Mass comes from a separate compilation {#s-088b369bc5}
The compiled species-average mass is 46.14 g, with Dunning as its source, and it incorporates both sexes. The one bird in the morphology record is not a verified count of the individuals behind this mass, so the compiled value is not that bird's measured weight. The original book, weighed birds, localities and dates were not examined; sex-specific, seasonal or regional mass ranges therefore remain unsupported. Mass and beak length do not establish feeding mechanics.

## Separate aquatic-prey niche from trophic level {#s-73ac987548}
The Aquatic predator niche category means that at least 60% of food resources come from vertebrate and invertebrate animals in aquatic systems. Fish, crustaceans and molluscs illustrate the definition; they are not a measured prey list for this species. Carnivore as trophic level means at least 70% of resources come from live invertebrate or vertebrate animals. These fields describe resource source and trophic level separately; they provide neither seasonal diets nor a percentage for particular prey. An aquatic-food category does not establish diving, dive depth or specific surface-feeding behavior.

## Separate marine habitat from aerial lifestyle {#s-7fe3439f39}
Marine habitat includes pelagic water, sea near coasts and associated near-sea breeding environments in the category definition; it does not locate the measured bird or its breeding island. Habitat density 3 means open habitat, including open water, not bird abundance. Aerial lifestyle means spending much time in flight and hunting or foraging mainly on the wing; an aquatic food niche does not change the lifestyle field to Aquatic. Migration category 3 indicates that most individuals undertake long-distance migration. These categories give no departure dates, distances, tracked birds or routes; the hand-wing index is not a substitute for measured flight speed. Historical map fields have not been interpreted here.

## Evidence limits {#s-e6024d22fc}
This account uses 1 of 10 selected historical AVONET v7 rows, same-name eBird data, older Oceanodroma or Halocyptena BirdTree links, catalogue names and relevant category definitions. Each of the 10 older names has a corresponding synonym in the COL hy/oc/ha shards examined, but this does not establish identical historical and current species boundaries. The one morphology bird, its complete set and the Dunning literature mass have separate denominators. Original specimens, Dunning's book, the full workbook and a fresh full reading of the methods paper were not examined. Aquatic predator niche, Carnivore trophic level and Aerial lifestyle remain separate fields. The sources here do not provide measured prey, dive depth, flight speed, migration routes, plumage, calls, breeding details, current distribution or conservation status. Historical map fields remain uninterpreted, and this is not a complete natural history.
