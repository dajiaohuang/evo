## Accepted name and placement {#s-58a9fa0812}
In the frozen COL26.8 release dated 20 August 2026, Leptodactylus melanonotus (Hallowell, 1861) is accepted at species rank. Its recorded placement is Anura (order), Leptodactylidae (family) and Leptodactylus (genus). This is the classification in that release; other catalogues need their own evidence for name identity.

## Compiled adult dimensions — AmphiBIO source {#s-45b4a13694}
AmphiBIO gives a maximum adult body length of 58 mm. For anurans this means snout–vent length, from snout to vent; it is a compiled maximum, not a typical size for all adults.

It does not identify the population, sex, date or wild/captive context for each value; the size field may refer to eggs or offspring.

Sex was unreported, origin was listed as wild and treatment fields were NA. This is one compiled assay estimate, not a species-wide upper temperature limit.

## A source-listed native regional occurrence {#s-1bdc73ef57}
A 2025 regional checklist lists Leptodactylus melanonotus as native to the Sierra Madre Occidental biogeographic province in western Mexico. It compiles state lists, museum data, VertNet and GBIF records rather than surveying every species anew. Its regional scope includes parts of Sonora, Chihuahua, Sinaloa, Durango, Zacatecas, Nayarit and Jalisco; the listing does not imply occurrence throughout those states or every mountain habitat.

## Adult tapeworm associations in a historical North American checklist {#s-a4d462395c}
The 2023 review includes Leptodactylus melanonotus among amphibian hosts with records of adult intestinal cestodes. Its named parasite associations are Cylindrotaenia americana. These describe compiled host–parasite records, not parasites measured in every member of the species. The scope is adult worms in wild continental North American herpetofauna; larval cestode records are excluded. An intestinal association alone does not establish disease severity, host mortality, zoonotic transmission or the species’ role in completing every parasite life cycle. Prevalence, intensity, examined-host denominators and experimental susceptibility are not supplied by this host checklist.
