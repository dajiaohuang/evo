## distribution {#s-4e08cfa3b5}
How concentrated was breeding in the North Pacific? A 2009 status assessment reported that more than 99% of Laysan albatrosses nested in the Northwestern Hawaiian Islands. It described birds foraging across the North Pacific and breeding on oceanic islands from Mexico to Japan. These figures draw on evidence through 2005, so they describe the assessment period rather than today's global distribution.

## ecology {#s-2372301158}
The assessment described Laysan albatrosses as open-ocean birds that come ashore to breed and identified commercial-fishery bycatch as an important human-caused source of post-fledging mortality during its review period. This is a historical threat summary, not a current mortality rate.

## catalogue-profile / limitations {#s-92f0792bf1}
This page brings together three historical compilations, each with its own sampling and taxonomic basis. AVONET's morphology values are species averages from the listed samples, not identification rules or ranges of individual variation; its mass estimate has a separate source. Habitat, diet and migration labels are broad categories, and the maps show historical breeding and resident ranges. The 2015 life-history summaries use medians (or the mean of the two middle values), except maximum longevity, which uses the largest record found. They are not direct measurements of one bird or current population estimates; older species and subspecies records may share inherited values, and wild or captive conditions are not consistently known. EltonTraits weights standardize literature descriptions rather than measure food intake or time use. Avian A/B diet codes retain uncertainty in sources and proportions; mammalian ABC diet or activity values can refer to a congener judged ecologically similar by the compilers, so they are not direct observations of this species in every case. This page does not use genus- or family-typical values, non-species-level avian foraging records or split records needing individual review to fill gaps. Individual, seasonal and local variation also remains unresolved. Historical name matches do not prove that these records represent the same species concept as COL26.8. The 2009 report's evidence ends in 2005, so it cannot establish current breeding proportions, threats, range or conservation status. The Chinese common name is an editorial translation; current ecology and conservation require other sources.
