## A tall herb with clasping leaves and clustered heads {#s-42b3fabd6a}
The Flora of Panama describes *Elephantopus mollis* as a coarse perennial herb, usually 30–150 cm tall and sometimes reaching 200 cm; it may grow from a creeping rootstock. Its erect, leafy stems are usually hollow and covered with long soft hairs, up to 1.5 mm. Most leaves grow along the stem. They are usually 7–22 × 2–7 cm, narrow toward the base, pointed at the tip and scalloped along the edge; their broadened bases clasp the stem. A much-branched cluster at the stem tip bears many rounded groups of flower heads. Each head has four florets, white or occasionally pinkish. The small dry fruits have ten ribs and a tuft of 5–8 bristles, the pappus. This is a historical regional flora description, with no observation date or population sampling frame; it is not a global account.
