## Population evidence {#s-61bf3591f7}
Aponte-Fernandes and Bosque revisited a red-howler population at Hato Masaguaral in the Venezuelan Llanos, which had been monitored almost continuously from 1970 to 1999. Their direct counts in 2011–2012 were 34% below the 1999 census, and the number of troops in the study area fell from 15 to 10. They reported a density of 29 individuals per km²; eight of the ten troops had no infants. The authors read the low proportion of immature females and the lack of infants in most troops as signs of limited reproductive capacity, while noting that other group-composition measures resembled stable howler populations. These are comparisons for one historically monitored population, not a current species-wide estimate.

## Evidence limits {#s-c73f722f5b}
Evidence concerns one population at Hato Masaguaral and compares counts from 1999 with 2011–2012. It does not establish the species’ present status or a range-wide trend.
