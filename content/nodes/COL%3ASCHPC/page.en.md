## Accepted name and placement {#s-5f487d47ba}
COL26.8 accepts Pristimantis venegasi Ortega, Brito-M. & Ron, 2022 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Strabomantidae (family) → Pristimantis (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## One adult of each sex and preservation-sensitive fringes {#s-effbb9134f}
The venegasi diagnosis reported one female at SVL 34.90 mm and one male at 24.49 mm. It described a visible tympanic membrane, basal toe webbing, an eyelid conical tubercle and black-and-white marbling on groins, thighs and ventral surfaces. Broad finger fringes could become inconspicuous after preservation, so their poor visibility in a specimen does not certify absence. Single-sex measurements are not species-wide ranges, and the two animals do not independently define the full degree of sexual dimorphism or live colour variation.

## Ankaku perches and a separately noted Sumaco voucher {#s-d151063987}
The locality account described venegasi around Ankaku, Llanganates, at 1,500–2,110 m, with nighttime trail-side captures on leaves or branches about 170 cm high in epiphyte-rich cloud forest. The same paragraph also noted a paratype collected at Sumaco in 2005, outside its short initial Ankaku summary. The separate voucher locality is retained rather than silently defining an Ankaku-only range. These records do not measure occupancy between sites, complete vertical habitat use or the egg-laying substrate.

## Data Deficient despite mapped habitat loss {#s-91287d56f3}
The authors recommended Data Deficient and stated that population status was unknown. Their 2018 land-cover information classified the Sumaco voucher locality as agricultural and estimated 26.1% deforestation within buffered known-site areas. These are land-cover proxies, not measured loss of 26.1% of the frogs or proof of local extinction. A nearby apparently undisturbed buffer also does not show that all sites are intact. The map dates and sparse vouchers delimit the account; no current population trend or present IUCN category is independently certified.
