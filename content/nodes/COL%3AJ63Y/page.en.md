## Reference specimens and field detections {#s-30d0833a76}
The 2017 study sequenced six Republic of the Congo reference specimens (USNM 584066–584071). Its field survey recorded no Astylosternus along the Loubomo–Mougagara Road corridor (LMR) in Nyanga, Gabon. The museum specimens and Gabon survey are different samples; non-detection at LMR is limited by the survey's effort and seasons and does not show regional absence or the species' full range.

## Barcode groups and uncertain names {#s-59d0e650c9}
All six specimens fell in one Barcode Index Number (BIN) and one Automatic Barcode Gap Discovery (ABGD) group. Their mitochondrial 16S sequences also clustered with GenBank references labelled batesi, schioetzi, and diadematus. The authors note inconsistent labels and a possible species complex; they do not confirm schioetzi or diadematus as local synonyms of batesi. Voucher checks are needed before treating those labels as additional local records.

## What two mitochondrial markers can show {#s-1b41970aa4}
For A. batesi, the identification workflow combines COI (mitochondrial cytochrome-c-oxidase I) barcode groups, mitochondrial 16S comparisons, morphology, and type-locality context. Both gene markers are mitochondrial; they provide neither independent nuclear confirmation nor a test of reproductive isolation. The neighbor-joining tree groups sequences by similarity, and the authors do not use it to infer evolutionary history. Most species were represented at only one or two localities, and not every GenBank voucher could be checked morphologically. Fieldwork took place in May–June 2012 and March–April 2013; database comparisons used the study's 2016 GenBank/BOLD snapshot, not a current audit.
