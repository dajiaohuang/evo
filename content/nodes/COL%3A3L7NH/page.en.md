## One fertile spawning after introduction of a rain chamber {#s-d96c6a9d18}
After six years without breeding success, staff placed the maintained betsileo in a rain chamber and soon recorded fertile eggs. This was the species’ single spawning event in the reported study, during the warm rainy season, with eggs deposited in water. The sequence supports a possible response to added environmental cues but is not a randomized isolation of rainfall effects or proof that wild breeding always requires artificial rain. The later discussion misspells the name as bestileo; the maintained-species account and pinned reader remain betsileo, without creating a separate taxon.

## Feeding larvae and the limits of captive-care evidence {#s-5b076fdd9d}
The study classified betsileo tadpoles as exotrophic and supplied an external food mixture. The general care paragraph reports seasonally varying water typically at 16–22 °C and regular water changes, but does not independently supply a betsileo-specific thermal optimum or minimum metamorphic interval. The one fertile spawning cannot establish repeatable annual production or survival of all offspring. Founder identification was morphological only; the captive observations remain a useful breeding record with unverified genetic concept equivalence, rather than a complete life-history account across the natural range.
