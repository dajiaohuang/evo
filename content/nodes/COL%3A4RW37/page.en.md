## Seven fields in a historical life-history archive {#s-d4702a7e8f}
Rhabdomys pumilio is accepted in pinned COL26.8 within Muridae. The 2015 life-history comparison covers seven focal fields, each linked to a cited species-level sparse-table row with the same name and family. Older taxonomic inheritance can remain in the summaries despite that match.

## Adult mass and birth mass describe distinct stages {#s-0860aa9b91}
The archive reports adult body mass of 45.87 g and birth or hatching mass of 2.67 g. Adult records can combine sexes and adults with unspecified sex; birth mass concerns newborns. The values are not a longitudinal series or a range of individual body masses.

## Four measures from conception to independence {#s-23f582d3c3}
The compilation gives female maturity, defined as age at first conception, as 54.18 d; offspring-or-egg count as 6; conception or fertilization to birth as 25.26 d; and age of dependence on food other than maternal milk as 14 d. Here d means days. The litter/clutch field combines two kinds of counts, and the measures describe separate events rather than one interchangeable cycle.

## Maximum longevity is the largest collected record {#s-acc4532813}
The archive reports maximum_longevity_y of 4.5 years, taking the largest record found under its method. This does not describe typical lifespan, mean lifespan in the wild, an inevitable ceiling, or a record currently known to recur.

## Reference labels do not measure sample size {#s-4118105fd1}
Same-name rows include Jones, K., Bielby, J., Cardillo, M. et al., 2009; de Magalhaes and Costa, 2009; Ernest et al., 2003; BC mammals - Savage et al., 2004, among others. The archive preserves field-level row numbers, values, labels, and full reference notes. ## Limits of the two historical source sets {#s-40e4453194}
The 2015 compilation uses medians or averages of the two middle values, except maximum longevity, which is the largest collected record. These are not measurements of one individual, individual ranges, mean wild lifespans, or current population surveys. Historical species and subspecies can share or inherit data. Matching names, family, and sparse rows support association but do not prove equivalence to COL26.8 or remove inherited material. Wild/captive conditions are not uniformly confirmed; underlying studies have not been individually reviewed. Missing diet, behavior, range, conservation, and field data require other sources.

EltonTraits is a historical same-name, same-family guide rather than a monograph. Its food and avian-stratum percentages are coarse standardized literature weights, not direct measures of stomach contents, intake, energy, or observation time. Avian A/B codes retain source and proportional uncertainty. Mammalian ABC diet or activity values may rely on a similar congener, so not every field is a direct observation of this species. Genus/family typical values, non-species-level avian strata, and split entries requiring individual review do not support definite claims. The source works have not been reread. Individual, seasonal, and local variation, current range, population, and conservation require further evidence.
