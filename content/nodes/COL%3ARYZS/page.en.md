## The pinned catalogue and taxonomic disagreement {#s-0dbe4abbf7}
COL26.8 accepts Cebus malitiosus Elliot, 1909; MDD v2.5 lists it as a synonym of C. albifrons. Colombia's national biodiversity atlas presents a separate map entry, but this does not settle the different taxonomic interpretation in phylogenetic studies.

## Genetic study and type-material reassessment {#s-f15ca609b9}
The 2019 study analyzed mitogenomes from 189 capuchins, two mitochondrial genes from another 394, and nuclear microsatellites in 41 Colombian individuals. The authors treated samples under the older names malitiosus and hypoleucus as one northern Colombian taxon and reported hybridization signals with a C. capucinus lineage. This is that study's taxonomic interpretation, not a settled consensus about every individual assigned to COL malitiosus.

A 2026 review of museum material found that the skin and skull registered together as the 1909 holotype (AMNH 14620) represent different individuals. Using an expedition report that identified AMNH 14620 as male, the authors recommend retaining the skull as the holotype and removing the female skin from the type series. This clarifies the name-bearing specimen's diagnosis; it does not settle species rank or provide field ecology.

## Modeled range and missing ecological evidence {#s-f33d703bef}
The 2020 Colombian atlas gives map-model values of 7,622 km² for potential range and 1,821 km² for remnant range. These are modeled layers, not confirmed field points, continuous occupancy measurements or population counts; the atlas also describes knowledge of the species and its populations as limited. Available sources remain insufficient for an independent life-history or range-wide ecological account.

## Localities and spelling of historical sample names {#s-5b8833364d}
Table II in a 2018 mitogenomic study lists five Colombian samples under the printed label C. a. malitosus from Tayrona National Park in the Sierra Nevada de Santa Marta, and one under C. a. malitiosus from Turbo, Antioquia. The spelling differs within the table. These are sample labels in a study of gracile capuchin systematics; they document the listed sample localities, not the present range, abundance, or ecological use of either place.

## Evidence limits {#s-972aaad680}
This page covers taxonomic disagreement, type review, historical sample localities, modeled maps and the 2019 handbook’s morphology summary. Independent field ecology attributable to the pinned COL concept remains insufficient, so the first-round gap stays open. Handbook dimensions do not separate sexes or document original samples; maps are not population counts, and pelage is not assigned to the name-bearing skull. .

## Separate handbook pelage from the type skin {#s-1ff75d6c45}
The 2019 EAZA malitiosus account describes a pale-brown crown, cinnamon-brown back and lighter ventral pelage extending over the shoulders and inner upper arms. Its body-size and tail fields are 45.7 cm and 43.3 cm, displayed across the sex columns without separate values; neither sex nor sample size is assigned here. The handbook’s pelage summary is distinct from the name-bearing skull discussed in the 2026 review. Skin and skull identities are not recombined.

## Natural-history gaps remain {#s-3498aad196}
The handbook lists lowland, submontane and montane forest, but lacks diet, reproduction, behavior and mass information. Historical Tayrona and Turbo sample labels, modeled distribution and this habitat summary remain separate evidence; they cannot jointly establish present occupancy or life history.
