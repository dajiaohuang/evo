## Horse evolution branched through teeth, toes, and diets {#s-cf70ae2acf}
Small, multi-toed early equids, browsing Anchitherium, and three-toed hipparionins with varied diets record several evolutionary paths, not a one-way march from forest to grassland.

## Small, multi-toed early equids {#s-dd281a4eda}
Between about 56 and 40 Ma, early equid branches included small, multi-toed animals with low-crowned teeth. They establish the starting diversity of the story; later horses did not simply inherit one straight path toward a single toe.

## Browsing persists {#s-d6f326c13b}
At Sandelzhausen, the tridactyl Anchitherium aurelianense retained low-crowned teeth. Its wear evidence indicates browse-dominated mixed feeding, showing that three-toed horses could persist as browsers rather than fitting a simple forest-versus-grassland split.

## Three toes, varied diets {#s-554fb56856}
From roughly 13 to 3 Ma, Old World hipparionins stayed tridactyl while lineages diversified in feeding ecology. The evidence ranges from browse-dominated and mixed diets to grazing, so open habitats did not produce one uniform horse form or diet.
