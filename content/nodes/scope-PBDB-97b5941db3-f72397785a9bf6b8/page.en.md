## atlas-profile / overview {#s-37db9fc49e}
Two Burgess Shale specimens preserve optic nerves, a possible compact head ganglion and a segmented ventral nerve cord. Elemental mapping supports a nervous-tissue interpretation, while the animal's broader arthropod placement remains disputed.

## atlas-profile / ecology / diet {#s-d4e78db2b6}
Not established for the profiled M. symmetrica. Micropredation is supported for the separate species M. plenovenatrix and should not be transferred to this sample.

## atlas-profile / ecology / habitat {#s-5244a88e43}
The cited specimens come from the marine Burgess Shale setting.

## atlas-profile / ecology / locomotion {#s-efe5f3e072}
Locomotor performance is unknown for M. symmetrica. Walking legs are documented in M. plenovenatrix, a different species.

## atlas-profile / ecology / bodySize {#s-60431de2cd}

## atlas-profile / evidenceSummary {#s-d923c8c4c4}
Elemental maps support identifying the carbon films as nervous tissue. The remains are incomplete, and other anatomical signals have led to competing placement hypotheses; these specimens do not establish an ancestral chelicerate nervous system.

## Nervous tissue preserved as carbon films {#s-ac6361a1d6}
Two Burgess Shale specimens, MCZ 1811 and USNM 305093, preserve carbonaceous films interpreted as optic nerves, a possible compact synganglion in the head, and a segmented ventral nerve cord. Elemental mapping supports identifying the films as nervous tissue. The possible head ganglion is an interpretation, so it is useful to keep that qualification beside the anatomical description.

## A specimen-level result, not a complete life history {#s-db4d17ba21}
The neural fossils and appendicular evidence have supported competing views of Mollisonia's position among arthropods, so this material does not settle the ancestral organization of chelicerates. It also does not establish diet or locomotion for M. symmetrica: a benthic-micropredator interpretation comes from the separate species M. plenovenatrix. The 508.1–505 Ma interval describes the cited Burgess Shale sample, not the genus's full duration or a chelicerate divergence date.
