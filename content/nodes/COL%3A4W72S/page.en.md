## Three-part leaves, recurved spines and a tentative comparison {#s-5e6d307a28}
The 1993 account uses the name *Rhus acocksii* and describes a semi-evergreen shrub that is prostrate to scrambling, or a woody climber, with short recurved spines. Its smooth bark peels. Leaves have three petiolated leaflets. Petioles are semiterete, shallowly grooved above and recorded as (4-)27(-46) mm; the dark-green leaflets dry olive-brown, are glabrous and hypostomatous, and have their own stalks. Terminal and lateral leaflet stalks are about ±6 mm and ±3 mm. Blades range from ovate to elliptic, with cuneate-to-attenuate bases, often twisted acuminate tips that may bear a small mucro, and entire margins. Venation is simple craspedodromous; the midrib is prominent below and impressed above, as are the other veins. The source gives terminal leaflets as (8-)45(-74) × (5-)23(-34) mm and lateral leaflets as (6-)35(-60) × (4-)18(-40) mm. It describes much-branched axillary and terminal panicles, with axillary peduncles persisting as recurved spines; flowers are called “normal.” Drupes are circular and globoid, green then glossy dark red, measuring 6.0 × 5.5 to 7.0 × 6.0 mm.

## Similarities are not the same as a settled relationship {#s-023be052bd}
Under the name *Rhus acocksii*, the account says it may be closely related to the co-occurring *R. chirindensis*. It distinguishes that species as a large, upright tree with long straight lower-stem spines, differently shaped leaflets and much shorter leaflet stalks. The account notes only a superficial resemblance to *R. monticola* from Malawi. It places *S. acocksii* among quartzites in the understory of forest margins along the upper riverine escarpments. These are statements from a regional species account; the excerpt supplies no dated sampling frame or population frequency.
