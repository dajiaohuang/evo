## This entry retains a historically named, revised species concept {#s-23538fd626}
Sinopterus gui is an accepted name in the fixed PBDB working roster, while the 2023 study refers its holotype BPV-077 to S. dongi and treats S. gui as a junior synonym. This entry retains the database record for traceability alongside the specimen interpretation. Two catalogue entries do not directly establish two separate biological species.

## Damaged bone surfaces limit observable anatomy {#s-6e03173e7f}
The 2023 account describes damaged, crushed bone surfaces in BPV-077. The skull is mainly exposed from the left, with breakage and displacement posteriorly. The rostrum lacks a premaxillary crest and the mandibular symphysis has a shallow crest; only the first wing phalanx is preserved, and metacarpal proportions and some cervical details cannot be reliably assessed. Missing portions do not supply a complete wing shape or exact skull posture.

## Unfused bones support youth while wingspan estimates differ {#s-61135c4fcd}
Unfused palatal and posterior skull bones, dorsal centra and neural arches, scapula and coracoid, pelvis and tibia/fibula support a very young individual. Its specimen account cites roughly 64 cm wingspan, while another holotype-comparison passage gives about 0.8 m. This internal difference is retained without choosing a definitive body size. The discussion also rejects the earlier claim of fused dorsals rather than using that old diagnosis to infer adulthood.

## Referral to S. dongi does not mean inclusion in every analysis {#s-893b18c5e1}
The revision interprets differences from S. dongi, especially the absent premaxillary crest, as compatible with growth and maintains synonymy. BPV-077 was excluded from the morphometric dataset because of incompleteness. Its referral therefore relies on qualitative comparison and the broader interpretation, rather than every statistical model directly measuring or independently proving this specimen’s species identity.

## Evidence limits {#s-7ab53f51ad}
The 2023 revision interprets juvenile holotype BPV-077 as a young S. dongi and lists S. gui as its junior synonym. The described anatomy and growth evidence support that reading, but the incomplete holotype was excluded from the study's morphometric dataset. PBDB still keeps a separate S. gui entry, so the two names are not evidence here for two biological species. One proxy collection and its broad age interval do not establish a complete distribution or the specimen's precise age. This account retains the database name alongside the revision; the original 2003 description, other specimens and later taxonomic work are not fully represented.
