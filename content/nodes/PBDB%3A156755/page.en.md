## The type mandible and its updated collection number {#s-f574edd645}
When Bakonydraco galaczi was named in 2005, holotype MTM Gyn/3 was a nearly complete toothless mandible; paratype MTM Gyn/4 comprised 21 dentary symphyseal fragments, held at the Hungarian Natural History Museum, Budapest. The 2011 paper maps the holotype to MTM 2007.110.1 and the paratype to MTM 2007.111.1, and refers another 22 symphyseal fragments, MTM V 2010.74.1–22. Updated numbers do not represent an additional holotype, and fragment counts are not established individual counts.

## What the approximately 29 cm mandible preserves {#s-26199f73e7}
The original description records a mandible about 29 cm long, with the symphysis extending approximately half the ramus length. Its dorsal outline is triangular, and the anterior symphysis is pointed and laterally compressed. The dorsal surface curves posterodorsally to a transverse ridge and bears two rows of slit-like foramina. The authors also describe a glenoid fossa not divided into medial and lateral cotyles. Slight dorsoventral compression has crushed ramus walls and obscured sutures; anatomical descriptions retain these preservation limits.

## The upper-jaw rostrum added in 2011 {#s-00cfd69974}
The 2011 paper refers MTM V 2010.80.1, a toothless premaxillary fragment preserving 114 mm, to this species. The authors distinguish it from mandibular symphyses using blunter lateral edges, a dorsal keel rather than a crest, tip angle, a single median canal and foraminal distribution. The preserved portion shows no sagittal crest; posterior crushing leaves the anterior margin of the nasoantorbital fenestra uncertain. The 114 mm measurement is a fragment length, without a complete skull or evidence joining both jaws in one individual.

## Bony jaw gaps and the unknown keratinous covering {#s-5e25c6e00d}
Matching the upper-jaw fragment to the mandible in the 2011 reconstruction reveals that the straighter upper occlusal surface does not closely fit the curved, transversely ridged anterior mandible. The authors interpret nutritive foramina as indirect support for a keratinous rhamphotheca, but its thickness, cutting edges, anterior extension and exact extent remain unknown. Irregular contact between bony surfaces therefore establishes neither the living closed-beak outline nor biting efficiency or one feeding action. This reconstruction relies on comparison of isolated material.

## Local neck and wing bones retain indeterminate species referrals {#s-a3d5fed7df}
The 2005 paper places isolated cervicals and several limb elements under Azhdarchidae indet., explicitly finding no direct evidence that they belong to B. galaczi. The authors consider a local referral possible, without an associated skeleton. The 2011 material continues to separate species-level jaws, Azhdarchidae indet. and Pterodactyloidea indet.; most new postcranial family referrals likewise rely on comparison rather than unique diagnostic features. MTM Gyn/453, tentatively a second or third wing phalanx in 2005, is reinterpreted in 2011 as a distal ulna, MTM V 2010.75.1. Shared locality and similar size do not assemble these bones into one complete animal.

## The 3.5–4 m wingspan comes from jaw-length comparison {#s-ae774e24fe}
The 2005 authors compare mandibular length with Quetzalcoatlus and Zhejiangopterus to estimate a wingspan of about 3.5–4 m. Separately, they estimate about 3–3.5 m from local cervicals of indeterminate species. Similar estimates inform their discussion of a possible common taxon; they are neither a measured wingspan from one skeleton nor proof of cervical referral. Methods and material remain separate, without turning an estimate into a fixed adult size.

## Fish, fruit and small animals remain possible foods {#s-e299c8b579}
The 2005 paper uses the pointed toothless mandible to discuss small-fish capture, frugivory and varied foods, while arguing against skim feeding. With an upper jaw added, the 2011 paper emphasizes uncertain occlusion and keratinous covering and discusses alternatives including stork-like or ground-hornbill-like generalism and frugivory. These are morphological comparisons and functional hypotheses, without stomach contents or direct feeding evidence for this species. They establish neither piscivory, frugivory nor a specific menu. The original rejection of skimming likewise remains the authors’ dated interpretation.

## Evidence limits {#s-0357f2c4bc}
The 2005 description and 2011 new-material paper document the jaws, specimen numbers and isolated postcranial bones summarized here. They do not join those bones into one skeleton. The wingspans come from comparisons, and the proposed foods remain functional hypotheses; no direct feeding evidence or complete keratinous beak is preserved. The selected database record gives a dated locality sample, not a complete range or a direct age for an individual. Later taxonomic work, the exact beak outline and much of the animal's biology remain unresolved.
