## Helichrysum album {#s-fbe0d3a88e}
## A woolly rosette with solitary heads

Hilliard's *Flora of Southern Africa* account describes a perennial herb growing from a woody base, with one or a few leaf rosettes. Lateral flowering stems reach 150 mm and are loosely woolly and leafy. The obovate basal leaves may reach 60 by 25 mm, commonly about 35 by 25 mm; stem leaves are smaller, oblanceolate and woolly.

Bell-shaped heads are solitary and about 20 mm long. Their glossy white bracts form roughly 11 loose overlapping rows, with crimson at the inner base. The account counts 172–224 florets, including 19–26 female florets; it classifies the others as homogamous. Smooth achenes are about 1.5 mm long. Pappus bristles equal the corolla in length, with barbed tips and bases that barely cohere. The account places the plant at about 2,000–3,300 m on steep broken turf slopes and low basalt cliffs.
