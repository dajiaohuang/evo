## Leaves, flowers, and fruit {#s-c2ab730ad9}
Codd's 2005 regional account describes plants with several stems. Their rigid, strongly veined leaves stand upright to ascending and measure 300–700 × 8–14 mm. In cross-section they are U-shaped rather than keeled, channelled above, and serrulate at the margin; the veins stand out on the lower surface and often bear scattered papillae. The flowering stalk is about as long as the leaves and 300–600 mm tall. The dense raceme is subcylindrical to ovoid, 80–140 × 50–60 mm. Buds range from orange or pale orange-red to coral, then become yellow to greenish yellow as flowers open. Ovate to ovate-lance-shaped bracts are 7–10 mm long, with tips from rounded to pointed and margins finely eroded and toothed. Pedicels are 1.5–3.0 mm long and lengthen to 6 mm in fruit. The nearly cylindrical perianth is 27–33 mm long; its ovate lobes are 3 mm and spread slightly. At flowering, the stamens extend 0–8 mm beyond the perianth. The nearly spherical fruit is 7–9 mm long. Flowering is reported from mid-January to early March.

These measurements and flowering observations come from a regional species account published in 2005; it gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency.

## Why pressed specimens may be difficult to identify {#s-eea853c71f}
Codd notes that the rigid, upright leaves are U-shaped in cross-section and may have margins that roll inward. Pressing can flatten them, obscuring that shape. Tiny papillae along the veins can make the surface feel rough and may cause confusion with *K. hirsuta*, whose leaves are hairy and V-shaped in cross-section.

## Habitat {#s-a1851cef0c}
The account records the species on grassy slopes, often among dolerite rocks.
