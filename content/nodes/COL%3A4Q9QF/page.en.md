## Accepted name and placement {#s-05376dcd6f}
COL26.8 accepts Ptychadena goweri Smith, Noonan & Colston, 2017 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Ptychadenidae (family) → Ptychadena (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## Southeastern rift-area forest clearings {#s-b66826bf1a}
The revision recorded the species southeast of Ethiopia’s Great Rift Valley at 1745–2550 m, in Harenna Forest clearings and nearby areas south of the Sanetti Plateau. These records describe the study sample, without establishing every occupied clearing or physiological elevation limits.

## Shallow-puddle night calling {#s-98794d3ee6}
Males called at night in shallow puddles in forest clearings or grassy meadows. Several other Ptychadena were recorded in the same broader area. These records document calling sites and co-occurrence, without assigning clutch sizes, larval requirements or direct species interactions.

## A geographically sampled mitochondrial and nuclear dataset {#s-6b1ee63724}
The 2023 Ethiopian Highlands study lists 11 mitochondrial samples and 9 nuclear-SNP samples for Ptychadena goweri in Table 1, with mitochondrial nucleotide diversity π=0.25%. Samples across the twelve-species study were collected in 2011–2019; these are analysed specimens, not a population census. All assembled mitochondrial genomes had the usual two rRNAs, thirteen protein-coding genes and twenty-two tRNAs, but no species had a completely resolved control region because repeats could not be confidently assembled from short reads. The phylogenetic alignment used selected genes (13,937 bp across 303 samples), rather than an alignment of complete control regions.

## A study-defined highland clade and regional distribution {#s-4e7b0c84ef}
The paper places Ptychadena goweri in its P. neumanni clade and reports it from the eastern side of the Great Rift Valley. These distributions combine the authors' geographic sampling and earlier range work; they are not independently updated complete range polygons. The twelve-species highland clade is a subset of Ptychadena. Its mitochondrial-clock analysis estimated a common ancestor around 11.7 million years ago, with a reported interval of 8.827–15.494 million years. The east-of-rift ancestral reconstruction had a model probability of 69%; neither that estimate nor inferred crossings is a directly observed migration event or a nuclear-genome divergence date for this species.

## A dated reassessment argument, with remaining demographic gaps {#s-695ca7d3c1}
The 2023 discussion calls for conservation assessment or reassessment using revised species concepts and genetic and geographic evidence.
