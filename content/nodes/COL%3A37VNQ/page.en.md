## Locality-specific recordings within a broad species concept {#s-773481302b}
The paper groups Eumseong (25 individuals, 700 notes) and Pyeongtaek (five individuals, 291 notes) as the northern side of Chilgap Mountain, labelled Dryophytes suweonensis in its figures but analysed within D. suweonensis sensu lato. Across five sites it recorded fifty-six individuals and 2,858 notes from evening to midnight. Eumseong recordings date to May–August 2012; the other sites were sampled in May2021 and/or June2022. These are separate recording cohorts, not one simultaneous survey, complete breeding calendars or a population census. Individuals were not captured; this paper does not supply new genetic verification of each caller's correspondence to the fixed accepted concept.

## Population means describe notes rather than species limits {#s-abb48e06c3}
Eumseong and Pyeongtaek maximum-power frequencies averaged 3,079.69±27.59 and 3,194.63±27.34 Hz, respectively; note durations were 0.15±0.001 and 0.14±0.001 seconds. Table2 identifies the quantities after ± as standard errors, not standard deviations or ranges. The maximum-power frequency is the frequency of greatest note power, distinct from the high-frequency boundary and the 90% bandwidth. Delta time is the duration of a note, not of a whole advertisement-call train. Repeated notes from a caller are not independent individual frogs; these means do not establish species-wide identification thresholds.

## Call variation motivates further integrated taxonomy {#s-1a97223b6e}
The study found significant population differences but no consistent north–south pattern matching the proposed mountain barrier. Both sides included notes with two to eight independent pulses. The authors conclude cautiously that their call data support population-level variation and ask for broader genetic and morphological work on flaviventris status. This is not a formal synonymization or proof that the two fixed accepted names represent one species. Table1 PCA retained five variables while excluding pulse count, high frequency and bandwidth, so a rejected variable is not independently certified biologically irrelevant.

## Temperature and roadside noise are separate claims {#s-b0ba26cd7b}
The recorded temperatures were 16.6–26.1°C; the authors state no significant temperature effect, partly referring to unpublished personal data. They propose traffic noise as an explanation for unusually high frequency boundaries at three roadside sites, but do not experimentally manipulate noise or present a controlled causal test. A high boundary is not the dominant frequency or an audiogram of frog hearing. The sampling conditions do not establish thermal tolerance limits, and the proposed noise mechanism remains untested here.
