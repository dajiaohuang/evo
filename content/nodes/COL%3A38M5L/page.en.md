## A stout, glabrous liana with slender follicles {#s-37ed5e515a}
The Flora of Panama describes a rather stout, glabrous liana. Its elliptic to ovate-elliptic leaves are shortly acuminate, obtuse to possibly rounded at the base, up to 17 × 9 cm, glabrous and subcoriaceous. Stout petioles are about 1–2 cm long and have 2–3 large stipule-like glands in the axils. The aggregate dichasial inflorescences are axillary and alternate, several- to many-flowered, with a 2–3 cm primary peduncle. Flowers have 4–8 mm pedicels; the narrow-lanceolate bracteole is recorded as “1(?)”, 2–3 mm long and variably placed. Calyx lobes are question-marked as possibly equal, narrowly lanceolate, about 3 × 0.5 mm; the squamella is thin with lacerate margins. The corolla is green to white, with a tube about 6 mm long, pubescent inside at the stamen attachment, and possibly lanceolate lobes 13–16 mm long. Stamens attach about midway along the tube and are about 3 mm long; the separate nectaries reach nearly the ovary’s height. Slender follicles are 7–8 cm long, and mature seeds are unknown. The historical account lists Costa Rica and Panama, but does not establish a current or complete distribution; ecology was not assessed in this source slice.
