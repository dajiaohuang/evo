## Name and historical records {#s-a02ea34f7e}
Mephitis macroura is an accepted species in the pinned COL26.8 classification of Mephitidae. The PanTHERIA 1.0 values below come from its MSW05 entry: the table is dated August 2008, and its biological data gathering ended in June 2004. Matching names do not resolve taxonomic changes made later.

## Body-size values and their definitions {#s-558e389805}
The historical sex-adjusted medians are 1097.76 g for adult body mass and 307.05 mm for adult head–body length. The mass field can include adults or animals of unspecified age, but excludes pregnant females. Head–body length excludes the tail; the forearm measure runs from elbow to wrist. These summaries are not measurements of one specimen or a range of individual sizes across the species.

## Reproductive timing and source definitions {#s-07207c33d4}
The historical fields report an active fetal-growth interval of 58.2 d and 4.68 offspring per litter. Here d means days; the fractional litter value is a summary, not a literal litter. Definitions differ across gestation, litter size, weaning and maturity: gestation excludes inactive fetal growth, while weaning may be marked by nutritional independence rather than the last nursing event. Interbirth intervals were consolidated separately as medians, and their source records are not all captive experiments.

## Activity, feeding categories and group data {#s-6d94d63f0f}
The entry classifies activity as mixed timing, including crepuscular or cathemeral activity, and feeding as carnivorous. It records one diet category and one habitat layer. Diet categories distinguish as many as eight resource classes, not eight food species. Habitat layers mean below ground, ground level, water and above ground, rather than landscape types such as forest or grassland. The activity and group fields use non-captive records; group size is a median, not a full account of social organization.

## Historical range-map values {#s-06ed76ac03}
The historical range map gives an area of 1791883.52 km² and latitude endpoints of 12.25° to 34.9°. An individual home range describes routine activity; the mapped area represents a historical species-level range. It does not show that every part was occupied, and the latitude endpoints are not a list of localities. These values do not establish current distribution or population size.

The archive reports 4.34 offspring or eggs per litter or clutch and 59.54 d from conception or fertilization to birth; d means days. ## Estimated weights of food categories {#s-5697060cd5}
The reported nonzero weights are mammals and birds 30%, invertebrates 20%, fruit 20%, other plant material 20% (a source category that also includes fungi), and seeds, nuts and spores 10%. The ABC code combines high, reasonable and partial confidence; some estimates may draw on a congener judged ecologically similar. These are standardized estimates from verbal descriptions, not direct measurements of this species’ diet. A zero does not show that a food is never eaten. Source classes such as Nect, Seed and PlantO are broader than their everyday labels and do not mean an exclusive diet of nectar, seeds or plants.

## What these records establish {#s-8fb98964bc}
The PanTHERIA values describe its historical 2008 WR05 entry. Continuous traits generally use consolidated medians, sometimes after GLM adjustment for sex, definitions or methods; missing values are not filled from *_EXT extrapolations. Depending on the variable, source material can be wild, captive, provisioned or unspecified, and primary, secondary or extrapolated. The summaries are not individual ranges, population censuses or species diagnoses. MSW93/MSW05 same-name correspondence and a matching COL name and family support an association, but do not make later split or lumped concepts equivalent. Current distribution, survival and conservation status need other sources.

The 2015 life-history values are historical same-name records. They are compilation medians or averages of the two middle values, except maximum longevity, which is the largest record found. The summaries are not measurements of one animal, individual ranges, mean wild lifespans or current population surveys. The compilation may share or inherit data across historical species and subspecies; matching names and families do not remove that possibility or prove equivalence to COL26.8 concepts. Wild or captive conditions are not confirmed uniformly, and the underlying publications have not been reviewed individually.
