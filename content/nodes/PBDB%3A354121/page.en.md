## Anatomy in articulated fossils {#s-a617dc0f73}
Articulated Kutorgina chengjiangensis fossils from the Chengjiang Lagerstätte preserve paired valves alongside a lophophore, digestive tract and pedicle. This formation-scale sample makes early brachiopod anatomy unusually tangible, while feeding function and ancestry remain interpretations.

## What the evidence supports {#s-d34049979e}
The Chengjiang material preserves valves and associated soft anatomy. The lophophore and pedicle support an attached, suspension-feeding interpretation, but behavior is inferred and this local sample does not establish the origin or full range of Brachiopoda.

## A feeding organ is preserved with the valves {#s-03bb92ff9a}
The described specimens preserve paired calcareous valves with an associated lophophore, digestive tract and pedicle. A lophophore is the tentacle-bearing organ used by brachiopods in suspension feeding; the described form has an early spirolophe arrangement. The fossils let researchers compare this soft anatomy with the shell, while attachment and feeding remain functional interpretations rather than behavior observed in life.

## One Chengjiang sample is not an ancestor ladder {#s-e58e3aa513}
The material comes from the Chengjiang Lagerstätte in Yunnan, South China, within an Early Cambrian study window of about 520–518 Ma. It records a particular species and formation, not the complete geographic range of that species or the global first appearance of brachiopods. The anatomy can inform comparisons among early body plans without making Kutorgina a direct ancestor of later brachiopods.
