## What does the historical account describe about its leaves, flowers and fruit? {#s-91080fd784}
The Flora of Panama account describes a deciduous, spineless shrub or small tree 1.5–6 m tall. Its few elongated branches soon become hairless. Leaves have petioles up to 10 mm long and short, soon-falling stipules 2–5 mm long. The papery blades are obovate, up to 18 by 7 cm, with an acute base, a rather blunt tapering tip and finely toothed edges. Young leaves are minutely hairy on both sides and have many translucent dots and lines; the midrib and side veins stand out below. Dense, stalkless clusters of flowers grow in leaf axils, often on leafless branchlets. The flowers are white. Flower stalks reach 6 mm and are jointed 2–2.5 mm above the base. The calyx is about 5 mm long with small, blunt, hooded lobes. There are seven or eight stamens and the same number of hairy staminodes (sterile stamens), joined briefly at the base; the ovary is pear-shaped and hairy, tapering into a smooth style with a conical head. The nearly spherical capsule is 8–13 mm long with six blunt lengthwise ridges; it splits into three valves. Its outer surface may be nearly hairless or slightly hairy, and the source marks its net-like texture as uncertain. Up to 13 nearly ovoid seeds, 3–3.5 mm long, occur in a capsule; each has a very finely pitted surface and an orange aril, the fleshy covering around the seed. These details come from the historical regional flora account; its population sampling and observation date are not specified.

## Where does the historical flora report it? {#s-080fc7c8f6}
The historical account reports the species from the West Indies, Costa Rica, Panama and northern South America. It also records “palo de la cruz” as a colloquial name in Panama, citing a specimen collection; the statement does not establish broader common-name use.
