## Many small bones from one bonebed {#s-2c69d8642a}
The oldest sampled caecilian relatives in this node are known from the Norian Blue Mesa Member of Arizona. Their bones belong to *Funcusvermis gilmorei*, which published analyses place on the caecilian stem. This extends the fossil record of the total group, but it does not date the origin of living caecilians.

The study names *Funcusvermis gilmorei* from skeletal elements representing at least 76 individuals in a U–Pb-bracketed bonebed. The age evidence establishes when the assemblage formed; the anatomical comparisons and phylogenetic analyses place the animal among stem gymnophionomorphs. Because the material consists of isolated elements, their association and the resulting placement retain some uncertainty.

## What the fossil range means {#s-910c462465}
The record pushes the sampled caecilian total-group history into the Late Triassic. A total group includes the living lineage and its closer extinct relatives; the crown group contains the last common ancestor of living members and its descendants. *Funcusvermis* is reported outside crown Gymnophiona, so this occurrence supports an older total-group record without assigning an age to the living caecilian crown.
