## What does the historical account describe about this small annual herb? {#s-c1e7385de2}
The Flora of Panama account describes a hairless, monoecious annual herb about 10–50 cm tall. Its smooth, round main stem is about 1–2.5 mm thick; narrow, pointed brownish scale leaves are 1.5–3 mm long. Smooth, deciduous side branchlets are usually 5–10 cm long and carry about 15–30 leaves. The thin leaves have 0.4–0.7 mm petioles and mostly ovate or elliptic blades about 7–17 by 3–9 mm, with pointed or slightly pointed tips, strongly uneven bases and often paler undersides. Each pair of narrow stipules is unequal; the longer is 2–2.7 mm. Small unisexual flower clusters may occupy the lower 2–12 nodes of a branchlet, sometimes with three to seven male flowers; the upper nodes bear solitary female flowers. Male flowers usually have five (rarely six) sepals and three stamens; their filaments are free or joined for up to three quarters of their length. Female flowers have five sepals, a shallow dish-shaped disc and a smooth ovary; the styles are divided into two branches. The smooth, straw-colored capsules are about 3 mm across. Dark brown seeds are 1.4–1.8 mm long and finely warted. These details come from the historical regional flora text, which gives no sampling frame or observation date.

## What range does the historical flora report? {#s-482b936d2f}
The historical Flora of Panama account calls the species one of the most widespread native American species and gives a span from central Texas south to northern Argentina. This is the account’s reported range, not a current survey or a complete global inventory.
