## Evidence in brief {#s-b5cbf03e74}
Selected evidence includes a 2017 southern India survey and a 2018 comparison of 711 specimens from 12 macaque species, including 79 bonnet macaques. That cross-species sample does not establish diagnostic variation within Macaca radiata. A separate 2018 study analyzed 82 fecal-derived mitochondrial D-loop sequences from 16 southern Indian locations; one mitochondrial marker does not establish nuclear or genome-wide structure, and the proposed human-mediated translocation explanation remains a hypothesis. These studies do not settle life history, full range or current formal conservation status.

## Local records, not a range-wide census {#s-fd985b8b54}
The COL26.8 checklist accepts Macaca radiata. The evidence summarized here comes from selected southern Indian forests, roads and human-use sites, plus museum specimens used in a comparison across macaque species. It does not provide a current map or a complete population trend.

## A forest occupancy estimate at Parambikulam {#s-b9900b8ee7}
In one survey of the Parambikulam landscape in Kerala, observers detected 54 groups in 33 of 64 grid cells. The fitted mean occupancy estimate was 0.51 ± 0.08 (SE), where occupancy is the modelled share of sampled cells used by macaques; detection probability was 0.25 ± 0.05. These estimates belong to that forest survey and its sampling design.

## Roadside counts connected to Mysore {#s-0c2ca75c22}
The study’s roadside counts fell from 889 in 2003 to 407 in 2015 on roads connected to Mysore. The 2015 resurvey covered 464 km and used a 15-m strip on each side of the road centreline. A roadside count measures animals visible within a defined survey band; the change is not a census of the species across southern India.

## Temple and tourist-site histories {#s-445b1cafba}
The same paper compared selected southern Indian temple and tourist locations over historical records and follow-up surveys from 2009–2015. About 31% of assessed locations recorded bonnet macaques; among reported translocation cases, roughly one fifth were followed by reappearance, while more than 48% were recorded as disappearance or removal. These site histories do not describe present-day occupancy across the full range.

## Bonnet macaques in a museum comparison {#s-04679b1f6d}
A 2018 comparison covered 711 museum specimens from 12 macaque species, including 79 M. radiata specimens (46 male and 33 female) attributed to southern and western India; subadults were also included. The paper reported a weak interspecific latitudinal pattern. Its mixed specimen origins and cross-species design do not establish diagnostic traits or variation within bonnet macaques.

## What these studies leave open {#s-833aaec7ce}
The selected sources do not synthesize the species’ life history, diet, locomotion, full distribution or current formal conservation category. A local recommendation in the 2017 article is not a statutory designation or an independently verified current status.
