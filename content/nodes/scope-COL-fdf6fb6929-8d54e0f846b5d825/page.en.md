## Bonnet macaques: local surveys and comparative samples {#s-e11ca442a5}
COL26.8 lists Macaca radiata as a species. Selected southern Indian surveys, a museum comparison across macaque species and a mitochondrial D-loop study address different questions. None is a current range map or a species-wide population trend.

## Evidence from surveys, specimens and mitochondrial markers {#s-b5cbf03e74}
One 2017 study combined local forest, roadside and temple records; a 2018 comparison examined 711 specimens from 12 macaque species, including 79 bonnet macaques. A separate study analyzed 82 fecal-derived mitochondrial D-loop sequences from 16 southern Indian locations. These designs do not establish within-species diagnostic variation or genome-wide structure; the proposed human-mediated translocation explanation remains a hypothesis.

## Different materials, different scales {#s-6bd7f5ea74}
Bonnet macaque evidence here includes local counts and occupancy estimates, site histories, museum craniodental specimens and mitochondrial sequences. Each source measures a different thing. The sections retain those differences instead of combining them into one range-wide account.

## Records from selected southern Indian sites {#s-fd985b8b54}
The COL26.8 checklist accepts Macaca radiata. The selected studies cover particular forests, roads, temples and tourist sites in southern India, plus museum specimens used for a cross-species comparison. They do not provide a current range map or full population trend.

## Occupancy estimate in Parambikulam {#s-b9900b8ee7}
In one Kerala survey, observers detected 54 groups in 33 of 64 grid cells. The fitted mean occupancy was 0.51 ? 0.08 (SE), with detection probability 0.25 ? 0.05. Occupancy is the modeled share of sampled cells used; these values belong to that survey and its design.

## Roadside counts on routes connected to Mysore {#s-0c2ca75c22}
Counts fell from 889 in 2003 to 407 in 2015 on roads connected to Mysore. The 2015 resurvey covered 464 km and a 15-m strip on each side of the road centreline. The change describes animals visible in that survey band, not a census across southern India.

## Temple and tourist-site histories {#s-445b1cafba}
The study assessed selected sites using historical records and follow-up surveys from 2009?2015. About 31% recorded bonnet macaques. Among reported translocations, roughly one fifth were followed by reappearance; more than 48% were recorded as disappearance or removal. These site histories do not establish present occupancy across the range.

## Bonnet macaques in a 12-species museum comparison {#s-04679b1f6d}
The 2018 comparison covered 711 specimens from 12 macaque species, including 79 M. radiata (46 males, 33 females) attributed to southern and western India; subadults were included. The reported weak latitudinal pattern is interspecific. Mixed origins and cross-species sampling do not establish diagnostic traits or variation within bonnet macaques.

## What the selected studies do not establish {#s-833aaec7ce}
The sources do not synthesize life history, diet, locomotion, full distribution or current formal conservation category. A local recommendation in the 2017 paper is neither a statutory designation nor a current independent status assessment.

## Limits of the local records {#s-e6b40c65b1}
The selected evidence does not establish a range-wide population trend, species-wide ecology or present formal conservation status. Survey results remain tied to their sites, periods and methods.
