## Stem surface {#s-a7432c7f0c}
The Brazilian Flora 2020 record describes the stem as pubescent, covered with short soft hairs that spread away from the surface. It lists simple hairs and capitate glandular hairs, whose tips form small heads. Branched and unbranched bristles, prickles, and short lateral shoots (brachyblasts) are recorded as absent.

## Leaves and inflorescences {#s-81a63185b9}
Stipules, small structures at leaf bases, are persistent. Extrafloral nectaries (nectar-secreting glands outside flowers) are absent, while projections between pairs of pinnae (the leaf’s primary side divisions) are present. The inflorescence, or flower cluster, is globose; floral bracts (small leaf-like structures) are shorter than the flowers before they open (anthesis).

## Flowers {#s-722ed01c7f}
The perianth (calyx and corolla together) is recorded as having four parts. The membranous calyx, the outer sepal whorl, has triangular lobes. Corolla (petal-whorl) lobes have one to three veins and are hairy. The androecium (the stamens collectively) is diplostemonous, with two stamen whorls; the filaments are pink.
