## Classification and source scope {#s-b1f1019edc}
COL26.8 lists *Syagrus guimaraesensis* as an accepted species in *Syagrus*, within the palm family Arecaceae. This account follows one regional Brazilian Flora 2020 morphology field and retains its alternative states.

The accepted species name follows COL26.8; the Brazilian Flora description is linked to its matching World Flora Online record.
This morphology is one regional dataset entry, not a global description or a complete account of variation.

## Stem, leaves, and inflorescence {#s-7670c1d48e}
The field describes a solitary or underground stem with leaf-sheath remnants. Green pinnate leaves have persistent sheaths arranged helically around the stem, with fibrous margins; the petiole is unarmed and the leaf axis slightly curved. Leaflets are leathery, set at one angle and arranged uniformly; they are erect and rigid, with tips recorded as acute, rounded, or notched and asymmetric. Their surfaces are dark green and the same colour on both sides. The inflorescence is spike-like, with a grooved, waxy-coated spadix, a hairless main axis, and no rachillae. The fruiting structure is exposed between the leaves.

## Fruit {#s-f63cb7b1ad}
Fruit is ovoid or elliptic. Its green or yellow-green outer layer is woolly, the middle layer fibrous and succulent, and the inner layer woody and fusiform. The endosperm is homogeneous.

The field records a solitary or underground stem with leaf-sheath remnants; green pinnate leaves with persistent helically arranged, fibrous-margined sheaths, unarmed petioles, and slightly curved rachises; leathery pinnae inserted at one angle, uniformly arranged, erect and rigid, with acute, rounded, or emarginate asymmetric tips and dark-green concolorous surfaces; a spiciform inflorescence and exposed interfoliar infructescence; a grooved, pruinose spadix with a glabrous rachis and no rachillae; ovoid or elliptic fruit with a green or yellow-green tomentose epicarp, fibrous succulent mesocarp, woody fusiform endocarp, and homogeneous endosperm.
These traits come from one morphology field in the regional Brazilian Flora 2020 dataset; the field does not establish global morphology, population variation, sex, or life stage.
The field does not say whether the described plants were wild, cultivated, managed, or fossil material.
