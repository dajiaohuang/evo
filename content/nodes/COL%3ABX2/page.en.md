## Start with the historical PanTHERIA records {#s-faf7a04d75}
The local PanTHERIA route for Leporidae contains 29 usable historical accounts. Begin with Brachylagus idahoensis, Bunolagus monticularis, Caprolagus hispidus, Lepus alleni, or Lepus americanus. Each account keeps its MSW05 name, variable-specific conditions, and missing fields. The route follows pinned COL26.8; its historical rows are not the current accepted-species count.

## What the mass endpoints compare {#s-ac64c3a95b}
Among the available consolidated adult-mass values in this route, Brachylagus idahoensis is at the low end (431.19 g) and Lepus othus at the high end (4,837.35 g). These are sex-adjusted medians from historical entries, not the smallest and largest bodies measured in the family. Read the life-history fields alongside their definitions; mass differences alone do not establish a shared lifestyle among descendants.

## Where life-history records are available {#s-124f9afcd5}
This route includes 44 historical life-history accounts. Adult mass and young per litter or eggs per clutch appear in 44 accounts; the interval from conception or fertilization to birth and the compilation's maximum-lifespan record each appear in 37. These counts describe the records collected here, not all accepted Leporidae. Before comparing values, check which event each field measures, its unit, and its source conditions.

## A broad EltonTraits comparison {#s-e8712d6984}
The EltonTraits route contains 54 historical entries: 52 include food-category weights, and all 54 include a foraging stratum and a mammalian activity-period code. Food weights are coarse estimates, while the other fields are categories or binary codes. They support comparisons among these records; they do not show that all hares and rabbits use the same foods, feeding positions, or activity periods.

## Five records with a single available field {#s-ad724e5f82}
A smaller EltonTraits route covers Lepus tibetanus, Sylvilagus cognatus, Sylvilagus robustus, Sylvilagus varynaensis, and Nesolagus timminsi. It contains a foraging-stratum field for each record, but no food-category weights or mammalian activity-period values. Treat these as isolated field entries, not as complete ecological accounts of the five species or the family.

## Limits of the narrower route {#s-2dc54dde7e}
These 12 accounts have at least one focal field, a matching historical name and family, and a one-to-one link in the source crosswalk. They are a subset of the collected material, not a census of accepted species. The comparison values are consolidated historical entries; they do not establish current species boundaries, present-day distributions, or conservation status.

## Follow the life-history entries {#s-d69fd34dbf}
The life-history compilation contains 18 historical accounts under Leporidae. Start with Brachylagus idahoensis, Bunolagus monticularis, Caprolagus hispidus, Lepus nigricollis, or Lepus callotis. These are same-name entries arranged along the pinned COL26.8 classification; 18 is the count in this route, not the family's accepted-species total.

## Three measures that need different readings {#s-42f050b36e}
Gestation appears in 15 accounts, female age at first reproduction in 13, and a maximum-lifespan record in 5. First reproduction is not the same event as first birth or sexual maturity, and a maximum-lifespan record is not an average lifespan. Values may come from different source conditions; a missing field for one species cannot be supplied by a relative's record.

## Limits of the historical life-history accounts {#s-0150776890}
These accounts are linked by matching names and families and by the source's one-to-one MSW93/MSW05 same-name table; that link does not prove equivalence to current species concepts. Except for maximum lifespan, fields generally average literature values with equal weighting. Inputs may be published averages, individual values, or range midpoints when other data are unavailable; sample sizes do not set the weights, and underlying literature may be reused. Maximum lifespan is only the longest recorded value. Adult mass is not interpreted in this route, missing values are not borrowed from relatives, and the source numbers remain on the species pages. The underlying species literature and all wild, captive, and locality conditions were not independently established here.

## Evidence limits {#s-e11adbe8b8}
The PanTHERIA and life-history routes link historical same-name records to the pinned classification; they are not complete lists of accepted Leporidae or proof that older species concepts match current ones. The EltonTraits routes contain same-name, same-family entries with at least two usable ecological field groups, or just one in the smaller route. Their coarse weights and codes do not make descendant traits family-wide. Each compilation has its own methods and source conditions. These records do not establish current taxonomy, distribution, conservation status, or complete biology.
