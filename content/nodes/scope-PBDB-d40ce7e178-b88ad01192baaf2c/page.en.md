## A branching fossil with tentacle impressions {#s-7d540e2dd7}
The specimen BGS GSM 105875 comes from the Bradgate Formation in Charnwood Forest, England. It preserves two bifurcating, polyp-like tubes inside a rigid organic skeleton, with tentacle impressions around the openings. These structures are observations in one centimetre-scale fossil; their role as feeding polyps is an interpretation, and the specimen does not preserve prey or colony physiology.

## What a branching body plan can suggest {#s-2ba3ba34c1}
The study's morphology analysis places Auroralumina within crown Cnidaria, but this is a hypothesis based on anatomical characters, not proof that it was a direct ancestor of living cnidarians. The tentative sessile-feeder interpretation follows the tentacle-bearing form; prey, exact habitat preference and feeding behaviour remain unknown. The 562–557 Ma interval describes the Bradgate Formation context of this specimen, not the age of the cnidarian crown or a global range for the genus.
