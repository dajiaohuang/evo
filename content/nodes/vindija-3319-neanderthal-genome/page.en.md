## From one Vindija bone fragment to a Neanderthal genome {#s-eed50685ce}
Vindija 33.19 was selected from nineteen cave-bone fragments screened for mitochondrial DNA. Researchers used 567 mg of the specimen for radiocarbon measurement OxA-32278, which returned an age greater than 45,500 years before present; a separate 41 mg extract with high hominin DNA content supplied the material for deep sequencing.

The sequencing produced a roughly 30-fold Neanderthal genome. Its approximately 52,000-year branch-shortening age, population splits and introgression proportions are genomic model estimates. They describe analyses of this individual’s genome, not direct dates for all Neanderthals or a definition of species boundaries.
