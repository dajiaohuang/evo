## The name and this data account {#s-7b91bff611}
In the pinned Catalogue of Life release COL26.8, Picumnus castelnau is accepted in Picidae. AVONET v7 records it under the HBW–BirdLife 2020 name. The dataset includes 4 measured individuals; 3 have a complete morphological set. The usable sample size can still differ by trait.

## Measured beak dimensions {#s-c8709cc88e}
Mean beak length, measured from the anterior edge of the nostril to the tip, is 9.1 mm. At that edge, mean width is 3.7 mm and mean depth is 4.4 mm. These are separate measurements at standardized landmarks, not field-identification thresholds.

## Wing, tarsus and mass {#s-501bd6ed3e}
Mean unflattened wing length, from the carpal joint to the longest primary feather, is 51.1 mm; mean tarsus length is 13.3 mm. A separate compilation reports mean mass of 11.4 g (source category Dunning). That species-average mass is not evidence that every bird in the morphology sample was weighed.

## Habitat, feeding and lifestyle {#s-5ee8658e04}
The dataset’s comparison categories are habitat “Forest,” trophic niche “Invertivore,” and lifestyle “Insessorial.” They are dominant categories for comparing species, not claims that this bird uses only one habitat or one kind of food.

## Movement and geographical summary {#s-03a74307a8}
AVONET classifies migration as sedentary. The historical breeding and resident maps run from latitude -11° to -3.14°. These bounds do not describe non-breeding movements and are neither a current location nor a global occupancy estimate. Geography and morphology come from separate compilation steps.

## Evidence limits {#s-85f28d28df}
The AVONET v7 values here are historical summaries. Morphological values are species averages for the listed samples, not field-identification criteria or ranges of individual variation. Mass comes from separately cited literature or weighing sources, so the morphology sample count does not apply to it. Habitat, food and migration are broad categories; mapped latitude covers historical breeding and resident ranges only. One-to-one historical crosswalks and a matching catalogue name link this record to older data, but do not show that species concepts remain equivalent after later splits or lumps. Other sources are needed for reproduction, present-day populations and conservation status.
