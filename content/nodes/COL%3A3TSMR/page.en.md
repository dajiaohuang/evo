## Name and classification {#s-61722ddb83}
COL26.8 lists Leptopelis kivuensis Ahl, 1929 as an accepted species in the order Anura, family Arthroleptidae and genus Leptopelis. This name and placement describe the catalogue release published on 20 August 2026; a name in another checklist or fossil database requires separate identity evidence.

## Scores within the Rwanda framework {#s-03710afbdb}
Table A4 reports DBS 2, TBS 0 and SBS 2, for a total species index (SI) of 4. DBS weights the number of Rwanda ecozones represented and national endemism. TBS uses national red-list proposals reported by earlier research, not a new global IUCN assessment; its score of 0 corresponds to LC in Table 1. SBS 2 denotes use only of near-natural habitats within disturbed areas. These are categories in the study’s Rwanda index, not experimentally measured tolerances, responses to every land use or a newly assigned threat status. A combined VU/DD score does not distinguish those two categories by itself.

## A species score is not a site score {#s-9a748a8589}
For one species, SI is DBS + TBS + SBS. A site’s RABI adds the SI values of all recorded species without dividing by species richness. Thus this species’ SI of 4 is not the RABI of a site where it occurs, an extinction probability or an effect size. The framework doubles weights between categories (DBS 1/2/4/8; TBS 0/1/2/4/8; SBS 0/1/2/4). Those steps express the authors’ prioritization design; they do not show that extinction risk or ecological sensitivity doubles. The reported positive correlations among sub-indices also mean their inputs were not empirically independent in this sample.

## Sampling limits and unresolved table differences {#s-00c0d86190}
The 51 sites are unevenly distributed: EZ1 has six, EZ2 twenty-one, EZ3 eight, EZ4 two and EZ5 fourteen. The accumulation curve did not reach saturation, so detections are incomplete and missing taxa may affect scores. The index uses assembled occurrences and literature-informed categories, not a controlled habitat experiment or an independently reanalysed time series. Applying it elsewhere requires local ecozones, inventories and national threat information, with suitable category changes. Two source-wide differences remain unexplained: the Results use 54 taxa while Table 4 says 55; for EZ3, maximum RABI is 38 in the Results and Table A1 but 33 in Table 7.
