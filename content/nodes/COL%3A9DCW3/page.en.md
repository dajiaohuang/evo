## Stem surface {#s-80c9fd6ed9}
The Brazilian Flora 2020 record lists the stem as hirsute or strigose—covered with coarse hairs or stiff hairs pressed against the surface—while separately describing hairs as patent, or spreading outward. It does not reconcile those descriptions. The record also lists simple hairs, thread-like unbranched bristles, and shield-shaped (peltate) glandular hairs; branched bristles, prickles, and short lateral shoots (brachyblasts) are absent.

## Leaves and inflorescences {#s-25c54115f2}
Stipules, small structures at leaf bases, are persistent. The record marks extrafloral nectaries (nectar-secreting glands outside flowers) and projections between pairs of pinnae (the leaf’s primary side divisions) as absent. The globose flower cluster has floral bracts (small leaf-like structures) longer than the flowers before they open (anthesis).

## Flowers {#s-78bb06a1d7}
The perianth (calyx and corolla together) is recorded as having four parts. The membranous calyx, the outer sepal whorl, has triangular lobes. Corolla (petal-whorl) lobes have one to three veins and no hairs. The androecium (the stamens collectively) is isostemonous, with stamens equal in number to the petals; the filaments are pink.

## Habitat category {#s-bf9d997d0f}
The regional dataset labels the habitat “Terrícola,” Portuguese for terrestrial. This category does not give a collection locality or establish the species’ full range.
