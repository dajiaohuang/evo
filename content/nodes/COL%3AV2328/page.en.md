## Accepted name and placement {#s-6de4292d4e}
COL26.8 accepts Stefania upuigmae Kok, 2024 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Hemiphractidae (family) → Stefania (genus). ## A dated description resolves a former satelles label {#s-e0c548e28e}
The 2024 paper describes Stefania upuigmae from Upuigma-tepui, formerly included under satelles. Earlier multilocus work referred to it as Stefania sp.5; the present description uses external morphology, preserved material and microCT comparisons. It does not newly sequence all populations or certify every historical specimen under the old name. The authors emphasize that closely related summit frogs can look very similar, so colour or one anatomical character alone is insufficient for identifying all records.

## The sex and maturity of the descriptive sample matter {#s-503b0c024f}
The description is based on one adult female holotype, IRSNB4222, with preserved snout–vent length53.1mm. The male, population range and developmental series are not represented by this specimen. The diagnosis combines a tuberculate dorsum, granular venter, basal toe webbing, enlarged outer tubercle and other characters; these are observations of the holotype rather than measured frequencies across the species. Two possible historical specimens were not examined.

## Live and preserved colours have different evidence {#s-5bfb60ce64}
The living holotype had metallic greenish-blue markings, a heavily mottled venter and a uniformly brown/copper iris. After almost twelve years in ethanol, those metallic markings disappeared and the ventral face became creamy brown. These are two conditions of the same specimen, not two species or a measured frequency of colour morphs. Some diagnostic contrasts use other species’ descriptions, and osteology is conserved enough that the authors find no unambiguous full-skeleton differences among the closest species.

## Known summit records and candidate extensions stay separate {#s-3b59a17847}
Stefania upuigmae is reported from the summit of Upuigma-tepui at 2,134m in Bolívar State, Venezuela. This is the examined locality scope, not a continuous elevational tolerance curve or an independently updated complete range. Two Caracas museum specimens previously called satelles are suggested as possible upuigmae, but the author could not examine them. Exact collection coordinates are not repeated in this reader.

## A short observation period does not establish absence of brooding {#s-50a1e778f0}
The female holotype was found jumping in low vegetation during the day, apparently disturbed by collectors. It carried no eggs or juveniles when collected in the rainy season. A disturbed daytime encounter does not establish a diurnal activity pattern, and a single non-brooding female does not prove absence of back-brooding, care or seasonal reproduction. Egg counts, development times and care success were not measured for this species.

## The 2024 CR proposal has range and threat assumptions {#s-cbab712109}
The authors propose Critically Endangered under criteriaB1 andB2a,b, using very small known occurrence, a single location and projected decline from fires and climate change. They give threshold conditions EOO<100km² andAOO<10km² and describe summit occurrence smaller than3km². These quantities are not interchangeable measured areas; the proposal is not a verified current official Red List entry. Population size and the number needed to maintain fitness remain unknown, so microendemism is not itself a measured decline rate.
