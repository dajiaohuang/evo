## Overview {#s-fff2ce69dc}
COL26.8 lists seven accepted species in *Tamarinus*: *T. inustus*, *T. subgrisescens*, *T. labiatus*, *T. mystax*, *T. kulina*, *T. pileatus* and *T. imperator*. A 2022 multilocus analysis proposed Tamarinus for the inustus and mystax groups; a 2023 genomic study supported that framework and distinguished *T. imperator* from *T. subgrisescens*. A separate 2023 taxonomic review reassessed mystax, pileatus and kulina. Higher-level combinations differ among authorities.

## Classification {#s-84a50624b0}
The local MDD v2.5 sidecar marks the exact-name join as unmatched for all seven COL IDs; several sources use the genus *Saguinus* or older subspecies combinations. This records a failed exact join between two local taxonomic snapshots, not a biological synonymy decision. The pages retain the fixed COL26.8 IDs and do not substitute identity based on an epithet or older combination.

## Choose a reading path by research question {#s-80ea65e159}
Start with the single-group Caquetá observations and seasonal Amanã surveys for *T. inustus*, comparing lower-forest foraging with new flooded-forest records. Then read the four-group scent-mark study of *T. mystax* and the Upper Urucu foraging study published under the older name *S. mystax pileatus*. Continue with the Pando mixed-group study of red-bellied tamarins and saddle-backs, then the Bolivian records later assigned to *T. subgrisescens*. Finish by comparing the sample and taxonomic evidence for the emperor-tamarin lineages, then read the single-group artificial-feeding experiment for *T. imperator* in Rio Branco to distinguish taxonomic comparison from local route choice. Follow with the description of *T. kulina* and genus-level systematics. This path connects evidence types; it is not a complete genus review.

## Scope of the evidence {#s-564dc07bf4}
This introduction is limited to the seven accepted species in pinned COL26.8. Exact-name joins to the local MDD v2.5 sidecar are unmatched for all seven; this is not used to infer biological taxonomy. Common names follow source usage or editorial display choices; Chinese renderings are editorial.
