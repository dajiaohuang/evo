## Captive water-filled holes and male attendance {#s-3e4b5e4dea}
At Mitsinjo’s facility, frogs identified morphologically as Anodonthyla pollicaris laid eggs in water-filled cavities, especially bamboo tubes and also film canisters. Males remained with eggs and larvae through metamorphosis. The discussion reported breeding mainly during December–May under ambient seasonal conditions in Andasibe, rather than a temperature-manipulation experiment. The authors observed guarding but did not demonstrate additional care behaviours or isolate its effect on survival. These are captive observations, not proof that every wild breeding event uses bamboo, that the species has no other season elsewhere, or that every founder’s identity was genetically verified.

## Nonfeeding larvae and an observed twenty-day minimum {#s-c1725c4e2e}
The study treated pollicaris larvae as endotrophic, relying on yolk rather than supplied external food, and allowed them to develop in the adult enclosure. The shortest reported interval from egg laying to completed metamorphosis was twenty days. This minimum is not a mean, a universal schedule or a developmental thermal limit. Many egg masses were culled for facility capacity, so the study’s retained-development observations do not constitute unrestricted survival estimates for every clutch. Founder identification was morphological only; the general absence of genetic checks remains a boundary on assigning the captive observations to a certified contemporary concept.
