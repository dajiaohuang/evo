These evidence sets address different questions and do not form a complete species account.

## Evidence at different scales {#s-49ec01992f}
The sources cover paternal responsiveness and infant outcomes in one captive colony, Brazil's passive rabies reports, and a model-based estimate from mitochondrial lineages. They do not establish wild life history, species-wide ecology, a complete distribution, nuclear-genome divergence or a current conservation category.

## From adult appearance to sampled lineages {#s-cb2fcd3ee4}
Adult coat comparisons, surveillance records and genetic samples each have distinct limits. The sections below retain those boundaries: coat traits overlap, occurrence maps reflect compiled records, colony results are not wild estimates, and the mitochondrial date applies to sampled lineages.

## Adult coat traits in a comparative sample {#s-498252cd53}
From 2015 to 2019, researchers compared 30 adult C. jacchus with 209 adult Callithrix from wild and captive sources. They described grey-tipped crown hairs, a grey chin, white ear tufts, a forehead star and dark or grey dorsal bands over orange underfur. Means of 13 traits did not differ significantly from C. penicillata in this sample; the description is not a stand-alone field test.

## Native and introduced records in the compilation {#s-ff15b76332}
The presence-only compilation classified northeastern Brazil's coastal and inland records as native, and records in S?o Paulo, Paran?, Rio de Janeiro and Minas Gerais as introductions. Observed records are separate from modeled habitat suitability. The compilation cannot define the complete range or show absence where no record was collected.

## Paternal responses in one captive colony {#s-0ba8ea2b6d}
At a Wisconsin research colony, investigators tested 24 experienced fathers' responses to infant distress calls and followed 62 offspring. Offspring of fathers classed as responsive were more likely to survive beyond one month (p = 0.002); group weights also differed at weeks 28 and 30. This is a captive-colony association, not a wild survival estimate or proof of causation.

## Brazilian passive rabies reports, 2008?2020 {#s-58d10a25d4}
Brazil's Ministry of Health recorded 67 reported outbreaks, 91 cases and 41 municipalities involving common marmosets from January 2008 through October 2020. The study also reported spillover of a Desmodus rotundus antigenic variant in northern and southern reporting areas. These surveillance records do not measure prevalence across the species' range.

## Maternal haplotypes in 18 putative hybrids {#s-2d4713fc85}
At four southeastern Brazilian localities, five of 18 free-living marmosets with putative hybrid appearance carried a C. jacchus maternal COX2 haplotype. The authors discuss hybridization involving threatened native C. aurita and introduced congeners as a conservation concern. This sample does not estimate hybrid frequency, population effects or formal status.

## A mitochondrial estimate for sampled lineages {#s-1414f0b020}
One study combined 49 newly sequenced Callithrix mitogenomes with published sequences. Its model placed sampled C. jacchus and Caatinga C. penicillata lineages as sisters, with an estimated divergence near 0.51 million years ago. This is a mitochondrial-lineage estimate, not a nuclear species tree or a definitive species-origin date.

## Limits of the selected evidence {#s-71e72b48a7}
These sources do not synthesize wild diet, habitat use, locomotion, body size, lifespan, complete distribution or population trend. They also provide no current conservation assessment; each result remains tied to its sample, place, period and method.
