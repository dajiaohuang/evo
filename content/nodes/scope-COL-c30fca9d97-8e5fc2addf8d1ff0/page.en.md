## atlas-profile / overview {#s-8703cc74fd}
The pinned COL26.8 checklist records accepted usage 697NS as Callithrix jacchus (Linnaeus, 1758). The studies gathered here ask narrower questions: how adults’ coats compare, what Brazilian occurrence and rabies reports record, what one captive colony found about fathers and infants, and how sampled mitochondrial lineages relate. Together they are a set of evidence windows, not a complete account of the species.

## atlas-profile / evidenceSummary {#s-49ec01992f}
The evidence spans a captive-colony association between paternal responsiveness and infant outcomes, Brazil’s passive rabies reports, and a model-based mitochondrial-lineage estimate. None establishes wild life history or species-wide ecology, a complete distribution, a nuclear-genome divergence date or formal conservation status.

## Body, life and relationships {#s-cb2fcd3ee4}
White ear tufts and a pale forehead star stand out in descriptions of adult common marmosets. Neither feature alone separates every close relative: coat patterns overlap in comparative samples and hybrids. The studies below move from appearance to care, reported Brazilian records and mitochondrial relationships, each at a different scale.

## A coat pattern described from adult samples {#s-498252cd53}
From 2015 to 2019, a Brazilian study compared 30 adult C. jacchus with 209 adult Callithrix specimens drawn from both wild and captive sources. It described grey-tipped crown hairs, a grey chin, white ear tufts, a distinct forehead star and dark or grey dorsal bands over orange underfur. Yet the means of 13 measured traits did not differ significantly from C. penicillata in that sample. The description helps compare adults; it is not a stand-alone field test, especially where hybrids have overlapping coats.

## Native and introduced records in Brazil {#s-ff15b76332}
In the study’s presence-only compilation, northeastern Brazil’s coastal and inland records were treated as native, while records from São Paulo, Paraná, Rio de Janeiro and Minas Gerais were classified as introductions. Those observed locations are distinct from modeled habitat suitability. Together they describe the compiled records, not the species’ complete range, and they cannot establish absence from places with no record.

## A captive study of fathers and infant outcomes {#s-0ba8ea2b6d}
At a Wisconsin research colony, investigators tested how 24 experienced fathers responded to infant distress calls and followed 62 offspring. Infants of fathers classed as responsive were more likely to survive beyond one month (p = 0.002); the paper also reported differences in group weights at weeks 28 and 30. Because this was one captive-colony study, the result is an association—not a wild survival estimate or proof that the response caused the outcome.

## What Brazilian rabies surveillance can show {#s-58d10a25d4}
Brazil’s Ministry of Health passive surveillance logged 67 rabies outbreaks affecting common marmosets—91 cases in 41 municipalities—from January 2008 through October 2020. The study also reported spillover of a Desmodus rotundus antigenic variant to C. jacchus in northern and southern reporting areas. These are reported outbreaks and a documented pathogen interaction, not a measure of rabies prevalence across the species’ range.

## A conservation question involving introduced relatives {#s-2d4713fc85}
Among 18 free-living marmosets with putative hybrid appearance from four southeastern Brazilian localities, five carried a maternal COX2 haplotype of C. jacchus. The authors discuss hybridization between threatened native C. aurita and introduced congeners, including the common marmoset, as a conservation concern. This small sample does not estimate hybridization frequency in C. jacchus, quantify population effects or establish the common marmoset’s conservation status.

## A mitochondrial lineage estimate, not a species origin date {#s-1414f0b020}
Combining 49 newly sequenced Callithrix mitogenomes with published sequences, one study estimated that its sampled C. jacchus and Caatinga C. penicillata lineages were sisters that diverged about 0.51 million years ago. That date belongs to the sampled mitochondrial lineages and model. It is neither a nuclear-genome species tree nor a definitive date for the origin of C. jacchus.

## atlas-profile / readerLimitations {#s-71e72b48a7}
These linked studies cover selected source-backed topics, not a full species review. They do not provide a synthesis of wild diet, habitat use, locomotion, body size or lifespan, and they do not supply a current conservation assessment or population trend.
