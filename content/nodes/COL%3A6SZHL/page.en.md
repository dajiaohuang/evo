## Gill findings in a laboratory exposure study {#s-63bc000104}
Adult Japanese medaka from one laboratory colony were exposed in a dedicated room to polyester or polypropylene microfibers at 10,000 fibers per litre for 21 days. Conditions were 24 °C and a 14:10 light:dark cycle. Histology (microscopic examination of tissue) was performed on six fish per treatment. The study reports fusion of secondary gill lamellae (small folds on the gills) in 33% of fish exposed to polyester and 67% exposed to polypropylene; no fusion was observed in controls. Some control gills also showed minor bleeding spots (petechiae) and lifting of the surface tissue layer. The article does not report calendar dates or a wild collection locality for the experimental fish. These findings describe one colony under the reported laboratory exposure, not wild-population responses, effects at lower environmental exposures, or a species-wide response. The concentration used is not an environmental threshold.
