## Read the study {#s-b88f0fc094}
A satellite-tracking study in northern Australia moved five adult male saltwater crocodiles 100–320 km from their capture sites. Their sea movements were generally directed toward the original sites, but none completed the return around Cobourg Peninsula. One control animal later made a roughly 900 km journey over six months before returning. The result shows that translocation outcomes depend on geographic barriers and individual variation; it is not a fixed movement rule for all saltwater crocodiles.

## Distinguish the measured length {#s-9f88404ba7}
Recorded dimensions are female maximum snout–vent length (SVL) 3100 mm; reported maximum total length (TL) 6000 mm. SVL runs from snout to vent and differs from total length including the tail. Compiled maxima are reported material, not theoretical growth limits or typical individual size. Sex-specific values may come from different localities or studies and alone cannot quantify population sexual dimorphism.

## Limits of these trait data {#s-a03a4422d3}
This account paraphrases the same-name ReptTraits v1-2 entry, retaining its 2023 taxonomic framework and sources for individual traits. One row per species compresses variation in sex, age, locality and populations; matching name and family do not ensure equivalent later concepts. Reported maximum length is not an absolute species limit, and reproductive means are not fixed counts in every clutch. Many mass entries are estimated from length equations and are not presented here as measured mass. Juvenile-length headers and definitions differ and are also omitted from reader conclusions. Conservation, venom, current distribution and population trends are not updated here; unrecorded fields remain gaps. Dataset citation labels preserve the source chain and do not imply that this project independently reread every original work.
