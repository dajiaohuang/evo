## A dated multicriteria conservation priority {#s-f40a936351}
In Table 4, the 2024 Chilean assessment gives Eupsophus vertebralis a conservation-priority index of 1.13 and places it in the low category. The study evaluates 58 selected species using literature, citizen-science records and government plans; it is not a new census of every population. The index combines distribution, threat, population-trend, taxonomic-uniqueness and body-size scores with a protected-area term. It is a management ranking under this model, not an extinction probability, a current IUCN assessment or a direct population-size measurement. “Low” does not mean the species is safe or needs no conservation.

## Paired-stage comparisons do not establish complete developmental independence {#s-300e2b22f3}
Across the sampled species, larval–adult shape dissimilarities have a weak but significant Mantel association (r 0.16, p 0.003)—a test of correspondence between distance matrices—while disparity correlations vary among families. The authors interpret the overall pattern as support for adaptive decoupling, but it does not show zero correlation, genetic independence, an experimentally selected trait or Eupsophus vertebralis’s own developmental trajectory. Phylogenetic analyses use 74 of the 83 sampled species. Reconstructed ancestral shape and timing are model results from living-species data, not new measurements of fossil morphology. For adults, K 0.44 with p 0.001 indicates less similarity among close relatives than the reference expectation. The paper’s phrase “not phylogenetically structured” therefore cannot be read to mean that the test is nonsignificant. Supplement S3 repeats family N totals of 112 for both stages; without the raw matrices, the adult individual denominator and imputed cells remain uncertified.
