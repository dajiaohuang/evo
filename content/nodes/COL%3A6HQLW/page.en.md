## Form and flowers {#s-35e30851cf}
The regional flora describes a slender, cormous geophyte 15–60 cm tall, with stems partly exposed and red-spotted below. The lower leaves have long, linear blades. Flowers are described as greenish, blue, or yellow, with a sweet or spicy scent; the anther lobes eventually diverge, and the capsules are beaked.

## Diagnostic details and comparison {#s-542a4866e4}
A separate SANBI account distinguishes the long, linear basal leaves from the shorter stem leaves and describes the stem as usually partly exposed. The cataphylls and lowest leaf sheath have a red flush and white speckling. Large flowers have a deep, relatively narrow floral cup, 17–20 mm deep and about 12 mm wide at the mouth. They are usually turquoise-grey to pale slate-blue, with a pale-yellow nectar guide lightly flecked darker on the outer tepal limbs. The account describes a faint sweet scent, often with almond, cinnamon, or vanilla overtones, as unique in the genus.

The account notes confusion with *Ferraria parva*, which has a similarly shaped narrow floral cup but is much smaller: its cup is about 6 mm deep and its outer tepals 18–22 mm long, compared with (30–)35–40 mm in *F. ferrariola*. *F. parva* sheaths and cataphylls also lack the red flush and white speckling described for *F. ferrariola*.
