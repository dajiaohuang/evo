## Overview {#s-19fbc76a99}
COL26.8 accepts Chiropotes sagulatus (Traill, 1821) under fixed taxon ID 5Y6L6. The Mammal Diversity Database uses “Guianan Bearded Saki” and summarizes its range as the Guianas and northern Brazil north of the Amazon and east of the Rio Branco. Its taxonomic notes describe C. sagulatus as split from C. chiropotes and relate the name to populations formerly attributed to C. israelita. Those notes record a taxonomic treatment; the page identity here remains the COL26.8 ID. The Chinese display name is editorial.

## Feeding research {#s-cb5d08d78c}
In a long-term study in continuous forest in Guyana, Shaffer recorded a diet spanning more than 175 plant species. Seeds made up 75% of the annual diet in that study. During the leanest fruit months, insects accounted for almost 40% of feeding time, alongside shifts toward mature fruit and flowers. These figures describe the studied Guyana population and period, not a range-wide average.

## Social behavior and organization {#s-7c015effaf}
A separate 15-month Guyana study collected 560 hours of observations on one free-ranging group. The group included at least 65 individuals but formed fluid subgroups, with a reported mean group size of 39 ± 10. Travel and feeding together accounted for about 70% of diurnal activity; males formed 65% of grooming dyads and had another male as nearest neighbor 66% of the time. These are observations of one group, not fixed species-wide values.

## Habitat use {#s-f0b01a36d8}
A 2023 study followed one group in an isolated 13-ha forest remnant at Brazil’s Biological Dynamics of Forest Fragments Project, a patch described as less than 3% of the species’ typical regional home range. Researchers recorded locations and visible behavior every five minutes to examine travel paths, canopy height, and distance from the forest edge. This unusually small patch is a distinct research setting; its observations should not be merged with continuous-forest results or generalized to the species.

## Evidence limits {#s-7c4367430e}
This source-linked introductory guide draws on studies from Guyana and one unusually small Brazilian forest remnant. Local diet, grouping, activity, and movement evidence does not establish species-wide averages, a complete range account, population size, or trend.
