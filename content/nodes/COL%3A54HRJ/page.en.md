## evolution {#s-03e5eeeb54}
Zebra finches are widely used to study learned song. Warren and colleagues' 2010 genome paper described the species as a model for vocal learning and analyzed gene expression relevant to the brain and song; this is context for the research, not a comprehensive field study of wild song behavior.

## evolution {#s-5f2f78dced}
The study sequenced and assembled a male zebra finch genome: about 1.2 billion bases in the draft assembly, with about 1.0 billion assigned to 33 chromosomes and three linkage groups. It also compared forebrain gene expression at 50 days after hatching, during the juvenile song-learning period, and at 850 days in adults; these two ages do not represent a complete developmental trajectory.

## evolution {#s-77267cca45}
Comparison with the chicken genome found broadly conserved synteny and karyotype, alongside many intrachromosomal rearrangements. This conclusion came from a comparison of two bird lineages at the time; it is neither a complete inventory of traits shared by all birds nor a substitute for later comparisons with more species and improved assemblies.

## Different stages in the mass fields {#s-74b10f6821}
Historical summary values are adult body mass 11.93 g; mass at birth or hatching 0.58 g. Adult mass can combine males, females and adults of unspecified sex; birth/hatching mass describes a different life stage. These are not a longitudinal series of the same individuals or a species-wide range of individual masses.

## Reproduction and developmental timing {#s-0bebd9a5a5}
Available compilation fields are female maturity field (defined by the source as age at first conception) 60 d; offspring per litter or eggs per clutch 4.5 offspring/eggs; oviposition to hatching 14.15 d; age at flight capability (leaving the nest in some source material) 21 d; d denotes days. The litter/clutch field combines offspring or egg counts, and a fractional summary does not mean a single event produced a fractional individual. Timing fields refer to different events and cannot be exchanged for one uniform reproductive cycle.

## Relative weights of food categories {#s-37f742a9d1}
The archive’s nonzero food weights are seeds, nuts and spores included in this category 100%. Source code A indicates high compiler confidence in the source and diet information. These percentages standardize verbal descriptions rather than measure diet composition; zero does not prove an item is never eaten. Categories such as Nect, Seed and PlantO are broader than their everyday names and cannot imply exclusively nectar, seeds or plants.

## Foraging strata differ from whole-day activity {#s-53ec3710ea}
Foraging-stratum weights flagged as species-level in the archive are in or just above the canopy 80%; trees or high bushes from 2 m upward but below the canopy 20%. They estimate relative use of feeding positions, not nest sites, all habitat or whole-day time budgets. Surface-water, understory, mid/high, canopy and aerial categories have different source definitions; they are not elevational ranges and cannot yield flight heights or habitat area.
