## Accepted name and placement {#s-1af98dd8e1}
COL26.8 accepts Ptychadena cooperi (Parker, 1930) as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Ptychadenidae (family) → Ptychadena (genus). This name and placement follow the COL26.8 release of 20 August 2026; the same spelling in another checklist or fossil database may refer to a different taxonomic concept.

## A geographically sampled mitochondrial and nuclear dataset {#s-89a37841cd}
The 2023 Ethiopian Highlands study lists 57 mitochondrial samples and 27 nuclear-SNP samples for Ptychadena cooperi in Table 1, with mitochondrial nucleotide diversity π=3.47%. Samples across the twelve-species study were collected in 2011–2019; these are analysed specimens, not a population census. All assembled mitochondrial genomes had the usual two rRNAs, thirteen protein-coding genes and twenty-two tRNAs, but no species had a completely resolved control region because repeats could not be confidently assembled from short reads. The phylogenetic alignment used selected genes (13,937 bp across 303 samples), rather than an alignment of complete control regions.

## A study-defined highland clade and regional distribution {#s-04280ec016}
The paper places Ptychadena cooperi in its P. cooperi clade and reports it from the both sides of the Great Rift Valley, south of the Blue Nile valley. These distributions combine the authors' geographic sampling and earlier range work; they are not independently updated complete range polygons. The twelve-species highland clade is a subset of Ptychadena. Its mitochondrial-clock analysis estimated a common ancestor around 11.7 million years ago, with a reported interval of 8.827–15.494 million years. The east-of-rift ancestral reconstruction had a model probability of 69%; neither that estimate nor inferred crossings is a directly observed migration event or a nuclear-genome divergence date for this species.

## A dated reassessment argument, with remaining demographic gaps {#s-394a216972}
The 2023 discussion calls for conservation assessment or reassessment using revised species concepts and genetic and geographic evidence. Earlier names and broad distributions can combine lineages that later work separated. The present genomic sample does not itself measure current abundance, fragmentation, decline or reproductive success in Ptychadena cooperi. Any Red List category mentioned in this paper belongs to its dated discussion, not a verified current official listing. Regional land-use and climate concerns support further investigation but do not quantify this species' current threat level.

## Four mitochondrial groups, three nuclear clusters {#s-725743344b}
Mitochondrial data separated Guru, Bale and Arsi populations east of the rift and a fourth western group. Nuclear PCA with approximately 1.25 million SNPs recovered the western group and two eastern groups, without separating Bale and Arsi. This partial agreement describes the sampled data; four mitochondrial groups are not four accepted species or a demonstrated absence of gene flow.
