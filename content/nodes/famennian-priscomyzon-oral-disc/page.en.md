## An oral disc and a later growth series {#reading-introduction}
The Devonian Priscomyzon holotype preserves an oral disc and branchial structures. A later study examined a growth series from the same genus, adding evidence about early life stages. The original description and the later interpretation remain separate accounts, rather than a complete modern lamprey life cycle.

## The oral disc preserved in one holotype {#s-2e4dc30693}
The holotype AM5750 of *Priscomyzon riniensis* is a little over 4.2 cm long. Gess, Coates and Rubidge's 2006 description places it at Waterloo Farm near Grahamstown, South Africa, in the Famennian Witpoort Formation of the Witteberg Group. They describe a large oral disc with a soft outer lip, supported by annular cartilage, and a circular mouth. Around it are 14 simple circumoral teeth; the posterior teeth appear more elongated than the others. The paper also describes a preserved branchial basket. These are observations of one fossil, not a preserved feeding act.

A later study placed the holotype beside larval and juvenile *Priscomyzon* specimens, including hatchling AM5813 and juvenile AM7538. In that sampled growth series, the larvae lack the defining traits of modern ammocoetes and instead show prominent eyes, a cusped feeding apparatus and posteriorly united branchial baskets. This is a genus-specific fossil series, not a life-history template for every lamprey.

The comparison sharpens a question about lamprey history: was the filter-feeding ammocoete an ancient vertebrate stage or a later lamprey specialization? From comparisons across four Palaeozoic stem-lamprey genera, the 2021 authors argue that the ammocoete stage arose later. Separately, the 2006 authors interpreted Priscomyzon's oral structures as evidence of a specialized parasitic-feeding adaptation. The holotype preserves anatomy, not a feeding act or evidence of migration.
