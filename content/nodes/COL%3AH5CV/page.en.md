## Repeat annotations are computational observations {#s-283478b7de}
The authors built repeat libraries for each species with RepeatModeler2, used RFSB to classify unknown elements, added vertebrate repeats and then applied RepeatMasker. The paper calls matches “insertions,” but a match alone does not show that an element is complete, expressed or mobile. A superfamily label can combine different families. Element counts, total occupied length and share of the genome are different quantities, and overlapping hierarchy categories cannot be added as independent fractions. No new repeat annotation or expression experiment was performed for Ascaphus truei here.

## Across-species correlations do not prove this species mechanism {#s-fa32c701d7}
The study compared 61 assemblies using models adjusted for relatedness among species, an earlier pruned tree, contig-N50 covariates and multiple-comparison correction. Within that sample, sequences associated with recently active Ty3/DIRS1 transposable elements (TEs) correlate with genome size. “Recent activity” is inferred from sequence divergence; it is not direct observation of ongoing transposition in Ascaphus truei. Length and open-reading-frame similarity filters do not demonstrate protein expression either. The authors' suggested developmental constraints are comparative hypotheses, not evidence that TEs cause this species' larval duration or clutch size.
