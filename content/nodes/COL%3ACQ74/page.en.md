## Gland types describe structure rather than chemical identity {#s-89e780c032}
Type I glands are chiefly alveolar, with a small lumen, a single layer of secretory cells and an incomplete myoepithelial covering; they lie near the epidermis. Type II glands have a more tubular-alveolar or tear-shaped secretory portion and are restricted to the swollen part of finger IV in the examined dendrobatids. They may be interspersed among type I glands or lie beneath them; that arrangement varies among individuals. The definitions combine morphology and histochemical reactions; they do not identify a particular secreted molecule or demonstrate a physiological effect.

## A proposed reproductive role remains untested {#s-3263269cb0}
The authors suggest that protein-containing specialized hand glands could mediate chemical courtship signals during contact with a female's head. They did not characterize the secretions, identify a pheromone, test female responses or establish whether delivery is olfactory or transdermal. Hand location and stain reactions therefore cannot certify that Ameerega parvula uses a particular chemical mating mechanism, causes ovulation or oviposition, or secretes an adhesive. A lack of these gland types likewise does not prove a species lacks every form of chemical communication. Behavioral observations cited in the discussion come from earlier studies and populations, not new mating experiments in this histological comparison.
