## Overview {#s-1699860d65}
COL26.8 accepts Simias concolor (4XF7W); the Mammal Diversity Database gives “Pig-tailed Langur” as its preferred English common name. Hadi and colleagues describe the species as endemic to the Mentawai Islands. The Chinese display name 豚尾叶猴 is editorial.

## Social behavior and organization {#s-e098706d1b}
A 2009 study described three free-ranging groups on Siberut. Their mean group size was 8.7 ± 1.1 individuals, with an adult male:female ratio averaging 1:3. The authors considered polygyny the most likely mating system, but three groups are too small a sample to treat these values as species-wide constants.

## Habitat comparisons {#s-b3696199ed}
In mixed primary forest in northern Siberut, GPS and scan/focal observations covered three Simias groups and two sympatric Presbytis potenziani groups. Simias used lower canopy levels (<20 m) more often and fed predominantly on leaves; the reported Pianka dietary-overlap index between the sampled species was 0.32. These are local interspecific comparisons, not a range-wide habitat or diet profile.

## Breeding {#s-09edeadf3a}
At the undisturbed Pungut site on Siberut, researchers recorded 11 births in eight groups over two years. Births occurred in seven months in that sample; the authors cautioned that sample sizes were very small. The result challenges an earlier report of births only in June and July at that site, but does not by itself establish timing across the species’ range.

## Diet, activity and foraging-stratum fields {#s-5ac4610211}
Simias concolor is accepted in pinned COL26.8 within Cercopithecidae. The EltonTraits 1.0 entry has 3 literature-derived ecological field groups; these summarize categories rather than direct measurements and do not establish equivalence to the current COL26.8 concept. The source follows earlier bird or MSW3 mammal taxonomy; a name match does not resolve modern splits or synonym disputes.

## Relative weights of food categories {#s-03a2f34425}
The archive’s nonzero food weights are other plant material (the source category also includes fungi) 80%; fruit 10%; seeds, nuts and spores included in this category 10%. Code ABC pools high, reasonable and partial confidence; some estimates can borrow from an ecologically similar congener. ABC cannot be rewritten as direct observations of this species. These percentages standardize verbal descriptions rather than measure diet composition; zero does not prove an item is never eaten. Categories such as Nect, Seed and PlantO are broader than their everyday names and cannot imply exclusively nectar, seeds or plants.

## Mammalian foraging-stratum assignment {#s-8a842d70e9}
The entry is assigned Ar: arboreal; code A records compiler confidence in source and assignment. Metadata collectively attributes mammalian stratum assignments to Nowak (1999). This is a category assignment, not a percentage of time among strata, and does not mean the species acts only there.

The archive marks feeding activity during daytime as 1. Several fields can be 1 together; they are not proportions adding to a daily time budget or schedules shared by every day, season and site. Activity certainty is ABC and can still use ecologically similar congeneric material; genus/family typical activity values do not fill gaps here.

## Sources differ across field groups {#s-9b8959fd83}
The food, activity and mammalian foraging-stratum fields use separate source groups. A shared reference label in another dataset may refer to a different work; labels are not independent species observations.

## Evidence limits {#s-664bc1b7ea}
Ecological, social and reproductive evidence here comes from a small number of groups at Siberut sites and cannot establish range-wide averages, current population size or trend. The Chinese display name is editorial.
EltonTraits 1.0 records literature-derived diet, activity and mammalian foraging categories; percentages are standardized weights, not measured feeding time or intake. Mammal ABC scores pool confidence levels and may draw on ecologically similar congeners, so they are not direct species-level observations in every field. Historical names do not establish equivalence to the pinned COL26.8 species concept.
