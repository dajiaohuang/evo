## When seven colonies sound alike {#preview}
An eight-colony study found similar peak call frequencies in seven Miniopterus fuliginosus colonies, while one colony had higher frequencies. The authors link that outlier tentatively to local background noise and bat movement, not to a universal species-wide call pattern.

## When seven colonies sound alike {#field-study}
Zhang and colleagues compared echolocation-call peak frequencies from eight colonies of M. fuliginosus. Seven colonies had very similar peak frequencies; one colony was significantly higher. The abstract reports that acoustic distance did not significantly track morphology, climate, geographic distance, or mitochondrial DNA distance. These results describe among-colony variation in the sampled calls, not the call of every individual or population.

The authors suggest that frequent dispersal and male-mediated nuclear gene flow may help explain similarity among seven colonies, while stronger background noise may have selected for the higher calls in the outlying JXT colony. They also report a positive association between acoustic and nuclear-DNA genetic distance that disappears when JXT is excluded. Because only the abstract was available in this review, sample sizes, recording protocols, and the full analysis remain unchecked.
