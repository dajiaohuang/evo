## What the feeding records describe {#s-c49e3cdbcd}
EltonTraits 1.0 links the name *Lophozosterops javanicus* to records of food and feeding position. The name and family match the species accepted in COL26.8, but the dataset uses an older bird taxonomy; that match does not settle later splits or synonym disputes.

## Relative weights of food categories {#s-c9bccc1e68}
The nonzero food weights are fruit 40%, invertebrates 30%, and nectar, pollen and plant exudates 30%. The source assigns diet code A, indicating high compiler confidence in the source and diet entry. These percentages standardize verbal descriptions; they are not measured shares of meals or energy. A zero weight would mean that the record assigns no weight to a category, not that the bird never eats it. Source labels such as Nect, Seed and PlantO also cover broader categories than their everyday names suggest.

## Foraging strata differ from whole-day activity {#s-50d34c68af}
The source marks these feeding-position values as species-level: trees or high bushes from 2 m upward but below the canopy, 60%; understory, edges or shrubs below 2 m, 40%. They estimate relative use of feeding positions. They do not describe nest sites, the full habitat, or time spent there over the whole day. The source’s surface-water, understory, mid/high, canopy and aerial categories have different definitions; they are not elevation bands and cannot be used to estimate flight height or habitat area.

## Trace each field group to its source labels {#s-8878a8a0dc}
The food fields point to Ref_13, the *Handbook of the Birds of the World*; the feeding-position fields point to Ref_44, MacKinnon’s *A Field Guide to the Birds of China*. Each label belongs to a particular field group, and identical Ref numbers in separate bird and mammal tables need not identify the same work. These references show where the dataset’s descriptions came from; they are not additional observations or a new synthesis of the underlying books.

## Limits of the feeding record {#s-0e9583f985}
These are historical, same-name and same-family entries in EltonTraits 1.0, not a complete species account. The name match does not prove that the older record and the COL26.8 species represent identical species concepts. Food and feeding-position weights are coarse values standardized from literature descriptions, not direct measurements of stomach contents, intake, energy or observation time; the diet code also retains uncertainty about sources and proportions. Group-level values, non-species-level feeding strata, borrowed values in the wider archive, and records affected by later taxonomic splits, do not establish traits of this species. The cited books are source locators rather than a synthesis of the underlying studies. These entries do not establish current range, local or seasonal variation, population trends or conservation status.
