## Bulbine sedifolia: metallic leaves and a bristly neck {#s-44eacec693}
A South African regional account describes a geophyte 70–300 mm tall, with a small tuber and laterally spreading roots. Four to 20 succulent leaves arise in a tuft from a short bristly neck or a membranous sheath, usually up to 20 mm long and rarely up to 40 mm. The pale-green leaves have a grey metallic sheen and microscopic, elongated whitish micropapillae. As they wither, they curve inward from their acute tips. The lower margins usually have a few widely spaced, blunt hairs. Yellow flowers grow on a wiry raceme with a curved scape. The ellipsoidal capsule is about 3 mm long.

The account records quartz pebbles and sand pockets on granite boulders as habitats.
