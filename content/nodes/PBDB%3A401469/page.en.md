## Compare the two tapejaromorphs {#s-abd9260d58}
Place Keresdrakon beside [Caiuajara dobruskii](../../../Neoazhdarchia/Tapejaroidea/Tapejaridae/Tapejarinae/Caiuajara/Caiuajara_dobruskii/page.en.md). Caiuajara has a downturned rostrum and crest; the Keresdrakon rostrum is longer, while the rear crest cannot be assessed from its incomplete skull. The 2019 description treats shared bonebeds and diagnostic anatomy as separate evidence. Nearby bones connect a locality, but do not make every element the same taxon or individual. Co-occurrence also does not establish closest relatives.

## Compare the growth evidence {#s-ad372f09ec}
The linked [Vespersaurus paranaensis](../../../../../../../Dinosauromorpha/Dinosauriformes/Dinosauria/Theropoda/Neotheropoda/Averostra/Ceratosauria/Noasauridae/Noasaurinae/Vespersaurus/Vespersaurus_paranaensis/page.en.md) study uses growth marks, remodeling and outer bone tissue to estimate maturity. Keresdrakon has two histology samples, not the same age-retrocalculation series used for Vespersaurus. The neonatal assumptions and femoral–tibial disagreement belong to that dinosaur’s sample. Compare observed tissue with the growth interpretation before drawing ecological conclusions.

## The holotype from bonebed C {#s-6b4a0bd12d}
The 2019 description names CP.V 2069, held at CENPALEO in Brazil, as the Keresdrakon vilsoni holotype from bonebed C at Cruzeiro do Oeste. It preserves the front of the skull, an almost complete lower jaw, selected neck and back vertebrae, the sternum, parts of the shoulder and pelvis, and incomplete limbs. Except for the jaws, the bones were associated but not articulated. Most additional assigned fossils are isolated elements. Skulls and first wing phalanges support the authors’ minimum of three individuals; that is neither a count of all fossils nor a population estimate.

## What the skull and jaws preserve {#s-a5b92b26bf}
The described jaws have no teeth. The skull is low, with a long pointed rostrum; when the jaws close, they do not leave the gap described in tapejarines. No sagittal crest is preserved on the front of the skull before the nasoantorbital opening, but the rear skull is incomplete, so a more posterior crest remains unknown. A small, thick dentary crest lies below the jaw symphysis, and a short blunt ridge near the rear upper edge contributes to the diagnosis. The preserved rostrum, missing crest region and whole-head reconstruction have different evidential limits.

## Diagnostic features beyond the skull {#s-abd80ef92a}
The original diagnosis also includes a foramen on the front half of the underside of the first wing-finger joint and another on the outer surface of the ischium. Other comparisons involve large lateral air openings in the neck vertebrae, the position of openings near the upper humerus, and an asymmetric coracoid joint with the sternum. The authors caution that large subadult Caiuajara and smaller juvenile Keresdrakon can be difficult to distinguish from postcranial bones. Size alone cannot identify an isolated element, and crushing can hide openings.

## Neck vertebrae and the rest of the skeleton {#s-9d11edfd9d}
Some middle neck vertebrae are long, with tall neural spines and large openings at the sides. A broken vertebra shows a hollow interior and a neural canal enclosed in a bony tube supported by trabeculae. The holotype also includes a thin sternal plate, slender gastralia, pelvis and incomplete thigh and shin bones. Assigned first wing phalanges are slightly curved and lack a fused extensor tendon process; some came from different individuals. This material supports anatomical comparisons but does not reveal the full neck count, soft-tissue wing outline, flight speed or exact gait.

## What fusion can say about maturity {#s-e469501f3a}
The holotype has fused premaxillae, some jaw bones and the centra and neural arches of the preserved middle neck vertebrae. Its scapula and coracoid, the end of the right humerus and pelvic bones remain unfused. The 2019 authors place the specimen between OS2 and OS3 in their pterosaur growth framework, interpreting it as a late juvenile or subadult. Those comparative stages do not give an age in years, sex or reproductive maturity, and one holotype cannot stand for the adult skeleton of the species.

## What two bone samples show {#s-40e6974097}
The study sampled the shafts of ulna CP.V 2077 and incomplete CP.V 2374, tentatively identified as a first wing phalanx. Both are mainly fibrolamellar bone; open canals near the outer bone surface and little advanced remodeling indicate continued growth. The ulna is interpreted as juvenile but not neonatal, while the second fragment preserves one deep growth mark. The authors relate the tissue to rapid growth, but vascular arrangement alone cannot rank growth rates in Keresdrakon and Caiuajara. Two samples do not make a growth curve, and one mark is not a definite age.

## How the wingspan was estimated {#s-b753289e66}
Comparing humerus length with material from Tupuxuara and Caupedactylus led the authors to estimate a wingspan of about three metres. The span is reconstructed rather than measured from a complete preserved wing, and the fossils are interpreted as immature. It therefore does not give the species’ maximum adult size, flight range or prey-size limit.

## What shared bonebeds establish {#s-89e244e425}
The 2019 study reports Keresdrakon, Caiuajara and Vespersaurus together in bonebeds A, B and C. Repeated finds in the same beds support the authors’ interpretation that the animals coexisted in the area. Most bones are disarticulated, so the paper proposes deaths and exposure near an oasis followed by burial during high-energy events. Shared beds give a closer locality link than simply belonging to one formation, but do not prove deaths at the same moment, predation or that nearby bones belong to one individual.

## Ecological hypotheses and their limits {#s-515df2732d}
The long, flattened rostrum, sparse fossils and animals found at the site prompted hypotheses that Keresdrakon scavenged or opportunistically hunted small animals and was less gregarious than Caiuajara. Eating carcasses, eggs or young animals is proposed ecology, not preserved gut content or observed behavior. The small sample may also reflect collection, identification and burial processes. A reconstructed food web cannot by itself show solitary living or obligate scavenging.

## A matrix result and an age dispute {#s-7cab37e249}
When Keresdrakon was added to an existing character matrix, the 2019 analysis recovered six most-parsimonious trees and placed it as sister to Tapejaridae: a non-tapejarid branch within Tapejaromorpha. This is the result of that analysis, not a rerun or a consensus across later studies. The same paper argues for a local Goio-Erê exposure, discusses disagreement with a mapped Rio Paraná assignment, and retains both Early and Late Cretaceous age possibilities. The PBDB parent path and broad age bracket are separate from those published interpretations.
