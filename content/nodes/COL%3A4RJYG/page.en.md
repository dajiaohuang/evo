## Raphia australis: towering leaves and a branched inflorescence {#s-ff5a4e934d}
The 1969 regional account describes Raphia australis as a stout, unbranched tree up to 16 m tall. Its stem reaches 10 m and is covered with persistent, erect leaf bases pressed against it. Trunks are 30 cm in diameter; two often join at the base and lean, with aerial roots at the base.

Leaves form a rosette: younger ones are erect, while the outer leaves spread. They are pinnate and reach about 10 m long and 2 m wide; older leaves are described as eventually lopped or hanging. The petiole is unarmed, concave and clasping below; the source describes it as soft and light, not woody. The rachis narrows into a channelled stalk for about two thirds of its length. Its two spiny margins are unequal in height and gradually converge until they merge, with the lower ridge disappearing; the leaf bends outward at that point. Pinnae attach on the outer side of these ridges. They are linear, 45–65 cm long and 5 cm wide, folded below, pale green above and waxy below. Their margins bear small, sharp, ascending, pale spinules about 4 mm long. The midrib is raised and has two rows of spinules on its lower half, merging into one row above.

The apical inflorescence is erect, central above the leaf crown, and conical, reaching 3 m tall. Its lowest flowering branches arise in the axils of upper leaves. About 13 subsequent leaves subtend flowering branches and gradually become smaller; the uppermost is about 50 cm long. The peduncle is 11 cm in diameter at its base and covered by two-ranked, clasping, overlapping, tubular, hard bracts. The basal bract of each branch differs from those above it: it is flattened, has two keels, and is two-pronged like a lobster claw, up to 40 cm long and 6 cm wide. This form recurs on later side branches and becomes progressively smaller higher up. The following bracts are tubular, with a truncate apex forming a sharp point; the points alternate from side to side.

Branches project beyond the crown and are laterally compressed, in a near-four-ranked arrangement with two close side branches on each side of the rachis. They are erect at first and spread when in fruit. Their bracts resemble the peduncular bracts but are smaller. Side branches bear numerous short fertile spikes close together; these are also arranged in near-four ranks, with two close rows on each side of the compressed rachis. Fertile spikes spread and twist. Unisexual flowers occur in two rows: female flowers occupy the lower part of a spike and male flowers the upper part; the apical spikes bear only male flowers. Lower spikes reach 40 cm and become shorter toward the apex. Fertile bracts resemble the branch bracts but are smaller.

Male flowers have a two-keeled bracteole enclosing a small, tubular, truncate calyx. The corolla projects from it and has 3 hard, linear-falcate segments, nearly free to the base, 10 mm long and 2 mm wide, with triangular, slightly swollen tips. There are 6 free stamens; their swollen, converging filaments are 4 mm long, three-angled and apiculate. Anthers have parallel, linear locules 6 mm long.

Female flowers have a two-keeled bracteole and a second, short, tubular, truncate bracteole inside it. The calyx and corolla enlarge after fruit set and project beyond the bracteoles. The tubular calyx splits irregularly into 3 truncate lobes as the ovary enlarges; it is 8 mm long. The corolla has 3 hard, beaked segments. A flattened staminal ring is attached to the corolla and has unequal blunt teeth, some bearing minute anthers. The ovary has 3 cells and 1 ovule; its scales are minute and fringed. There are 3 sessile, apical, erect, tongue-shaped stigmas. At anthesis they show through a small apical opening in the corolla before it enlarges.

The fruit is one-seeded and ellipsoid, 6–9 cm long and 3–5 cm in diameter. It is hard, shiny, yellow-brown and shortly beaked. Its reflexed, convex scales form 6 vertical rows; the median groove is shallow, and the edges have a short golden fringe of hairs.
