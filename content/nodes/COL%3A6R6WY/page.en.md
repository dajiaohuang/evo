## Matayba ingifolia {#s-ed91b4d5cf}
The Flora of Panama describes *Matayba ingifolia* as a small tree with coarse reddish-brown hairs on its leaf stalks and rachises, small flowers, and densely hairy immature capsules. Its range statement is regional: western Panama, with Costa Rica mentioned only as a possibility.

## A small tree with hairy leaf axes {#s-fe8331078f}
The account describes trees to about 8 m tall. Young stems are cylindrical and covered with rusty hairs, becoming less hairy with age. The pinnate leaves usually carry long, coarse, reddish-brown persistent hairs on the petiolules and rachises—the short stalks of individual leaflets and the central leaf axis. Each leaf has two to four pairs of lance-shaped or narrowly lance-shaped-oblong leaflets, 3–15 cm long and 1.3–5 cm wide. Their tips are long-pointed, their bases range from blunt to acute, and their margins are entire. The leaflets are thin-textured and described as hairless on both surfaces, although some have hairs on the strongly raised upper midrib and along the major veins beneath. Axillary panicles are racemiform and 3–10 cm long; their 2–3 mm pedicels have a joint in the lower third and sparse hairs below it.

## Small flowers and densely hairy young capsules {#s-cc9234bf76}
Flowers are about 4 mm long. The bowl-shaped calyx is 2.5–3 mm across and divided nearly to its base; its small lobes are sticky-hairy outside and become hairless inside. The white, narrow-oblong petals are about 2 mm long. Each has a scale nearly as long as the petal, joined to its lower third and split deeply at the point of attachment into two narrow, spatula-shaped, fringed and long-haired parts. The plate-shaped floral disc has a thin, wavy margin; one edge turns upward into two semicircular lobes. The lower half of the stamens is long-haired. In staminate flowers the stamens are 3–3.5 mm long, the anthers are small and broader than long, and the minute pistil is densely hairy. Bisexual flowers are not known from this account. Immature capsules are sessile, two-lobed and two-chambered, subglobose and up to 12 mm long; they are densely covered with reddish-brown hairs outside and long silky hairs inside. The account distinguishes the plant from *M. glaberrima* by the brown-hairy fruits and the coarse brownish hairs on the petioles and leaf rachises.

## A bounded western Panama range statement {#s-1a29c7f04e}
The Flora of Panama says the species is known from wetter middle-elevation regions on the Caribbean slope in western Panama, and may also occur in Costa Rica. The latter is stated as a possibility, not a confirmed occurrence in this excerpt. This is a historical regional flora statement, not a current or global range inventory. COL26.8 anchors the accepted species name used for this page; the regional WFO-linked account does not establish full equivalence between taxonomic concepts across releases.
