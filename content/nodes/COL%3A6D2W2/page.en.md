## Name and classification {#s-f53e635b0d}
The COL26.8 release of 20 August 2026 accepts *Diasporus amirae* Arias, Chaves, Salazar, Salazar-Zúñiga & García-Rodríguez, 2019 as a species in Anura, family Eleutherodactylidae, genus *Diasporus*. The 2019 description recognized the frog as a distinct lineage using mitochondrial 16S and COI sequences together with morphological, morphometric and acoustic evidence. The linked morphology v3 record places Brachycephaloidea in its family field; that source label is retained separately rather than treated as a revised classification.

## A lineage described at Talamanca {#s-108d336bd7}
The description places *D. amirae* in Tropical Wet Forest on the northeastern foothills of Costa Rica’s Cordillera de Talamanca, at about 1,000 m. The authors reported a brown to brown-gray back and a gray-blue underside with pale blotches; males measured 18.0–23.0 mm in snout–vent length (SVL, snout tip to vent). Their analysis found a modulated single-note advertisement call, averaging 0.256 ± 0.015 seconds, and genetic distances above 5.6% in 16S and 16.7% in COI. They treated these as parts of a combined case for species recognition, not as distance thresholds alone; the closest reported relatives were *D. vocator* and an unnamed lineage from western Panama.

The 2019 account knew two populations, on Plátano Peak and a nearby unnamed peak, between 1,000 and 1,121 m. Across eight nights and 158 person-hours of searching, the authors found no females, juveniles or eggs. Those records describe the study’s sites and effort, not the species’ complete range or life history.

## What the linked morphology row records {#s-34e87975a1}
The morphology v3 release links one measurement row (Anura.csv row 1555) and one bibliography row (Reference.csv row 1648) to this name. Its sex field is NA, and it has 18 populated measurement fields: SVL 21, HL 6.1, HW 8.2, ED 2.4, TD 1.2, IOD 2.5, UEW 1.6, EN 1.2, TL 9.1; toe lengths 2.4, 3.3, 4.8, 6.9 and 5.3; and finger lengths 2.1, 2.8, 3.7 and 3.1. The original article reports its comparative measurements in millimetres for six *D. amirae* specimens, but the deposited row itself does not confirm units or identify a unique animal. Read it as a compiled record, not a new specimen measurement or a complete account of variation. The dataset’s Brachycephaloidea family field also remains unreconciled with COL26.8’s Eleutherodactylidae placement.

## catalogue-profile / limitations {#s-7cddb7f4ec}
The source-backed record covers a 2019 species description and one linked morphology row. It does not establish current occupancy across the full range, unsampled life stages, population-wide variation or conservation status.

## Research text pending {#s-719f75b216}
The 2019 description supports a distinct lineage from a small set of Costa Rican mountain sites. The later catalogue and morphology archive retain different classification labels, and the archive row does not supply confirmed units or specimen identity. These records do not by themselves give a complete account of present-day distribution, life history or conservation.
