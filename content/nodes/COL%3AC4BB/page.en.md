## Aloiampelos gracilis: pale leaf sheaths and red flowers {#s-7e183e06d4}
A South African regional account describes a succulent shrub up to 2 m tall, with slender branches from the base and no persistent dried leaves. The dull-green leaves have no spots, are narrowly lanceolate and reach 25 cm long. Their sheaths are not auriculate, lack ciliate margins, and are faintly striated and pale green; the leaf margins have firm white teeth. Cylindrical, slightly conical racemes are somewhat dense, simple or branched, and 20–30 cm long. The flowers are bright red to scarlet, yellowish at the opening and 40–45 mm long; the stamens and stigma remain enclosed or project only slightly.

The diagnostic account says the vegetative form resembles *A. striatula*, but the leaves are duller green and the sheaths less distinctly striated. The flowers are similar to those of *A. ciliaris*, but the leaf bases differ and the racemes are much more open. The account also notes that flowers are borne on a loose raceme with somewhat longer pedicels.
