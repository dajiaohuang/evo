## Accepted name and placement {#s-57850ada53}
COL26.8 accepts Mixophyes australis Mahony, Bertozzi, Guzinski, Hines & Donnellan, 2023 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Myobatrachidae (family) → Mixophyes (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## Reading route {#s-347de85760}
Begin with the accepted record and its parent genus, then use the family and order pages to locate related catalogue entries. This page supplies a taxonomic orientation. Species-specific morphology, ecology, life history, distribution, evolutionary evidence and conservation information still require reviewed biological sources.

## One adult male provides a genome and six tissue transcriptomes {#s-48c417c368}
A wild-caught adult male sampled in September2021 supplied heart/kidney DNA and RNA from brain, male gonads, dorsal and ventral skin, liver and spleen. PacBio HiFi and Hi-C produced a genome of approximately3.12–3.13Gb, with scaffoldN50 about369.3Mb and over95% in twelve major scaffolds. These are assembly properties, not population genetic diversity or biological evidence from six independent frogs. The twelve scaffolds are inferred chromosome-scale units rather than a newly observed karyotype of this individual.

## Completeness depends on the resource and metric {#s-f60ba4e12a}
Table1 lists91.3% complete genome BUSCOs, while the Results prose says91.8%; these incompatible values are retained as a reporting conflict. Global-transcriptome BUSCO completeness is96.1%, and annotated proteins score86.0%, measuring different resources. Merqury completeness93.5% andQ60+ are another assessment, not percentages that can be pooled with BUSCO. The mitochondrial scaffold is annotated with21tRNAs, two rRNAs and thirteen protein-coding genes; that count is an assembly annotation rather than proof of a universal species-wide tRNA loss.

## Fourteen peptide candidates are sequence-based discoveries {#s-44a3b8c18e}
Homology searches identify twelve cathelicidin genes and two beta-defensin genes, with full-length transcripts in the global assembly. Cathelicidins cluster on scaffold5 and defensins on scaffold2. Predicted signal regions and mature cleavage products use computational tools; the actual amphibian cleavage enzyme is unknown. These findings are not fourteen purified secreted peptides, experimentally measured pathogen killing or demonstrated resistance to chytrid disease.

## Tissue expression is preliminary in one individual {#s-5c31009aa9}
Transcript-per-million values indicate different candidate expression patterns across six tissues, including skin and internal organs. MA-CATH6 is prominent in liver and MA-CATH7–8 in spleen in the discussion; such patterns were not tested across multiple specimens. TPM values are transcript abundance estimates, not measured peptide concentrations or immune efficacy. The authors explicitly state that replication was unavailable and propose future peptide synthesis and cellular functional assays.

## Gene-tree clusters do not establish unique genus adaptations {#s-1eed13c050}
Some cathelicidins form well-supported species-specific clusters, while several other relationships and the defensin pairing have weak support. Duplication timing and evolutionary isolation are proposed explanations. Other Mixophyes peptide repertoires had not been characterized, so the apparent species-specific clusters could extend more broadly. Greater microbial exposure is another hypothesis, not a measured property of this individual or species. A peptide gene tree is not a complete species phylogeny.

## Monitoring applications remain proposed {#s-8ce5f165cf}
The authors propose using the mitochondrial resource to develop metabarcoding markers and clarify the relationship of southern australis and northern balbus ranges. This study does not validate an eDNA assay, measure sensitivity or specificity in field water, or map current range overlap. Its threatened-status language is dated source context rather than an independently verified current listing. Genomic quality alone does not demonstrate population recovery or successful conservation monitoring.

## A reference assembly in a comparative genome study {#s-96fdfc91af}
The study published online in December2025 and in the 2026 volume lists Mixophyes australis assembly GCF_014858855.1, with an assembled genome-size summary of 3.13 Gbp and contig N50 10,748.0 Kbp. These describe this listed assembly and its continuity, rather than a direct measurement of DNA per cell, a universally fixed species genome size or a demonstrated ploidy. The source selected NCBI reference assemblies on 29October2024. Original sequence data, assembly quality and specimen identity have not been independently reassessed here.

## Assembly metadata has limits {#s-f98e657ecf}

## Repeat annotations are computational observations {#s-a6b3f99d88}
The authors built species-specific repeat libraries with RepeatModeler2, classified unknown elements with RFSB and supplemented the libraries with vertebrate repeats before applying RepeatMasker. Its hits are termed insertions in the paper, but a hit is not independently demonstrated to be a complete, expressed or mobile element. Superfamily labels can aggregate distinct families. Element counts, total occupied length and genome proportions are separate quantities; overlapping hierarchy categories cannot safely be added as independent fractions. No new repeat annotation or expression experiment for Mixophyes australis was performed here.

## Across-species correlations do not prove this species mechanism {#s-1f0dc26fbb}
The study compared61 assemblies using phylogenetically adjusted models, an earlier pruned tree, contig-N50 covariates and multiple-comparison correction. Recently active Ty3/DIRS1-associated sequences correlate with genome size across that sample, but recent activity is a divergence-based proxy, not direct observation of current transposition in this species. Length and open-reading-frame similarity filters likewise do not demonstrate active protein expression. The authors' suggested developmental constraints are comparative hypotheses; they do not establish a causal TE effect on this species' larval duration or clutch size.

## Conflicting source identifiers prevent a repeat-data assignment {#s-990af76c9e}
Main Table1 links Mixophyes australis to GCF_014858855.1, while supplementary TableS1 row54 labels Mixophyes australis with GCA_036250195.1. That supplementary accession appears under Leptobrachium leishanense in main Table1. The species-name and accession correspondence therefore cannot be authenticated by simply joining these tables. The supplementary repeat counts and proportions are retained as conflicting source data, without treating them as verified traits of this accepted species. TablesS2–S3 supply55 clutch and39 larval-period rows by accession without individual provenance or unit labels in their headers; those life-history values are also withheld from species trait assignment pending reconciliation.
