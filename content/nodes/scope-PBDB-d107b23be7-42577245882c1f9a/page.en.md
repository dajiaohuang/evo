## A large bell, many tentacles, an uncertain branch {#s-855ae5ecb7}
The Raymond Quarry sample contains 182 compressed middle-Cambrian fossils of Burgessomedusa phasmiformis, with a large bell and many marginal tentacles. The authors support a medusozoan affinity, while its precise position changes with the analysis.

## Anatomy in the compressed fossils {#s-401df844c3}
The Raymond Quarry sample includes 182 compressed fossils. The umbrella is cuboidal and reaches about 20 cm in height, with more than 90 short marginal tentacles; radial internal features are visible in the compressed material.

## Medusozoan affinity, model-sensitive placement {#s-fc6241d836}
The analysis supports a medusozoan affinity, while stem-cubozoan, stem-Acraspeda or alternative placements depend on character coding. The broad affinity is therefore clearer than the exact branch.

## Swimming and feeding remain interpretations {#s-7f2b208c14}
Bell shape and specimen associations support free swimming and a possible nektobenthic predatory habit. A near-seafloor position and prey capture are inferred; no gut contents document a meal.

## atlas-profile / readerLimitations / en {#s-d40d7ccf34}
The described fossils come from Raymond Quarry and define a locality-bounded sample, not a global range. Neither prey capture nor measured swimming performance is directly recorded.
