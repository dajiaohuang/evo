## *Voyria aphylla* {#s-e47bf4000d}
## A pale stem and solitary flowers

The Flora of Panama excerpt describes an herb up to 20 cm tall, occasionally 25 cm. Its upright, simple stem is white or yellowish, slender, round in cross-section, and hairless. A few, widely spaced stem scales are 5–8 mm long and much shorter than the gaps between leaves; opposite scales may be fused for about 1/5–1/3 of their length, or less. Flowers are solitary, with parts in fives, and may reach 6 cm long. Their stalks are usually short but sometimes elongated. The translucent, cylinder-to-bell-shaped calyx is up to 7.5 mm long; its narrow triangular lobes are acute or taper to a point and are about 1/3 the tube’s length.

## Corolla, reproductive parts, and seed

The same excerpt describes a yellow corolla up to 6 cm long. Its narrow, slightly ribbed tube reaches 48 mm and narrows above the ovary and below the anthers; the spreading lobes reach 12 mm and taper at the tip. Fine hairs occur toward the inner base of the lobes. The sessile anthers are 1–1.25 mm long, inserted below the corolla throat, and may be free or sometimes joined. The gynoecium reaches 4.2 cm; its narrow, stalked-base ovary reaches 20 mm, the thread-like style 22 mm, and the head-shaped stigma is about 1.5 mm across. The capsule splits along its internal partitions; the winged, thread-to-spindle-shaped seeds reach 1 cm.
