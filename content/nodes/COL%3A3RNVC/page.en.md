## What leaves and flowers are described? {#s-2b5d7645eb}
This compact, felted, rounded shrublet grows to 40 cm. Its leaves are arranged in crossing pairs and pressed against the stems; they are narrowly elliptic or lance-shaped. Flowers, apparently solitary within small heads, are cream-colored and 3–4 mm across. The stigma is head-shaped with long papillae.

## How does the monograph distinguish its inflorescence from relatives? {#s-f23e1f8a46}
A 2001 monograph says the bracted umbel produces one mature flower at a time, projecting beyond the bracts; immature flowers, fruits and stalks remain enclosed. In L. funicaulis, three to six flowers open at once, and the floral scales sit inside the upper flower tube, from midway to four-fifths of its length. In L. penicillata the scales arise at the tube mouth and project outward. The monograph identifies L. penicillata as a sister species of L. pendula based on two shared traits: inflorescences on the main flowering branches and a hairy style. It distinguishes L. penicillata by its erect inflorescences and leaf edges with woolly hairs that later become sparse.

## What habitat and elevation are reported? {#s-fcbe4050b9}
The regional account reports sandstone slopes at 300–1,330 m elevation. It gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency.
