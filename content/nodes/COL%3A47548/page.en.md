## Nerine marincowitzii: a detailed regional description {#s-b9d7d82bd7}
A 1995 regional account describes plants up to 20 cm tall. The solitary bulb is depressed-globose, 45–55 mm across, with thinly fibrous, cream-coloured tunics that are fleshy inside; it extends into a slender neck up to 60 mm long. There are 5–6 foliage leaves, absent at flowering. They are two-ranked, suberect to recurved, narrowly strap-shaped, gradually tapering toward the tip, channelled, somewhat succulent, bright green and hairless, up to 30 cm × 3–4 mm.

The compact, hemispherical inflorescence has 20–40 flowers and is 10–16 cm across. The rigid, sigmoid scape is elliptical in cross-section, 11–14 cm × 2.5–3.5 mm, reddish brown, and breaks at ground level when dry. There are two narrow-lanceolate, papery, wine-red spathe valves, 35–55 × 3–5 mm. Thread-like bracteoles reach 10 mm. Pedicels radiate widely; they are straight, round in cross-section and 40–75 mm long, shiny green before turning straw-coloured and rigid in fruit.

The perianth is strongly bilaterally symmetric, pink with a whitish throat at first that turns brown with age, and scentless. The tepals are briefly fused near the base, oblong-lanceolate, sickle-shaped and upturned at flowering, flat, and 17–22 × 4–6.5 mm. Filaments are slightly declined and thread-like, 13–17 mm long, widening and joining near the base into a tube up to 0.25 mm long. The dorsifixed anthers are about 5 mm long and wine-red before opening; the pollen is whitish. The ovary is dark wine-red. Each locule has one ovule or rarely two, while the innermost flowers lack ovules. The slender style is declined and about 20 mm long; nectar occurs around its base, and the stigma is shortly three-lobed.

The capsule is small, papery and loculicidally dehiscent, and is rapidly outgrown by the seeds. The ellipsoidal seeds are succulent, wine-red and 6–7 mm across; the seed coat has stomata. The account reports flowering for about three weeks in May, depending on the onset of autumn rain, and adds the phrase “probably until the end of April,” whose referent is unclear. Leaves appear in spring during rapid growth and die back during a summer dry spell.
