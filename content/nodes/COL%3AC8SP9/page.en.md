## The type account distinguishes it by its spores {#s-8c5707d67a}
The species description presents the type basidioma—the specimen's fruiting body—and microscopic characters. It distinguishes *Amanita orientalis* from related taxa by spore shape and dimensions; the evidence summarized here does not supply the measurements themselves.

## Where the described mushrooms fruited {#s-8c6724a800}
The authors report solitary or scattered fruiting on soil in subtropical forests with Fagaceae and Pinaceae trees, such as oaks and pines. The account describes field collections and does not establish a physiological host relationship, resource dependence, or a complete ecological range.

## What the sampled tree can and cannot show {#s-416d0f283c}
The study's combined multilocus analysis groups *A. orientalis* with *A. griseofolia* and two South Korean collections that the paper identified as *A. ceciliae*. The authors flag those two identifications as erroneous, so the grouping should be read as a result for the study's sampled material, not as a secure relationship to correctly identified *A. ceciliae* or a broad evolutionary history.

## A reported range based on a small set of collections {#s-a8d47a99e7}
The paper reports the species from eastern China and lists the type and an additional collection from Huangshan in 2018. The type voucher EFHAAU 1367 was collected on 15 September 2018; the additional specimen was collected on 14 July 2018. These records describe the material in that paper, not an exhaustive global range.
