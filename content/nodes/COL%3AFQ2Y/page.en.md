## Body mass changed with age in one hatchery cohort {#s-8ca6853d08}
The accepted name and taxonomic placement follow Catalogue of Life COL26.8 (ChecklistBank dataset 316115; source checklist dataset 1130).

In a University of Miami National Resource for Aplysia hatchery cohort, 200 animals from one egg mass were weighed monthly. Mean body mass increased before an inflection during the ninth month and then declined; the smoothed trend peaked around 9–10 months after hatching. This age-specific curve describes one captive cohort, not diagnostic adult morphology or a species-wide size range.

Among the 53 individuals whose natural mortality was tracked in this cohort, median lifespan was 363 days and the final recorded mortality occurred 422 days after hatching. The cited sections do not state calendar dates for the rearing study. These observations describe the captive cohort and do not estimate species-wide longevity or survival in the wild.

## What the cohort does not establish {#s-dc28fd1fee}
The cohort weight curve does not establish diagnostic morphology, adult body dimensions, sex differences or size variation across wild populations.

A single captive cohort and 53 tracked natural mortalities do not establish species-wide, wild or population-comparative lifespan; reproduction and early development were not assessed.

The hatchery cohort study does not assess natural habitat, wild diet, ecological interactions or range-wide ecology.

No species-specific evolutionary comparison or population-genetic analysis was assessed.

The cohort's parental geographic origin is not stated, and the study did not assess distribution.

The study did not assess fossil evidence or a bounded fossil-record search.

The study did not assess current conservation status, population trends or threats.
