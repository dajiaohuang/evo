## The 2015 life-history entry and this name {#s-97f571772e}
Pinned COL26.8 accepts *Echis ocellatus* in Viperidae. The 2015 life-history archive supplies four focal fields through cited, species-level sparse-table rows with the same name and family. Its summaries may still inherit data across historical taxonomic concepts; a matching row name does not remove that possibility.

## Different stages in the mass fields {#s-aec84d8247}
The historical summary reports adult body mass as 142 g. This field can combine males, females and adults of unspecified sex, so it is not a range of individual masses. No birth or hatching mass is reported here.

## Reproduction and developmental timing {#s-b9ff5a4564}
The archive reports 14 offspring or eggs per litter/clutch and 41 d from oviposition to hatching; d means days. The litter/clutch field combines offspring and egg counts. A fractional summary would not mean that one event produced part of an individual, and this timing value refers only to oviposition through hatching.

## A longest record differs from usual lifespan {#s-bea5e9a91e}
The archive’s `maximum_longevity_y` value is 23.8 years, the largest record in the collected material under its source method. It is not typical lifespan, mean wild lifespan, a survival ceiling for every individual or a current, repeatedly attainable record.

## How to trace these values to sources {#s-0d04ac982a}
Same-name sparse rows carry source labels including Boback and Guyer, 2003; Reptile Incubation book; de Magalhaes and Costa, 2009; and Carey, J.R. and D.S. Judge, 2002. The source archive retains the field-level row numbers, values and labels, along with the table’s complete reference explanations. A label can refer to a paper, book or another database; row counts are not counts of measured animals, and repeated citations are not independent replication.

## Life-history evidence limits {#s-ee7f9722f2}
The 2015 archive reports medians or averages of the two middle values, except that maximum longevity uses the largest record found. These are compiled values, not measurements of one individual, ranges of individual variation, mean wild lifespans or current population surveys. Historical species and subspecies may share or inherit data. Matching name and family support the association but do not establish equivalence to COL26.8 concepts or remove inherited components. Conditions (wild or captive) are not uniformly confirmed, and the underlying publications have not been individually reviewed. Other biological and current-status claims need separate sources.

## Name and this data account {#s-f31cea0ec9}
The separate ReptTraits v1-2 record uses the Reptile Database 2023 name framework and keeps sources attached to individual traits. It is a compiled species row, not a uniform description of every population.

## Distinguish the measured length {#s-a124227455}
The ReptTraits row reports a maximum total length (TL) of 670 mm. Total length includes the tail, unlike snout–vent length (SVL). This compiled maximum is not a typical size or theoretical growth limit.

## Where and how it is active {#s-fe1efe571a}
The ReptTraits row labels the microhabitat “Terrestrial.” This broad category is not a detailed habitat account and does not establish how habitat use varies among places, seasons or life stages.

## How to read reproductive records {#s-b4bd5cadf2}
The reproductive-mode field is labelled “oviparous” (egg-laying). That recorded category does not establish that all populations are identical; no clutch or litter count is interpreted here.

## Limits of these trait data {#s-2868f42e09}
This ReptTraits account is limited to the cited trait rows. Its compiled species record may compress differences among individuals and populations; matching name and family do not guarantee equivalence across later taxonomic concepts. The reported maximum length is not an absolute species limit. Conservation, venom, current distribution and population trends are not updated here, and the source labels do not mean that every underlying work was independently reviewed.
