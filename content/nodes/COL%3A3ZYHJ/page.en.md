## Mesembryanthemum decurvatum {#s-24435440d1}
## Down-curving branches carry hard, persistent leaves

Bolus’s 1928 description portrays a rigid, woody plant with tiny surface papillae and tangled branching. The branches soon bend downward and reach up to 5 mm across. Opposite leaves spread widely at first, then curve down; they are nearly cylindrical, taper to acute tips, and harden while persisting.

Day-blooming flowers are 2–2.5 cm across on 1–2 cm stalks. Four unequal sepals are 6–10 mm long, and the petals are rose-salmon, up to 1 mm wide. Many sterile stamens radiate around the conical group of fertile stamens. The ovary sits slightly above the flower base and bears four narrow, pointed stigmas up to 3 mm long. The habitat record is lower rocky hillsides.
