## Testing the catfish root {#brief}
One deep question in catfish history is which of three major lineages branches first. A 2018 ten-gene study showed how the answer changed when the researchers reduced evolutionary-rate differences among sequences, while also identifying a taxon with extensive missing data that complicated one filtering result.

## A root can depend on how fast lineages evolve {#early-branches}
Rivera-Rivera and Montoya-Burgos compared the three major siluriform lineages with a 10-gene dataset. The complete molecular dataset placed Loricarioidei first, unlike morphological analyses that placed Diplomystidae first. After applying LS3 or LS4 subsampling to reduce rate heterogeneity, the analyses instead supported Diplomystidae as the earliest branch. LS3 misplaced Scoloplax sp., which had among the highest proportions of missing data; LS4 removed less data and also retained known relationships within the lineages. The paper’s result is a method-sensitive reconciliation, not a claim that all catfish relationships are settled.
