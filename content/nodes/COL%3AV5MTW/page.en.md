## Accepted name and placement {#s-64ac471881}
COL26.8 accepts Boulenophrys lushuiensis (Shi, Li, Zhu, Jiang, Jiang & Wang, 2021) as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Megophryidae (family) → Boulenophrys (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## Western Yunnan locality account {#s-ad1cc6ad16}
Male holotype CIB YN201909290 came from Gutan River, Luzhang, Lushui, Yunnan, at approximately 2358 m. Examined material included Linjiapu, Dahaoping and Zizhi in Tengchong at approximately 2090–2210 m. The monograph summarized known Lushui, Tengchong, Yingjiang and Longchuan localities at 1900–2400 m along the China–Myanmar border region. This locality account does not establish occurrence across the border in Myanmar or continuous occupancy throughout the interval.

## Thirty-three examined Tengchong adults {#s-3b35ce4db0}
The diagnosis reported thirteen adult males at 32.3–38.0 mm snout–vent length and twenty females at 37.8–47.4 mm. These are the examined Tengchong measurements; separately listed juvenile male paratypes are excluded from adult denominators. Diagnostic anatomy included weak vomerine ridges without teeth, a prominent horn-like eyelid tubercle, rudimentary basal toe webbing with narrow fringes, and tiny dense nuptial spines on finger I and II pads in breeding males, with a subgular vocal sac. These anatomical observations do not supply a breeding calendar or functional measurements.

## Previously separate candidate lineages treated as one species {#s-1756f353c0}
The remarks traced earlier candidate labels M. sp2, M. sp39 and M. sp40 and noted that the original Lushui description had not included those earlier datasets. The monograph analysed them together, reported a monophyletic lineage with B. lushuiensis, and found no distinguishing morphology between northern and southern Tengchong material and the Lushui types. It treated them as conspecific and proposed geographic isolation as a possible explanation for genetic divergence. The taxonomic decision and geographic hypothesis remain attributed, not independent evidence of reproductive compatibility or a proven cause of divergence.

## A character cited in the gutu comparison {#s-0412760bd2}
The 2025 gutu description lists Boulenophrys lushuiensis as with heels overlapping when the thighs are held at right angles to the body. This is an attributed comparator statement within that paper’s taxonomic scope, not a new measurement series for this species. The gutu adult sample spans 34.4–44.7 mm in seven males and 36.2–52.8 mm in eight females; those numbers belong to gutu, not to this comparator. The selected character does not establish population-wide frequency, complete diagnosis, diet or developmental biology.
