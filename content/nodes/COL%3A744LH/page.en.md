## Minipaa is a published subgenus proposal {#s-acd026d5e6}
The 2023 analyses place minica in a distinct lineage with an unnamed voucher, separate from the three previously recognized subgenera. The authors propose Minipaa, with minica as its type species, while acknowledging unresolved relationships among that lineage, Paa and Nanorana. A supported separate lineage is evidence within this taxonomic framework, not an independently certified rank requirement or proof that every unnamed voucher is minica.

## Small size and toe traits are cited diagnosis evidence {#s-8b245ca77e}
The subgenus diagnosis, explicitly based on Dubois and Ohler–Dubois descriptions, lists male adult snout–vent length 28.5–33 millimetres and female adult length 30.5–41. Toe tips are markedly expanded and webbing deeply incurved; vocal sacs are present. These are cited diagnostic ranges and traits rather than a new measured minica type series in this paper. They should not be treated as complete population extrema, a growth curve or a measured ecological performance.

## Pale spines and eggs are cited characters, not a new breeding cohort {#s-7eb4be1095}
The cited diagnosis describes large, countable translucent or cream nuptial spines on fingers I and II and the chest, and entirely whitish or cream eggs without a coloured animal pole. The authors use these traits to distinguish Minipaa. The account provides no new spawning observation, fecundity estimate, incubation duration or seasonal breeding survey; an egg-colour character alone does not establish the site or mode of development.

## Unnamed low-distance vouchers remain provisionally identified {#s-6c6937f537}
The discussion reports 16S distances of 0.7–0.9% between minica and three unnamed RAS vouchers, suggesting possible conspecificity pending morphology. One such voucher enters the combined-tree lineage. This is a taxonomic hypothesis, not an accepted identification, confirmed new occurrence or an extra sampled minica population. The cited country-level Nepal, India and Bhutan range is background, not a newly surveyed full distribution.
