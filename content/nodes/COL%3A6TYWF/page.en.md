## The name and this data account {#s-25ad51dcce}
COL26.8 accepts *Pelecanoides urinatrix* in Procellariidae. AVONET v7 uses the HBW–BirdLife 2020 name and records 7 measured individuals, 4 with a complete set of morphological measurements. Sample sizes can differ by trait.

## Measured beak dimensions {#s-95b825c4fe}
The recorded beak means are 13.1 mm from the front edge of the nostril to the tip, 6.4 mm wide and 7.2 mm deep at the nostril edge. These are separate dimensions at defined landmarks, not field-identification cutoffs.

## Wing, tarsus and mass {#s-074e7ca54d}
Mean unflattened wing length, measured from the carpal joint to the longest primary, is 121.7 mm; mean tarsus length is 23.9 mm. The compiled mean mass is 152.06 g, from source category Dunning. Mass comes from separate species-average information, so the morphology record does not show that every measured bird was weighed.

## Habitat, feeding and lifestyle {#s-2d7c1e0ed1}
The dataset's dominant categories are Marine for habitat, Aquatic predator for trophic niche and Aquatic for lifestyle. They support cross-species comparisons, but do not mean the bird uses only marine habitat or eats only aquatic prey.

The migration field classifies the record as partially migratory. Historical breeding and resident maps span latitudes -63.56° to -24.03°. They do not provide a full account of non-breeding movements or show current positions or global occupancy. Geographic and morphological data come from separate compilation steps.

## Evidence limits {#s-30626288d1}
This page summarizes historical AVONET v7 data. Morphological values are species averages from the listed samples, not identification diagnoses or individual variation ranges. Mass comes from separately cited literature or weighing sources; the morphology sample count does not apply to mass. Habitat, diet and migration are broad categories, and the map covers historical breeding and resident ranges. Historical crosswalks and a matching catalogue name support the association but do not prove equivalent species concepts after later splits or lumps.
