## One sequenced individual and a separate DNA-content estimate {#s-003594dfc9}
The study extracted liver DNA from one Thorius lunaris specimen as part of a sixteen-species, three-genus comparison, targeting ten gigabases of short-read data per species. Repeat analysis randomly sampled merged reads at roughly 0.1-fold coverage. Table 1 gives a genome C-value estimate of 24.9 picograms, based on blood-smear densitometry and species means from earlier genome-size work. Picograms of DNA are not gigabases, body mass or a complete genome assembly; the sequenced individual and the species-mean DNA-content estimate have different evidence roles. The sample does not determine variation among sexes, populations or ages.

## Detected repeat abundance is a method-dependent lower estimate {#s-9305ecd231}
Table 1 reports 74.8% detectable repeat abundance for Thorius lunaris, including uncategorized repeats. Table 2 reports LTRs at 13.9%, and Table 3 separates ERV at 9.534% from Ty3/Gypsy at 4.276%. These are percentages of sampled bases identified by the study's repeat library, not percentages of animals, newly inserted elements or measured gene expression. Mutation can degrade repeats beyond recognition; the authors regard short-read masking as likely underestimating true repetitive content. Rounded table categories need not sum exactly, and uncategorized repeats should not be silently assigned to a known TE superfamily.

## Repeat divergence suggests relative expansion histories {#s-2775cf7bf4}
The study groups lunaris, narisovalis and spilogaster as the three smaller-genome Thorius. Their ERV peaks were below Kimura distance four, with low-distance reads in several superfamilies interpreted as recent or ongoing expansion. Gypsy activity was comparatively weak. These repeat-distance profiles do not measure an insertion rate, date an evolutionary event in years or demonstrate the deletion/silencing mechanism; one sequenced specimen per species limits population generalization.
