## Desmanthus virgatus: fine leaflets and narrow pods {#s-e39f063bc0}
*Desmanthus virgatus* is described in a Flora of Panama entry as a small shrub or subshrub. That historical account gives a detailed picture of its leaves and seed pods, while its distribution statement does not establish the plant’s current range or where it is native.

Its leaves are bipinnate, meaning each leaf is divided into smaller, leaf-bearing branches called pinnae. A leaf has one to seven opposite pairs of pinnae, usually two to four; each pinna mostly carries 10–20 pairs of leaflets. The leaflets are narrow, about 2–8 mm long and mostly 1–2 mm wide. A small, cup-shaped gland sits where the lowest pair of pinnae joins the leaf stalk.

Flower heads grow from the leaf axils near the ends of the branches. The source describes them as small and few-flowered, with whitish flowers. The flat, narrow pods are mostly 4–6 cm long and 3–4 mm wide; they split along both sides, and their seeds lie at an angle.

The flora’s distribution field lists Florida and Texas, Argentina, the West Indies and the Galápagos, adding “probably introduced elsewhere.” This is a historical regional flora statement, not a current or global range survey. It does not distinguish native, introduced, cultivated or escaped occurrences, and the account gives no population sampling frame or observation date.
