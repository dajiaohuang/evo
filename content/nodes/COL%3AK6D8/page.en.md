## Leaves and flowers {#s-39fe41d17a}
The 2012 regional account describes a stemless corm-bearing geophyte up to 6 cm tall. Its ovate leaves are held at right angles to their sheaths, nearly flat and smooth, with crisped margins. Two to six flowers are borne in a short, low-lying spike. They are two-lipped, creamy green flushed with mauve, with cream-coloured nectar guides. The perianth tube is 22–25 mm long, the upper tepal about 30 mm; the inner bract is forked at the tip, and the ovary is smooth.

These traits and measurements come from a regional South African account published in 2012; the excerpt gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency.

## Scent and young leaves {#s-918cf5dc80}
A 2020 diagnostic account notes that the broad, ovate leaves, with wavy, crisped margins, are distinctive within the genus. The floral bracts are largely dry and thinly papery, pale straw-coloured, and flecked with minute light-brown marks. Even in full bloom the plant can be inconspicuous: its pale bluish-green to pale-yellow flowers sit close to the ground. Their strong, sweet, spicy scent resembles cinnamon and clove and is produced both day and night; the account suggests, but does not establish, moth pollination. Young foliage differs: seedlings and plants with immature corms have slender, linear leaves with a few long, soft hairs.

These diagnostic observations come from a regional South African account published in 2020; the excerpt gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency.

## Habitat {#s-08f189679a}
The regional flora records the species on stony lower slopes, mainly on granite.
