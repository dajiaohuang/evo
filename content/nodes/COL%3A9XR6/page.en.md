## Five basal veins and a complex flower {#s-e6d85dcab3}
The Brazilian Flora 2020 v393.147 entry describes petiolate leaves with broadly elliptic or lance-shaped blades, acute at both base and tip. Their margins are listed as entire or barbed and tomentose (covered in dense, woolly hairs), with five basal acrodromous veins—major veins arising near the base and running toward the tip. The corymb-like thyrse (a branched flower cluster) is recorded as terminal or pseudo-axillary, with leafy bracts and triangular bracteoles. Flowers are five-parted and have a tubular hypanthium; inner calyx lobes are blunt and outer lobes narrow-triangular. Petals may be white or pink, with rounded or asymmetric tips. Stamens differ in form and size. The anthers are slightly curved and awl-shaped, with rough pollen sacs and pores at the dorsal tip; an ascending, linear, two-lobed appendage and a narrow triangular spur are also recorded. The ovary is free inside the hypanthium, obovoid, slightly ribbed and truncate at its tip; the source also records “3 loculicida” without expanding that term. The fruit-type label is velatídio and is described as unribbed; seeds are obpyramidal, or inverted-pyramid-shaped. The listed alternatives do not quantify variation or define a complete diagnostic range.

## A single habitat category {#s-1bd9ded31e}
The dataset labels the recorded habitat “terrícola,” meaning terrestrial. It supplies no collection locality, survey date or range coverage, and does not state whether the record concerns wild, cultivated, managed or fossil material. This category is not a complete ecological account.
