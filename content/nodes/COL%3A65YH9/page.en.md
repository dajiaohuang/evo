## Larval and adult habitat labels stay separate {#s-e7e1a73792}
The study's row under *A. vanzolinii* codes larvae LE (lentic, associated with still water) and adults TER (terrestrial). These labels belong to different life stages: the adult code does not automatically describe tadpoles, or the reverse. Lentic is a broad habitat category, not a measured flow tolerance; “saxicolous” or terrestrial labels do not define the full geographic range. The source also classifies the tadpole as endotrophic (using internal reserves), a category distinct from direct development, so a larval stage is still recorded. These assignments were compiled from species accounts rather than experimental measurements of each species' feeding, movement or habitat choice.

## What the paired-stage analysis can support {#s-60023ba414}
Across the sampled species, larval and adult shape dissimilarities have a weak but statistically significant Mantel association (a test comparing two distance patterns; r = 0.16, p = 0.003), and disparity correlations vary among families. The authors read the overall pattern as support for adaptive decoupling; it does not show zero correlation, genetic independence, experimental selection or the developmental trajectory of *A. vanzolinii*. Phylogenetic analyses include 74 of 83 sampled species, while ancestral shape and timing are model reconstructions from living data rather than new fossil measurements. The adult phylogenetic-signal statistic K (0.44, p = 0.001) indicates less similarity among close relatives than the reference expectation, so the paper's phrase “not phylogenetically structured” should not be read as a non-significant test. Supplement S3 lists family sample counts that sum to 112 for both stages; without the raw matrices, the adult individual denominator and imputed cells cannot be reconciled independently.
