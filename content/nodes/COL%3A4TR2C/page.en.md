## Ruschia leucosperma {#s-0c4462a6c7}
## Magenta flowers and a short-lived, spiny fruit stalk

Buys’s 2013 Greater Cape flora describes a richly branched shrub about 10–20 cm tall. Young stem segments between the nodes (internodes) are covered in small bumps. The pale-green leaves are nearly round and have a raised ridge, with little fusion at their bases.

Each flower cluster has three magenta flowers; largely fused, bract-like leaves (bracteoles) enclose their bases. The six sepals accompany narrow petals about 1 mm wide and magenta sterile stamens (staminodes). The six-chambered fruit has a bell-shaped base and rounded top. Its covering membranes bear closing rodlets, and its small closing body is hook-shaped. The short expanding keels fall away at ripeness, leaving a spiny stalk. The habitat account records granitic soil, sand and shale.
