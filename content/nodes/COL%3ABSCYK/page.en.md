## Classification {#s-9d3639dea2}
COL26.8 and the 2025 ICMBio assessment recognize Cacajao novaesi at species rank. The 2022 molecular revision proposed five bald-uakari species, while its genetic samples retain the earlier subspecies name C. calvus novaesi. The assessment uses the species name and cites the revised taxonomy. This page keeps the fixed COL name and shows where the field evidence was originally published under the older combination.

## molecular sample attribution {#s-8e767756a5}
The 2022 paper lists five cytochrome-b sequences under the historical label C. calvus novaesi. The authors question two earlier sample assignments: INPA5241 is treated as Cacajao sp., and UFPA-Ccn1 lacks a skin or skull and came from a locality more than 350 km north of the then-known range. These are paper-era taxonomic assignments, not five confirmed current-species records or a census.

## regional distribution {#s-6d6d0b5b9c}
The 2021 field-and-collection study, published under the older name C. calvus novaesi, places the taxon in the Gregório–Tarauacá interfluvium in Amazonas, Brazil. It reports records on the right bank of the Rio Gregório and no evidence on the left bank, a limit also supported by residents' accounts. The ICMBio assessment describes the rivers Gregório and Tarauacá as range boundaries and says the southern limit remains uncertain pending surveys along the upper Tarauacá. Its extent-of-occurrence estimate is 3,757 km² using a minimum convex polygon; this is not a population estimate or a complete range map.

## habitat and field note {#s-ec7710fdd6}
The assessment, citing the 2021 study, reports use of both várzea and terra-firme forest. It also relays one observation from 2005 of a group of more than ten uakaris crossing the igarapé do Coatá with Sapajus macrocephalus and Saimiri boliviensis; the source attributes this to a personal communication, not a published behavioral survey. The assessment says there is no species-specific diet study and that the species has not been the subject of ecological studies.

## conservation assessment {#s-602db9765c}
The ICMBio SALVE sheet was published in 2025, but its Near Threatened category is dated 27 September 2019. It reports a declining population trend, with total population size unknown, and identifies hunting and habitat loss associated with road access as concerns; some supporting detail comes from interviews and a 2022 personal communication. A MapBiomas 6.1 calculation reports 0.56% habitat loss across the known distribution from 1990 to 2020. The assessment calls for further ecology, density and threat research.

## Evidence limits {#s-b6e96c9f20}
Source-linked regional distribution and conservation introduction, not a comprehensive ecology. The distribution study used the earlier subspecies combination; its records are connected to the current COL species through the 2022 taxonomic revision and 2025 assessment. The southern range limit, population size, abundance and species-specific diet remain unresolved; some threat evidence is unpublished or interview-based.
