## Accepted name and placement {#s-5396c674cb}
COL26.8 accepts Ptychadena levenorum Smith, Noonan & Colston, 2017 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Ptychadenidae (family) → Ptychadena (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## High-elevation records with a lower type-locality exception {#s-42d91f39fa}
Records lay on the northern and southern sides of the Sanetti Plateau, without a record on the plateau itself. Most reported elevations were 3015–3404 m, while Katcha, the type locality, was at 2410 m. The lower record is preserved separately rather than replaced by a claim that all populations occur above 3000 m.

## Calling and the reported hybridization context {#s-51508cf406}
Individuals called at night from shallow grassy puddles or forest clearings, generally in small observed numbers. The revision reported genetic evidence of hybridization with P. robeensis where their ranges overlap. That genetic report is distinct from a claim based only on shared calling sites, and neither establishes mating compatibility at every locality.

## A geographically sampled mitochondrial and nuclear dataset {#s-262e32eae9}
The 2023 Ethiopian Highlands study lists 10 mitochondrial samples and 9 nuclear-SNP samples for Ptychadena levenorum in Table 1, with mitochondrial nucleotide diversity π=0.12%. Samples across the twelve-species study were collected in 2011–2019; these are analysed specimens, not a population census. All assembled mitochondrial genomes had the usual two rRNAs, thirteen protein-coding genes and twenty-two tRNAs, but no species had a completely resolved control region because repeats could not be confidently assembled from short reads. The phylogenetic alignment used selected genes (13,937 bp across 303 samples), rather than an alignment of complete control regions.

## A study-defined highland clade and regional distribution {#s-b1d7ed1fdb}
The paper places Ptychadena levenorum in its P. erlangeri clade and reports it from the eastern side of the Great Rift Valley. These distributions combine the authors' geographic sampling and earlier range work; they are not independently updated complete range polygons. The twelve-species highland clade is a subset of Ptychadena. Its mitochondrial-clock analysis estimated a common ancestor around 11.7 million years ago, with a reported interval of 8.827–15.494 million years. The east-of-rift ancestral reconstruction had a model probability of 69%; neither that estimate nor inferred crossings is a directly observed migration event or a nuclear-genome divergence date for this species.

## A dated reassessment argument, with remaining demographic gaps {#s-6ce7edf718}
The 2023 discussion calls for conservation assessment or reassessment using revised species concepts and genetic and geographic evidence. Earlier names and broad distributions can combine lineages that later work separated. The present genomic sample does not itself measure current abundance, fragmentation, decline or reproductive success in Ptychadena levenorum. Any Red List category mentioned in this paper belongs to its dated discussion, not a verified current official listing. Regional land-use and climate concerns support further investigation but do not quantify this species' current threat level.
