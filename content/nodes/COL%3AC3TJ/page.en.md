## Aloe maculata: spotted leaves and varied racemes {#s-59c4a50b5f}
A South African regional account describes plants ranging from stemless and freely suckering to short-stemmed and solitary, 200–500 mm tall excluding the inflorescence. There are 12–20 leaves, spreading to slightly drooping, narrowly to broadly lanceolate and 130–500 × 50–120 mm. The upper surface is flat to slightly channelled, dark green and marked by many pale spots in irregular crosswise rows. The lower surface is convex, paler green and lineate, with fewer spots or none. The inflorescence has (1–)4–12 terminal branches and reaches 0.4–1.5 m. The racemes are head-shaped. Taper-pointed triangular bracts measure 7–16 × 2.5–3.5 mm and have three distinct to many indistinct veins. Flowers range from yellow through pink and orange to red, are 20–45 mm long, and widen above a basal constriction. The outer segments are free for 6–15 mm; the inner segments are free but attached along the back to the outer ones. Pedicels are 10–45 mm long, lengthening to 35–75 mm in fruit. Anthers project 1–5 mm. The pale-green ovary measures 5–10 × 2–4 mm, and the style projects 3–5 mm. Bluish-grey fruit measures 18–40 × 6–15 mm. The charcoal-grey seeds are about 6.0 × 2.5 × 1.5 mm and broadly winged.

The diagnostic account says *A. maculata* has fewer racemes per inflorescence than *A. branddraaiensis* or *A. swynnertonii*. Pale-pink flowers occur in *A. branddraaiensis* but are not reported for *A. maculata*; *A. swynnertonii* often has two-coloured flowers, which the account says *A. maculata* lacks. The flowers of *A. swynnertonii* are glossy, while those of *A. maculata* are less shiny and lack a waxy bloom. Differences from *A. mudenensis* are referred to that species’ account.

The habitat account places the species in grassland where the grass may be dense enough to nearly crowd out other herbaceous plants, or sparse with much bare soil between tufts. Substrates range from consolidated beach dunes to clay and stones, at elevations from sea level to high mountains.
