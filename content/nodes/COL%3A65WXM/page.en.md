## Allobates ignotus beside streams {#primary-intro}
Allobates ignotus is a small frog described from Colombia’s Serranía de Perijá. Its lateral markings help distinguish adults, while later fieldwork connected streamside sightings with a description of free-swimming larvae. What can each sample tell us?

## Small adults and a changing outline {#primary-body}
The original diagnosis reports adult males of 16.4–18.3 mm snout–vent length (n = 3) and females of 16.9–20.9 mm (n = 7). This length runs from snout to cloacal opening. The diagnosis combines a pale dorsolateral stripe, a ventrolateral stripe and an oblique series of pale spots. These different markings should not be simplified into three identical stripes.

## Sightings along Canime Creek {#primary-habitat}
During surveys in 2010–2014, researchers recorded 187 frogs in three Canime Creek sections, beside shallow, low-flow streams with closed-canopy gallery forest. Table 1 lists 76 sightings on leaf litter and 65 on rocks. These are substrates beneath observed frogs, not a measured preference corrected for substrate availability.

## Resemblance is a diagnostic starting point {#primary-comparison}
The 2012 author compared A. ignotus especially with A. wayuu, distinguishing their lateral markings and ventral colour patterns. Morphological resemblance here supplies a comparison for identification, not a tested sister-group topology.

## Eleven free-swimming larvae {#primary-study}
The later study described 11 larvae from La Veguita Creek at Gosner stages 25–29, a numbered developmental scheme. Their oval bodies had translucent skin through which intestines were visible. Identification relied on matching metamorphosing frogs at the site and finding no other dendrobatoid frog there; it was not a genetic identification.

## Accepted name and placement {#s-9d2fc8e754}
COL26.8 accepts Allobates ignotus Anganoy-Criollo, 2012 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Aromobatidae (family) → Allobates (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## Scope of this partial account {#s-2e2f99c85f}
This partial account combines the primary species treatment with later streamside and larval observations assigned to Allobates ignotus. Adult diagnosis, site observations, and larval assignment come from distinct samples and methods; the larvae were associated indirectly through metamorphic animals observed at the site, not DNA identification. The account does not establish population-wide ecology or a complete life history.
