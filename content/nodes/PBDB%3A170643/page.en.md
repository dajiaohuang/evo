## A holotype without a skull {#s-cf8938f649}
Holotype EME PV.313 comes from Maastrichtian deposits at Sebeş, Romania. It is a partial articulated skeleton of one animal, preserving trunk and tail vertebrae, girdles and many limb bones but no skull. The 2010 description also lists referred limb bones from Tuştea, possibly from another, larger individual. Holotype material, referred bones and records from two collections are different units.

## Fused hand bones and a reduced third digit {#s-56afb6782b}
Wrist and metacarpal bones form a fused carpometacarpus, and the third manual digit is strongly reduced; the 2015 reassessment notes that no terminal claw is preserved on that digit. The lower leg and foot also show extensive fusion into a tibiotarsus and tarsometatarsus. The study compares these features with early birds and dromaeosaurids. Fusion can evolve independently in different lineages, so one fused element does not decide affinity.

## Different readings of the enlarged foot claws {#s-a379472c2d}
The 2010 description emphasizes enlarged claws and hyperextension-related anatomy in the first two pedal digits. The 2015 reassessment highlights the absence of a strongly ginglymoid second-metatarsal joint and of the sharply falciform claw and prominent flexor tubercle typical of many dromaeosaurids. This challenges the double-clawed-predator interpretation. The bones do not directly record prey capture or actual toe mobility.

## Dromaeosaurid and early-bird hypotheses {#s-89372c3870}
The original description interprets Balaur as an unusual island dromaeosaurid. In 2015, Cau, Brougham and Naish re-scored two matrices: 152 taxa with 860 characters, and 121 taxa with 1,549 characters. Their most-parsimonious results place it in Avialae. However, forcing a dromaeosaurid placement costs only three or nine additional steps, with no significant difference in Templeton tests. The study favors an avialan position while leaving the alternative insufficiently excluded.

## Diet remains a morphological inference {#s-1672c2079d}
Using the reduced hand, foot anatomy and avialan placement, the 2015 authors propose herbivory or omnivory as more plausible than typical dromaeosaurid predation. They explicitly note the absence of craniodental remains and direct dietary evidence. This remains their hypothesis, without a known food list or a measured ecological process of island adaptation.

## Known and unknown parts of the reconstruction {#s-594b4e6f85}
Figure 7 of the 2015 paper marks known bones in white and unknown parts in grey, explicitly as a speculative reconstruction. Head anatomy, teeth and plumage are not directly preserved in this specimen. The authors infer feathers by comparison and interpret reduced forelimbs as potentially secondary flightlessness. A complete image does not fill the material gaps or directly demonstrate flight in its ancestors.

This specimen and taxonomic-dispute guide combines selected original-description sections, the 2015 re-scoring study and fixed PBDB occurrences. The holotype lacks a skull; diet and flightlessness remain interpretations, and this batch does not adjudicate competing affinities.
