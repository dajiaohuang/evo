## What a Devonian shark preserves—and what it cannot date {#s-42bbed5c96}
Several partly articulated Maghriboselache mohamezanei skeletons from Morocco’s Famennian Thylacocephalan Layer preserve three-dimensional braincases, a broad cartilage-enclosed snout, widely separated nasal capsules, large pectoral fins and a high-aspect-ratio tail. One specimen, PIMUZ A/I 5155, contains a roughly 40 mm actinopterygian in its stomach, recording a feeding event.

The sample documents unusual sensory and body-plan anatomy, but most skeletons lack the tail end and some skull regions are compressed. Body length, sex and ecology therefore remain bounded interpretations. This Moroccan record is not a global first appearance, a direct ancestor, or a date for the origin of crown sharks.
