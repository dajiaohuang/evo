## Name and classification {#s-ffc2c662dc}
The COL26.8 release of 20 August 2026 lists *Haddadus binotatus* (Spix, 1824) as a species in Anura, family Craugastoridae, genus Haddadus. This identifies the name used in that catalogue; it does not by itself show that every historical record under the same name belongs to the same biological species.

## Development and egg-laying site in an Atlantic Forest compilation {#s-73fbbe6857}
A trait compilation for the Atlantic Forest codes *Haddadus binotatus* as direct-developing, with terrestrial oviposition. The study defines direct development as a life cycle without a free-living larva: a froglet hatches instead of a free-living tadpole. This does not mean embryonic development is absent or that eggs do not need humidity. These are literature-compiled categories, not a new observation of spawning or a developmental experiment on this species. The source taxonomy has not been matched exactly to COL26.8.

## Juvenile habitat and egg-site codes {#s-4bdf550e26}
The same row codes juvenile habitat as terrestrial and places eggs on the ground, rocks, or in burrows within its seven oviposition-site categories. These broad codes do not identify a particular nest structure, substrate species, or egg-laying height. The juvenile-habitat code does not establish adult habitat. This compiled record adds no new evidence about clutch size, egg dimensions, parental attendance, spawning season, or offspring survival.

## Length entries include inferred values and uncertain units {#s-207f53e3f7}
The workbook marks head width and tibia length for this row as inferred, not measured on a new specimen. The study filled missing traits using a phylogenetic maximum-likelihood method; across its full species pool, it inferred 139 head widths and 146 tibia lengths. This row retains values for body length, head width, and tibia length. The Methods use means when available and otherwise maxima, but the workbook length headers do not state units and do not consistently identify sex or life stage. These entries are not new diagnostic millimetre measurements, a sex-specific range, or measured species-wide variation. The upstream measurements have not been independently checked.

## Host association in a 2025 Molineidae review {#s-a65e3a59bb}
The 2025 review lists *Haddadus binotatus* as a host in its Molineidae checklist and associates that written host name with *Schulzia travassosi*. This is a literature-compiled association across studies, not a newly examined sample or evidence that every frog is infected. The host also appears in additional-host entries in the parasite accounts. The cited reports and archived specimens have not been independently reidentified. The checklist gives no count of frogs examined for this association, prevalence, infection intensity, pathology, or mortality.

## Locality reported for the host entry {#s-cf360b28e2}
The host entry associates *Schulzia travassosi* with Angra dos Reis [ANG; Brazil (BRA)]. Codes are expanded only where the inspected methods define them; other abbreviations remain unresolved. This is a locality for the host association, not the parasite's full distribution or the frog's complete range. The reports come from historical literature and do not establish current occupancy, native or introduced status, or absence of the parasite elsewhere. A matching frozen accepted name links the names but does not authenticate every historical host under the current species concept.

## Scope of the bibliographic survey {#s-f94b9af4f0}
The review searched BioOne, ISI, Jstor, PubMed, Scielo, Scopus, Web of Science, and monographs for regional Molineidae literature from 1819 to 2025. It excluded incomplete records, unpublished theses and meeting data, and parasite records not identified to species. The authors also sought species-level molecular records in the literature and GenBank. The inspected methods do not give search strings or database search dates, so a missing entry is not a reproducible finding that no record exists. The review covers Caribbean, Panamanian, and Neotropical regions using its stated divisions; this page reports named host entries rather than a reconstructed global parasite inventory. Supplementary Table S1, deposited sequences, and original source material have not been independently reviewed.
