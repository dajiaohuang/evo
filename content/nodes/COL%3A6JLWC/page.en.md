## The name and this data account {#s-d21489db40}
Fregetta tropica is accepted in COL26.8 within Oceanitidae. AVONET v7 records it under the HBW–BirdLife 2020 name. The selected record contains five measured individuals, four with a complete morphological set; the usable sample can differ by trait.

## Measured beak dimensions {#s-74420eb306}
The compiled beak means are 8.2 mm from the front edge of the nostril to the tip, 3 mm wide and 4.4 mm deep at that edge. Length, width and depth are separate measures taken at standard landmarks, not identification thresholds.

## Wing, tarsus and mass {#s-185bb50d61}
Mean unflattened wing length, measured from the carpal joint to the tip of the longest primary, is 164.4 mm; mean tarsus length is 39.3 mm. The separately compiled species mean for mass is 52 g, attributed to Dunning. The five morphology specimens are not necessarily the individuals represented in that mass estimate.

## Habitat, feeding and lifestyle {#s-32811a97f1}
AVONET assigns the habitat category Marine, trophic niche Aquatic predator and primary lifestyle Aerial. These broad categories support comparisons across records; they do not imply exclusive use of marine habitat or one food type.

## Movement and geographical summary {#s-675adc1a73}
AVONET classifies the record as migratory. Its historical breeding and resident maps span latitude endpoints -63.34° to -43.7°. These endpoints do not give a complete account of non-breeding movement or current occupancy, and the map summary comes from a different compilation step than the morphology measurements.

## Evidence limits {#s-7ddd8b65c7}
AVONET v7 reports species averages from this historical record, not individual ranges or identification diagnoses. Four of the five measured birds have a complete morphological set, but that count does not apply to every trait. The 52 g mass estimate is separately attributed to Dunning and has its own source scope. Marine habitat, Aquatic predator niche, Aerial lifestyle and migratory status are broad categories; the latitude endpoints describe historical breeding and resident maps only. The name association does not establish identity with later species concepts. Reproduction, current populations and conservation status require other sources.
