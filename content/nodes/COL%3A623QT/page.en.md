## A fossil record and a living-tree revision {#s-2da9f10f82}
Two lines of evidence meet in Fabaceae: a 65.35 Ma fruit and leaflets from Corral Bluffs, and a broad phylogeny that revises the classification of living legumes into six subfamilies. They answer different questions about the family.

## What the evidence supports {#s-4886698695}
The Corral Bluffs fossils support a family-level occurrence, not a subfamily assignment or crown age. The living-family phylogeny revises classification but does not identify the fossil’s placement, a family origin centre or continuous global occupancy.

## The fossil documents family presence {#s-4b311a879f}
A fruit and associated leaflets from Corral Bluffs in Colorado are assigned to Fabaceae and dated to 65.35 Ma. These preserved plant parts make a bounded fossil record for the family. They do not assign the specimen to one of the living subfamilies, set the family’s crown age, or show where Fabaceae first originated.

## A phylogeny reorganizes living legumes {#s-c94722aa57}
The Legume Phylogeny Working Group assembled broad taxonomic sampling to support six subfamilies and replace the traditional, broadly defined Caesalpinioideae arrangement. This is a classification of sampled living legumes. It does not place the Corral Bluffs fossil in a subfamily, date the family’s origin or turn the fossil into an ancestor of modern plants.
