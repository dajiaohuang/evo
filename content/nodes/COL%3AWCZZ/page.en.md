## How does the record describe Clusia candelabrum? {#s-53708dd99e}
The plant has a taproot and additional roots, and its stem surface is not recorded as peeling in rings. Its stalked, leathery leaves are obovate, oblanceolate, or elliptic, with inconspicuous secretory channels. Pendulous flower clusters or solitary flowers are recorded. Flowers have four or five sepals and five petals, white at the base and red at the tip. Male flowers have more than 40 stamens joined only at the base, resin-bearing stamens or staminodes, and a pistillode; the ovary has five chambers. At maturity, sepals remain on the fruit while petals fall; staminode persistence varies in the record. Seeds have an aril, a fleshy covering.
