## What is recorded about its root parasitism and flowers? {#s-8d5a721902}
Hyobanche sanguinea is described as a root parasite with scale-like leaves and blunt bracteoles. Its crimson or pink flowers are hooded and densely hairy, with stamens hidden inside the tubular flower.

## Which ground settings are recorded? {#s-5c9992a0c0}
The habitat field lists sandy slopes and flats. This is a broad setting, not a collection locality or complete range.

## What historical observations accompany the description? {#s-a13853fa68}
The regional account notes that farmers in the Queenstown district used the plant to make ink and called it “Ink-plant” (“Boioker”). It says the calyx lobes may fuse in groups of two or three, giving the calyx an almost two-lipped appearance, and that the surface hairs are bead-like. It also records a colored drawing made by Masson at the Cape of Good Hope in 1775, then held at the British Museum.
