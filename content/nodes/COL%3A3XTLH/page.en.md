## Overview {#s-2787d4a192}
COL26.8 accepts Mandrillus sphinx (Linnaeus, 1758) as species ID 3XTLH under genus ID 62ZM2; MDD v2.5 supplies the display name Mandrill. The ecological studies below come from different Gabonese sites, study periods and sampling units.

## Field ecology {#s-78877053d9}
Lahm’s 1986 study drew on observations and feeding remains collected in northeastern and central Gabon from November 1982 to October 1983. Fruit was the main dietary item, alongside other plant parts, insects and occasional small vertebrates; observations also covered primary, secondary, riparian and inundated forests. This is historical multi-site evidence, not a current range-wide diet profile.

## Home-range research {#s-9159e0ac67}
White et al. used radiotelemetry to follow one wild horde of about 700 mandrills in Lopé National Park for six years. Its documented home range covered 182 km², including 89 km² of suitable forest; the horde used gallery forests and isolated forest patches intensively and avoided savanna and marsh. This exceptional horde does not define a typical group or species-wide range.

## Seasonal feeding {#s-5150971984}
At Moukalaba–Doudou National Park, Hongo et al. measured fruit availability and camera-trap visits on 11 transects in 2012–2013 and analyzed feces collected over 25 months in 2009–2013. During the fruit-scarce season, feces contained more woody tissue and a wider variety of food types, while visits were distributed more evenly among transects. These seasonal results are local to that study area.

## Different stages in the mass fields {#s-73f970038d}
Historical summary values are adult body mass 23000 g; mass at birth or hatching 613 g. Adult mass can combine males, females and adults of unspecified sex; birth/hatching mass describes a different life stage. These are not a longitudinal series of the same individuals or a species-wide range of individual masses.

## Relative weights of food categories {#s-69bda6fe9d}
The archive’s nonzero food weights are fruit 40%; seeds, nuts and spores included in this category 30%; invertebrates 10%; unspecified vertebrates 10%; other plant material (the source category also includes fungi) 10%. Code ABC pools high, reasonable and partial confidence; some estimates can borrow from an ecologically similar congener. ABC cannot be rewritten as direct observations of this species. These percentages standardize verbal descriptions rather than measure diet composition; zero does not prove an item is never eaten. Categories such as Nect, Seed and PlantO are broader than their everyday names and cannot imply exclusively nectar, seeds or plants.

## Evidence limits {#s-ad441e1599}
The pages cited here describe different places, years and groups; the Lopé horde is a single exceptional social unit. These records are not combined into a species-wide population, home range or shared genus-level behavior. The Chinese display name is editorial.
