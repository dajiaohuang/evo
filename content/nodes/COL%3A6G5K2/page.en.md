## A lancelet known from a small atoll sample {#summary}
Cooper’s original account described Heteropleuron maldivense from four specimens collected at Mahlosmadulu Atoll in the Maldives. It emphasized a broad, shovel-shaped oral hood and the form of the dorsal and ventral fins; the small sample and later, poorly preserved Zanzibar material set limits on the comparison.

## A webbed oral hood and fin rays {#form}
In Cooper’s four Maldive specimens, the dorsal fin carried closely spaced rays, about three to four per myotome. The ventral fin continued from the right metapleural fold and had single fin rays, unlike the paired rays Cooper attributed to Heteropleuron bassanum. The broad, shovel-shaped oral hood bore 23 cirri: most were webbed about halfway up, while the last four on each side were small and free. These are observations from the original species account, not a modern reassessment.

## Four specimens from Mahlosmadulu Atoll {#atoll-record}
The original description reports four specimens from Mahlosmadulu Atoll. The largest measured 2 cm. Cooper reported finding no adults or larvae in Minikoi’s lagoon despite searches there in June, July, August and September; that is a result of the reported survey, not proof of absence throughout the island or at other times. The account describes fin and body form but does not establish a broader life history.

## A comparison with an incompletely known relative {#comparison}
Cooper considered the Maldive form most similar to Heteropleuron bassanum from Bass Strait, while distinguishing it by smaller size and fin shape. He also stated that the anatomy of H. bassanum had not been described, so he could not tell whether it shared the other anatomical features observed in the Maldive animals. This is a historical comparative diagnosis, not a tested phylogenetic placement.

## Later Zanzibar specimens had preservation limits {#later-material}
After the initial account, Cooper examined additional Heteropleuron maldivense specimens collected at Zanzibar by Crossland. He reported that these extended the observed size range, but the specimens were poorly preserved: some gonads had broken away and the position of others was uncertain. Their measurements therefore do not remove the limits of the original four-specimen description.
