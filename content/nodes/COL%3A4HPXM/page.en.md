## urban field study {#s-14e4a5cb7d}
In a 2022 breeding-season study, researchers approached ground-foraging Eurasian magpies in public parks and green areas in Prague. After excluding sites with only one observation, the analysis covered 167 birds (138 adults and 29 juveniles) at 11 sites. Noise level did not significantly predict alert distance, the point at which a bird showed alert behavior. Higher ambient noise was associated with a lower flight-initiation-distance-to-alert-distance ratio (FID/AD), which the authors interpreted as a slower escape response after alerting. The study measured behavior during human approaches; it did not test whether noise caused the response or affected survival or reproduction.
