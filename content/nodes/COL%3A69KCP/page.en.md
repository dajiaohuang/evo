## Habitat context {#s-bf3d984033}
At Nyungwe National Park in Rwanda, a 2008–2009 survey focused on 32 km² of bamboo and bamboo–forest mosaic near Nshili, at 2,031–2,624 m. Eight 1-km transects were repeatedly walked, contributing to 185 km of survey effort. Researchers made 15 direct group observations, with a mean minimum group count of 3.6 and an encounter rate of 0.081 groups per kilometre. Dense bamboo, terrestrial habits and secretive behaviour make line-transect counts likely to miss animals or underestimate group size; these results describe the Nyungwe site, not the whole species.
