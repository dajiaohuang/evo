## Five amphibian fossils, five evidence limits {#s-215de6e07f}
Five fossils show how anatomy, age, and classification can answer different questions about amphibian history. Stem groups, living crown groups, direct ancestry, and the earliest known global record remain separate claims.

## A mosaic outside the living crown {#s-92ba4e4ba2}
The Gerobatrachus holotype combines frog-like and salamander-like features in the original analysis. Its placement supports a stem batrachian, outside the living crown group of frogs and salamanders; it does not make this fossil a direct ancestor of living species.

## CT scans reveal a longer trunk {#s-b45b592178}
Micro-CT scans of the unique Triadobatrachus holotype reveal caudal vertebrae and a long trunk. The anatomy supports stem Salientia, outside crown Anura, and does not show the specialized jumping seen in living frogs.

## Many individuals, separate bones {#s-e9c9732cb6}
A dated bonebed contains elements from at least 76 Funcusvermis individuals. The sample supports Gymnophionomorpha, while the animal remains outside crown Gymnophiona, the group containing living caecilians.

## Articulated salamanders below a dated flow {#s-4b6ae0a076}
Specimens PKUP V0601–V0606 preserve articulated salamandroid skeletons beneath a volcanic flow. Zircon dating of the overlying rock supports an Oxfordian record; neoteny, or retention of juvenile features, is an interpretation rather than a directly observed life history.

## A provisional comparison with living caecilians {#s-d154e0a7b0}
DGM 1462-R supports an Oligocene caecilian compared with living Typhlonectidae. A formal phylogenetic test and an unambiguous family-level shared feature are lacking, so the family assignment remains provisional.
