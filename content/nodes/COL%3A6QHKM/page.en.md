## What the length numbers measure {#s-e0f6c26ec8}
The row reports a maximum snout–vent length (SVL) of 56.8 mm and a female maximum of 48.6 mm. SVL measures from the snout to the vent and excludes the tail. These compiled maxima are neither typical sizes nor biological ceilings. The two sex-specific values may come from different studies or places; by themselves they do not measure how much males and females differ within one population.

## What this record leaves unanswered {#s-03487fc8bf}

It therefore cannot describe the species' full body, habitat, or breeding. Related species and empty fields cannot fill these gaps.

## Limits of this account {#s-903c2e1ab5}
This row supports two length values, not a full description of the species' body or ecology. The page follows the same-name ReptTraits v1-2 entry, retaining its 2023 name framework and trait-specific citations. One row compresses differences in sex, age, locality, and population; the same name and family do not prove that a historical record matches every later taxonomic concept. The reported maximum is not an absolute size limit. ReptTraits also includes mass estimates derived from length equations; they are not presented here as measured mass. Juvenile-length fields have differing headers and definitions, so they are not used as conclusions. This page does not update conservation status, venom, current distribution, or population trends. The citation labels retain the dataset's source chain; they do not mean this project independently rechecked every original study.
