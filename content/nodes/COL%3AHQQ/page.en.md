## Compare body size and life history {#s-81b6628735}
Among available consolidated adult-mass values in this batch, Helarctos malayanus records 57075.78 g and Ursus maritimus records 371703.81 g. These are endpoints of historical medians adjusted for sex. Continue to fetal development, weaning, activity and group fields after reading their definitions; body-size differences alone do not establish a shared lifestyle for all descendants.

## Life-history fields need their own definitions {#s-a57b5baa4f}
The separate life-history route records adult mass, age at first conception, offspring per litter or clutch and the interval from conception to birth. These fields answer different questions and may have different source conditions. In particular, age at first conception is not interchangeable with age at first birth or sexual maturity.

## Diet weights and activity codes are not observations {#s-cec09c0fa7}
EltonTraits records food-category weights, foraging strata and activity periods for historical bear entries. The weights translate descriptions in literature into coarse relative values, while strata and activity are coded categories. They summarize the source material; they are not direct measures of the behavior of every bear.

## Three historical routes retain different limits {#s-8834a39739}
PanTHERIA body-mass summaries, the life-history archive and EltonTraits use separate selection and name-matching rules. Their historical entries do not establish current species equivalence or a complete family account. For the 2015 Amniote archive, non-longevity values are medians or interpolated medians; maximum longevity is the longest recorded value, not an average. EltonTraits is semiquantitative, and some mammalian ABC fields can borrow information from a congener.

## First reproduction and longest life are distinct {#s-55ce603f33}
The archive separates gestation, female age at first reproduction and maximum lifespan. First reproduction is not the same event as first birth or sexual maturity; maximum lifespan is the longest record collected, not an average. Different fields may reflect different source conditions, and missing data need another source.

## Limits of these historical data {#s-1da5d6670c}
This entry covers this batch’s authored historical life-history records, not every accepted descendant or complete group biology. Species accounts use matching names and families plus a one-to-one MSW93/MSW05 same-name table; equivalence to current species concepts remains unproven. Except for maximum lifespan, relevant fields generally average literature sources with equal weighting. Inputs can include published averages, individual values or range midpoints when other data are unavailable. Sample sizes do not determine weights, and underlying literature may be reused. Maximum lifespan is only the longest record, not typical lifespan. Adult mass is not interpreted in this batch, and relatives do not fill missing fields. Original reference numbers remain on each page; underlying species literature and all wild, captive and locality conditions were not individually established here.

## Ursidae in the living-carnivoran tree {#living-tree}
The cited extant-carnivoran supertree recovers Ursidae as one living family branch. Its family-level relationships and crown dates are estimates assembled from gene trees and calibrations, and extinct stem bears are not comprehensively sampled.
