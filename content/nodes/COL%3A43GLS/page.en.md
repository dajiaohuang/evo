## Stem surface {#s-40d733b177}
The Brazilian Flora 2020 record describes a strigose stem surface, with stiff hairs pressed against it and pointing backward. Simple hairs are present; branched and unbranched bristles, glandular hairs, prickles, and short lateral shoots (brachyblasts) are absent.

## Leaves and inflorescences {#s-252a7dfbd5}
Stipules, small structures at leaf bases, are persistent. Extrafloral nectaries (nectar-secreting glands outside flowers) and projections between pairs of pinnae (the leaf’s primary side divisions) are absent. The inflorescence, or flower cluster, is globose; its floral bracts (small leaf-like structures) are shorter than the flowers before they open (anthesis).

## Flowers {#s-e2df1b7291}
The perianth (calyx and corolla together) is recorded as having four parts. The membranous calyx, the outer sepal whorl, is marked as lacking lobes. Corolla (petal-whorl) lobes have one to three veins and a strigose covering of stiff, straight hairs lying close to the surface. The androecium (the stamens collectively) is isostemonous, with stamens equal in number to the petals; the filaments are pink.

## Habitat category {#s-b584486fdb}
The regional dataset labels the habitat “Terrícola,” Portuguese for terrestrial. This category does not provide a collection locality or establish the species’ full range.
