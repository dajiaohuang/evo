## Larval and adult categories differ {#s-50ee5743e7}
The compilation codes A. coppingeri as larval LO (lotic, associated with flowing water) and adult TER (terrestrial). These labels stay with their respective life stages. Lentic (still-water), lotic and torrential codes describe broad habitat associations, not measured flow tolerance; “rock-associated” and “terrestrial” do not define a geographic range. Endotrophic tadpoles are categorized separately from direct development, so the label does not remove the larval stage. The authors compiled these assignments from species accounts rather than experimentally measuring feeding, water-flow response, movement or habitat choice.

## A cross-species pattern is not this species' trajectory {#s-2ff0f13201}
Across the sampled species, larval–adult shape dissimilarities had a weak but significant Mantel association (r = 0.16, p = 0.003), and disparity correlations varied among families. The authors interpret the overall pattern as evidence for adaptive decoupling, but it does not show zero correlation, genetic independence, an experimentally selected trait or A. coppingeri's own developmental trajectory. Phylogenetic analyses included 74 of 83 species; ancestral shape and timing are model reconstructions from living-species data, not measurements of fossils. Adult K = 0.44 (p = 0.001) indicates less resemblance among close relatives than the reference expectation. The paper's phrase “not phylogenetically structured” therefore does not mean the test was nonsignificant. Supplement S3 reports family sample sizes that sum to 112 for both stages; without the raw matrices, adult denominators and imputed cells cannot be checked.
