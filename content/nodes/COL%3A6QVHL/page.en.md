## Roots and leaves {#s-f16492b7f4}
The Brazilian Flora 2020 record allows roots to be buttressed or not and describes the stem as glabrous. Leaves are alternate and odd-pinnate. Axillary buds are elliptic or oblong, 6–9 mm long; the leaf rachis has pulvini (swollen joints). Each leaf is listed with 7–13 alternate leaflets, lanceolate, elliptic-lanceolate, or ovate-lanceolate, glabrous and somewhat leathery. Leaflets are 6–15.3 cm long and 1.4–5 cm wide. Their bases are obtuse, heart-shaped, or truncate; their tips acute, acuminate, or cuspidate (with a sharp point).

## Inflorescences {#s-9e26291b30}
The inflorescence is a terminal thyrse, a branched flower cluster. It is elongated and densely flowered, with the flowers arranged in two ranks (distichous). Its bracts are deciduous.

## Flowers {#s-8e8e6706ba}
Flower buds are erect and 1.4–2 cm long. Each flower has five free, unequal, lance-shaped sepals, pubescent and brown to olive. The five petals are free, yellow, obovate, and glabrous. There are 4–7 free stamens, with zero or one staminode (sterile stamen), rarely two to five. Anthers are elongated, unequal, glabrous, erect, yellow to cream, attached at the base (basifixed), and release pollen through pores (poricidal); the filaments are reduced. The gynoecium has one carpel, rarely two. The ovary is sessile, pubescent, compressed, and has one ovule; the stigma is papillate.

## Fruit {#s-5874756548}
The fruit is an indehiscent samara: a dry, winged fruit that does not split open at maturity. It is leathery, glabrous, one-seeded, oblong, and compressed from side to side, with an oblique base and a well-developed wing. The source lists a length of 11–19 cm and a width of 4–8 cm.

## Habitat category {#s-9032abfbbd}
The regional dataset labels the habitat “Terrícola,” Portuguese for terrestrial. This category does not give a collection locality or establish the species’ full range.
