## Río Zuñag cloud-forest records {#s-f205a854ed}
The description knew the species from Río Zuñag Ecological Reserve in the upper Pastaza basin, Ecuador, within the southeastern buffer area of Llanganates National Park. Reported elevations were 1300–2190 m in montane cloud forest. These reported records do not establish a complete current range or physiological elevation limits.

## Several vegetation substrates above the ground {#s-e0a588b083}
Specimens were found within mature forest on herbs, shrubs, palms, ferns, bromeliads and aroids, 1–4.5 m above ground. These collection perches describe the surveyed specimens rather than an exclusive height band or demonstrated dependence on one plant group.

## Amplexus and egg deposition after capture {#s-28ff67a6d9}
One amplectant pair was found in January 2012; the female laid a clutch in a field bag while being transported to camp. Two further amplectant pairs and an adult female were found in October 2017. Bag deposition is retained as an observation after capture, without identifying the natural nest site, and the two dated encounters do not define a complete annual breeding season.

## A downstream molecular signal is not a local range extension {#s-b4faa7b48c}
The Tena study assigned water-derived molecular units to Pristimantis mallii without observing the frog at its six monitoring sites. It places this taxon among mountain frogs associated with the higher Colonso-Chalupas exploration area, reported at 1,518 m elevation. The authors interpret the downstream detections as possible signals from outside the sampled sites and call them false positives for site-level presence. They are not new live-animal records establishing a lower elevational range. The paper itself gives inconsistent elevation descriptions for the monitoring area—600–710 m in Study site versus below 600 m in the Discussion—so an exact lower elevation is not certified here.

## Transport and contamination remain different explanations {#s-aa3be3c349}
Downstream transport of genetic material is the authors’ preferred explanation for the Pristimantis mallii signal. Rainfall, drainage and slope provide context, but the study did not directly trace DNA from an upstream individual to a downstream sample. Reported negative field, amplification and sequencing controls reduce detected contamination concerns; they do not logically exclude every cross-contamination event, index misassignment or sequencing error. Species assignments used a short 16S match threshold above 97%, an operational rule rather than a universal species-delimitation test.
