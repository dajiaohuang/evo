## What does the brief description emphasize? {#s-282b435b13}
One source describes Plantago major as a perennial herb whose leaves form a rosette, with elliptic, egg-shaped, or inversely egg-shaped blades on petioles (leaf stalks). It notes scattered hairs, greenish flowers, and 15–25 seeds in each capsule.

## What does the Panama account add? {#s-7de266c7f3}
A 2013 Flora of Panama account describes plants 10–50 cm tall at flowering, with a short rhizome and mostly shallow, fibrous roots. Leaves may reach 20 cm wide and often look wrinkled in living plants. The small flowers form a long spike. Its capsule opens around the middle, and Panamanian material usually had 14–18 seeds (the account gives a wider range of 9–25).

## What habitat does the source mention? {#s-45e47de49d}
The source calls it a weed of moist, sunny places. This is a habitat description, not a survey of its full ecological range.

## What comparison does the source make? {#s-ad8abdddf6}
The 2013 account says much Old World temperate material had fewer, slightly larger seeds and more conical capsules than the Panamanian material it discusses. This is a comparison between described materials, not a statement of the species’ range.
