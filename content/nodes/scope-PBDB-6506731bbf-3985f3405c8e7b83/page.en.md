## What the evidence supports {#s-f9b1edb207}
The prey remains are direct evidence from a single Šárka Formation specimen. They do not establish prey choice, feeding rate or a genus-wide diet, and the 466–463 Ma interval is formation context rather than a global range.

## The gut preserves an identifiable meal {#s-c7fabc7de5}
Synchrotron microtomography, a three-dimensional X-ray imaging method, reveals the alimentary tract in inventory specimen 8. Inside it are remains identified as ostracods, hyoliths and stylophorans. These fossils record what was present in this one meal and make a rare specimen-level dietary observation; they do not show how often the trilobite fed or which prey it usually selected.

## One specimen has a narrow geographic and taxonomic scope {#s-5064ada44a}
The animal comes from the Šárka Formation in the Prague Basin, with a formation-level interval of 466–463 Ma. The profile retains the specimen’s identification as Bohemolichas incola while noting that PBDB treats Bohemolichas as a subjective synonym of Uralichas. The gut contents do not settle that broader naming question or establish a genus-wide distribution.
