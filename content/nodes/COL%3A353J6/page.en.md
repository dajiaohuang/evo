## Climbing stems and segmented fruit {#s-f7668093ad}
A Brazilian Flora 2020 regional entry records no xylopodium, a woody underground structure. The stems climb, and the branches are described as hooked and four-angled. Leaves are alternate, each with three ovate to elliptic leaflets; their secondary veins reach the edge. Stipules are free and deciduous, neither ear-shaped nor stem-clasping. Lilac flowers grow in pseudo-racemose or paniculate clusters; their stamens are described as pseudo-monadelphous, with the filaments appearing grouped together. The fruit is a loment, a segmented pod with uniform triangular sections joined at the margin. It is not twisted and bears hooked hairs. These traits come from one regional dataset row, not a complete or global description.

## A terrestrial habitat label {#s-c93d549875}
A separate habitat field in the same dataset labels the species terrestrial. The field gives a habitat category only; it supplies no locality, range or sampling coverage.
