## Trilobites and chelicerates: evidence, models and disputed relationships {#s-ce3d289eb3}
Across trilobites, horseshoe crabs, scorpions and other fossils, each study asks a different question. Read anatomy and reconstructed family trees separately; the sequence is not an ancestor ladder.

## A model tests the trilobite’s abrupt fossil appearance {#s-95fb559ecb}
Parsimony and Bayesian tree analyses of early artiopodans—arthropods related to trilobites—place the inferred trilobite origin shortly before the oldest widespread trilobite body fossils. Tree shape, clock priors, character coding and the uneven survival of fossils all affect that interval. A modelled origin is not itself a fossil occurrence or a known ancestor.

## Ash preserves a view beneath the trilobite {#s-cc281829de}
X-ray microtomography reveals the underside of articulated trilobites preserved in ash. The scans show antennae, four pairs of head limbs behind the antennae, a labrum, a slit-like mouth and digestive tissues. This rare local window informs anatomical comparisons; it does not establish feeding behavior or anatomy for every trilobite.

## Does a limb branch preserve a gill? {#s-29f2527d8f}
Triarthrus filaments have a narrow center with inflated margins, while Olenoides preserves part of a body-wall attachment. Those structures can be compared with flattened branches used for respiration in other animals. Gill function, blood flow and limb homology remain interpretations from two taxa, not direct measurements of gas exchange.

## One gut preserves one last meal {#s-436dfbc1d8}
Synchrotron microtomography maps ostracod shells, hyolith material and stylophoran plates through inventory specimen 8 at 11.35-micrometre voxel size. The shelly contents suggest rapid, broad feeding and a neutral-to-alkaline gut, but both are inferences from one preserved meal. Soft foods and variation among individuals are absent from this sample.

## Adult agnostids test the trilobite relationship {#s-ba905eafa1}
Adult Peronopsis and Ptychagnostus preserve two-branched limbs and anatomy used in enrolment. An explicit character matrix recovers them near polymeroid trilobites, but that result depends on sampled taxa and characters. Crustacean-like limb form and inferred life habit can arise through convergence and remain open to interpretation.

## Urokodia links different appendage forms {#s-8b60e40733}
Three-dimensional reconstructions of Urokodia show short, pincer-like great appendages, overlapping exite flaps and a seven-segmented head. The proposed homologies and its placement high on the chelicerate stem depend on how the fossil is reconstructed and scored. It is a possible morphological bridge, not a settled ancestor in a linear sequence.

## Chelicerae enter the Cambrian fossil record {#s-43d9256db0}
The Chengjiang specimen KUMIP 314091a,b preserves large chelicerae, five limb pairs behind them on the prosoma, and lamella-bearing appendages on the rear body. These features support its identification and comparisons with other Cambrian arthropods. A predatory role and a bridging position between habeliids and synziphosurines are interpretations drawn from one specimen and a phylogenetic matrix.

## Nerves and limbs give competing signals {#s-b45595abd8}
MCZ 1811 and USNM 305093 preserve optic nerves, a possible compact head nerve center called a synganglion, and a segmented ventral nerve cord. The front of the fossils is incomplete, and nervous-system characters do not align simply with appendage characters. Several placements and mosaic-evolution scenarios therefore remain possible.

## A giant claw does not measure the whole animal {#s-6f5b44a0cb}
A free cheliceral ramus with associated cuticle directly documents a giant pterygotid claw from the Early Devonian Willwerath sample. The often-cited body length of about 2.5 metres assumes that the claw-to-body proportions resembled those of comparable animals and that positive allometry was limited. It is an estimate from one appendage, not a measurement of a complete skeleton.

## Xiphosuran relationships enter an expanded matrix {#s-40f46425ff}
The analysis samples fossil and living horseshoe crabs, or xiphosurans, and rescored other living and fossil chelicerates in an expanded character matrix. It challenges the traditional grouping of synziphosurines and revises relationships within Xiphosura. Those tree results remain sensitive to missing anatomy, coding choices and which taxa are included.

## Internal anatomy does not settle habitat {#s-f9aac0a641}
Scorpion holotype UWGM 2162 and paratype UWGM 2163 preserve a scorpion body plan and internal structures compared with respiratory and circulatory anatomy. The proposed homology with book lungs and the implications for life on land are interpretations. Nearshore deposits, transport and incomplete respiratory structures do not establish where the animal lived.

## Genomes have not fixed one arachnid root {#s-e6bd4010d2}
Large datasets of living species and subsets of slowly evolving genes produce competing trees: some recover Arachnida as a group, while others place horseshoe crabs among land-living chelicerates. Gene choice, uneven sequence composition, species sampling, morphology and model fit can change the result. The root and the number or timing of transitions onto land remain model-dependent.
