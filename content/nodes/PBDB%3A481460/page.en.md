## Three skull roofs, not a whole fish {#s-7f22ff980b}
Ruedersdorfia berlinensis is represented by three ornamented skull roofs from the Lower Anisian Muschelkalk at Rüdersdorf. The fossils support a distinctive skull anatomy, but preserve no body outline from which to reconstruct the whole fish.

## What the evidence supports {#s-dc0d5dad50}
The type and referred skull roofs document a fused, ornamented skull with a diagnostic feature combination. The taxon remains Teleosteomorpha incertae sedis, an uncertain placement within that broader fish lineage; ecology and global range are not established.

## A skull roof supplies the diagnosis {#s-ea23106724}
The holotype MB.f.19910 and paratypes MB.f.14888 and MB.f.19911 are skull roofs, not complete fish skeletons. Their fused roof has an orbital region nearly as long as the postorbital region, a short nasal area and middle pitline, and distinctive surface ornament. These features form a combination used to recognize Ruedersdorfia berlinensis; they do not reveal its full body shape, diet or swimming style.

## The fossils have a place and an uncertain branch {#s-fe559130f0}
The skull roofs come from the Rüdersdorf mine in Germany’s Central European Basin and are placed in the Lower Anisian, at about 244 Ma in the cited study. The authors place the fish within Teleosteomorpha but leave its more exact position uncertain. This local sample neither defines a global range nor establishes an early member as an ancestor of later teleosts.
