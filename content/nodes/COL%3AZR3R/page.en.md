## What traits distinguish Croton sclerocalyx? {#s-d6ed97d11c}
Young branches have star-shaped or outward-radiating star hairs. Short-stalked oval leaves bear star-shaped hairs on both surfaces, looping side veins, and toothed edges. A sessile or nearly sessile nectar gland occurs near the top of the leaf stalk; young branch tips lack leaf-like stipules. Terminal thyrses have unisexual flower clusters near the base. Male flowers are sessile or nearly so and bell-shaped; female flowers are nearly sessile, with separate sepals, a four-part style, and equal calyx lobes. Female petals are threadlike, the bud arrangement is valvate, and the fruit columella has weakly distinct apical lobes.
