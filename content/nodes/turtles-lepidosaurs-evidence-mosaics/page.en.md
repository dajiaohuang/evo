## Nine evidence mosaics in turtle and lepidosaur origins {#s-208a03fae3}
Move from named bones and CT volumes to competing matrices, clock calibrations and explicit Mosasauroidea–Mosasauridae boundaries without turning separate fossils into an ancestor ladder.

## Rib growth helps test how the turtle shell formed {#s-8ba8cbde14}
NHM PV R 4949 preserves successive layers of bone in a broadened Eunotosaurus rib; other specimens preserve shoulder bones. The tissue is observed directly, while its homology, function, and place on the turtle stem are tested interpretations.

## Paired belly bones are not a complete shell {#s-23045b8b9d}
The Vellberg Pappochelys sample combines broad ribs with strong, paired gastralia—belly bones—but has neither a fused plastron nor a complete carapace. Their proposed relationship to shell bones is a homology hypothesis, not a literal stage in an ancestor sequence.

## A plastron is preserved without a complete carapace {#s-d7c71afbb6}
Three Odontochelys specimens preserve a developed plastron, expanded ribs, and neural plates, but no complete carapace. The proposed order of shell formation and a marginal-marine habitat are interpretations; fossils do not show anatomy changing through time.

## One fossil supplies a minimum age, not an origin date {#s-d26076f939}
Caribemys holotype MNHNCu P-3209 supplies a 155.6 Ma hard minimum for crown Testudines in one best-practice analysis. A much older posterior age comes from a relaxed molecular clock and depends on the tree and its priors; it is not a directly dated fossil origin.

## A CT-scanned skull is tested in several trees {#s-772f697d6c}
Micro-CT reveals the mix of cranial features in Taytalura holotype PVSJ 698. Several analyses place it on the lepidosauromorph stem, but one skull does not provide whole-body anatomy, prove direct ancestry, or establish a global range.

## One fossil joins anatomical and molecular datasets {#s-0a0dddb8d1}
CT anatomy of Megachirella PZO 628 was combined with fossil morphology and DNA data from living species. The analysis places it on the squamate stem and estimates deeper divergence times; neither result is a directly observed first appearance of the crown group.

## A near-complete skeleton still combines different traits {#s-8581d2b4e3}
Synchrotron scans of Bellairsia NMS G.2022.1.1 reveal both crown-like and retained features in one Jurassic skeleton. Its stem placement and proposed skull movement depend on character matrices and functional interpretation.

## Researchers disagree about the same fossil {#s-3f937d9c11}
Researchers disagree about the bones, referred material, and character scores for Cryptovaranoides NHMUK PV R36822. Their analyses produce both crown-squamate and non-squamate trees, so the fossil's position remains contested rather than establishing a secure Triassic first appearance for crown squamates.

## A broad mosasaur group differs from the named family {#s-146c0c76f7}
Under the cited definitions, Mosasauroidea is broader than the node-defined family Mosasauridae. Dallasaurus retains relatively unspecialized, plesiopedal limbs, so its family placement and the evolution of fully paddle-shaped limbs are separate questions.
