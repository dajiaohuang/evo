## Aegicetus: anatomy behind a tail-propulsion model {#s-3b7cb98c90}
At WH-203 in Egypt’s Lower Gehannam Formation, holotype CGM 60584 preserves a long rear trunk, unfused sacral vertebrae, no defined iliac auricular surface and shortened limbs. Referred specimen CGM 60583 adds an associated femur and tibia. Together, these bones anchor a model of changing propulsion in early whales.

The shift toward movement driven by the body’s middle and tail is inferred from anatomy, not observed swimming. No tail fluke is preserved, and the paper reports conflicting microfossil and sequence-stratigraphic placements near the Bartonian–Priabonian boundary.
