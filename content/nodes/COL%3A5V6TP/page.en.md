## Gland types describe structure rather than chemical identity {#s-7f110d8be9}
Type I glands are chiefly alveolar, with a small lumen, a single layer of secretory cells and an incomplete myoepithelial covering; they lie near the epidermis. In Anomaloglossus stepheni, type III glands lie beneath type I glands and were observed along the swelling on finger IV and the wrist, as well as finger III without obvious swelling. Their staining indicates proteins without neutral or acidic mucopolysaccharides. The definitions combine morphology and histochemical reactions; they do not identify a particular secreted molecule or demonstrate a physiological effect.

## A proposed reproductive role remains untested {#s-a687a62816}
The authors suggest that protein-containing specialized hand glands could mediate chemical courtship signals during contact with a female's head. They did not characterize the secretions, identify a pheromone, test female responses or establish whether delivery is olfactory or transdermal. Hand location and stain reactions therefore cannot certify that Anomaloglossus stepheni uses a particular chemical mating mechanism, causes ovulation or oviposition, or secretes an adhesive. A lack of these gland types likewise does not prove a species lacks every form of chemical communication. Behavioral observations cited in the discussion come from earlier studies and populations, not new mating experiments in this histological comparison.
