## Distinguish the measured length {#s-174afe3b43}
Recorded dimensions are reported maximum snout–vent length (SVL) 75 mm; female maximum snout–vent length (SVL) 59.3 mm. SVL runs from snout to vent and differs from total length including the tail. Compiled maxima are reported material, not theoretical growth limits or typical individual size. Sex-specific values may come from different localities or studies and alone cannot quantify population sexual dimorphism.

## How to read reproductive records {#s-d9fe7f4e61}
the reproductive-mode field uses the original label oviparous; consolidated mean offspring or eggs per litter/clutch 5.5; lowest reported clutch/litter mean 5; highest reported clutch/litter mean 6. Clutch/litter summaries can use midpoints of reported means or, when means are unavailable, extrema; they are not necessarily arithmetic averages of observed clutches. Fractions do not describe non-integer young in a litter. The lowest/highest fields are defined as endpoints of reported means rather than guaranteed smallest/largest individual clutches. Reproductive and sex-determination labels do not establish fetal nutrition or uniformity across all populations.

## MDHC 283–284: sacral vertebrae {#s-879880dd5a}
Tschopp (2016) examined MDHC 283, 284 as part of a museum skeletal comparison. Vertebral structures were inspected under a light microscope. The material comes from the MDHC collection at the University of Turin; no additional animals were collected for this study.

## What the vertebrae show {#s-c28043b8da}
In the sampled sacral vertebrae, the anterior centrosynapophyseal lamina is confined to the first sacral element. The posterior centrosynapophyseal lamina becomes more robust toward the second. This records variation along the column rather than a single condition for the whole skeleton.
