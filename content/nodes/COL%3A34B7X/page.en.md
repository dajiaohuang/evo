## Overview {#s-916f06dfc9}
COL26.8 accepts Daubentonia madagascariensis (Gmelin, 1788) as species ID 34B7X under genus ID 426L; MDD v2.5 supplies the display name Aye-aye. The studies below examine distinct forests and small samples, so they are local field accounts rather than a complete species-wide natural-history survey.

## Field ecology {#s-4bb335033f}
During three months of radio-tracking in degraded secondary forest in northeastern Madagascar, field observers recorded activity only after dark and daytime nests used singly; occasional aggregations occurred at feeding sites. The sample also showed varied foods, with flower nectar noted as a preference. These observations show that aye-ayes used this particular degraded forest during the cool dry season, not that all populations or seasons are alike.

## Movement in the canopy {#s-cbb3dbf34d}
At undisturbed Torotorofotsy forest, a 2017 study followed one adult female and one juvenile male from May to December. Leaping made up 47.9% and 50.1% of recorded locomotor activity for those two individuals. Quadrupedal walking was associated with the main canopy, while head-first descent was associated with the low canopy. These are individual-level observations, not population-wide proportions.
