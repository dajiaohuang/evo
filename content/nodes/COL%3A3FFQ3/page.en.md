## Bite force and pressure at the tooth tip {#s-8419e423b9}
Can lower bite force coexist with high pressure at a tooth tip? This guide follows the accepted COL26.8 record 3FFQ3 and reads the study at its stated sample and geographic scale. A bite-force study spanning 23 living crocodilian species found the gharial to be a statistical low-force outlier after accounting for body size, yet it generated high pressure at its front caniniform teeth. The authors linked this combination to its extremely slender snout and narrow teeth with very small contact areas: lower force does not mean lower local tooth pressure (Erickson et al., 2012: Table 1; Fig. 3A; Table 2; Fig. 4A).

Continue with Crocodylus johnstoni (COL C6Q4M) to compare tooth-position and body-size effects in the same experiment. The Crocodylia group guide provides the wider reading route; the neighboring page retains its own sources and limits.
