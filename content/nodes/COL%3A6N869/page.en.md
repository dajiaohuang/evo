## What this record covers {#s-8ebcf426eb}
COL26.8 accepts Indicator exilis in Indicatoridae. The measurements below belong to the AVONET v7 record under the HBW–BirdLife name used in 2020. Five birds contributed measurements, and four supplied a complete set; each trait can therefore have a smaller usable sample than the overall count.

## Three beak dimensions {#s-517ed76ea9}
The recorded means are 5 mm from the front edge of the nostril to the tip, 4 mm wide and 4.3 mm deep at the nostril edge. Length, width and depth describe different axes at standardized landmarks; none is an identification threshold.

## Wing, lower leg and mass {#s-1a258ef4a8}
The unflattened wing mean, measured from the carpal joint to the longest primary, is 68.8 mm; the tarsus mean is 13 mm. A separate compilation gives mean mass as 17.92 g and labels its source category Dunning. That mass record does not show that the measured birds were weighed.

## Broad ecological categories {#s-66ff3f9e11}
AVONET classifies the habitat as Forest, the trophic niche as Invertivore and the lifestyle as Insessorial, meaning the bird is primarily associated with perches above ground. These are broad comparison categories, not claims that it uses only forest or eats only invertebrates.

## Movement and mapped latitude {#s-14388da603}
The migration field labels the record sedentary. Historical breeding and resident maps reach from 13.36° south to 13.07° north. Those mapped limits do not describe non-breeding movements or current occupancy, and they were compiled separately from the measurements.

## A second, historical ecology record {#s-593703933b}
The EltonTraits 1.0 entry is also named Indicator exilis and placed in Indicatoridae, but it reflects an earlier taxonomy. The comparison here uses two field groups—diet and feeding position—with their source labels. A matching name does not settle later splits or synonym questions.

## Estimated food proportions {#s-915316adc9}
EltonTraits assigns relative weights of 50% to nectar, pollen and plant exudates, 30% to invertebrates and 20% to fruit. Its A code marks high compiler confidence in the source and diet entry. The weights translate published descriptions into comparable proportions; they are not measured intake. A zero in another category would not prove that the food is never eaten, and broad labels such as Nect, Seed and PlantO should not be read as exclusive diets.

## Where feeding is reported {#s-45d55091ac}
The species-level foraging entry splits feeding positions evenly: 50% in understory, edges or shrubs below 2 m, and 50% in trees or tall bushes from 2 m up to the canopy. These are relative feeding-position estimates, not nest locations, a map of habitat use or a full-day activity budget. The source's water, understory, mid/high, canopy and aerial classes have distinct definitions; they do not give altitude or habitat area.

## How far the record supports interpretation {#s-f30d8e893a}
AVONET measurements are species means from the stated samples, not diagnostic limits or a description of individual variation. Mass has separate provenance and must not inherit the morphological sample count. Habitat, trophic niche and migration are broad labels, while the map summary covers historical breeding and resident ranges. The name crosswalk links this older record to the current catalogue entry; it does not prove that taxonomic concepts remained identical through later splits or lumps. Other sources are needed for reproduction, present-day range, populations and conservation.

EltonTraits supplies a historical same-name, same-family ecology record, not a complete species account. Its food and foraging percentages are standardized interpretations of literature descriptions, not measurements of intake, energy or time spent feeding. The stated avian A code records compiler confidence, but the proportions remain approximate. No congeneric inference is identified for these selected bird fields; nevertheless, the underlying Handbook source has not been reread here. Individual, seasonal and local variation, current distribution, population and conservation require other evidence.
