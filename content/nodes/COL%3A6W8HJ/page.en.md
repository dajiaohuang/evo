## Accepted name and placement {#s-0abe64b800}
The COL26.8 release issued on 20 August 2026 records Pristimantis carlosceroni Valencia, Bejarano-Muñoz & Yánez-Muñoz, 2013 as an accepted species. Its recorded classification is Anura (order) → Strabomantidae (family) → Pristimantis (genus). This path identifies the catalogue name; biological claims below remain tied to their cited sources and taxonomic scope.

## A green frog from two Nono specimens {#s-f3ef10675e}
The 2013 description documents one adult male holotype from near Cascada Chaupiurco (3,402 m) and one referred juvenile from Quebrada Murillo (3,200 m), both in Nono, Quito Metropolitan District. It diagnoses the frog by its green dorsum, three-ringed iris in life and absent vomerine odontophores; females were unknown in that account.

## What the original diagnosis observed {#s-a6561e54cd}
The authors distinguish the described frog by a green dorsum in life, a golden iris bordered by three rings (blue, white and dark brown), strongly areolate skin on the flanks and belly, and absent vomerine odontophores. The 26.1 mm snout–vent measurement belongs to the single adult male holotype; the paper reports no adult female. These are observations from the material available for the description, not a range-wide estimate.

## A tentative group placement {#s-de1b07ded4}
The paper tentatively places the species in the Pristimantis unistrigatus species group and compares its green colour with P. ornatissimus. The authors state that the evidence then available did not identify a close relationship between the two; they call for molecular tools and additional material. Their comparison therefore supports diagnosis, not a confirmed sister-species claim.
