## Helichrysum palustre {#s-57e176afed}
## Silvery rosettes in high-elevation marshes

Hilliard's *Flora of Southern Africa* account describes a perennial herb with leaf rosettes growing in tufts from a woody rhizome. One or two lateral flowering stems rise to about 150 mm and are loosely gray-woolly. Lance-shaped, tongue-like basal leaves reach 50 by 7 mm and remain silvery with silky hairs; narrower stem leaves become smaller upward.

Bell-shaped heads are solitary or rarely paired. Their radiating white bracts have a dull crimson patch inside near the base. Heads contain 145–224 florets. Smooth, five-ribbed achenes are about 1.5 mm long; numerous rough pappus bristles equal the corolla and lightly cohere in bundles. The source records marshes, water or raised hummocks, and marshy stream edges at about 2,300–3,400 m.
