## Ecological evidence {#s-93c350d759}
In a field comparison at Sector Santa Rosa in Costa Rica's Área de Conservación Guanacaste, white-faced capuchins (Cebus imitator) fell between Geoffroy's spider monkeys and mantled howler monkeys in average monthly fruit share. That ranking came from the authors' review of long-term field diet studies, not a direct measurement of each species' complete diet in the focal observations. Across 2,107 feeding bouts and 26,094 fruit-investigation sequences, capuchins used manual touch more often than the other two species, and had the highest thumb-to-index-finger ratio in the sample. By contrast, they sniffed fruit less often than both spider monkeys and the less-frugivorous howlers, so fruit share alone did not predict sniffing in this small comparison. The authors proposed that manual dexterity may relate to touch, but an observational association does not establish causation.

The study also compared dichromatic and trichromatic individuals: after controlling for species, dichromats sniffed and bit/rejected fruit more often, while trichromats touched cryptic fruit more often. This is a pooled result across the three primates, not an independent effect demonstrated for Cebus alone.

## Evidence limits {#s-48ef214f43}
Source-linked guide to a local comparative study. Behavioural data came from a limited set of sympatric groups, shared foods, and one seasonal Costa Rican dry forest; fruit-share estimates came separately from long-term studies selected by the authors. Cebus has a flexible diet, and the study does not describe its full range, year-round diet, or species-wide sensory mechanisms.
