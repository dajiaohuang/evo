## Ecological evidence {#s-31c68fdd84}
Lewis and O’Riain followed a Cape Peninsula group of chacma baboons that relied exclusively on natural foods, recording behaviour across four seasons for 13 months and tracking movement with handheld GPS units. The group ate marine foods in all four seasons, but these made up less than 5% of total feeding time; its estimated range was 45.3 km² and daily travel averaged 5.9 km. It spent more time feeding and travelling than previously studied Peninsula groups that used anthropogenic foods. This is a comparison involving particular groups, not a species-wide pattern or proof that human food alone caused the differences.
