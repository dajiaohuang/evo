## Skin alkaloid measurements retain individuals and assay scope {#s-a9301957ff}
In the 2024 study, Leucostethus fugax is represented by 8 frogs analysed with GC-MS. Table1 reports 3–8 detected alkaloid compounds per individual, with median 4.5. Its printed log-total-integrated-area range is 12.57–15.33, with median 14.00; these are chromatographic signal summaries, not micrograms, tissue concentration or a toxic dose. The logarithm base is not inferred from the column label. Compound counts include the source’s isomer distinctions and should not be read as numbers of independently tested toxins. The phenotype column prints undefended; defended/undefended are prior broad chemical-content bins, not measured predator-deterrence outcomes, while NA is an unassigned source category.

## Collection and chemical interpretation limits {#s-918461cfb7}
The GC-MS sample of Leucostethus fugax belongs to the 2014 Ecuador/Colombia collection series. Whole skins were placed in methanol after lidocaine euthanasia; species sample sizes were opportunistic, with random subsets chosen within Epipedobates. Standards were unavailable for most frog alkaloids, so absolute quantities cannot be recovered from these reported signal values. The two instrument/pipeline datasets are not directly comparable. Full-skin GC-MS results were not mass-corrected, and co-eluting compounds with the same base peak could not always be distinguished. The authors did not serially sample each species across seasons and a broad range. Raw chromatograms, vouchers and supplementary individual-level records have not been independently reanalysed here.

## Passive accumulation remains a mechanistic hypothesis {#s-7fd76fe0ae}
The paper uses these skin observations in a proposed sequence from dietary toxin exposure and some resistance, through slower elimination and temporary retention, to derived transport or storage associated with sequestration. For Leucostethus fugax, a measured skin profile alone does not establish dietary origin, elimination rate, a binding mechanism or transport specifically to skin. Presence in skin does not by itself distinguish passive retention from specialised sequestration. The proposed ancestral condition and evolutionary sequence require broader sampling and tissue/time-series experiments; they are not direct observations of ancestors or a reconstructed molecular pathway in this sampled species.

## Species-specific detections and their evidence limits {#s-cc3f523014}
Across eight individuals the authors identified twelve alkaloids, including196A,225C,222–1 and222–2 plus eight new compounds. Per-individual detections ranged from three to eight, so the pooled twelve should not be assigned to every frog. The results are discussed alongside earlier thin-layer chromatography, whose source interpretations differ.
