## How do its male and female flower clusters differ? {#s-ec18f64903}
A 2011 account describes *Nevillea vlokii* as a tufted plant 0.5–1 m tall, with fertile stems arising on short, spreading rhizomatous (with a horizontal underground stem). Male inflorescences measure 20–25 by 15–25 mm and carry 2–10 spikelets (small flower clusters); those spikelets have 10–20 flowers. Female inflorescences measure 20–50 by 8–20 mm and also carry 2–10 spikelets, but their spathes extend beyond and obscure them. Each female spikelet has 2–4 flowers. The account records the fruit as unknown.

## Do the collections point to seepage? {#s-ae4cc90973}
The account says all collections came from sandy or loamy soils derived from sandstone. Most were from seepages, although one came from well-drained soil and some from permanently wet sites. It suggests the species might need seepage and says it is sometimes locally dominant or at least common. These are account-level observations without a dated sampling frame.
