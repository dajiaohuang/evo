## Accepted name and placement {#s-6828b6d1cf}
COL26.8 accepts Chiropterotriton totonacus Parra-Olea, García-Castillo, Rovito, Maisano, Hanken & Wake, 2020 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Caudata (order) → Plethodontidae (family) → Chiropterotriton (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## Twenty types with a broad locality-error estimate {#s-137e5ef255}
Female holotype MVZ 163945 was collected west of Las Vigas de Ramírez on 25 July 1979 at 2420 m; the coordinate estimate recorded a maximum error distance of 5.71 km. Nineteen paratypes comprised ten males and nine females. The diagnosis summarized ten males with mean SVL 35.7 mm, range 32.0–38.6 mm, and ten females with mean 35.5 mm, range 31.8–38.3 mm. The locality is not treated as a precisely located point, and these similar sample means do not by themselves establish absence of population-level sexual dimorphism.

## Long tails, expanded tips and overlapping limbs {#s-6225af7597}
Mean tail/SVL ratios were 1.16 in males and 1.20 in females. The diagnosis described wide hands and feet, expanded digital tips and webbing extending to the base of the terminal phalanx. Adpressed limbs closely approached or overlapped, with mean interval −0.6 costal folds in males and 0.0 in females. Male maxillary teeth averaged 32.9, range 18–48, and females 52.6, range 45–60. These are sampled anatomy and ratios rather than tail-performance measurements or universal tooth counts.

## Three photographed live animals and separate preserved types {#s-a7f091610f}
Live-colour description came from photos of IBH 31030, 31031 and 30998 because no live type-series information was available. Their reddish-brown dorsal bands could be regular, broken or absent against dark brown-grey backgrounds, with dark golden-brown irises. The preserved holotype was medium brown with an obscure stripe, and other preserved types varied. The later photographed animals are not conflated with the live holotype, and neither bands nor their absence form an invariant standalone diagnosis.

## Under logs in disturbed mossy pine forest {#s-3817c50b9b}
The account knew ridge localities between Cruz Blanca and Las Vigas at 2200–2450 m and La Joya at 2000 m in Veracruz. It described the species as terrestrial in mossy pine forest and reported recently collected specimens beneath logs in disturbed pine forest. The lower La Joya record remains separate from the ridge interval. Such encounters show use of surveyed disturbed habitat, not universal disturbance tolerance, an egg site beneath every log or a complete current range.
