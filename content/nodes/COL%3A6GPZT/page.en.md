## *Erigeron karvinskianus*: variable leaves and radiating flower heads {#s-baca36ccde}
The historical Flora of Panama account, retrieved on 8 September 2026, describes a small, branched subshrub, sometimes with stems lying along the ground. Its leaves vary in shape and may have shallow to deep teeth; the leaves near the flower clusters are smaller. Most flower heads sit singly on stalks that rise above the foliage. Each radiate head has outer ray florets, reported as white, bluish, or purplish, around yellowish disc florets when dry. The account’s historical distribution statement places the species in upper elevations of tropical America, from Chile and Venezuela north to Mexico and the Antilles, and says it has become established in parts of the Old World, with records from Sri Lanka, the Philippines, and Tanzania. This is the cited historical regional account, not a current range inventory.
