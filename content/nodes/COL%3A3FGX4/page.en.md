## Flowers and floral structures {#s-b4bd7cfc87}
The regional account compares this species with *Geissorhiza heterostyla*: its flowers are held upright, with a glossy purple perianth and dark-purple centre, short purple filaments, and a style that always divides below the anthers.

## Diagnostic comparison {#s-149d1ac5a7}
The SANBI diagnostic account describes two basal leaves with narrowly winged margins and a raised, winged central vein, like those of its presumed immediate relative *G. heterostyla*. It also notes derived pollen grains with complex apertures and an operculum, a feature often found in that species. In living plants, the glistening purple-pink flowers with a dark-purple centre, purple filaments, and short purple style are described as distinctive. The style is about 3 mm long and divides opposite the lower third of the filaments; its nearly straight branches are unusual in the subgenus. The account suggests they may be associated with pollination by hopliine beetles, members of which have been captured on the flowers.

The account says the two species are nearly identical vegetatively, making *G. heterostyla* especially difficult to distinguish in herbarium material. In living plants, *G. heterostyla* is described as having blue to mauve or nearly white flowers, a pale-yellow to green throat and tube usually edged in darker blue or mauve, and approximately white stamens and style branches.
