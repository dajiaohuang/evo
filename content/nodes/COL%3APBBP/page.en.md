## Jaw recordings from five juveniles {#s-2a3ea8221a}
Integumentary sensory organs (ISOs) are small skin structures associated with sensory nerves. Di-Poï and Milinkovitch’s 2013 electrophysiological experiment included five spectacled caimans weighing 700–1200 g, alongside five Nile crocodiles. Local skin sensory organs were recorded under anesthesia, with caiman electrodes on the jaws. Embryonic and neonatal tissues served other methods, not the same freely moving behavioral trial. Histology showed ISO spots concentrated on the head and no Nile-crocodile-like postcranial ISOs; this does not mean that postcranial skin lacks nerves. Similar lingual structures must also be distinguished from the skin electrode sites, without assuming that each tongue organ’s functions were individually measured.

## Separate brushing, temperature and pH stimuli {#s-5c73f879bf}
In 2013, electrodes were placed near individual skin sensory organs in anesthetized juveniles: jaw and dorsal regions in Nile crocodiles, and jaws in spectacled caimans. Brushing, local warming or cooling, and acidic and alkaline droplets elicited electrical activity. Visible thermal responses summarized as above about 43°C or below about 15°C depend on the stimulation and recording setup; they are not preferred habitat temperatures, lethal limits or freely chosen temperatures. RT-PCR from neonatal tissues and embryonic in situ hybridization showed relevant channel expression. Expression, local electrical activity and whole-animal perception or choice remain distinct evidence levels.

## A pH response is not a salinity response {#s-2c3d86fb5b}
Recorded spectacled caiman jaw ISOs responded to brushing, temperature and acidic or alkaline droplets, but showed no corresponding response to 0.5 M NaCl or KCl. Stimulus-specific results do not establish sensing every water constituent: all solutes, pollutants and wild habitat choices were not tested. Caiman tongue tissue lacked the Nile crocodile’s lingual salt glands while retaining sensory-like structures. Gland differences, salt secretion, salinity detection and wild distribution were not connected into a complete causal chain by these experiments.
