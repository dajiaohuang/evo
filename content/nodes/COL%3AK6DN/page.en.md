## Leaves, flowers, and season {#s-70832602ca}
The 2013 regional account describes a corm-bearing geophyte 120–200 mm tall. Its nearly linear leaves are pleated, loosely twisted, wavy-edged, sparsely hairy, and cobwebby at the base. Five to ten flowers form a dense, nearly upright spike. The two-lipped flowers are mauve marked with violet and cream, and rose-scented. The perianth tube is 10–12 mm long and the upper tepal 18–25 mm; the ovary and bracts are smooth, with the inner bracts forked in their upper third. Flowering is recorded from July to mid-September.

These traits, measurements, and flowering period come from a regional South African account published in 2013; the excerpt gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency.

## Distinguishing Babiana spiralis {#s-d20d98ec16}
A 2020 diagnostic account distinguishes *B. fimbriata* by its hairless or nearly hairless stem, woolly or cobwebby scale leaves and leaf-sheath bases, and slightly twisted leaves 3–4 mm wide. The flowers are dark purple to almost brown; the lower lateral tepals have greenish-yellow, prominent ear-shaped lobes at their bases. The upper lateral tepals point forward or curve slightly inward near the base, a feature described as unique in the genus.

The account notes that *B. spiralis* has been confused with it. *B. spiralis* has smooth scale leaves and leaf sheaths, a velvety stem, and bright-pink or blue flowers; its lower tepal parts also have ear-shaped lobes. Its upper lateral tepals curve outward just above the base in a spreading orientation.

These diagnostic comparisons come from a regional South African account published in 2020; the excerpt gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency.
