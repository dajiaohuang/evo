## Begin with what the two holotypes preserve {#s-6e19f7d650}
[Dineobellator notohesperus](Eudromaeosauria/Velociraptorinae/Dineobellator/Dineobellator_notohesperus/page.en.md) is based on SMP VP-2430, with at least 20 identifiable skeletal elements including skull fragments. [Balaur bondoc](Balaur/Balaur_bondoc/page.en.md) has no skull in holotype EME PV.313, but preserves trunk, girdles and limb bones. Neither is a complete animal. Different preserved parts support different anatomical questions; reconstructions do not supply actual teeth, complete plumage or population variation.

## Forelimb differences change functional interpretations {#s-559f32c302}
Dineobellator’s humeral muscle-attachment crest and manual flexor tubercle underpin a hypothesis of stronger forearm flexion and grasping. Balaur has a fused carpometacarpus and strongly reduced third finger, used by the 2015 reassessment to question typical dromaeosaurid predation. This compares how the studies infer function from bones; it does not measure which animal was stronger or make their traits habits shared across the family.

## Feather-attachment evidence and reconstruction assumptions {#s-9b60c8b96a}
Bumps on Dineobellator’s ulna are interpreted as quill knobs; modern owl feathers in the paper illustrate their position. Balaur’s plumage is assumed by the 2015 authors through comparison, rather than preserved in its holotype. Bone attachment features, inferred plumage and flight ability are separate questions. Feathers in a reconstruction do not establish colors or past flight.

## Keep catalogue placement and study trees distinct {#s-f0f552b78e}
The smaller 2020 Dineobellator matrix resolves it in Velociraptorinae, while a larger theropod matrix leaves relationships within the family unresolved. Both updated 2015 Balaur matrices favor Avialae, but constraint tests do not significantly exclude a dromaeosaurid position. The recorded PBDB path organizes these pages without adjudicating that dispute. Follow the [Dineobellator genus guide](Eudromaeosauria/Velociraptorinae/Dineobellator/page.en.md) and [Balaur genus guide](Balaur/page.en.md) for material and limits before comparing methods.

## Evidence limits {#s-812eee7fd3}
This page provides a selective comparison of two holotypes, without treating their anatomy, plumage, diets or locomotion as traits of every roster member. Existing occurrence, collection and age sections organize database fields rather than biological abundance or actual origin/extinction dates. The Dineobellator description and Balaur reassessment retain their study scopes; the full original Balaur description and later literature are not assembled. Recorded PBDB placement remains separate from phylogenetic hypotheses, without reclassification.

## Dromaeosaurid trees include unstable sampled taxa {#s-2352436078}
Turner and colleagues’ specimen review and large paravian morphology matrix recover subclades but also leave some sampled taxa unstable. The 167–66 Ma interval is the review’s sampled Jurassic–Cretaceous envelope, not exact global family endpoints, continuous occupancy, a divergence date or a direct-ancestor assertion.
