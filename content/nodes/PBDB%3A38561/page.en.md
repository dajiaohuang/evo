## What two dromaeosaurid holotypes preserve {#s-6e19f7d650}
Dineobellator notohesperus is based on holotype SMP VP-2430, with at least 20 identifiable skeletal elements, including skull fragments. Balaur bondoc has no skull in holotype EME PV.313 but preserves trunk, girdles and limb bones. Neither specimen is complete; their different preserved parts support different anatomical questions. Reconstructions do not supply actual teeth, complete plumage or population variation.

## Forelimb differences change functional interpretations {#s-559f32c302}
Dineobellator’s humeral muscle-attachment crest and manual flexor tubercle underpin a hypothesis of stronger forearm flexion and grasping. Balaur has a fused carpometacarpus and strongly reduced third finger, used by the 2015 reassessment to question typical dromaeosaurid predation. This compares how the studies infer function from bones; it does not measure which animal was stronger or make their traits habits shared across the family.

## Feather-attachment evidence and reconstruction assumptions {#s-9b60c8b96a}
Bumps on Dineobellator’s ulna are interpreted as quill knobs; modern owl feathers in the paper illustrate their position. Balaur’s plumage is assumed by the 2015 authors through comparison, rather than preserved in its holotype. Bone attachment features, inferred plumage and flight ability are separate questions. Feathers in a reconstruction do not establish colors or past flight.

## PBDB placement and study trees answer different questions {#s-f0f552b78e}
The smaller 2020 Dineobellator matrix places it in Velociraptorinae, while a larger theropod matrix leaves relationships within the family unresolved. Both updated 2015 Balaur matrices favor Avialae, but constraint tests do not significantly exclude a dromaeosaurid position. The PBDB path organizes records without deciding between these study results. Read the Dineobellator genus guide and Balaur genus guide for their separate material and limits.

## Dineobellator and Balaur holotypes, two study contexts {#s-812eee7fd3}
This page compares Dineobellator notohesperus holotype SMP VP-2430 with Balaur bondoc holotype EME PV.313. The Dineobellator forelimb and quill-knob evidence comes from its specimen study; Balaur’s reduced third finger, inferred plumage and competing affinities come from a separate reassessment. These observations and interpretations do not define traits shared by all Dromaeosauridae. The two studies address different specimens and questions. This comparison uses the Dineobellator specimen study and the Balaur reassessment; the original Balaur description and later literature are not assembled here.

## Some dromaeosaurid placements remain unstable {#s-2352436078}
Turner and colleagues review dromaeosaurid specimens and analyze a large paravian morphology matrix. Their analysis recovers subclades but leaves some sampled taxa unstable, so it does not supply a resolved ancestor chain. The 167–66 Ma interval is the review’s sampled Jurassic–Cretaceous envelope, not exact global family endpoints, continuous occupancy, a divergence date or a direct-ancestor assertion.
