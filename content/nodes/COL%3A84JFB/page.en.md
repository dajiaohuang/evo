## Overview {#s-d88e1c6281}
Schüßler et al. sampled five northeastern lowland-rainforest sites between 2008 and 2017, spanning 42–462 m, and formally described Jonah's mouse lemur in that study. The paper discussed morphology, ecology and locality data alongside molecular work from the same region; these conclusions concern the northeastern study area, not island-wide sampling.

## Distribution and habitat {#s-2e3e8620b0}
Holotype TiHo B34 came from community-protected forest near Ambavala, at about 342 m and roughly 20 km from Mananara-Nord. The study also sampled five lowland rainforests; these sites do not define the species' full range.

## Ecological evidence {#s-38614bbf75}
The paper's 25 captured individuals were recorded between 42 and 356 m; 14 were found in 2–4 m stands of Madagascar cardamom (Aframomum angustifolium), sometimes mixed with edge trees. The authors reported no sightings in treeless secondary vegetation except dense cardamom stands. These are local captures and observations, not a species-wide habitat-preference test.

## Evidence limits {#s-b0f033513c}
This page summarizes only northeastern study sites, the type locality and capture observations; it does not infer island-wide distribution, population trend, complete habitat preference or current conservation status. The Chinese name is editorial.
