## atlas-profile / evidenceSummary {#s-50c43a548c}
Studies use different datasets and methods to estimate cycad history. Fossil-integrated models support older regional histories, but a model age is not itself a fossil occurrence.

## One analysis combines living plants and fossil leaves {#s-79055eea29}
Coiro and colleagues combined molecular data from living cycads with leaf morphology scored for living and fossil taxa, including 60 fossil species. The fossil leaf assignments and character codings are inputs to the analysis, not conclusions supplied by the model. The resulting tree and historical estimates describe the sampled dataset and analytical choices; they are not a fixed topology for every member of Cycadophyta.

## The age estimate applies to crown Cycadales {#s-55b5317e38}
In the study's fossilized birth–death analysis, crown Cycadales has a posterior median age of 330.4 million years, with a 95% highest posterior density interval of 296.2–358.9 million years. The displayed 330.4-to-present interval is a navigation summary linking that model estimate to living cycads. It is not a directly observed fossil first appearance for all Cycadophyta, and the interval does not mean fossils are known continuously through that time.

## A modelled range contraction is not a complete map {#s-e708500669}
The fossil-integrated biogeographic model supports an interpretation of broader high-latitude occupation followed by contraction. Ancestral areas and the number of local disappearances depend on model assumptions and the sampled fossils. Living cycads are concentrated in tropical and subtropical regions, but that modern pattern alone does not identify where the group originated or why its range changed.
