## One skeleton gives a bounded portrait {#s-ef26b69f97}
Rhaeticosaurus mertensi is known from the subadult holotype LWL-MFN P 64047, a partly articulated skeleton from a Rhaetian bed in Germany's Exter Formation. It preserves the back of the skull, lower jaw, spine, shoulder and hip girdles, and left limbs. The published total-length estimate is 237 cm; it describes this individual, not a size range for the genus. The specimen extends the sampled plesiosaur record into the Rhaetian, but it is not identified as an ancestor of later plesiosaurs.

## A matrix position and a swimming comparison {#s-286136b844}
In a published analysis of 35 taxa and 270 characters, the species falls within Plesiosauria as a basal member of Pliosauridae. That is the result of one character matrix, not a visible family relationship in the rock. Four similar flippers and a short tail invite comparisons with underwater hydrofoils and paraxial swimming; neither a swimming gait nor speed is measured from the fossil. The partial skeleton also gives no direct evidence of a particular prey or diet.

## What the bone tissue can and cannot show {#s-8d8df20d59}
The sampled humerus and femur contain highly vascularized bone tissue, which the study uses to discuss growth and physiology. Histology informs an interpretation; it does not directly record metabolism or behavior. The holotype comes from a named clay pit and a specific Exter Formation bed, 21 m below the Triassic–Jurassic boundary. That documented horizon is a locality record, not a complete global range or a precise date for the individual.
