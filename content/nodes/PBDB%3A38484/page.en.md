## Similar filtration, different oral structures {#s-181d63435a}
Liaodactylus primus and Balaenognathus maeuseri offer two different cases for studying dental filtration in pterosaurs. The 2017 Liaodactylus description is based on a skull, mandibles, and the first two neck vertebrae; it reports 152 closely spaced teeth that shorten toward the rear and interprets them as a filter. Balaenognathus is known from a near-complete skeleton with at least 480 teeth, crown hooklets, and a spatulate, toothless front edge; its authors modelled water flow through the mouth. The proposed pumping mechanism for Balaenognathus is not evidence of behavior in Liaodactylus.

## Different structures can support dental filtration {#s-5952cf7aae}
The 2023 Balaenognathus paper compares fine-toothed forms including Ctenochasma, Gnathosaurus, and Pterodaustro. All raise the question of how water passes through a dental filter, but their teeth and snouts differ. In Balaenognathus, crown-tip expansions limit tooth closure and a toothless spatulate front forms an opening; the Pterodaustro comparison emphasizes very slender lower teeth and smaller, rounder upper teeth. The paper proposes buccal and gular pumping for Balaenognathus; that model should not be generalized to all these pterosaurs.
