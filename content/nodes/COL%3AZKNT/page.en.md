## Limits of these trait data {#s-0c7450d618}
This account paraphrases the same-name ReptTraits v1-2 entry, retaining its 2023 taxonomic framework and sources for individual traits. One row per species compresses variation in sex, age, locality and populations; matching name and family do not ensure equivalent later concepts. Reported maximum length is not an absolute species limit, and reproductive means are not fixed counts in every clutch. Many mass entries are estimated from length equations and are not presented here as measured mass. Juvenile-length headers and definitions differ and are also omitted from reader conclusions. Conservation, venom, current distribution and population trends are not updated here; unrecorded fields remain gaps. Dataset citation labels preserve the source chain and do not imply that this project independently reread every original work.

## Distinguish the measured length {#s-078724fa0e}
Recorded dimensions are reported maximum total length (TL) 4000 mm. SVL runs from snout to vent and differs from total length including the tail. Compiled maxima are reported material, not theoretical growth limits or typical individual size. Sex-specific values may come from different localities or studies and alone cannot quantify population sexual dimorphism.

## Where and how it is active {#s-a69cc9c5d1}
In the entry, microhabitat is Arboreal/Saxicolous/Terrestrial; active timing is Diurnal. These categories summarize activity and resource use rather than an exhaustive habitat list or fixed schedule. Positions such as arboreal and terrestrial can be combined. A carnivorous category does not specify prey, and populations, seasons and life stages may differ.

## How to read reproductive records {#s-684b848f01}
the reproductive-mode field uses the original label oviparous; consolidated mean offspring or eggs per litter/clutch 3. Clutch/litter summaries can use midpoints of reported means or, when means are unavailable, extrema; they are not necessarily arithmetic averages of observed clutches. Fractions do not describe non-integer young in a litter. The lowest/highest fields are defined as endpoints of reported means rather than guaranteed smallest/largest individual clutches. Reproductive and sex-determination labels do not establish fetal nutrition or uniformity across all populations.

## Molecular evidence and Nile mummy haplotypes {#s-6a338fabe7}
How did molecular and historical-specimen evidence help distinguish an African crocodile lineage? This guide follows the accepted COL26.8 record ZKNT and reads the study at its stated sample and geographic scale. Comparisons of mitochondrial and nuclear markers and karyotypes from African crocodile samples and Egyptian mummies supported a cryptic lineage corresponding to the West African crocodile, Crocodylus suchus, within the Nile crocodile complex. Mummy haplotypes also indicate that two Crocodylus lineages occurred historically along the Nile. These are phylogenetic and historical-specimen findings, not a current range boundary or abundance estimate (Hekkala et al., 2011: Abstract).
