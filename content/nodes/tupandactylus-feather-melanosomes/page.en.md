## Feather-like structures in Tupandactylus {#s-9d5e8ec58d}
Five slabs of Tupandactylus specimen MCT.R.1884 preserve part of a skull and crest with simple filaments and branched feather-like structures. Melanosomes occur in sampled skin and filaments, but exact colors and signaling roles are not directly preserved.

## Structures preserved with the crest {#s-11d4bdb39f}
MCT.R.1884 includes a partial skull and cranial crest distributed across five slabs. The study describes both unbranched monofilaments and branched, feather-like structures. It also reports different melanosome geometries in skin and filament samples—microscopic evidence from this specimen, not a whole-body color record.

## What pigment evidence can establish {#s-14ab56bad0}
Melanosome geometry was compared across the sampled tissues, but it does not directly reveal an exact visible color or prove a signaling function. The proposed evolutionary implications for feather-like coverings depend on anatomical homology and phylogenetic interpretation; this one pterosaur does not show that every pterosaur had the same covering.
