## One sequenced individual and a separate DNA-content estimate {#s-341eea2ca5}
The study extracted liver DNA from one Bolitoglossa veracrucis specimen as part of a sixteen-species, three-genus comparison, targeting ten gigabases of short-read data per species. Repeat analysis randomly sampled merged reads at roughly 0.1-fold coverage. Table 1 gives a genome C-value estimate of 34.9 picograms, based on blood-smear densitometry and species means from earlier genome-size work. Picograms of DNA are not gigabases, body mass or a complete genome assembly; the sequenced individual and the species-mean DNA-content estimate have different evidence roles. The sample does not determine variation among sexes, populations or ages.

## Detected repeat abundance is a method-dependent lower estimate {#s-a451e223f0}
Table 1 reports 56% detectable repeat abundance for Bolitoglossa veracrucis, including uncategorized repeats. Table 2 reports LTRs at 15.97%, and Table 3 separates ERV at 7.314% from Ty3/Gypsy at 8.657%. These are percentages of sampled bases identified by the study's repeat library, not percentages of animals, newly inserted elements or measured gene expression. Mutation can degrade repeats beyond recognition; the authors regard short-read masking as likely underestimating true repetitive content. Rounded table categories need not sum exactly, and uncategorized repeats should not be silently assigned to a known TE superfamily.

## Repeat divergence suggests relative expansion histories {#s-bc46e75c22}
The study places this species among four Bolitoglossa with ERV peaks at Kimura distances of 11–13, lower than the main peaks in the six larger-genome Bolitoglossa. Their Gypsy/Ty3 profiles have lower, broader peaks extending to smaller distances; rufescens has a reported additional low-distance peak. These are relative sequence-divergence histories, not absolute dates or direct observed insertion rates. The study could not distinguish element deletion from more effective silencing.
