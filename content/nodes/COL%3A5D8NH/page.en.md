## What the measurement sample covers {#s-e94ac3f5a3}
COL26.8 places *Zosterops natalis* in Zosteropidae. AVONET v7 records five measured individuals under the HBW–BirdLife 2020 name; four have a complete set of morphological measurements. The usable sample can differ among traits, so each reported value is a sample average, not a range for individual birds.

## Measured beak dimensions {#s-35d870b460}
Mean beak length from the front edge of the nostril to the tip is 8.1 mm. At that same landmark, mean width is 3.1 mm and depth is 3.1 mm. The three measures describe different dimensions at standardized points; they are not cutoffs for identifying an individual in the field.

## Wing, tarsus and mass {#s-779ea8917f}
Mean unflattened wing length, from the carpal joint to the longest primary feather, is 59.9 mm. Mean tarsus length—the measured lower-leg segment—is 19.9 mm. A separate compilation reports mean mass of 9.97 g. That estimate is not tied to the five measured individuals, and the source does not show that all five were weighed.

## Habitat, feeding and lifestyle {#s-46a8f0cca1}
AVONET classifies the habitat as forest, the feeding niche as omnivorous and the primary lifestyle as insessorial, meaning mainly perching. These broad categories support comparisons among species; they do not mean that the bird uses only forest, eats every food type equally or never uses another activity pattern.

## Movement and geographical summary {#s-ec4f2a0c37}
The migration field is coded as sedentary. Historical breeding and resident maps span latitude endpoints from −10.58° to −10.42°. Those endpoints summarize the mapped range; they do not describe all non-breeding movements or show present-day occupancy. The geography and body measurements come from different parts of the compilation.

## Food records and foraging height {#s-52fdfb2287}
EltonTraits 1.0 also contains an older same-name, same-family record for *Z. natalis*. Its taxonomy predates COL26.8; matching names connect the records but do not resolve later species splits or synonym disputes.

## Relative weights of food categories {#s-abeeeaac19}
The archive assigns relative weights of 40% to fruit, 30% to seeds, nuts and spores, 20% to nectar, pollen and plant exudates, and 10% to invertebrates. EltonTraits uses code A for high compiler confidence in the source and diet information. These percentages standardize verbal descriptions; they are not measurements of food intake. A category with no recorded weight would not prove that the bird never eats those foods, and the plant-food groups cover more than their short labels suggest.

## Foraging strata differ from whole-day activity {#s-f27e55eb0e}
The archive marks its foraging-height estimates as species-level: 40% for understory, edges or shrubs below 2 m; 20% for the ground; 20% for trees or tall bushes from 2 m to the canopy; and 20% in or just above the canopy. These weights describe relative use of feeding positions in the source account, not nest sites, all habitat or time spent across a whole day. The categories are not elevation ranges and do not establish flight height or habitat area.

## Trace each field group to its source labels {#s-47ae4d2cee}
The diet estimates are linked in the archive to *Handbook of the Birds of the World*, and the foraging-height estimates to Longmore’s 1991 guide to honeyeaters. Those underlying works have not been checked independently here. The percentages remain EltonTraits’ standardized summaries of literature descriptions, not additional observations; its bird and mammal reference tables also use separate numbering.

## Evidence limits {#s-cc5134e9d5}
AVONET’s body measurements are means from its listed sample, not diagnostic limits or a measure of individual variation; the separate mass source must not inherit that sample size. Its habitat, feeding and migration labels are broad categories, and its latitude endpoints refer to historical breeding and resident maps. EltonTraits’ diet and foraging percentages are literature-derived weights rather than direct measurements of intake or time budgets. The older names link records for comparison but do not prove that their species concepts match COL26.8. The cited works have not been independently reread; local and seasonal variation, current distribution, population size and conservation status need other sources.
