## Morphology and image evidence {#s-1af54f8cf2}
The selected set has three species rows. Two larval rows include study photographs; the others use images from literature. Larval analyses use 32 landmarks, while adult analyses use 10 linear measurements, so the methods differ. ## Coded habitat and life-history categories {#s-f7dd4201d7}
For the selected rows, larval codes are LE 3, LO 0, END 0, and ST 0; adult codes are SAX 0, SEMAQ 1, TER 2, and TOR 0. These abbreviations denote lentic, lotic, endotrophic, and semiterrestrial larvae, and saxicolous, semi-aquatic, terrestrial, and torrential adults. Counts apply to this subset, not the genus as a whole. Endotrophic larvae are a different category from direct development. The broad water/land labels do not measure flow tolerance, diet, breeding success, or habitat use at every life stage.

## Larval and adult shape results {#s-ee95da422f}
Across the study sample, a Mantel test—a comparison of distance patterns—found a weak but statistically significant association between larval and adult shape (r = 0.16, p = 0.003), with disparity patterns differing among families. The authors interpret this as adaptive decoupling, but the result does not demonstrate complete genetic independence or a causal developmental mechanism. Phylogenetic analyses included 74 of 83 sampled species. Reconstructions of ancestral PC1 (the first principal component) and geological dates are model estimates, not observed fossils. Adult K was reported as lower (p = 0.001), while family-level sample-size and statistic inconsistencies remain unresolved. These results do not define universal diagnostic traits for each linked species.
