## Name and this data account {#s-0286a36607}
COL26.8 accepts Trimeresurus hageni in Viperidae. The ReptTraits v1-2 entry follows the Reptile Database 2023 name framework and retains citations for individual traits. Its species row summarizes the records assembled for this name; it does not imply that all populations share one state.

## Distinguish the measured length {#s-3b6fe20b4f}
The reported maximum total length (TL), including the tail, is 1,300 mm. Snout–vent length (SVL) ends at the vent and is a different measurement. This compiled maximum describes the source records, not a typical individual or an absolute growth limit. Values for different sexes may come from separate localities or studies, so they do not establish size differences within a population.

## Where and how it is active {#s-f2baf7decb}
The entry records arboreal microhabitat, a carnivorous diet, and nocturnal activity. These broad labels do not name prey or describe every habitat or activity period. The compiled categories also do not show whether populations, seasons, or life stages differ.

## How to read reproductive records {#s-7d57e9a2d3}
The entry labels the species oviparous and reports a consolidated mean of 18.5 offspring or eggs per litter or clutch. Such summaries may be midpoints of reported means, or of reported extremes when means are unavailable; they are not necessarily averages of observed clutches. A fractional value is a summary, not a count of part of an animal. The dataset defines lowest and highest values as endpoints of reported means, not as the smallest and largest individual clutches. These labels do not establish embryo nutrition or uniformity among populations.

## Evidence limits {#s-c63c0b3e13}
This account paraphrases the same-name ReptTraits v1-2 entry, retaining its 2023 taxonomic framework and sources for individual traits. One row per species compresses variation in sex, age, locality and populations; matching name and family do not ensure equivalent later concepts. Reported maximum length is not an absolute species limit, and reproductive means are not fixed counts in every clutch. Many mass entries are estimated from length equations and are not presented here as measured mass. Juvenile-length headers and definitions differ and are also omitted from reader conclusions. Conservation, venom, current distribution and population trends are not updated here; unrecorded fields remain gaps.
