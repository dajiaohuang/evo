## Tooth rows raise a question about filtering {#s-658511c46a}
The 2017 Liaodactylus description covers a skull, mandibles and the first two neck vertebrae. Its 152 close-set teeth shorten toward the rear, which the authors interpret as an adaptation for filtering. The 2023 Balaenognathus study describes a near-complete skeleton, at least 480 teeth, hooked crowns and a broad, toothless front edge, alongside water-flow models. Both raise questions about filtering, but their specimens and proposed mechanisms differ. Pumping proposed for Balaenognathus is not known behavior in Liaodactylus.

## The two studies test different evidence {#s-79b751ed70}
The Liaodactylus primus study compares skull shape and tooth rows, including a morphospace analysis that compares measured form. The Balaenognathus maeuseri paper studies tooth hooks, the snout opening and shallow-water flow models. Their oldest-record and relationship claims come from separate analyses; they do not provide a complete natural history for the group.

## Liaodactylus: PBDB classification route {#s-a127faab45}
PBDB records Liaodactylus at genus rank and links one species name beneath it. The selected occurrence rows provide a route to member records, not a genus-wide account of anatomy or ecology.

## Member material linked to Liaodactylus {#s-ffdccc371c}
The selected catalogue entry names Liaodactylus primus and one collection in the Tiaojishan Formation. Its occurrence and collection counts describe database rows, not the number of animals or a past population.

## Limits of the selected Liaodactylus records {#s-7c4d9f72b2}
The filtering proposal for Liaodactylus is an interpretation of its tooth row, not an observation of feeding. Balaenognathus supplies a separate specimen and water-flow model; that model does not establish pumping behavior in Liaodactylus. The oldest-record claim is bounded to the 2017 study and its sampled material.
