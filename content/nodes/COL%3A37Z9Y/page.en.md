## Relative weights of food categories {#s-281b5f21bb}
The archive records these nonzero food-category weights for this entry: fruit 100%. Source code B indicates reasonable source confidence with potentially uncertain relative proportions. The percentages are standardized readings of verbal descriptions, not measured portions of the diet. A zero weight does not show that a food is never eaten. Nect, Seed and PlantO are broader source categories than their everyday names; their labels do not establish a diet made up exclusively of nectar, seeds or plants.

## Foraging strata differ from whole-day activity {#s-986c041d3d}
The archive marks these foraging-position weights as species-level: in or just above the canopy 80%; trees or high bushes from 2 m upward but below the canopy 20%. They estimate relative use of feeding positions; they are not nest sites, all habitat or a whole-day time budget. Source definitions for surface-water, understory, mid/high, canopy and aerial categories differ. These weights are not elevation ranges and cannot be used to infer flight heights or habitat area.

## Life-history evidence limits {#s-1d48fd5abf}
This is a reading guide to historical same-name, same-family foraging records in EltonTraits 1.0, not a complete species monograph. Matching names and classification do not establish equivalence between historical species concepts and COL26.8. Food and avian stratum percentages are coarse relative weights standardized by the authors from literature descriptions, not direct measurements of stomach contents, intake, energy or observation time. Avian A/B diet codes retain source and proportional uncertainty. Mammalian ABC diet or activity codes can also refer to a congener judged ecologically similar by the compilers; this page does not claim direct observations of this species for every field. Genus/family typical values, non-species-level avian strata and split records needing individual review do not fill definite conclusions. Underlying books and papers have not been individually reread. Individual, seasonal and local variation, current range, population and conservation still require other sources.
