## Stem surface {#s-e487fcb12c}
The Brazilian Flora 2020 record describes a strigose stem surface, with stiff hairs lying close to the surface and pointing forward. It lists simple hairs, thread-like unbranched bristles, and capitate glandular hairs whose tips form small heads. Branched bristles, prickles, and short lateral shoots (brachyblasts) are absent.

## Leaves and inflorescences {#s-e3ee059ef3}
Stipules, small structures at leaf bases, are caducous and fall early. Extrafloral nectaries (nectar-secreting glands outside flowers) and projections between pairs of pinnae (the leaf’s primary side divisions) are absent. The inflorescence, or flower cluster, is globose; its floral bracts (small leaf-like structures) are shorter than the flowers before they open (anthesis).

## Flowers {#s-7b02967bf3}
The perianth (calyx and corolla together) is recorded as having four parts. The membranous calyx, the outer sepal whorl, has triangular lobes. Corolla (petal-whorl) lobes have one to three veins and are listed as hairless or pubescent, with short soft hairs; the source does not choose between these descriptions. The androecium (the stamens collectively) is diplostemonous, with two stamen whorls; the filaments are pink.

## Habitat category {#s-b7e0b2b56e}
The regional dataset labels the habitat “Terrícola,” Portuguese for terrestrial. This category does not give a collection locality or establish the species’ full range.
