Based on the presence of these genres, Brandoni et al. (2012) mention that the Salicas Formation has a greater affinity with the Cerro Azul Formation in the province of La Pampa, than with the El Jarillal Member of the Chiquimil Formation and the Andalhuala Formation in the province of Catamarca, and consider that the fauna of the Salicas Formation belongs to the late Miocene without specifying more until new fossil discoveries and radio-isotopic dating allow a better calibration of it

The finding of N. coagmentatus reported here contributes new elements to the discussion of the age of the Salicas Formation. This species was recognized by Ameghino in 1888, near Villa Cura Brochero; in the Brochero Formation [Montehermosense - Chapadmalalense (early Pliocene - late Pliocene)] (Cruz et al., 2013). In addition to the holotype, there is another reliable quote for this species, which consists of the presence of osteoderms of the dorsal breastplate, originating from the El Polvorín Formation (upper Chapadmalalense, [late Pliocene]) of the Sierras Bayas, Olavarría (Buenos Aires) (Zamorano et al., 2015). Although it is still premature to affirm the precise age of this finding, it could confirm the late Miocene age suggested by Brandoni et al. (2012) and even show the presence of somewhat younger levels of the Salicas Formation that could reach the Pliocene in the town of Suri Yaco. An intensive search for fossils in this locality will allow us to determine the age of these levels

Cerdeno and Schmidt 2013: the details on geographic provenance and stratigraphic correlations are described in this paper. Tauber (2005) assigned the faunal remains to the Huayquerian Age (late Miocene) and Brandoni et al. (2012) established correlations with the well-known Neogene beds of the Andalhuala and Chiquimil formations, in Catamarca Province. They considered that the Salicas assemblage can roughly correspond to the range from 6.02 to 6.68 Ma obtained for these levels (Marshall and Patterson, 1981), closer to the recently proposed Huayquerian/Montehermosan boundary for the North- west of Argentina (Reguero and Candela, 2011).
