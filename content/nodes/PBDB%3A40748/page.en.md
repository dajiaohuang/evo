## How PBDB organizes fossil occurrences {#s-65d32583b2}
PBDB groups the selected fossil-occurrence records for *Adapiformes* under its recorded classification path and links them to collections, strata, age brackets, and references. This hierarchy organizes database records; it remains distinct from COL taxonomy and is not a phylogenetic hypothesis.

## Named adapiform records across fossil localities {#s-084807c4e0}
The route includes *Caenopithecus lemuroides*, *Masradapis tahai*, *Afradapis longicristatus*, *Namadapis interdictus* and *Darwinius masillae*. Their occurrence records link collections and strata such as Birket Qarun, Messel, Jebel Qatrani and Caijiachong. Read each locality’s age and identification with its own record; these entries do not estimate ancient population size.

## How to interpret the displayed age brackets {#s-9943ac574d}
These PBDB age brackets bound the registered occurrences shown here. They do not by themselves establish a taxon’s global first or last appearance or a clade’s origin or extinction.

## Early primate fossils test competing relationships {#s-a8717c4236}
Adapiformes includes fossils studied through dental, cranial and postcranial anatomy. A systematic review compares how different analyses place these early euprimates relative to living strepsirrhines and anthropoids; the results do not point to one uncontested ancestor line.

## The Eocene interval is a review sample {#s-5e15ad71a9}
The displayed 56–34 million-year interval summarizes the Eocene adapiform material covered by that review. It is a sample window, not a direct lineage from adapiforms to any living primate group.
