## Recorded classification and direct parent {#s-2af71f348f}
COL26.8 records Macrovipera at genus rank, with Viperinae as its direct parent.

Pinned COL26.8 places Macrovipera in Viperidae. The source-rich ReptTraits v1-2 route has one same-name, same-family species record: Macrovipera lebetinus. The workbook uses its 2023 name framework; this single record is not the genus's accepted-species count.

## Which length measures can be compared? {#s-8110692ea5}
Length measures must match before values are compared. Turtle records use straight carapace length (SCL), while snake records use snout-to-vent length (SVL); these are not interchangeable or suitable for one size ranking. Other comparisons also need the same field definition and source basis.

## Limits attached to these records {#s-08182a0c72}
This guide covers only this batch’s entries with at least four sourced focal traits, a size or reproductive value, and matching COL name and family. It is not an exhaustive descendant list. Maximum sizes are reported compiled records, not individual averages or identification thresholds. Turtle carapace length and snout–vent length in other groups are different measures. Consult sources and aggregation rules by field; historical one-row records do not resolve every taxonomic revision or population difference.

## How should the limited-field record be compared? {#s-9ecb2112b1}
The Macrovipera razii page supports two focal fields. Compare only fields with the same definition and a source for each record; an absent value does not establish a biological absence, and a related species cannot supply it. Keep turtle SCL separate from snake SVL.

## What do these two routes cover? {#s-8e5081ad06}
The source-rich route selects entries with at least four cited focal traits plus a size or reproductive value; the limited-field route can include an entry with one or more sourced focal traits. Both require a name and family match to pinned COL26.8, but neither is a complete Macrovipera species list. Historical one-row records may conceal taxonomic changes and population differences. This page provides no current conservation or population-status assessment.
