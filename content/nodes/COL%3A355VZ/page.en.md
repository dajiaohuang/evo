## A low, prostrate perennial {#s-2ad1382794}
This multi-stemmed perennial grows close to the ground, reaching 0.05 m, and regrows from a woody rootstock. Its hairless, striated, nearly round stems are 140–500 mm long and under 2 mm wide; internodes are usually 15–30 mm, sometimes 40 mm. The simple, linear leaves measure 6–12 by 1–2 mm and become more reduced toward the stem tip. A terminal, umbrella-shaped flower cluster bears 8–12 rays, with 0–12 smaller side clusters; the primary rays are 10–18 mm long and the tiny raylets 1–3 mm. Flowers have five parts and bisexual organs, with yellow petals 0.5–1.2 by 0.5–1 mm and sepals reduced to minute teeth. The elliptic fruit measures 2.8–3.3 by 1.8–2.3 mm and carries warty, finger-like projections. It splits into two similar halves (mericarps), usually with one slightly smaller and often aborted; each is five-ribbed, with 2 commissural and 3 vallecular oil ducts. Flowering is reported from February to May.

## Features used to distinguish the species {#s-fa17bad6d8}
The account identifies the prostrate growth form and warty projections on the ovary and fruit as distinctive within *Deverra*. Compared with the other two southern African species, it describes slender branches under 2 mm wide, basal internodes under 35 mm, and smaller umbels with rays under 15 mm. The projections also appear denser than in *D. burchellii*; those of *D. aphylla* are described as woolly. These are regional diagnostic comparisons, not a global survey of variation.

The description is regional and supplies no dated sampling frame or population-frequency estimate.

## Limestone habitat {#s-28b5c603df}
The regional account places both reported populations on limestone outcrops in the Savanna Biome, including Postmasburg Thornveld and Ghaap Plateau Vaalbosveld. It describes the plant as common on weathered limestone beside the Groenwaterspruit river and notes that it is multi-stemmed and regenerates from a woody rootstock.
