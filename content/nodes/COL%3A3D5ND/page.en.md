## Euryops galpinii {#s-a2b38f0214}
## Sessile flower heads, ciliate leaf margins and a short pappus

Nordenstam describes a dense, rounded, much-branched shrub up to 1 m tall. The glabrous branches are leafy above, but lower down lose their leaves and retain short leaf-bases or scars. Crowded, leathery leaves are 5–10 by 2–4 mm, elliptic to narrowly oblong or obovate, and either entire or two- to three-lobed at the tip. They are green, distinctly veined and short-mucronate, with rigid, fine cilia along the margins; lobes may occur on the same plant as entire leaves.

Heads are sessile and terminal, solitary or paired, with a woolly tuft at the base. Their yellowish-green involucres are bell- or cup-shaped, 6–12 mm high, with 8–13 bracts in one row. Each head usually has 8–15 bright-yellow ray florets and about 13–35 disc florets. The pappus is sparse and very short, 0.2–0.5 mm and sometimes 1 mm; brown dry fruits have about 8–11 thick ribs.

Sessile heads, stiffly ciliate leaf margins and the short, sparse pappus distinguish the species. The account records Karoo-type vegetation, often in open, exposed sites, and sometimes grassland invaded by karroo or rhenosterbush.
