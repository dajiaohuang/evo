## Accepted name and placement {#s-c336891fa7}
COL26.8 accepts Nanorana laojunshanensis Tang, Liu & Yu, 2023 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Dicroglossidae (family) → Nanorana (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## Six July 2019 types delimit the measured size sample {#s-80c6a7d726}
The adult male holotype GXNU YU090314 was collected on 24 July 2019 at Mt. Laojun, Lijiang, Yunnan, at 3,982 metres above sea level. Four adult male paratypes and one adult female came from the same locality and collection event. Table 5 gives male snout–vent lengths of 33.3–38.5 millimetres for five males, including the 36.1-millimetre holotype, and 42.9 millimetres for the single female. This is a type-series sample rather than a population size distribution, growth series or statistical test of female–male size difference.

## External diagnosis uses a combination of characters {#s-ac283e7e54}
The published diagnosis combines a visible tympanum, distinct vomerine teeth, head wider than long, a slender supratympanic fold, indistinct subarticular tubercles, no dorsolateral fold and absent vocal sac. The holotype has blunt, unexpanded finger tips, nearly equal fingers I and II, and fully webbed toes; head width is 11.3 versus head length 10.7 millimetres. These external traits belong to the examined taxonomic material, not a skeleton or larval diagnosis. The diagnosis says supernumerary tubercles are absent, while the holotype description calls basal finger tubercles small and indistinct; that textual conflict remains unresolved.

## Brown live pattern and yellow limb venter change in preservation {#s-a451bcb81b}
The living holotype is brown with irregular dark dorsal and lateral patches, darker head bands, and a grey-white venter with yolk-yellow areas beneath the limbs. Its iris is dark with golden-brown mottling. In preservative the dorsum fades toward grey-brown and the venter is described as white. The single female has a dorsal midline stripe and smoother dorsal surface. These observations retain specimen and preservation context; one female does not establish an invariant sex-specific colour rule.

## Male spines suggest a season without documenting spawning {#s-7517864e47}
Examined males had nuptial pads and small brown spines on the bases of fingers I and II, paired chest patches, and an M-shaped spine patch along the lower-jaw edge. The authors infer a breeding season around July and August from these male characters and the collection context. They did not collect tadpoles or report direct spawning, clutch size, larval duration or a courtship recording for the new species. The inference should not be rewritten as a year-round breeding calendar or a measured developmental sequence.

## Known marsh and pond occurrence is restricted to the type site {#s-36d4f50239}
At publication in 2023, laojunshanensis was known only from its Mt. Laojun type locality, where it inhabited marshes and ponds. The holotype elevation is a record, not an experimentally measured altitude tolerance or a whole-species elevational range. The authors distinguish this eastern Jinsha River lineage from the Zhongdian Plateau ventripunctata lineage. No comprehensive range census, population trend or verified current conservation category is supplied by this taxonomic account.

## Three mitochondrial loci and one nuclear locus support a lineage {#s-d6b0938e81}
The study analysed 16S, COI, cytochrome b and RAG-1, with aligned lengths of 575, 674, 965 and 1,189 bases. Both 16S and combined analyses placed the new lineage sister to the ventripunctata–pleskei clade. Table 4 gives 16S p-distances of 1.6% to ventripunctata and pleskei, 2.0% to bangdaensis and 1.6% to parkeri; corresponding COI values differ markedly. These marker distances are not whole-genome divergence, divergence dates or a universal species threshold. The new lineage is supported by combined morphological and molecular evidence rather than a fixed three-percent cutoff.

## Size-adjusted comparisons distinguish sampled close relatives {#s-f5deb262c8}
The authors measured eight pleskei and eight ventripunctata specimens in addition to the six new-species types. Size-adjusted principal-component analyses included males and females. The comparison with ventripunctata separated samples along PC2, interpreted as narrower upper eyelids, larger eyes and greater nostril–eye distance in laojunshanensis. The pleskei comparison separated along PC1, interpreted as narrower internarial distance and eyelids and longer limbs. Separate pairwise analyses have different axes; they are not direct measures of locomotor performance or a universal identification boundary. Supplementary individual measurements were not independently reanalysed for this page.
