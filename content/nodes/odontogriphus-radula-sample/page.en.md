## Odontogriphus: repeated tooth rows in a large fossil sample {#s-4d0063b73b}
How can a soft-bodied animal’s feeding apparatus be reconstructed when no one fossil preserves it all? Across 189 Burgess Shale specimens, Odontogriphus repeatedly shows paired transverse tooth rows on a membrane, with new rows added from behind, alongside a broad foot and structures in the mantle groove. The repeated geometry is observable in the sample; identifying the apparatus as a molluscan radula and placing the animal on the molluscan stem are comparative interpretations, not direct ancestry.
