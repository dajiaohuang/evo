## Accepted name and placement {#s-eda22bb1d6}
COL26.8 accepts Mantidactylus danieli Rabibisoa, Welt & Raxworthy, 2023 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Mantellidae (family) → Mantidactylus (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## Granular brown colour and a narrow inguinal streak form part of the diagnosis {#s-e4612f4729}
The account reports males at 33–42 mm and females at 42–59 mm snout–vent length. Dark brown surfaces carry black granules, with a narrow, sometimes interrupted pale yellow inguinal streak; a continuous lower-flank pale stripe and obvious white eye spot are absent. Toe IV has 1.5–2 inner phalanges free, the reported webbing score is greater than seven, and the tibiotarsal articulation extends beyond the nostril. Maximum disc width divided by base width is below 1.70. These sampled characters should be used together, retaining the source’s score rather than treating it as millimetres.

## River-bank observations include sex-specific perch heights {#s-2b76745fac}
The natural-history account records encounters from 10:30 to 23:00, more often at night, at river banks reported at 350–1,580 m. Males were mostly on banks, less often leaves and rarely rocks; females were more often in trees than on rocks. Reported male perch heights are 20–100 cm and female heights 50–300 cm. The authors describe an apparent river association and females often near slow water. These are observational tendencies, not experimental preferences, density estimates or proof that individuals never use other substrates.

## Two elevation summaries in the source remain distinct {#s-0fb050973f}
The distribution paragraph places danieli in northern Madagascar and the Sambirano region in subhumid to humid settings at 180–1,200 m. The natural-history paragraph instead gives 350–1,580 m. These are inconsistent summaries within the same study; neither is silently substituted for the other or merged into a newly certified 180–1,580 m range. The regional account does not demonstrate complete occupancy or establish the cause of the difference. Specimen-level locality verification is still needed to reconcile the bounds.

## Two older femoralis-labelled sequences belong to this molecular group {#s-9734ed19d6}
The comments connect danieli with earlier aff. femoralis material from Tsaratanana. Its molecular group includes AY324818, FGMV 2002.929 from Montagne d’Ambre, previously candidate species 42, and AY324816, FGMV 2002.825 from Manongarivo; these had been labelled cf. femoralis. The connection concerns specified vouchers and a mitochondrial grouping. It does not transfer all historical femoralis localities to danieli, establish genomic isolation or resolve the identity of every unsequenced specimen.
