## Accepted name and placement {#s-65240bc3e1}
COL26.8 lists Arthroleptis taeniatus Boulenger, 1906 as a species under Anura (order) → Arthroleptidae (family) → Arthroleptis (genus). This is the placement recorded in the release dated 20 August 2026. It identifies the catalogue entry; it does not independently confirm the study's specimen identification.

## From a catalogue name to sampled vouchers {#s-dc17a283f5}
This page connects the COL26.8 accepted-name record to five vouchers from a survey in Gabon. The paper assigned those samples to Arthroleptis taeniatus using barcode comparisons, reference sequences and specimen morphology. Read this as the study's identification of its sampled material, not as a complete range account or independent confirmation of the catalogue concept. The AmphiBIO link is a separate historical record.

## The AmphiBIO record is a separate source concept {#s-18d181081f}
AmphiBIO v1 has a record under this name, using a source concept that follows Frost 2011's *Amphibian Species of the World*. Its recorded classification and values belong to that historical dataset; equivalence with COL26.8 has not been established. The TetrapodTraits v2.0.1 binding is a dataset-wide revision notice for body-mass and microhabitat entries, but the affected original rows remain unverified. Use the AmphiBIO link as historical source context, not as direct evidence about the study's LMR vouchers.

## What did the LMR survey sample? {#s-726f6d5b95}
The study lists five United States National Museum (USNM) vouchers, 558520–558524, from the LMR sample. Four were first labelled *Arthroleptis* sp.; USNM 558520 was first identified as *Cardioglossa*. LMR is the roughly 52 km study band along the projected Loubomo–Mougagara Road, extending 2–3 km to either side in Nyanga Province, Gabon. The paper's “RC” means Republic of the Congo, not the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Museum specimens from other sites and the RC are comparison material, not LMR observations. Field sampling took place on May 20–June 19, 2012, and March 13–April 23, 2013. Non-detection is bounded by those methods and periods; it does not establish regional absence or a complete range.

## How did the authors assign the taeniatus name? {#s-1eb40a8233}
Four LMR specimens initially labelled *Arthroleptis* sp. (USNM 558521–558524) were placed in one Barcode of Life Database (BOLD) Barcode Index Number (BIN) for cytochrome oxidase subunit I (COI) sequences and one Automatic Barcode Gap Discovery (ABGD) group. Their mitochondrial 16S large-ribosomal-subunit sequences formed a comparison clade with GenBank records labelled *A. sylvaticus* and *A. taeniatus*. The authors distinguished this group from their other *A. sylvaticus* clade and used type-locality context to identify it as *A. taeniatus*; the type locality cited is “Zima” (= Sangmelima), southern Cameroon. A fifth specimen, USNM 558520, was first labelled *Cardioglossa*; barcode comparison and a second specimen examination supported its identification as *A. taeniatus*. The paper therefore does not count its initial label as a *Cardioglossa* observation at LMR.

## What can the barcode methods resolve? {#s-03c8f3612e}
For *A. taeniatus*, the study combines COI BIN/ABGD groups, 16S comparisons with GenBank, and morphology/type-locality evidence. The two markers are mitochondrial; they provide no independent nuclear confirmation and do not test reproductive isolation. Neighbor-joining (NJ) trees were used to cluster similar sequences, which the authors explicitly distinguish from reconstructing evolutionary history. Most species in the library were represented by only one or two localities, and the authors could not examine every GenBank voucher morphologically. Reference labels and sequence coverage are therefore uneven. Field sampling covered two periods in 2012 and 2013; GenBank and BOLD comparison queries used records available on 11 November 2016, not a current database search.

## Study-wide counts are not taeniatus population estimates {#s-369e46cc23}
The paper reports 540 sequenced specimens across its multispecies library: 524 COI barcodes, 528 16S sequences, 94 BOLD BINs and 85 ABGD groups. These totals are not counts for *A. taeniatus* or a census of currently accepted species. The methods describe 80 first-season and 97 second-season LMR specimens, while Table 1 lists 79 and 97 sequenced or retained entries. The methods also state that 275 reference specimens came from the Republic of the Congo, but the Table 1 locality rows sum to 274. The paper does not explain these collection-versus-analysis differences.

## catalogue-profile / limitations {#s-e1066e4c3b}
The paper's name assignment is specific to five LMR vouchers and the reference sequences available to the study. Its mitochondrial markers, limited locality sampling and uneven voucher comparisons do not establish a complete range or independently confirm the current COL26.8 species concept.

## What did the reference index return? {#s-8af018cc53}
The 2026-10-03 reference index returned one entry for the accepted name *Arthroleptis taeniatus*. Queries for the archived names *Schoutedenella taeniata* and *Arthroleptis variabilis pica* returned zero. These are index counts, not reviews of the cited works or evidence that other research is absent. No biological claims were assigned from the index; the study evidence above comes from the separately bound 2017 paper.
