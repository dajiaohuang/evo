## A clade revised with molecular evidence {#brief}
The 2025 Labridae revision defines Sparisomatini by a minimum crown clade and places several familiar parrotfish genera within it. Its history shows why a clade account must distinguish earlier morphology-based groupings from later molecular trees and state which lineages lack molecular data.

## Anatomical characters used in the diagnosis {#diagnostic-structures}
Near and colleagues’ 2025 clade account lists incompletely coalesced oral teeth forming four plates, three rows of teeth on the upper pharyngeal jaw, 11 branched pectoral-fin rays, nine abdominal plus 16 caudal vertebrae, four or five median predorsal scales, and one row of cheek scales as diagnostic apomorphies. The account attributes these characters to earlier anatomical work; they are a proposed diagnosis for the clade, not a guarantee that every specimen has been checked for every character.

## Molecular trees revised the grouping {#clade-relationships}
The 2025 treatment defines Sparisomatini as the least inclusive crown clade containing Cryptotomus roseus, Leptoscarus vaigiensis and Sparisoma chrysopterum. It reports that molecular analyses consistently recover the tribe as monophyletic, with Leptoscarus plus Calotomus sister to a group containing Sparisoma, Cryptotomus and Nicholsina; an earlier analysis of 143 morphological characters instead found the traditional grouping paraphyletic. The synthetic Labridae phylogeny used 1,009 UCE loci as its molecular foundation and added taxa using published relationships or explicit taxonomic evidence. The authors note that molecular data were unavailable for Sparisoma griseorubrum.
