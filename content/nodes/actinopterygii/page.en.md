## Bones and genomes reveal different parts of ray-fin history {#reading-introduction}
CT scans expose structures inside Cheirolepis fossils; comparisons of living genomes test relationships between gars, bowfin and teleosts. These approaches answer different questions. A molecular estimate of genome duplication adds a timescale, while remaining distinct from the dates of fossil specimens.

## The dated record and its limits {#s-6323041294}
The evidence-linked Actinopterygii navigation envelope starts at 390.4 Ma, the older edge of the late Eifelian interval represented by CT-scanned Cheirolepis trailli specimens, and extends to living ray-finned fishes at 0 Ma; it is not a global class FAD or a claim that Cheirolepis was a direct ancestor.

## A Devonian fin skeleton complicates the early picture {#early-skeleton}
At Tynet Burn and Gamrie in Scotland, late Eifelian specimens of *Cheirolepis trailli* supplied a rare view inside an early ray-finned fish. High-resolution CT scans of named museum material revealed parts of the braincase, jaw suspension and pectoral-fin endoskeleton, including an imperforate propterygium—the leading bone of the paired fin—and scales with peg-and-socket joints. These are observations from a sampled fossil, not a complete body plan for Actinopterygii. The 390.4–388.1 Ma interval is the biostratigraphic age assigned to the Achanarras Limestone on the study's cited timescale, not a direct date on the specimens or a global first appearance.

## A living-genome comparison places Holostei beside teleosts {#living-branches}
One phylogenomic study combined 243 one-to-one protein orthologs from 25 jawed vertebrates with bowfin transcriptome data and a spotted-gar genome. Its supported tree places Holostei (gars plus bowfin) as the sister group of Teleostei. The genome assembly came from one wild-caught adult female spotted gar from Bayou Chevreuil, St. James Parish, Louisiana, sequenced at 90× coverage. This is an inference from a defined set of living genomes and transcriptomes; it is not a fossil date or a sample of every ray-finned lineage.

## A genome duplication predates crown-teleost estimates {#genomic-timescale}
A separate molecular-clock analysis used 30 teleost ohnologue pairs—duplicate genes retained after a whole-genome duplication—to estimate the teleost-specific 3R event at 286.18–267.20 Ma. In the same analysis, the crown-Teleostei interval was 254.36–234.16 Ma. These are 95% highest-posterior-density model intervals, not fossil occurrences or a single observed instant. Their separation is compatible with delayed rediploidization and does not support 3R as a deterministic cause of later teleost richness.
