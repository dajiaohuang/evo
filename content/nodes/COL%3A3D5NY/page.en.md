## Euryops latifolius {#s-7d0c9fcaa8}
## Broad, many-veined leaves rise above summit fynbos

Nordenstam describes an erect, hairless shrub 1–2.5 m tall, loosely branched above. Its flat, leathery leaves crowd the branch tips; lower stems become bare and bear scale-like leaf scars. Leaves are elliptic-ovate, 1.5–4 cm long and 6–15 mm wide, grey-green or glaucous, with many distinct veins, blunt tips and densely spaced forward-pointing teeth. Their bases partly clasp the stem.

Several or many flower stalks arise from upper leaf axils in corymb-like clusters. The stalks are 2–4 cm long and have tiny reduced leaves in their axils. The involucre is a very short, broad bell with 13–15 bracts in one row. Heads have 11–15 yellow ray florets and about 50–90 disc florets. White pappus bristles are numerous, 1.5–2.5 mm long and densely barbed. The dark brown to honey-coloured dry fruits are hairless, 3–4 mm long, with 13–20 close ribs and a short white tip formed by the stylophore.

The broad, flat, many-veined, serrated leaves distinguish the species within *Euryops*. Seedlings and young plants have much narrower, more divided leaves. Nordenstam considered *E. algoensis* the closest living relative, based on its flat leaves and similar involucres.

The regional account describes a mountain mostly covered by dense bushveld, with a small fynbos area on the 650 m summit. *E. latifolius* grows there among *Leucospermum*, *Protea*, *Erica*, *Metalasia* and *Polygala*, and is among the tallest plants; its long stems rise above the dense cover. *Oldenburgia arbuscula* is mentioned separately as confined to rocky outcrops, not strictly part of the same community.
