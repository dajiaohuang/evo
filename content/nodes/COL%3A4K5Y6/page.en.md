## Two syntypes at different developmental stages {#s-ef3cd5487a}
The 2025 catalogue lists RMNH.MAM.39088 as a subadult female and RMNH.MAM.39089 as a juvenile of unknown sex, both syntypes of Callithrix donacophilus preserved as mounted skins with skulls in situ. Both give Bolivia, with the added collection period [January–September 1832]; 39088 also records accession in 1834, which is not its collection year. Two types at different developmental stages are not a sample for a growth curve, adult size range or population age structure.

## The plate name and later description have different years {#s-8d8dee6c13}
The Naturalis authors interpret the Plate 5 caption as the first valid publication in 1835 rather than the commonly used 1836; Table 2 specifies October 1835, and the detailed description followed in 1847. This records their bibliographic interpretation without changing pinned COL26.8 name-authority years or freshly examining original fascicles and recalculating publication dates. Name publication, specimen collection and species origin are different times; none of these bibliographic years is a biological origin date.

## Collection labels do not alone settle lectotype status {#s-66cb1d9384}
The authors list Paris database specimen MNHN-ZM-2007-1505 labeled holotype and numbers 1506, 1525 and 1527 labeled paratypes, noting that the original series evidently contained several specimens. They find Rode’s 1938 reason for selecting one unclear and do not regard it as a valid lectotype designation. This is an interpretation of historical records and nomenclature, without a fresh Paris database query or formal new lectotype designation here. Moxos itinerary information and Bolivia labels retain different precision and do not assign type identity to the Santa Cruz urban-park behavioral sample described here.

## Behavioral records {#s-32842a4278}
In an ecotourism park near Santa Cruz, Bolivia, researchers followed six family groups of 4–6 animals and compared activity and responses to a simulated human intrusion along a highway-noise gradient. Adult pairs spent less time moving as noise increased, while groups farther from the highway gave more alarm calls to a mannequin.

## Physiology {#s-5e82bee2a4}
Fecal cortisol in the same study did not vary significantly with the acoustic noise indices. The authors noted that noise and human presence covaried at the site; this result describes the park sample and does not establish that the species is generally unaffected by disturbance.

## Evidence limits {#s-40dd6f605b}
Noise and human presence covaried in one Santa Cruz ecotourism park; behavior data came from six family groups, and cortisol analyses used 30 fecal samples from 19 animals. These observations cannot isolate noise as the cause or establish a species-wide response. The Naturalis catalogue’s type and locality interpretations concern historical specimens and remain separate from the park sample and pinned COL26.8 identity.
