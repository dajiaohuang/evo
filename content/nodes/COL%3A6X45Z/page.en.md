## COL26.8 name and the ReptTraits row {#s-ae87fee94c}
COL26.8 records Rafetus euphraticus as an accepted species. The ReptTraits v1-2 row gives its Family field as Trionychidae under the Reptile Database 2023 name framework. This association uses an exact accepted-name match and matching family; it does not establish that historical and current taxonomic concepts are identical.

## Length values in the archived records {#s-5b671a36c7}
The row reports a maximum straight carapace length (SCL) of 680 mm. ReptTraits attributes this field to Aubert et al. 2005, Feldman & Meiri 2013.

## Habitat use and activity labels {#s-44b3482e2a}
The microhabitat field classifies the recorded position as Aquatic/ground-dwelling (Terrestrial) [source label: Aquatic/Terrestrial]. It is a habitat-use category, not a full description of every setting used. ReptTraits attributes this field to IUCN 2023. The diet field classifies the entry as plant- and animal-eating (Omnivorous); this category does not identify particular prey or foods. ReptTraits attributes this field to Harrington et al. 2018. The foraging-mode field records ACT (active foraging). ReptTraits attributes this field to Rhodin et al. 2011.

## Reproductive fields in ReptTraits {#s-168d1fb3cb}
The compiled mean for offspring per litter or eggs per clutch is 30. It is a mean summary, not the count from one litter or clutch. ReptTraits attributes this field to De Magalhaes and Costa 2009, Feldman et al. 2015. The highest value among reported litter or clutch means is 32; the field does not make it the smallest or largest individual litter. ReptTraits attributes this field to Biricik and Turğa 2011. The reproductive-mode field records egg-laying (oviparous); this is the source's category label. ReptTraits attributes this field to Zimin et al. 2022.

## Reading this compiled trait record {#s-b9da44ab7e}
ReptTraits compiles separately cited fields into a species-level row. Its values may come from different source materials, localities, or study samples and should not be treated as observations from one animal.
