## A supported local clade is not a complete taxonomic decision {#s-3731003a71}
The main text reports statistical support for the cesarae-associated clade while retaining unresolved polytomies among Cebus lineages. These data do not fully establish the monophyly and relationships of all proposed species. The authors recommend a broader four-species arrangement pending additional mitochondrial and especially nuclear markers. That 2018 opinion remains distinct from this catalogue’s pinned COL26.8 species identity. A local clade neither compels a catalogue merger nor resolves every nearby sample’s identity.

## What two mitochondrial markers can address {#s-734ee71b16}
Martins-Junior and colleagues (2018) analyze mitochondrial cytochrome b and the control region. Across the full study, new material comes from 72 capuchins, yielding 137 new sequences, alongside previously published sequences. These study-wide counts are not individual counts for this page’s species. Both markers belong to the mitochondrial genome, rather than two independent nuclear-genome lines of evidence. Clustering can trace historical names without measuring feeding, reproduction or population size, or independently establishing complete species boundaries.

## The pinned name and synonym treatment {#s-153d02ef06}
COL26.8 accepts RYZ7 as Cebus cesarae Hershkovitz, 1949. MDD v2.5 lists the 2013 combination C. cesarae as a synonym of C. albifrons. This page records that disagreement and does not automatically treat regional evidence under older names as evidence for a modern, stable species boundary.

## A provisional taxonomy from limited sampling {#s-f0ea7c9d7f}
Boubli et al. (2012) built a time-calibrated phylogeny from mitochondrial DNA sequences for 50 untufted capuchins sampled across the group's range. Their provisional reassessment recognized C. cesarae and C. versicolor as northern Andean species, while explicitly calling for more extensive geographic sampling. This is a historical phylogenetic proposal, not an ecological account or a resolution of the current COL–MDD treatment difference.

## Evidence limits {#s-f59e81f6c6}
This page combines taxonomic history, genetic groupings, the 2019 handbook’s morphology and habitat summary, and a type record reported by a 2021 literature review. The type has not been linked to existing genetic samples or field groups. Names and taxon scopes differ among sources; current evidence does not establish independent ecology, a complete range or population information. Handbook dimensions lack original sample and method information, and missing traits are not filled from other species. Historical sample names, sequence associations, type diagnoses and current COL identities remain distinct. The 2018 molecular associations do not establish current species limits or complete field ecology.

## The handbook’s pale phenotype {#s-0f0461f9e0}
The 2019 EAZA cesarae account describes a pale phenotype with a brown-orange crown and more orange coloring along the central back, forearms and lower legs than on the flanks. Its body-size and tail fields are 35.3–38.5 cm and 46.1–50 cm for females, and 34.8–40.7 cm and 41.9–49.5 cm for males; mass data are lacking. Samples and methods are unspecified, and overlapping ranges cannot quantify sexual dimorphism. These remain records under the handbook’s taxonomy.

## Forest types do not supply a feeding study {#s-58d2dabfad}
The account lists semi-deciduous dry-forest patches, gallery forests and mangroves. It supplies no locality-specific feeding or reproductive observations, and its reproduction, diet and behavior fields lack data. The habitat compilation is kept separate from observations and is not promoted to range-wide ecology of the pinned COL species.

## The name-bearing type reported by the 2021 review {#s-741547a355}
García-Restrepo and Montilla’s 2021 literature review traces Cebus albifrons cesarae to page 356 of Hershkovitz’s 1949 description. It reports an adult male holotype, USNM 281588, collector number 525, collected by P. Hershkovitz on 24 August 1942. The type locality is Río Guaimaral, a channel of the Cesar River in Magdalena, Colombia, at 140 m elevation. This anchors the name to a specimen, but the review supplies no new genetic sequence from it. Locality alone cannot assign nearby research animals to the name.

## A provisional subspecies treatment in the review {#s-3a3547cb1f}
The paper provisionally treats versicolor and cesarae as subspecies of C. versicolor. It points to sparse genetic sampling in the Magdalena Valley and Caribbean region, unclear geographical limits and the need for detailed morphological comparisons. This is the authors’ 2021 taxonomic proposal. The pinned COL26.8 species entry remains in place; the review does not complete a diagnostic link between the name-bearing type, genetic groups and field ecology.
