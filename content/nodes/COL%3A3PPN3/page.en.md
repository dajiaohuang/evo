## Leaves, flowers and pod {#s-2634258bcb}
An older Flora of Panama account describes a small tree whose young branchlets are rather hairy and later become hairless. Compound leaves have two to four leaflet pairs. The 1–2 cm leaf stalk (petiole) is hairy and narrowly winged except at its base; the 2–10 cm central axis (rachis) has prominent wings between leaflets, up to 5 mm wide, and stalkless saucer- or cup-shaped glands where the smaller leaflet stalks attach. Leaflets range from egg-shaped to elliptic; the terminal ones reach 15 × 7 cm and the basal ones are about 5 × 2.5 cm. Their tips may be sharp, blunt or rounded; bases are blunt or rounded. Curved, close-lying hairs occur on both surfaces, and the veins stand out below. Broadly ovate stipules (small leaf-base appendages), 2–3 mm wide, fall early. Dense flower spikes arise from leaf axils near branch tips. The account leaves their number uncertain (“one to a few?”); the stalk is usually under 1 cm, and the compact flower-bearing part is 1–3 cm long. Small, lance-shaped bracts (leaf-like structures beside flowers) accompany crowded white flowers. The tubular calyx (outer floral cup) is 6–9 mm long, with lengthwise lines and small hair tufts at its lobes and sometimes its base; it is deeply split on one or two sides, making the teeth uneven. The tubular-funnel-shaped corolla (petal whorl) is 13–16 mm long, silvery-hairy outside, with lobes about 3 mm long. Stamens (pollen-producing parts) reach 4 cm including their fused basal tube. The nearly hairless ovary (the flower's seed-containing base) has four angles from side indentations, and the stigma (pollen-receiving tip) is head-shaped. The flattened, yellow-woolly pod is linear-oblong, up to 17 × 2 cm. This historical regional description gives no population sampling frame or observation date.
