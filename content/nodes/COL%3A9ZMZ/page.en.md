## Accepted name and placement {#s-324a8d65b6}
COL26.8 accepts Adenomera thomei (Almeida & Angulo, 2006) as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Leptodactylidae (family) → Adenomera (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## Reading route {#s-cbe82bccb0}
Begin with the accepted record and its parent genus, then use the family and order pages to locate related catalogue entries. This page supplies a taxonomic orientation. ## Why the compiled measurements are difficult to compare {#s-0aacebfc8f}
The workbook retains numeric entries for body length, head width, and tibia length, but the row has no head-width or tibia-length inference flag. A missing flag is not an independent check of how each upstream value was measured. The Methods use means when available and otherwise maxima; the workbook length headers omit explicit units and do not consistently identify sex or stage. These cells therefore establish neither diagnostic measurements in millimetres nor a sex-specific range or species-wide variation. The original references remain listed, but their measurements have not been independently checked.

## Development and egg placement in the compiled trait record {#s-825d4a9f74}
The Atlantic Forest trait compilation links one row to the source concept Adenomera thomei and records larval development with terrestrial oviposition. These are literature-coded categories: they do not give larval duration, the full set of larval habitats, or dependence on standing water, and they are not a new breeding observation or experiment on this species. The match between the source taxonomy and the currently accepted species concept remains uncertified.

## Juvenile habitat and foam-nest categories {#s-4813ebd24b}
The same row places juvenile habitat in the terrestrial category and marks foam nests among seven oviposition-site categories. These broad codes do not describe nest architecture, substrate species, or oviposition height; the juvenile category does not by itself establish adult habitat preference. The compilation adds no new result for clutch size, egg dimensions, parental attendance, breeding season, or offspring survival.
