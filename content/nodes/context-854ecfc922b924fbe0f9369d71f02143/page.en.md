## The source concept {#s-f60969707c}
AmphiBIO v1 lists Leptopelis concolor in row Anf0220, with genus Leptopelis, family Arthroleptina and order Anura. The source says its species concepts follow Amphibian Species of the World (Frost 2011); a link to a current COL26.8 concept has not been established. The traits below describe this archived row, not a new species assessment.

## Foraging strata {#s-eeecd272c8}
The row marks Ter=1, Aqu=1 and Arb=1 in its foraging-stratum fields. These are recorded categories, not measures of how often or how long an individual used each stratum; this row does not describe seasonal or life-stage changes. Later TetrapodTraits revisions reported corrections to some inherited microhabitat entries, but this row has not been compared field by field. Treat these archived flags as awaiting row-level review.

## Activity periods {#s-5aa99f217e}
Noc=1 is the night-activity category recorded for this row. It is a category label rather than a time series and does not show how activity varies between nights or seasons.

## Seasonal activity {#s-54fd7cfbc0}
Wet_warm=1 is the seasonal-activity category marked for this row. AmphiBIO compares conditions during activity with the local annual climate in these fields; they do not name fixed calendar months or establish activity throughout the year.

## Compiled adult dimensions {#s-91d8dea0e9}
AmphiBIO reports Body_size_mm=40 mm as a maximum adult body size. For Anura, this field denotes snout–vent length. It is a compiled maximum, not a sample distribution or a claim that every adult reaches this size.

## Compiled life history {#s-aabdae2a0c}
AmphiBIO records Reproductive_output_y=1, interpreted in its literature compilation as a maximum of one reproductive event per year. The row does not identify the population, sex, observation date or wild-versus-captive conditions behind that maximum, so it cannot be read as a schedule for every population.

## Developmental strategy {#s-8b181105e3}
The row marks Lar=1 as its developmental-category field; AmphiBIO labels this as a larval stage. The flag does not describe parental care, breeding location or timing.
