## Homo sapiens: distinct fossils, dates and datasets {#s-3d15332921}
The accepted name Homo sapiens covers a history wider than any single fossil or genome. The examples here include two Laos fossils dated from surrounding sediments, a Ranis isotope sample, a southern African genomic cohort and one directly dated Siberian femur. Their places, methods and limits remain distinct.

## Evidence from four different research settings {#s-f6a6685769}
The examples include TPL 6 and TPL 7 at Tam Pà Ling, 10 human and 52 faunal remains from Ranis, 28 people from southern African archaeological sites and one Ust’-Ishim femur. Sediment estimates, calibrated context dates, genomic comparisons and a direct radiocarbon date answer different questions.

## Bones, cave chemistry and ancient DNA {#s-f531560279}
A frontal-bone fragment, a proximal tibia, collagen isotopes and ancient genomes are different evidence. Tam Pà Ling ages come from surrounding sediments; Ust’-Ishim was directly dated; Ranis collagen was analyzed for dietary signals. Each finding stays tied to its material, method and place.

## No single study covers human history {#s-6b30405a0f}
The pinned COL26.8 checklist accepts Homo sapiens. These studies examine particular fossils, archaeological settings and genomic samples. They cannot be combined into a complete account of human origins, present-day diversity or migration.

## Contextual ages for TPL 6 and TPL 7 {#s-37c18714c4}
At Tam Pà Ling cave in northern Laos, TPL 6 is a left frontal-bone fragment and TPL 7 the upper end of a left tibia, both attributed to H. sapiens. Sediment-based models estimate 70 ± 3 thousand years for TPL 6 and as old as 77 ± 9 thousand years for TPL 7. These are contextual, not direct bone dates; TPL 7 may be older, and post-burial alteration limits measurement.

## Ranis collagen isotopes and a cave-specific diet signal {#s-322a3eb93f}
At Ilsenhöhle in Ranis, researchers measured collagen isotopes from 10 human remains and compared them with 52 animal remains from the same archaeological setting. Results indicate a large-terrestrial-mammal diet in a cold steppe–tundra environment. The layers date to about 47,500–45,770 and 46,820–43,260 calibrated years before present; these are not direct dates for each human bone. One cave cannot define human diets generally.

## A 28-person genomic comparison south of the Limpopo {#s-20b7d2059e}
A whole-genome study analyzed 28 people from archaeological sites south of the Limpopo in central and southern Africa, spanning 10,200 to 150 calibrated years before present. In that study's comparison panel, samples older than 1,400 years fell outside the variation represented by its modern samples and carried many variants the authors classified as H. sapiens-specific. The result depends on the sampled and comparison individuals and the authors' definitions.

## One directly dated Ust’-Ishim femur {#s-c8e859803a}
The Ust’-Ishim 1 femur from western Siberia has a direct radiocarbon age of 46,880–43,210 calibrated years before present at 95.4% probability. The genome comes from this one person; the date is not the origin of H. sapiens or the species' first or last appearance.

## Separate sites do not make one migration route {#s-eafe524fba}
The Laos fossils, Ranis diet signal, Siberian individual and southern African genomes differ in place, dating method and question. Together they provide several views of ancient H. sapiens, not a continuous migration route or the species' current range.

## Limits of the named human samples {#s-851d15b5f8}
These examples cover only the specified fossils and datasets. They do not describe all living populations, full life history, present range or species origin. Sediment estimates, direct fossil dates and genomic comparisons have different meanings.
