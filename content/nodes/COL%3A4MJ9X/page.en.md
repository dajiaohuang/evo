## Coverage and community turnover do not prove a species trend {#s-f8021de67d}
Reported global sample coverage is 97.4%, with habitat values 93.8–100%; this is not 97.4% of all provincial species discovered or proof that no rare species remain. The Methods’ sentence describing the next-record probability at high coverage reverses the usual observed-sample interpretation, and its effort wording does not resolve all per-habitat totals. Jaccard clustering and year-five diversity changes describe mixed herpetofaunal records, not a measured decline or recovery of this species. Raw yearly/habitat matrices and detection models have not been independently reconstructed.

## Species-labelled rows conflict with a genus-only identification statement {#s-6013863d04}
Methods state that Pristimantis individuals were conservatively identified only to genus, without molecular analyses. Table 2 nevertheless prints Pristimantis aureolineatus and other named Pristimantis species alongside five separate Pristimantis sp. rows. This conflict remains unresolved: the named row is presented as an attributed source record, not independent certification of species identity. The five unnamed rows are not assigned to this species or pooled into its 1 records. Original diagnostic photographs, vouchers or author clarification are needed before treating the names as verified occurrence evidence.
