## Stem surface {#s-718b43fd1a}
The Brazilian Flora 2020 record describes the stem as hirsute, meaning covered with coarse hairs; they spread outward rather than lying flat against the surface. It also lists simple hairs, thread-like unbranched bristles, and capitate glandular hairs, whose tips form small heads. Branched bristles, prickles, and short lateral shoots (brachyblasts) are recorded as absent.

## Leaves and inflorescences {#s-9cad7c6ef7}
The stipules, small structures at leaf bases, are caducous: they fall early. Extrafloral nectaries (nectar-secreting glands outside flowers) are absent, while projections between pairs of pinnae (the leaf’s primary side divisions) are present. The inflorescence, or flower cluster, is globose; its floral bracts (small leaf-like structures) are smaller than the flowers before they open (anthesis).

## Flowers {#s-b818ed8cdc}
The perianth (calyx and corolla together) is recorded as having four parts. The membranous calyx, the outer sepal whorl, is marked as lacking lobes. Corolla (petal-whorl) lobes have one to three veins and a tomentose covering of dense, matted hairs. The androecium (the stamens collectively) is diplostemonous, with two stamen whorls; the filaments are pink.

## Habitat category {#s-ea1ac284fc}
The regional dataset labels the habitat “Terrícola,” Portuguese for terrestrial. This category does not specify a collection locality or the species’ full range.
