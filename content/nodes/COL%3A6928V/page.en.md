## Accepted name and placement {#s-26823678bf}
COL26.8 lists Brachycephalus brunneus Ribeiro, Alves, Haddad & Reis, 2005 as an accepted species, with the recorded path Anura (order) → Brachycephalidae (family) → Brachycephalus (genus). This is the placement in the release issued on 20 August 2026. Names in another checklist or fossil database need their own identity evidence before they can be matched to this record.

## Sex-specific measurements retain variable-specific sample counts {#s-fd129c5d97}
In the 2025 comparison, male snout–vent length is 8.7–10.6 mm (N = 16) and female length is 9.2–11.4 mm (N = 10). Dorsal skin is described as smooth and lateral skin as moderately rough, each from 32 animals (n = 32). These are variable-specific counts, not extra specimens to add across measurements. The observed ranges are not population-wide limits, a statistical test of sexual dimorphism or a growth series. The study's “smooth” category can include small, inconspicuous warts; absence of an external digit alone does not establish skeletal absence.

## Live and preserved colours are separate evidence {#s-7814b9057b}
The authors describe live animals as brown with yellow ventral patches and preserved specimens as darker with cream patches. These are comparative observations, not measurements against a standardized colour chart. Counts vary by trait, and similar colours occur in multiple species, so a hue alone is not a full identification rule. Keep the live and preserved appearances distinct.

## Advertisement-call comparisons use distinct acoustic units {#s-558795259c}
In the note-centred comparison, note groups and attenuated notes are both recorded as present; the maxima are three notes per group and four pulses per note (N = 20 for each measure). Here N counts animals with one or more recorded and analysed calls, not the number of notes or complete calls. A call sequence, a note, a note group and a pulse are different units. These results describe the recordings examined, not fixed limits for every population; earlier call-centred descriptions may use different units.
