## Research observations {#s-eb362695b3}
A five-year study at Colosó followed individually marked groups and recorded group composition, stability, birth seasons and dispersal. The researchers observed both monogamous groups and groups with two pregnant females; males and females both dispersed to neighboring groups. These observations describe the study period and reserve, not a single mating or dispersal pattern for every group.

## Breeding {#s-6bbb84af3a}
Reproductive records collected at two Colombian field sites from 1994 to 2008 showed that births usually occurred once a year, litter sizes ranged from one to three and twins were most common; the study documented its first triplet litter during the observation period. Infant mortality was highest in the first week, and survival increased across successive litters. These are long-term records from the sampled sites, not a range-wide birth rate.

## Population evidence {#s-c2d8e68866}
A Colombian survey in 2005 and 2012 used a tailored lure-strip transect method in 43 accessible forest parcels, which the authors estimated represented about 30% of the species’ range. The estimated population in the surveyed region changed from about 2,050 to about 1,900. If unsurveyed forests had similar densities, the authors projected about 6,946 tamarins in the wild in 2012; that figure depends on an assumption about unsurveyed areas and was not a direct range-wide count.

## Evidence limits {#s-e71a624dd7}
Social observations, reproductive samples and forest surveys come from different sites, dates and designs; estimates for surveyed parcels remain distinct from extrapolation to the full range. Field papers use the combination Saguinus oedipus, while page identity follows the pinned COL26.8 ID..
