## Accepted name and sources {#s-941f92e8de}
COL26.8 accepts Varanus brevicauda in Varanidae. The ReptTraits v1-2 profile retains the workbook’s Reptile Database 2023 name framework and field-specific citations. This page also consults a separate 2015 life-history archive; the two sources are kept distinct because they use different records and summaries.

## Length stops before the tail {#s-57ff287943}
The reported maximum snout–vent length is 126 mm, with a female maximum of 91.5 mm. Snout–vent length measures to the body opening, excluding the tail. These compiled maxima do not describe typical size or define a growth limit; they may represent different samples and do not alone establish population-wide sexual dimorphism.

## Ground habitat, daytime, and mixed foraging {#s-4d938843b8}
The ReptTraits row lists a terrestrial microhabitat, carnivorous diet, diurnal activity, and mixed foraging mode. These categories do not name prey or a specific place, and the mixed mode does not specify how foraging changes. They are summaries, not a complete behaviour record for every season or life stage.

## Reproductive traits and their summaries {#s-970854f0d8}
The profile records oviparous reproduction, or egg-laying. Its consolidated mean is 2.92 offspring or eggs per litter or clutch, with reported mean endpoints of 1 and 8. The dataset may summarize reported means or use midpoint values when means are absent; these figures are not guaranteed counts in individual clutches. The endpoint fields describe reported means, not necessarily actual smallest and largest clutches.

## A separate historical life-history source {#s-b35f2288bd}
A 2015 archive contributes five focal fields from same-name, same-family sparse rows: adult mass, female maturity, clutch or litter size, birth or hatching mass, and incubation. The archive may preserve historical taxonomic inheritance. The shared name supports a cautious association, not proof that its concept exactly matches the COL26.8 placement.

## Mass at two different stages {#s-171eccb4d9}
That archive summarizes adult body mass as 10 g and birth or hatching mass as 1.9 g. Adult records may pool males, females, and adults of unspecified sex; hatchling mass refers to another life stage. These figures are not a growth trajectory or an individual mass range.

## Maturity, clutch size, and incubation {#s-0914d0dc18}
The archive reports the female-maturity field, defined there as age at first conception, as 699.97 days. It gives a separate litter or clutch summary of 3 and an oviposition-to-hatching interval of 77 days. The count of 3 is not the ReptTraits mean of 2.92; each comes from its own compilation. Maturity and incubation refer to different events, not one cycle.

## Where the historical citations lead {#s-6e60cf3763}
Source labels include Meiri (2010), BC Reptiles (Meyers et al., 2009), Pianka, King, and King (2004), and King and Green (1999), among others. Field-specific row numbers and values are retained in the archive. A row count is not a number of measured animals, and a repeated citation does not constitute an independent replication.

## Evidence limits {#s-c3f9e2ba21}
The ReptTraits profile keeps the workbook’s 2023 taxonomy framework; the second source is an August 2015 archive that can share or inherit data across historical species and subspecies. Name and family agreement do not establish equivalence with the pinned COL26.8 concept. Archive values are compilation summaries, not direct measurements of one animal, population ranges, or current surveys; the source conditions are not uniformly identified, and underlying papers were not individually reviewed here. Estimated mass fields and missing traits should not be treated as observed species-wide facts. Current range, conservation, venom, and other unrecorded traits remain unaddressed.
