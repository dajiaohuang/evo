## Waptia mouthparts across a large fossil sample {#s-4205bcbc16}
The Burgess Shale sample includes more than 1,800 *Waptia fieldensis* specimens. ROMIP 56432 shows mandibles and maxillules (mouth appendages), while ROMIP 64294 preserves opposing mandibles separated within the larger series. Together, the fossils document a bivalved body plan with jaws. Placement among Pancrustacea—the lineage that includes crustaceans and six-legged animals—and a swimming-predator lifestyle are interpretations from anatomy and phylogenetic analysis, not direct observations of behaviour or stomach contents.
