## Euryops brevipapposus {#s-d4a039999f}
## A short, sparse pappus distinguishes this narrow-leaved shrub

Herman and Retief's account describes a shrub 0.5–2 m tall, occasionally reaching 3 m. Its leaves are entire, flat and linear, 15–90 by 0.8–3 mm. The involucre is bell-shaped, brown to yellowish brown, and its bracts are joined only to the middle or less. The pappus is short and often sparse, 0.5–1 mm and sometimes 1.5 mm; the dry fruits are densely silky. Flowers are yellow, with flowering reported in July and from October through December.

Nordenstam compares it with several other narrow-leaved *Euryops*. The combination of a bell-shaped involucre, flat hairless leaves, short pappus and silky fruits helps distinguish it; unlike some similar species, the fruit hairs do not become mucilaginous when wet. In the southern part of the range, bracts are usually thin and joined only at the base; in the north they are firmer and more joined, and the pappus is especially short and sparse. Northern forms also vary more in leaf dimensions.

The regional habitat account lists scrub, bushveld and open forest, especially rocky places or riverbanks.
