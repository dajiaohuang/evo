## Strumaria picta: flower bands and hidden leaves {#s-acc03239a3}
A 2012 regional account describes a bulbous geophyte reaching 13 cm. Its two leaves are dry at flowering and spread outward; they are broadly strap-shaped, leathery and finely fringed with hairs. The widely bell-shaped flowers are scented and somewhat ascending. They are white with broad reddish-brown bands on the underside, and sit on finely hairy pedicels; the scape is often deflected sideways.

A 1994 systematic account distinguishes the species by widely bell-shaped flowers with a broad brownish-red median band on the underside of each tepal, broadened filaments and an ovoid style. It describes the spreading leaves as strap-shaped and glaucous, with margins densely fringed by minute hairs. The account calls *S. picta* the only species in the genus with 1–3 reduced-blade leaves remaining hidden in the bulb. It says these are not comparable to other species' cataphylls because they do not appear in a regular sequence from year to year.
