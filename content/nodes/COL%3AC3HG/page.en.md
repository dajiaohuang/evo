## Aloe chortolirioides: clustered rosettes and a capitate raceme {#s-c21db0b757}
A South African regional account describes a short-stemmed grass aloe 200–300 mm tall, much branched and forming dense tufts; its roots are spindle-shaped. The leaf entry includes the unclear phrase “38-51 cm a rosette.” It also describes linear, channelled, dull-green leaves 90–250 × 2–5 mm, with toothed margins; the first measurement is retained as printed because its meaning is unclear. The simple, head-shaped raceme is carried on a 150–250 mm peduncle with sterile bracts. The triangular, taper-pointed bracts measure 13–17 × 4–5 mm and have 3–5 veins. Cylindrical flowers range from yellow to red and measure 20–35 mm; their segments are free almost to the base, and the pedicels are 10–25 mm long. Anthers project up to 2 mm. The green ovary measures 5–6 × 2–3 mm, the style projects up to 5 mm, and the fruit is about 14 × 7 mm.

The diagnostic account says individual leaf heads may be confused with *A. kniphqfioides* (spelling as printed). It describes *A. chortolirioides* as forming clumps with 50 or more leaf heads, with a short stem, no bulb and dense, head-shaped racemes. It contrasts *A. kniphofioides*—spelled differently later in the source—as solitary, stemless and bulb-forming, with loose cylindrical racemes. *A. dominella* is described as similar but with wider leaves and shorter flowers.
