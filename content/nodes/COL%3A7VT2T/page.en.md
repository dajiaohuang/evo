## The 2015 life-history entry and this name {#s-ee7fc342c3}
*Melanerpes santacruzi* is accepted in pinned COL26.8 within Picidae. The 2015 archive has a same-name, same-family entry; six focal fields are supported by cited species-level rows. This links the page to that historical entry without resolving taxonomic inheritance.

## Different stages in the mass fields {#s-de8ed920e2}
The archive summarizes adult body mass as 81.91 g. The value may combine male, female and sex-unspecified records; it is a compilation summary, not an individual range.

## Reproduction and developmental timing {#s-73a02fa2eb}
Reported fields are female maturity 365 days (the source defines this as age at first conception), 4.5 offspring or eggs per litter or clutch, 13 days from laying to hatching, and 30 days to flight capability. Some source material defines the last as leaving the nest. A fractional clutch or litter is a summary across records, not a fractional brood.

## A longest record differs from usual lifespan {#s-1c731aab5a}
Maximum longevity is 5.7 years, the largest record found for that field. It is not a usual lifespan, mean wild lifespan or guaranteed survival limit.

## How to trace these values to sources {#s-34104191bd}
Source labels in the same-name rows include Dunning (1992), Szekely, Lislevand and Figuerola (2007), Boback and Guyer (2003), and Bennett (1986), among others. The archive preserves field-specific row locators, values and full reference explanations; its row count is not a count of measured animals.

## Life-history evidence limits {#s-28a75c9837}
These values come from the 2015 historical life-history archive, not a current field survey. Summaries are compiled medians or averages of the two middle values, except maximum longevity, which is the largest record found. They are not independent measurements of one individual; historical species and subspecies may share inherited data. Matching name, family and cited rows support the association but do not establish equivalence to the current COL26.8 concept or remove all inherited components. Wild or captive status is not consistently known, and the underlying publications have not been individually reviewed. Diet, behavior, current range, population and conservation require other sources.
