## Accepted name and placement {#s-194f95de0c}
COL26.8 accepts Ptychadena beka Goutte, Reyes-Velasco, Freilich, Kassie & Boissinot, 2021 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Ptychadenidae (family) → Ptychadena (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## An elevation sample from 106 barcoded individuals {#s-b108fad027}
The revision reported 1695–2596 m for 106 barcoded individuals and records on both sides of Ethiopia’s Great Rift Valley, mostly to the west. These sample elevations are not physiological limits, and the recorded sites do not demonstrate occupancy throughout the intervening region.

## Rain-associated calling in shallow water {#s-7065ba4f69}
Males called in roadside puddles or agricultural fields, with activity greatest during rainy months. Calling usually began after 22:00 but could start as late as 02:00 in dry weather and ceased before dawn. The account reported numerous small two-coloured eggs in the same water body, without providing a clutch-size distribution or complete developmental schedule.

## A geographically sampled mitochondrial and nuclear dataset {#s-3d4d3c9b2f}
The 2023 Ethiopian Highlands study lists 44 mitochondrial samples and 16 nuclear-SNP samples for Ptychadena beka in Table 1, with mitochondrial nucleotide diversity π=0.27%. Samples across the twelve-species study were collected in 2011–2019; these are analysed specimens, not a population census. All assembled mitochondrial genomes had the usual two rRNAs, thirteen protein-coding genes and twenty-two tRNAs, but no species had a completely resolved control region because repeats could not be confidently assembled from short reads. The phylogenetic alignment used selected genes (13,937 bp across 303 samples), rather than an alignment of complete control regions.

## A study-defined highland clade and regional distribution {#s-00e6c052ac}
The paper places Ptychadena beka in its P. neumanni clade and reports it from the both sides of the Great Rift Valley and north of the Blue Nile valley. These distributions combine the authors' geographic sampling and earlier range work; they are not independently updated complete range polygons. The twelve-species highland clade is a subset of Ptychadena. Its mitochondrial-clock analysis estimated a common ancestor around 11.7 million years ago, with a reported interval of 8.827–15.494 million years. The east-of-rift ancestral reconstruction had a model probability of 69%; neither that estimate nor inferred crossings is a directly observed migration event or a nuclear-genome divergence date for this species.

## A dated reassessment argument, with remaining demographic gaps {#s-89c22caaa2}
The 2023 discussion calls for conservation assessment or reassessment using revised species concepts and genetic and geographic evidence. Earlier names and broad distributions can combine lineages that later work separated. The present genomic sample does not itself measure current abundance, fragmentation, decline or reproductive success in Ptychadena beka. Any Red List category mentioned in this paper belongs to its dated discussion, not a verified current official listing. Regional land-use and climate concerns support further investigation but do not quantify this species' current threat level.

## Three sampled regions without detected geographic structure {#s-e920292a6f}
The authors investigated this species because it occurred in all three defined regions. Nuclear PCA with approximately 0.9 million SNPs agreed with the mitochondrial finding of no geographic structure: north-of-Blue-Nile and east-of-rift samples were nested within western diversity south of the river valley. This supports the proposed recent expansion scenario but is not a measured expansion date or complete migration history. The discussion reports negative Tajima’s D as nonsignificant (p=0.07).
