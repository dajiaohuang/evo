## The August 2015 entry and its name {#s-b1f8d1565f}
COL26.8 accepts Butorides virescens within Ardeidae. The August 2015 life-history archive provides 7 focal fields in cited species-level rows with the same name and family. That supports a historical association, but the archive may retain inherited taxonomy; matching row names do not remove it.

## Mass at two life stages {#s-5ed88dae01}
The archive reports adult body mass of 212 g and birth or hatching mass of 16 g. Adult records may combine males, females and adults of unspecified sex. The two values concern different life stages, not a longitudinal series or a range of individual masses.

## Reproductive and developmental timing {#s-61951130b6}
The source-defined female maturity field (age at first conception) is 730 d; litter or clutch size is 3.75 offspring or eggs; oviposition-to-hatching time is 20 d; and age at flight capability is 23 d (some sources define it as leaving the nest). Here, d means days. A fractional summary is not a fraction of an individual, and these fields describe different events.

## The archive’s longest record {#s-8d031939b0}
The maximum_longevity_y value is 11.6 years, selected as the largest record under the archive’s method. It is not a typical or mean wild lifespan, an individual survival ceiling or a current record known to be repeatedly attainable.

## Tracing the life-history values {#s-475883382d}
Same-name rows cite Dunning, 1992; Szekely, Lislevand, and Figuerola, 2007; de Magalhaes and Costa, 2009; and Bennett, 1986, among others. The archive keeps each field’s row number, value and source label with the full reference explanations. A row count is not a specimen count, and repeated citations are not independent replication.

## Limits of the 2015 archive {#s-866ffee54b}
This page summarizes an August 2015 compilation, not a complete species account or a set of measurements from one individual. Values are medians or averages of the two middle records, except maximum longevity, which is the largest record found. Data may be shared or inherited across historical species and subspecies; same-name rows do not prove equivalence to the current COL26.8 concept. Wild or captive conditions are not consistently confirmed, and the source papers have not been individually reviewed. Diet, behavior, current range, population and conservation require other sources.
