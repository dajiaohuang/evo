## What plant and flower structure does the taxonomic account describe? {#s-beedd138ca}
A 2003 taxonomic account describes erect, much-branched shrubs about 2 m tall. Young stems are densely covered with white felt-like hairs that flake from older branches, exposing yellow-brown cork in lengthwise patches; prominent leaf scars remain. The grey-green leaves overlap closely along the stems. They are linear to lance-shaped, folded lengthwise, 3.5–6.5 mm long and 0.8–2 mm deep, with bristly margins; the upper face is hairy, while the lower face is wrinkled and hairless. Flower spikes usually have 8–12 flowers and may continue growing. Bracts (small modified leaves at the flower cluster) are lance-shaped and up to 7 mm long. The floral envelope is about 5.9 mm long, yellow-pink during pollination and red to brown after pollen release. The narrow floral cup (hypanthium) is about 1.7 mm long near the ovary. The ovary is about 2.4 by 0.9 mm. The dry, one-seeded fruit (an achene) is about 2.5 by 1.2 mm and initially remains within the persistent floral cup, which splits as it dries, shedding the sepals and stamens.

## How does the account distinguish it from related species? {#s-b9a29fb0e4}
The account diagnoses P. drakensbergensis by its closely appressed leaves, up to 6.5 mm long, and lance-shaped bracts up to 7 mm long whose tips lack a leaf-like extension; membranous wings are absent. It may be confused with P. montivaga, which has leaves up to 8 mm long and bracts with a straight or threadlike leaf-like tip, or with P. montana, which has terminal, nearly head-shaped spikes and leaves up to 4 mm long with a broadened base and a prominent upper midvein that curves at the pointed tip.

## Which local habitats are reported? {#s-ccf5f2ce93}
The 2003 account reports the species as frequent at Cathedral Peak above Ndedema Gorge, in a belt between forest and grassland. It also records it along streams and riverbanks and on mountain slopes. These are the account's regional observations, not a dated population survey.
