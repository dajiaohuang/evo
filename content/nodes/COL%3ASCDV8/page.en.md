## Accepted name and classification {#s-0640e1c201}
In the 20 August 2026 COL26.8 release, Pristimantis gretathunbergae Mebert, González-Pinzón, Miranda, Griffith, Veselý, Schmid & Batista, 2022 is an accepted species. Its recorded classification path is Anura (order) → Strabomantidae (family) → Pristimantis (genus).

## Reading the source records {#s-972b7f9942}
The catalogue name is accompanied by selected observations or compiled values from named sources. Each result remains tied to the source concept and any stated sample, place, period, and method; together they do not make a complete account of the species.

## Supported Panamanian records and a border possibility {#s-53aec59868}
The description included several central and eastern Panamanian mountain areas, using specimens, photographs and genetic records with different evidence scopes. It suggested possible occurrence in nearby Colombian mountains but did not confirm a Colombian locality. Those evidence types and the border possibility are kept distinct.

## Night perches and daytime shelter {#s-0c989c3b35}
Records ranged from 718 to 1439 m in montane forest. At night, frogs were seen 0.5–3 m above ground on bark and bromeliads; by day they hid between bromeliad leaves. These observations describe the study material and do not establish all activity periods or exclusive microhabitat requirements. Diet was explicitly unknown; the authors’ comparison with other Pristimantis is not treated as a measured diet.

## Three egg-guarding females {#s-f6a98d9388}
Three females were observed guarding egg clutches for at least four nights in bromeliads or on moss-covered branches. Males also called during rainy-season observations. The guarding duration is a minimum observation interval, not total care duration, and the records do not establish every population’s reproductive season or offspring survival.
