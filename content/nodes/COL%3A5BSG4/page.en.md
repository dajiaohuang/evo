## How body size was consolidated {#s-4763a79976}
The PanTHERIA summary gives an adult body mass of 1317.13 g and an adult head–body length of 374.23 mm. Both are sex-adjusted before median consolidation. Mass can include adults or animals of unspecified age, but excludes pregnant females. Head–body length omits the tail; the source defines forearm length as elbow to wrist. These summaries are not measurements of one specimen or a species-wide range.

## Reproductive and developmental timing {#s-9006e9a1ba}
PanTHERIA reports 51 d of active fetal growth, 2.36 offspring per litter, a weaning-age measure of 65.56 d, sexual maturity at 294.06 d, and a 365 d interbirth interval. The fractional litter value is an average, not a partial animal. The source adjusts for differing field definitions: active fetal growth excludes inactive gestation, and weaning can mark nutritional independence rather than the final nursing event. Interbirth interval was consolidated separately; its records are not all established as captive experiments.

## Activity, feeding and groups {#s-8ad794b7ee}
The PanTHERIA row classifies activity as nocturnal only and feeding as omnivorous; it records a diet-category count of 6 and a habitat-layer count of 1. Diet breadth counts resource classes (up to eight), not named food species. Habitat layers mean below ground, ground level, water, or above ground, rather than habitat types such as forest. Activity and group fields use non-captive material; group size is a median summary, not a complete account of social organization.

## Gestation and first reproduction {#s-8fbf417a33}
Ernest's compilation reports active gestation of 1.71 months and female age at first reproduction of 9.87 months. Active gestation excludes delayed implantation. First reproduction means the age when a female can conceive at her first breeding; when a source gave first-birth age, the method subtracted gestation. It is not the same as sexual maturity or first birth. These are across-source summaries, not a timetable for every animal.

## From newborn offspring to weaning {#s-a5d071ce97}
The reported averages are 26.28 g for a newborn and 2.18 months to milk independence. The newborn value is for one offspring; the source definition excludes records measured more than 12 hours after birth. Weaning here means independence from milk: age is measured from birth, and any mass value refers to that transition. Wild, captive and locality conditions are not consistently identified, so these numbers do not supply a growth curve or parental-care account.

## Litter size and frequency {#s-2e0f05719f}
The compilation averages 2.45 offspring born per litter and 1 litter per female per year. Litter size counts newborns, excluding estimates based only on embryo counts or reproductive scars; a fractional value is an average, not a literal litter. The annual frequency counts births across 12 months. The authors excluded captive studies designed to maximize production where possible, but not every record is thereby confirmed as wild. Multiplying these averages would not give an individual female's annual reproductive success.
