## Accepted name and placement {#s-f9a71cd015}
COL26.8 accepts Amolops minutus Orlov & Ho, 2007 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Ranidae (family) → Amolops (genus). ## A later revision of two names {#s-8c76f5d4ba}
Liu and colleagues compared type and type-locality material of A. minutus with A. ottorum material and mitochondrial evidence. Their 2024 study treated A. ottorum as a junior synonym of A. minutus. A reported 0.7% divergence in the 16S marker was part of a combined argument, not a universal species threshold. Earlier studies using the name A. ottorum need to be read in that dated taxonomic context.

## An expanded diagnostic scope {#s-3f6024288f}
The revised diagnosis reports adult male snout–vent length of 29.7–38.3 mm and adult female length of 38.5–51.1 mm. Vomerine teeth were absent or weakly developed, and dorsal colour ranged from nearly uniform green to mostly brown. These ranges belong to the revised diagnostic material and literature scope; they are neither fixed population limits nor sufficient alone for identification.

## The 2024 geographic account {#s-16a7f2799f}
The study reported A. minutus from Lai Chau and Son La provinces in northern Vietnam and Guanyinshan Provincial Nature Reserve in Yuanyang, Yunnan, China. The Chinese record was based on nine collected specimens and molecular comparison. These named localities establish the study records, without implying continuous occupancy between them or a complete contemporary range.

## Separate specimen groups and sexes {#s-bb01ac72e3}
Table 2 separates A. minutus types (eight males, five females), its topotypes (eight males, seven females), A. ottorum types (two females), and the Chinese sample (six males, three females). Their snout–vent lengths are respectively 29.7–36.4 and 38.5–50.2 mm; 30.8–34.0 and 42.5–47.5 mm; 47.5–48.2 mm for the two females; and 34.6–38.3 and 46.7–51.1 mm. The broader revised diagnostic range pools these groups. It does not describe the extrema of one population or a tested difference among populations.

## Corrections after type re-examination {#s-0eaef83083}
The authors reported absent or weak vomerine teeth and no male gular pouches after re-examining material, in contrast to the strongly developed teeth and paired pouches in the 2007 description. The expanded diagnosis distinguishes absent true dorsolateral folds from distinct dorsolateral glandular folds. These wording differences matter for identification. Absence of a vocal sac does not demonstrate that frogs are silent, and the paper does not establish a species-wide call repertoire.

## What the short mitochondrial marker supports {#s-42d409f830}
New 16S sequences were approximately 850 or 570 base pairs long. The analysis reported 0.7% divergence between A. minutus topotypes and A. ottorum and 0.6% between Chinese specimens and the topotypes, alongside reported Bayesian posterior support of 1 and ultrafast bootstrap support of 96 for the relevant grouping. These results contribute to the morphological and taxonomic argument. They are not a nuclear or genome-wide assessment, a universal divergence threshold, or a direct measurement of contemporary gene flow.
