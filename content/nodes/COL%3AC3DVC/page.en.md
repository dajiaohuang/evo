## A burrowing rodent known mostly from specimens {#field-study}
Caspar’s 2022 review reports that von Heuglin described the species in 1864 from the upper Bahr el Ghazal region, now in western South Sudan. The original account, as summarized by Caspar, mentioned signs near the Wau River and through Bongo and Dembo west to the Kosanga River, but the exact type locality cannot be reconstructed. Heuglin described animals in woodland and tall grass and noted burrowing in abandoned termite mounds. The sole collected female became the holotype, SMNS 1095.

The review’s own voucher synthesis places records in tropical savanna and forest–savanna mosaics and includes a specimen from Mount Morungole, Uganda, at 2,520 m. The reviewed records do not establish a continuous range, especially in the west, and the author found no direct evidence for occurrence in Cameroon. Caspar also concludes that genetics and karyology remain severely understudied; relationships within the northern Fukomys group have not been systematically tested. This is a synthesis of records, not evidence that every habitat or locality is occupied.
