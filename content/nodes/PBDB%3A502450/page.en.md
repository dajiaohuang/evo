## What the material shows {#s-3daca852f3}
Several partly articulated Famennian skeletons preserve body proportions, and specimens AA.MEM.DS.12 and PIMUZ A/I 5159 retain three-dimensional neurocrania with a broad rostrum and widely spaced nasal capsules. PIMUZ A/I 5155 contains one small actinopterygian as stomach content.

## Feeding {#s-c0d62ef5b5}
One specimen records a more or less articulated actinopterygian about 40 mm long in the stomach. It documents a feeding event, not the species’ full diet.

## Setting {#s-3b6da62009}
The fossils come from the marine Famennian Thylacocephalan Layer; preferred habitat in life is unresolved.

## Movement {#s-4ba150ac90}
A high-aspect-ratio tail and large pectoral fins are preserved, but swimming performance was not measured.

## Size {#s-533dacced8}
Proportions of the studied specimens yield estimated in-life lengths of 0.8–2.5 m; this is a specimen-based range, not a complete genus-wide envelope.

## Ecological role {#s-89fdabbe53}
Ecological guild remains unresolved.

## Preserved features {#s-541fe910b5}
The snout is broad and enclosed in cartilage.

## Preserved features {#s-1bb6caeb3e}
The large nasal capsules are widely separated.

## Preserved features {#s-9faaa02688}
The tail is high-aspect-ratio and lunate.

## Preserved features {#s-ddda62db49}
Strap-like pectoral-fin radials are preserved.

## How to read the evidence {#s-3ee99cef01}
Specimens document articulated bodies and cranial anatomy. Published character matrices place the genus among early symmoriiforms, while alternative stem-holocephalan and stem-elasmobranch placements remain in discussion.
