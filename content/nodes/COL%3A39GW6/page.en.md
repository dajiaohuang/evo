## Emilia limosa {#s-d9843d3f1f}
## Wetland heads vary between Emilia limosa and E. integrifolia

Herman and Retief describe leafy stems that are weakly crisped-hairy but may be nearly hairless through the middle. Basal leaves range from oblanceolate-linear to narrowly elliptic or linear-lance-shaped, with tapered bases. Each loose inflorescence has one to four disc-shaped heads; involucres are 4.5–6.5 mm long. Flowers are pink to purple, and the dry fruits range from hairless to sparsely hairy.

Cron's 2014 review notes that *E. limosa* may be very similar to, or even the same as, *E. integrifolia*. Typical *E. integrifolia* has more heads—up to 14—in loose or crowded terminal corymbs, but the heads are smaller. Intermediate specimens from Zambia and Kenya combine head number and size in different ways, leaving the boundary between the two names uncertain.

The account records very wet habitats: pool and dam margins, vleis, dambos, permanent swamps, peat bogs and nearly soil-less wet places. Plants may also grow on flat hilltop rocks where water collects in puddles. Recorded substrates include granite, sandstone and laterite.
