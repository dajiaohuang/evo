## Read ostrich as a branching reference {#s-da9bf901d2}
The 2019 study reuses a Struthio camelus genome and recovers ostrich as the earliest split among sampled palaeognaths. It informs comparisons of other lineages and flight-loss history without implying that living ostriches are unchanged ancestors. Other Struthio species are absent from this material. Repeated regulatory-region acceleration is a cross-lineage statistical result, not an identified functional mutation in every ostrich.

## Life-history evidence limits {#s-0ddef5e7a0}
The route presents two historical life-history accounts and one ecological account, not a complete catalogue of Struthioniformes. The life-history values retain the archive’s summary method, maximum-record and historical-taxonomy limits; a route-level view does not imply a shared reproductive mode or life history. The ecological categories and weights are source-specific, not traits established for the whole order. Genome discussion is bounded to Sackton et al. (2019), including its published main text, four figure captions and figure 1 pixels; the sampled material, marker extraction, species-tree methods and selected results and discussion passages of Cloutier et al. (2019); and ten NCBI WGS master records. Companion papers share material rather than independent population replicates. Other figure pixels, supplements, raw sequences and analysis code were not independently examined or rerun, and the companion paper was not reviewed paragraph by paragraph in full. Candidate regulatory acceleration, open chromatin and a single-region chicken reporter assay are distinct evidence levels, not functional validation across all species. These sources do not provide a complete account of the order’s biology or current status.
