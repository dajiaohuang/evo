## Stems and clustered flowers distinguish the species {#s-1c0ffd5320}
The cited southern African treatments describe *Quaqua multiflora* as forming stout, often mottled clumps up to 0.5 m across. Small raised stem bumps, or tubercles, are irregularly arranged and tipped with hard, often sharp teeth. Flowers form clusters of up to 30 near the stem tips, with most opening at about the same time. The flower tube, the fused lower part of the petals, is whitish with dark spots near its mouth. The corolla lobes, the separate segments around the flower's rim, are usually dark, often purple-black; the inner surface is velvety, with large papillae (small rounded projections) around the opening. These lobes run parallel or broaden toward inward-curving tips, a feature used in the cited treatment to distinguish the flowers. The flowers are also described as unpleasant-smelling. The Greater Cape flora lists stony slopes among bushes as habitat. These regional accounts do not quantify population variation.
