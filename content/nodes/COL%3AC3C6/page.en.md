## Aloe affinis: leaf margins and flower colour {#s-faff1b0b83}
A South African regional account describes a solitary, stemless succulent herb up to 300 mm tall. It records 16–20 lineate leaves. Their dimensions are printed as “200-100 x 50-100 mm”; the first interval descends as written, so it is left unresolved. The leaf margins are usually maroon and horny between the teeth, while spots on the leaves are obscure. The inflorescence has 3–8 terminal branches, with conical racemes longer than wide. The flowers are dull brick red.

These characters and measurements come from a regional South African flora account cited to a 1997 publication. The excerpt gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency and does not establish variation across the full range. It does not independently classify the material as native, naturalised, cultivated or domesticated.

The account records Bushveld and sandy loam as habitat and substrate.
