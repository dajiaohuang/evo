## Accepted name and placement {#s-0d80cfa2c5}
COL26.8 accepts Pristimantis altamazonicus (Barbour & Dunn, 1921) as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Strabomantidae (family) → Pristimantis (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## A survey’s attributed count, 2013–2018 {#s-b43b970230}
Table 2 of the 2026 paper attributes 45 records to source-labelled Pristimantis altamazonicus in Fátima parish, Pastaza, Ecuador, from September 2013 to September 2018. The total is a cumulative record count, not a verified number of distinct marked frogs, population density or current abundance. Surveys used daytime and night-time visual and photographic encounters, ten 8 × 8 m quadrats, four 100 × 20 m transects and fifty pitfall traps across six habitats. ## The species-name and genus-only identification conflict {#s-b13a3875b7}
The Methods say that Pristimantis individuals were conservatively identified only to genus and that no molecular analyses were done. Yet Table 2 prints Pristimantis altamazonicus and other named species alongside five separate Pristimantis sp. rows. This conflict is unresolved: the named row is an attribution in the paper, not independent confirmation of species identity. The five unnamed rows are not assigned to this species or included in its 45-record count. Diagnostic photographs, vouchers or clarification from the authors would be needed to verify the printed names.

A 2025 Molineidae review lists Pristimantis altamazonicus as a host associated in earlier literature with Oswaldocruzia cassonei, O. mazzai and O. tcheprakovae. These are compiled host records from different studies, not parasites found in a newly examined sample or in every frog of this species. The review identifies this host as the original type host for O. tcheprakovae, meaning the host named in the parasite’s description; it is not the amphibian’s type specimen. The cited reports and archived specimens have not been independently reidentified. The checklist gives no examined-animal denominator, prevalence, infection intensity, pathology or mortality for this host.
