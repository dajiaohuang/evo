## What the tracks can—and cannot—identify {#s-e65a1c1bcc}
Clawed tracks from the Snowy Plains Formation in Australia may place an amniote-crown minimum close to the Devonian–Carboniferous boundary, if the trackmakers are interpreted as near the amniote crown. Because no body fossil identifies the makers, the 358.9–0 Ma route is a contested evidence synthesis rather than a precise fossil age or an observed divergence.

The cited evidence is a set of Early Tournaisian clawed footprints. Their maker could be a primitive sauropsid or could lie near the amniote crown node. Tracks record contact with the ground, but they do not preserve the body needed to settle that taxonomic placement.

## A boundary-based minimum {#s-229956eb1b}
The route uses 358.9 Ma, the rounded Devonian–Carboniferous boundary, as an inferred minimum under the preferred trackmaker interpretation and extends to living amniotes. That endpoint is not the exact age of the tracks, and the route does not represent a directly observed split.
