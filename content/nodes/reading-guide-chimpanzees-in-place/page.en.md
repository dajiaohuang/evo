## What can one chimpanzee community tell us about habitat? {#lead}
Where do chimpanzees spend time and feed in a landscape of forest and farmland at Bossou, Guinea? A year of follows brings the question into focus.

## Put the study location back into the story {#section-0}
Researchers followed one wild western chimpanzee community at Bossou, Guinea, from April 2012 to March 2013, for up to six hours a day, including ten adult focal individuals. Matching activities to habitats showed that mature forest was the most selected habitat overall. Forest was a living space in which these animals made daily choices.

## What changed near cultivated fields? {#section-1}
Among feeding events in uncultivated habitats, observations more than 200 metres from fields exceeded chance expectations, while those within 0–200 metres fell below them. The comparison concerned feeding locations relative to chance, rather than just where animals occurred. The authors suggested that field-associated risks might influence feeding locations; the study did not establish that as the sole cause.

## One species, different local lives {#section-2}
The observations connect chimpanzee life with place and a landscape altered by people. But the Bossou community does not represent all chimpanzees: food, habitats and human activity differ between regions. The species page places this local account alongside studies of tool use, development and population history.

## Focal individual {#term}
An individual selected for sustained observation, rather than an average representative of an entire species.
