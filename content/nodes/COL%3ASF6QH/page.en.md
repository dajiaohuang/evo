## Accepted name and placement {#s-1812e98b97}
COL26.8 accepts Rhinella kumanday Caicedo-Martínez, Henao-Osorio, Arias-Monsalve, Rojas-Morales, Ossa-López, Rivera-Páez et al., 2024 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Bufonidae (family) → Rhinella (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## Andean forest-floor observations {#s-3fdf271c52}
The description reported Colombian Central Cordillera records near Los Nevados at 2404–3690 m. Frogs were associated with litter or rotten logs during early morning and twilight, including secondary forest and stream edges. Some behavioral observations were attributed to earlier reports under Rhinella sp.; the revision identifies their taxonomic context without turning all observations into newly collected data.

## Three preserved stomachs {#s-84ed2e3d6c}
Dissection of three preserved specimens yielded three invertebrate prey items assigned to beetles and mites. This small preserved sample documents consumed prey, without establishing dietary proportions, specialization or seasonal feeding variation.

## Ovarian eggs without observed breeding {#s-1f6aa2c173}
Three preserved gravid females contained 96, 38 and 81 eggs. Their reproductive timing was unknown; researchers had not observed tadpoles or amplectant pairs and failed to obtain calls in captivity. These specimen egg counts are not observed laid-clutch sizes or evidence of a particular breeding site or season.
