## A proposed change along one branch {#s-95fb426f2d}
Living horsetails belong to a much longer fossil story. One fossil-inclusive study proposes a shift toward smaller bodies and more compact reproductive strobili along a particular horsetail lineage. The node’s 372.2–0 Ma span follows a broader sphenopsid lineage, not the origin of the living genus *Equisetum*.

In the phylogeny recovered by Elgorriaga and colleagues, the lineage containing Equisetaceae and *Neocalamites* shows a possible trend toward reduced size and more compact strobili. The authors also identify combinations resembling modern horsetail traits by the Jurassic. This is a pattern inferred from that branching hypothesis; it does not describe every member of Equisetopsida.

## What the fossil-inclusive tree can show {#s-e050966449}
An analysis combining fossil and living forms places *Equisetum* among the sampled sphenopsids and reconstructs changes within horsetails. That placement depends on the study’s character matrix. Incomplete fossils and character coding leave deeper relationships model-dependent, and the result is not a date for the origin of Equisetopsida.

## A broad fossil-lineage span {#s-fa8f53ca5a}
The displayed 372.2–0 Ma range runs from a Devonian sphenopsid record to living horsetails. The older bound is a named-interval conversion, not a specimen-level date. This broad navigation lineage is not the origin of crown *Equisetum*, a chain of direct ancestors, or evidence that one species persisted throughout.
