## Accepted name and classification {#s-38c046bbb2}
COL26.8 accepts *Arthroleptis adelphus* Perret, 1966 as a species in the genus *Arthroleptis*, family Arthroleptidae and order Anura. This name and placement follow the release dated 20 August 2026.

## Locality records from the sampled material {#s-0bafd6b9b4}
The 2017 study reports seven specimens from the Loubomo–Mougagara Road corridor (LMR) in Nyanga, Gabon—USNM 558505–558511—and one specimen from the Republic of the Congo, USNM 584017. These are records from the study’s sampled material. A museum reference specimen is not automatically an observation from LMR, and the Republic of the Congo is distinct from the Democratic Republic of the Congo. A survey non-detection is limited by its effort and season; it does not establish regional absence or the species’ full range. The cited vouchers and present-day occurrence are not independently verified here.

## Barcode groups leave a possible species complex unresolved {#s-ae733d93b5}
The seven LMR specimens fall in one BIN (Barcode Index Number) and two ABGD groups (Automatic Barcode Gap Discovery). The Republic of the Congo specimen falls in another BIN and group. Their 16S sequence clades associate with different Bioko and Cameroon reference samples. Given the Cameroon type locality, the authors retain the name *adelphus* while noting a possible species complex. These group assignments do not formally describe a cryptic species.

## Two mitochondrial markers do not settle identity {#s-a1cc7313e0}
The study combines COI (cytochrome c oxidase subunit I) barcode clusters, 16S mitochondrial ribosomal RNA comparisons, and morphology and type-locality context. COI and 16S are both mitochondrial markers; they are not independent nuclear confirmation or tests of reproductive isolation. The authors use neighbor joining to group similar sequences and explicitly limit that method’s use for reconstructing evolutionary history. Most species in the reference library have material from only one or two localities, and not every GenBank voucher could be examined morphologically. Reference labels and missing sequences remain limits on identification. Fieldwork took place in May–June 2012 and March–April 2013; GenBank and BOLD comparisons use the source library’s 2016 snapshot, not a current database audit.

## Study-wide counts are not estimates for this frog {#s-4baec7f504}
Across its multi-species reference library, the study reports 540 sequenced specimens, 524 COI sequences, 528 16S sequences, 94 BINs and 85 ABGD groups. These totals are not counts for *Arthroleptis adelphus*. The methods describe 80 first-season and 97 second-season LMR specimens, while Table 1 lists 79 and 97 sequenced or retained entries. The methods also describe 275 Congo reference specimens, but Table 1 locality rows total 274. The page preserves these collection-versus-analysis differences without inferring a reason for them. Group totals are not a census of currently accepted species, and the raw traces and database assignments have not been independently reanalysed.
