## How geologic evidence dates change through deep time {#s-3feb30946d}
Trace nine cases from dated minerals and measured strata to biological interpretation, keeping each uncertainty attached to the evidence.

## Oxygenation has a bracket, not an instant {#s-21684bdd78}
Dated igneous rocks and changes in sulfur-isotope signals bracket the onset of the Great Oxidation interval. Ocean basins followed different redox histories, so the evidence does not describe one simultaneous change in every sea.

## Zircon places Avalon after the Gaskiers ice {#s-cf16afb0c9}
Zircon ages place the Gaskiers glaciation before the younger Avalon fossil assemblages. The dates establish a sequence between ice and fossils; they do not directly date when animals first originated.

## Cambrian fossils show a pattern; models explain it {#s-b0fabe1a5e}
Cambrian body fossils, trace fossils and growing disparity—the range of body forms represented—document a major radiation. Ecological and developmental models offer explanations, but remain competing syntheses of the fossil pattern.

## Ordovician diversification varied by region {#s-cb144e0aa6}
Species richness and ecological expansion increased at different rates among Ordovician regions and groups. The records describe several regional histories, not a single global moment when marine life changed everywhere at once.

## Ash beds time the main Meishan pulse {#s-9fe2c74dd1}
Dated ash beds at the Meishan reference section constrain its principal extinction pulse to roughly sixty thousand years, with analytical uncertainty. This is a tightly bounded interval in one section, not a direct measure of the same duration at every site.

## Recovery followed different paths {#s-3183dbc9a1}
Triassic recovery varies among ecosystems, ecological guilds and regions. A review can organize these records into stages, but its synthesis is not one event measured at a single place and time.

## Impact and extinction coincide within dating precision {#s-9f11da04e7}
Boundary geochemistry identifies impact ejecta, and radioisotopic ages compare the impact with the extinction interval. Within the published precision, the two are synchronous; that result does not by itself measure every ecological consequence.

## PETM warming appears across several proxies {#s-c5add0b66d}
Carbon-isotope, temperature and biotic records track the PETM perturbation. These proxies are indirect measures of past conditions, so estimates of its magnitude and duration remain sensitive to the proxy and age model used.

## Megafaunal losses have regional histories {#s-bd757bc5b7}
Late-Quaternary megafaunal losses differ across continents. Comparative models test climate and human influence, but the records do not reveal one universal mechanism acting identically everywhere.
