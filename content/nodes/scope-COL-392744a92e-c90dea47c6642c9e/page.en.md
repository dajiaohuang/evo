## Branching axes and a distinctive conducting strand {#s-714514bb92}
Edwards’s redescription of new and historical Rhynie Chert material established Aglaophyton major and reconstructed both low, decumbent axes and upright, repeatedly forked shoots. The upright axes ended in sporangia, the structures that produce spores. In the stems, the conducting strand was divided into three zones but lacked differential wall thickenings.

These specimens support a reconstruction of the plant’s sampled anatomy, not a placement in a living higher plant group. A separate U–Pb study constrains the age of the Rhynie locality; that date is not the duration of Aglaophyton as a genus, a global range, or proof that it was a direct ancestor of later plants.
