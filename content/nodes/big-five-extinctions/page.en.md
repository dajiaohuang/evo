## Five mass extinctions: reading change in the fossil record {#s-69ab1d3b4a}
Follow changes in marine and terrestrial fossils alongside climate and impact evidence, while keeping each event’s timing and scope in view.

## End-Ordovician: ice, sea level and marine loss {#s-0112a56bb3}
Glaciation and falling sea level coincide with a major loss of marine diversity near the end of the Ordovician. Their timing makes them part of the explanation under study, but the fossil record alone does not reduce the event to one cause.

## Late Devonian: several crises over time {#s-ae058ba936}
Reef systems and other marine communities were affected by multiple crises across an extended interval. The record describes a sequence of losses, rather than one brief event at a single boundary.

## End-Permian: the deepest known restructuring {#s-4eac2a79f0}
The end-Permian crisis produced the deepest known restructuring of Phanerozoic life. Its scale is visible across the sampled fossil record, while the pattern among groups and environments remains part of the evidence to compare.

## End-Triassic: turnover before Jurassic dominance {#s-cb31f91efd}
Marine and terrestrial ecosystems both show turnover across the end-Triassic interval. The change precedes the Jurassic dominance of dinosaurs; that sequence in time does not by itself describe every local cause or ecological response.

## K–Pg: an impact boundary and selective survival {#s-eb6717ec14}
The Chicxulub impact marks an abrupt global boundary at the Cretaceous–Paleogene transition. Survival was selective: extinction and persistence varied among lineages rather than affecting all organisms in the same way.
