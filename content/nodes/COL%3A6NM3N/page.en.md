## Leaves, raceme, and flowering season {#s-8dcb29a5bb}
Codd's 2005 regional account describes plants that are usually single-stemmed. The narrow leaves first ascend and later curve back; they measure 200–750 × 3–7 mm, have a keel, and have smooth margins or, rarely, a few scattered teeth. The flowering stalk rises above the recurved leaves and reaches 250–800 mm long. The elongated, oblong raceme is fairly loose, usually with flowers arranged along one side, and measures 60–280 mm long. Its ascending buds are greenish brown to maroon. Flowers eventually hang down; they are creamy yellow or yellow-green to greenish brown, rarely tinged dull red, and faintly scented. Ovate bracts are 4.0–6.5 mm long, usually widest near the middle before narrowing abruptly to a pointed or tapering tip; their margins are entire. Pedicels are 1–2 mm long and lengthen slightly in fruit. The cylindrical perianth is 7–10 mm long, with ovate, non-spreading lobes 1 mm long. At flowering, the stamens extend 1–2 mm beyond the perianth and later retract. The nearly spherical fruit is 5–6 mm long. Flowering is reported mainly from January to March, although occasional specimens may flower as early as August.

These measurements and flowering observations come from a regional species account published in 2005; it gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency.

## Raceme orientation and a herbarium look-alike {#s-69b07a3e9b}
Codd describes *K. parviflora* as usually having a one-sided raceme, which the account calls unique in the genus. As flowers begin to open, the inflorescence leans to one side and the flowers turn that way. In bud, its narrow cylindrical raceme may be confused in a herbarium with *K. breviflora*; Codd distinguishes *K. parviflora* by its longer racemes, greenish-yellow to pale greenish-brown flowers, and broader, more ovate bracts.

## Habitat {#s-d16fd01cae}
A 2019 Eastern Cape flora records the species in moist grassland and vleis, and names the Drakensberg Grassland, Sub-Escarpment Grassland, Sub-Escarpment Savanna, and Indian Ocean Coastal Belt.

This habitat list comes from a regional species account published in 2019; it gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency and does not establish the full species range.
