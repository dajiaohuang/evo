## Life history {#s-1f690d39f0}
At Kirindy Forest in Madagascar, researchers followed 12 wild infants from the 2017 and 2018 breeding seasons and compared maternal prenatal fecal glucocorticoid metabolites with early development. Higher maternal values were associated with faster growth and earlier independent foraging and play. The 2018 cohort was not followed for a full six months, and this observational study does not show that maternal stress caused the differences.

## Ecological evidence {#s-35ab2b8a5d}
At Kirindy Mitea National Park in southwestern Madagascar, M/LWS opsin genotypes in one wild population showed variation consistent with polymorphic trichromacy. Individuals in groups containing trichromats had higher body-mass indices and more fruit-feeding time, with longer fruit-feeding bouts. Genotype is a proxy for visual phenotype; the proposed dry-season fruit-detection advantage remains a hypothesis.

## Ecological evidence {#s-5b17a50203}
In the same field research at Kirindy, maternal fecal glucocorticoid metabolites were higher during low-rainfall periods and late pregnancy. The authors used rainfall as a local proxy for food availability. This association is limited to monitored mothers, the site and two breeding seasons; it does not define the species' climate niche or establish causation.

## Evidence limits {#s-491e1aec6d}
This is a focused introduction to studies at two Madagascar sites, not a complete review of Verreaux's sifaka. Infant cohorts had different follow-up windows; visual findings rely on genotype proxies and local feeding samples, and the proposed seasonal advantage is not established as a general function.
