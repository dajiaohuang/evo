## The 2015 life-history entry and this name {#s-e4ced1d91e}
In pinned COL26.8, Eupsittula canicularis is accepted within Psittacidae. The six life-history fields below come from a historical same-name, same-family record and have cited species-level rows. The archive may carry information across earlier taxonomic groupings, so matching names do not rule out inherited data.

## Different stages in the mass fields {#s-52f2805729}
The historical compilation summarizes adult body mass as 83.25 g. It may combine records for males, females and adults with unspecified sex, and is not a range of individual masses. Birth or hatching mass is not included here.

## Reproduction and developmental timing {#s-5d09173c74}
The compilation reports female maturity at 363.47 d (the source defines this as age at first conception), 4 offspring or eggs per litter/clutch, 30 d from egg laying to hatching and 42 d to flight capability (some sources record leaving the nest). Here, d means days. The litter/clutch field combines young and egg counts; a fractional summary would not mean part of an offspring in one event. These times describe different events, not one interchangeable reproductive cycle.

## A longest record differs from usual lifespan {#s-2ea23a4db0}
The archive gives 28.6 years for maximum_longevity_y, the largest record found under its method. It is not typical or average wild lifespan, a limit for every individual, or evidence of a current record that can be repeated.

## How to trace these values to sources {#s-d93410368a}
Matching source rows include Dunning, 1992; de Magalhaes and Costa, 2009; Schuck-Paima, C., Alonsob, W.J., and E.B. Ottonia, 2008; and Bennett, 1986, among others. The archive preserves field-specific row numbers, values and labels with the reference table's full explanations. A label may point to a paper, book or database. Row counts are not animal counts, and repeated citations are not independent experiments.

## Life-history evidence limits {#s-2b30c2bf16}
These are historical same-name records from the 2015 life-history archive. Values are compilation medians or, for an even number of records, averages of the two middle values; maximum longevity instead uses the largest record found. They are not one animal's measurements, individual variation ranges, mean wild lifespans or current population surveys. The source may share information across historical species and subspecies. Matching name and family plus same-name rows support this association but do not establish equivalence with COL26.8 concepts or remove all inherited components. Wild versus captive conditions are not consistently confirmed, and underlying publications have not been reviewed individually. Other sources are needed for diet, behavior, current range, conservation and missing fields.
