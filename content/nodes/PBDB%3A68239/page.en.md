## Two additional specimens supply bones missing from the holotype {#s-6840181563}
Hwang, Norell, Ji and Gao describe CAGS 20-7-004 and CAGS 20-8-001 in 2002, slabs from Qianyang near Yixian, Liaoning. Teeth, femoral crests and mid-tail vertebrae support their referral to M. zhaoianus. Holotype IVPP V12330 lacks or does not expose much of the hand, shoulder girdle and dorsal column; the new specimens supply comparisons in those regions. Specimen 20-8-001 spans seven slabs split along different planes, with articulated portions. Specimen 20-7-004 is semi-articulated with displaced bones, and poor preparation and shellac applied before acquisition affect observation. They cannot be combined into one individual complete in every region and one posture.

## Posterior teeth preserve denticles; anterior teeth remain missing {#s-6b6bf83364}
Identifiable skull remains in 20-7-004 consist mainly of dentaries and splenials. Preserved posterior teeth have denticles only on the rear edge, constricted crown–root junctions and decreasing size toward the back. Eight of the last nine positions retain teeth in the right dentary. Using the holotype illustration to estimate about ten additional anterior positions, the authors suggest roughly nineteen teeth per side. Nineteen is not a count of fully preserved teeth. Missing anterior teeth prevent independent confirmation of the original unserrated-anterior-tooth account, and resemblance does not directly identify consumed animals.

## Elongate dorsals and a tail wrapped in bony rods {#s-13decbed2b}
Dorsal centra in the added material are elongated, about twice as long as tall, with correspondingly squarer neural spines than the short, blocky dorsals of some dromaeosaurids. Extended prezygapophyses and chevrons form bony rods around much of the tail. Both new specimens have about twenty-six caudals, differing from the cited twenty-four or twenty-five in the holotype. The authors discuss possible individual variation, although gaps and discontinuous slab contacts remain. Broken and displaced rods at a bent tail segment support a comparatively stiff-tail interpretation, not an entirely immobile living tail or measured turning performance.

## Shoulder girdle, sternum and free uncinate processes add comparisons {#s-1359ed7faa}
In 20-8-001, scapula and coracoid fuse into an angled L-shaped structure, the glenoid faces laterally and the humerus exceeds the scapula in length. Left and right sternal plates remain unfused at their midline contact. Free uncinate processes among the ribs have fan-shaped bases and tapering shafts without fusion to the ribs; 20-7-004 also preserves similar structures. These regions enable comparisons with basal avialans and other dromaeosaurids. Fusion in different bones does not yield an exact age, and birdlike skeletal features do not directly demonstrate sustained flapping, respiratory efficiency or flight behavior.

## Read claw sheaths and toe orientation through preservation {#s-ed195384ee}
Two manual unguals and their sheaths survive in 20-8-001, identified as digit I and probably digit II. Long, recurved sheaths show why bone alone can understate the external claw’s extent. The right second toe retains developed articulations, a proximal ventral heel and strongly curved ungual; digits III and IV are slender. Compression prevents determining whether digit I is reversed. Obscuring or crushing of the proximal third metatarsal also prevents these specimens from confirming the originally described partially arctometatarsalian condition. A foot photograph does not supply grip posture, frequency of tree use or prey type.

## Comparing composite and separate specimen coding {#s-75a44ca185}
The 2002 analysis uses 208 characters, thirteen ordered, either combining three specimens into Microraptor coding or coding them separately. Both approaches yield 240 most-parsimonious trees of six hundred steps and matching consensus topologies; separate coding groups the three specimens sister to other dromaeosaurids. Small size is interpreted within that contemporary paravian framework, explicitly without favoring a particular flight-origin model. The result precedes later Microraptor names and additional material. Its basal position is not adopted as the only current hypothesis, and matrices or limits among the three accepted species were not reanalyzed here.
