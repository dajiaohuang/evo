## Follow shared bonebeds to the two pterosaurs {#s-38e90b29ea}
Open the accumulation and growth sections of [Caiuajara dobruskii](../../../../../../../../../../../Pterosauromorpha/Pterosauria/Pterodactyloidea/Azhdarchoidea/Neoazhdarchia/Tapejaroidea/Tapejaridae/Tapejarinae/Caiuajara/Caiuajara_dobruskii/page.en.md), then the associated holotype and two histological samples of [Keresdrakon vilsoni](../../../../../../../../../../../Pterosauromorpha/Pterosauria/Pterodactyloidea/Azhdarchoidea/Tapejaromorpha/Keresdrakon/Keresdrakon_vilsoni/page.en.md). This supports its coexistence interpretation without independently establishing simultaneous death, particular prey or a single individual for every holotype bone. ## The coexistence route retains a hypothetical food web {#s-23a486cde0}
The 2019 study discusses possible scavenging or small-prey feeding by Keresdrakon and possible predation on Caiuajara by Vespersaurus. These proposed links are not an observed food chain. The separately presented 2020 histology study does not turn growth marks into evidence of particular prey. Compare preservation, growth and feeding hypotheses across the three pages while retaining each study’s material assignment, date and evidence strength.

## Eleven hind-limb bones are not eleven associated skeletons {#s-2812b0e2e1}
The 2020 study samples five femora and six tibiae housed at CENPALEO from the site yielding the type series. Assignment relies on similarity to referred bones and the authors’ then-current view that no other theropod had been recovered or identified there. The 2021 Berthasaura description establishes another theropod at the site. This page dates the 2020 premise without independently reidentifying or rejecting the histological sample. No articulated femur–tibia pair is reported; eleven bones do not supply paired growth histories for eleven complete individuals.

## Predominantly parallel-fibered bone and cyclical marks {#s-931347106e}
The sampled cortices predominantly contain parallel-fibered bone, interpreted as relatively slow deposition compared with the fibrolamellar pattern common in many theropods. Femora preserve annuli and single, double or triple lines of arrested growth; tibiae also show cyclical marks, with small patches of woven bone in some samples. Annuli indicate reduced deposition and LAGs a stronger growth pause, rather than identical tissue phenomena. These features compare growth patterns without directly measuring annual gains in body length.

## Age retrocalculation depends on missing marks and neonatal assumptions {#s-0cc492b053}
Medullary expansion and remodeling can erase early growth marks. The authors extrapolate from spacing among preserved marks to estimate missing intervals. Without neonatal Vespersaurus, they assume a one-millimetre neonatal shaft radius by comparison with Masiakasaurus. Highly incomplete or obscured samples are not assigned retrocalculated ages. Some sections are away from midshaft and may underestimate the record. Tabulated years depend on these models and counting conventions; they are not independently measured ages or a demonstrated maximum lifespan.

## Two tibiae show evidence of completed growth {#s-08853c7440}
Tibiae CP.V 2385 and CP.V 4130 exhibit an external fundamental system: poorly vascularized outer tissue containing tightly spaced growth lines. The authors interpret at least these tibiae as having completed linear growth and estimate ages at death of about thirteen and ten years, respectively. The largest sampled femur, CP.V 2277, lacks an EFS and cannot be declared fully grown from size alone. These results belong to individual bones and their retrocalculation models, without establishing a common growth-stop age.

## Femoral and tibial maturity estimates remain separate {#s-5e78a819ac}
The 2020 authors interpret narrowed growth zones and reduced vascularization as possible onset of reproductive maturity: around seven to ten years from femora and three to five from tibiae. Without paired bones from one individual, comparisons use proportions from related taxa and propose allometry as a possible explanation. Reproductive maturity is inferred from tissue changes, without direct evidence of sex, reproductive organs or egg laying. Both estimates and their disagreement remain visible, rather than being averaged into one species-wide age.

## An unfused tibiotarsus does not alone establish juvenility {#s-0699f9b4ad}
Tibiae with an EFS still lack signs of fusion to proximal tarsals. The authors suggest that these elements may remain unfused in Vespersaurus or fuse only very late in development. Histological maturity and joint fusion are different measures; fusion schedules from other theropods cannot be transferred directly. The study does not independently validate functional monodactyly mechanically, so growth evidence remains separate from foot-behavior hypotheses.

## Environmental explanations of slow growth need comparison {#s-3847306b9d}
The authors relate slow deposition to small size and discuss resource limitation in arid habitats as a possible influence, alongside comparisons where extreme environments did not alter dinosaur growth patterns. They also propose the tissue pattern as a possible derived feature of Noasaurinae, explicitly requiring wider sampling. Climatic, size and phylogenetic explanations remain hypotheses; desert deposition and one sample do not determine starvation history, metabolic rate or a shared growth strategy for the whole clade.

## The holotype and tentative referrals have different scopes {#s-590d2d895f}
Holotype MPCO.V 0065d in the 2019 description includes part of the vertebral column, left pelvis and a partly articulated right foot. The sediment block also contains pterosaur bones. Absence of duplicates, proximity and matching phylogenetic signal support a tentative assignment of the dinosaur remains to one individual. Other isolated bones are referred mainly through size and morphological compatibility. The 2021 Berthasaura paper discusses possible mixed or chimeric material; this page therefore does not combine every referred bone into one established complete skeleton.

## Keep the stratigraphic interpretation beside the database record {#s-6dfd9be9f5}
After field reassessment, the 2019 authors assign the fossil-bearing beds to the Rio Paraná Formation, differing from earlier Goio-Erê attribution, and discuss both Early and Late Cretaceous age schemes. This page does not overwrite the fixed database’s Goio-Erê label and broad age bracket with one interpretation. Wet interdune deposition indicates that the desert was not uniformly waterless, without establishing the species’ full activity range or diet.

## A robust middle metatarsal beside narrow neighbours {#s-318758edbe}
Metatarsal III is conspicuously robust, whereas the shafts of II and IV are laterally compressed. The holotype foot was formerly more complete, with proximal material lost before description; the authors use older photographs for some observations. Those photographs do not restore missing bones as measurable objects. The partly articulated right foot permits comparisons between the central digit and shorter neighbouring digits. Functional monodactyly does not mean anatomical possession of only one toe.

## Single-digit loading is a conditional functional interpretation {#s-975077d94e}
Robust metatarsal III, shorter neighbouring digits and asymmetric unguals lead the authors to propose main loading through digit III, with adjacent toes held close or possibly off the ground. Yet non-ungual phalanges of digits II and IV are also robust, and compressed unguals can support weight in other dinosaurs. The paper explicitly cautions against excluding those digits from locomotion. Slicing or scratching functions likewise remain hypotheses requiring more mechanical study, rather than observed hunting actions or a demonstrated gait.

## Similar tracks do not identify their maker {#s-314aff197d}
The paper compares possible functionally monodactyl tracks with the species’ foot while acknowledging uncertainties in preservation in aeolian sandstone. Similar dimensions and morphology make the comparison informative, but neither connect the tracks to the holotype individual nor establish Vespersaurus as their maker. Tracks and body fossils are different records, and the compared tracks are not added as new species occurrence rows here.

## One isolated tooth cannot supply a complete dentition {#s-1490208fbb}
Referred crown MPCO.V 0020c is not directly associated with the holotype but comes from mixed dinosaur and pterosaur material. The original authors tentatively assign it to the species and suggest an anterior jaw position; the 2021 comparison retains the attribution condition. The tooth informs comparisons with toothed predatory dinosaurs without establishing a complete upper and lower dentition, particular prey or food proportions, or the condition at every developmental stage.

## Relationships vary with incomplete sampling {#s-b9d80230d2}
The 2019 analysis supports placement within Noasaurinae, while internal relationships remain limited by missing material and unstable entries. A sister relationship with Velocisaurus appears only in a further reduced consensus after taxa are pruned, and that node retains low support. This page distinguishes complete and reduced results. Directory parents organize material without replacing matrix reanalysis or review of all later classifications.
