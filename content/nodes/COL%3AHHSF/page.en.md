## Crowded leaves and a woolly ovary {#s-62e60012e7}
The Brazilian Flora 2020 field records white latex, cylindrical branches with no thickened bark, and crowded alternate leaves, one at a time on alternating sides of the branch, with flattened bases. Flower clusters occur at or just below the branch tips and are described as non-rigid. The sepals, the outer leaf-like parts of a flower, are similar in length. The petal lobes, the separated segments at the edge of the fused petals, are recorded as shorter than, equal to, or longer than the tube formed by fused petals; the flower's ovary, the part that later develops into fruit, is woolly-haired. The smooth fruit has visible ribs, a stalk, and conspicuous lenticels (small openings in the surface). The source lists alternative petal-lobe proportions without explaining whether they reflect variation.
