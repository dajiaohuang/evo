## Accepted name and placement {#s-e83f2b30bc}
COL26.8 accepts Boulenophrys puningensis Wang, Zeng, Lyu, Xiao & Wang, 2022 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Megophryidae (family) → Boulenophrys (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## Longkeng type-locality records {#s-bb317f892d}
Male holotype SYS a005770 came from Longkeng Village, Daping, Puning, Guangdong, at approximately 120 m. The monograph cited Wang and colleagues (2022) for records only from the Puning type locality at 120–300 m. These dated locality records do not establish absence elsewhere or a measured elevation tolerance.

## Six examined adults {#s-b45621448c}
Four adult males and two adult females formed the examined type material. Diagnostic snout–vent lengths were 31.7–34.6 mm for males and 37.8–38.3 mm for females. These measurements retain the reported sample counts and do not define universal size limits or a statistical estimate of differences between sexes.

## Spiny tubercles and male nuptial pads {#s-27211f656f}
The diagnosis described vomerine ridges and teeth, rough granular dorsal skin with conical tubercles, and large ventral tubercles. Tubercles on the posterior belly, ventral thighs and around the cloaca bore tiny spines. Toes had rudimentary basal webbing without lateral fringes. Breeding males had nuptial pads carrying fine, dense black spines on the dorsal bases of fingers I and II and a subgular vocal sac. These are sampled anatomical characters; their functional effects and breeding season were not established in this treatment.

## A character cited in the gutu comparison {#s-d0efea83d9}
The 2025 gutu description lists Boulenophrys puningensis as with vomerine teeth. This is an attributed comparator statement within that paper’s taxonomic scope, not a new measurement series for this species. The gutu adult sample spans 34.4–44.7 mm in seven males and 36.2–52.8 mm in eight females; those numbers belong to gutu, not to this comparator. The selected character does not establish population-wide frequency, complete diagnosis, diet or developmental biology.
