## Stems and flowers {#s-9dd37863c2}
The 2012 regional account describes a rhizomatous perennial up to 40 cm tall, forming diffuse tufts. Its stems are slightly flattened, branch in pairs, and root at the nodes. The leaves are sword-shaped; the soft, blue flowers nod, and the greenish translucent spathes have slightly torn edges. The capsules are short and three-lobed.

These traits come from a regional South African account published in 2012; the excerpt gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency.

## Trailing stems and seeds {#s-dbb50c392f}
A 2020 diagnostic account describes leaf tufts at ground level and persistent, aerial, stolon-like stems that root at nodes some distance from the parent plant—a feature described as unique in *Aristea* and giving the plant a trailing form. Slender flowering stems arise from leaf clusters, are usually branched in pairs, and bear drooping flower clusters. The rounded seeds are approximately doughnut-shaped, with a depression at the end bearing the chalaza; they differ from the tangentially flattened, plate-like seeds of other members of subgenus *Aristea*. The seed stalk (funicle) persists, is fleshy, and forms an irregularly lobed body.

These diagnostic details come from a regional South African account published in 2020; the excerpt gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency.
