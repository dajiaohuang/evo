## Branchiostoma japonicum: spawning has a season {#intro}
Can warming make a lancelet reproduce throughout the year? A laboratory study treated *Branchiostoma japonicum* separately from *B. belcheri*. Warming induced release of eggs and sperm during the tested breeding periods, but the experiment did not achieve year-round spawning in *B. japonicum*.

## The two species remained separate experimental animals {#separate-animals}
The researchers identified wild-collected adults as *B. japonicum* or *B. belcheri* using earlier descriptions and cultured them separately. Mature laboratory offspring were later mixed with their parents within the breeding stocks. Gonads, the reproductive organs holding eggs or sperm, provided the visible maturity criterion for selecting animals; this study is not a fresh anatomical diagnosis separating the species.

## Warmer culture did not yield year-round spawning {#seasonal-test}
Under natural light cycles, 270 similar-sized *B. japonicum* were assigned to three culture groups at 18–19 °C, room temperature, or 25–27 °C, observed from 24 November 2011 to 22 March 2013. In the February–May 2012 breeding period, 42 of 84 animals in the warm group and 16 of 85 at room temperature spawned twice; the cool group spawned once. No spawning was observed outside the breeding season in any group. These denominators are the reported animals for the outcomes, not the initial 270 split into three equal surviving samples.

## A temperature shift released gametes during the tested seasons {#temperature-induction}
For induction, mature *B. japonicum* were held at 19 °C for at least five days and then transferred to 24 °C. The protocol subsequently isolated them in about 20 mL of filtered warm seawater and switched off the lights at 13:30. Eggs or sperm could then be collected during the 2011 and 2012 experimental breeding periods. The protocol supplied embryos for research; its success in seasonal laboratory animals does not establish year-round reproduction or a universal spawning cue.
