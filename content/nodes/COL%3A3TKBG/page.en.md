## The revised male diagnosis uses external and cranial characters {#s-8f28f4cd22}
Male snout–vent length is reported as 46.1–53.3 mm, with a head wider than long, elongated thigh glands and axillary glands behind arm insertion. Small toe webbing reaches halfway between the first and second subarticular tubercles beside toe IV. The iris is light grey, sometimes blue-tinged, with a black sclera; the brown venter lacks conspicuous reticulation. Cranial features also enter the diagnosis, including frontoparietals joined at a few midline sites but mostly separated by a narrow fontanelle. These combined examined characters do not establish female size or permit identification by eye colour alone. The final cranial sentence is malformed in the source and is not reconstructed here.

## Mitochondrial separation restricts earlier bompu assignments {#s-e75bec99da}
The revision places bompu as sister to the newly named mechuka, reporting 16S divergence of 3.6–4.2%, and 4.5–4.7% from somani. Its broader phylogeny uses mitochondrial 12S, tRNA-Val and 16S, not nuclear genome-wide evidence. The Medog material previously labelled bompu is attributed to mechuka elsewhere in the revision; those occurrences are not retained as confirmed bompu records. The new names are absent from the pinned COL26.8 species list and remain pending catalogue mapping, rather than being assigned a fabricated identifier or merged into this species.

## The larval summary refers to Gosner stage thirty-six {#s-bdf1844629}
The revision summarizes an earlier stage-36 larval description: a flattened elliptical body, rounded snout, lateral nostrils nearer the snout, dorsolateral eyes, left spiracle and right vent tube. The muscular tail is about two-thirds of total length, with a rounded tip and higher dorsal than ventral fin. A large ventral oral disc has conical marginal papillae, keratodont formula 5(2–5)/5(1–4), and U-shaped upper and V-shaped lower jaw sheaths. These are cited stage-specific morphology, not a newly measured diet, larval growth rate or reproduction experiment. No adult SVL is inferred from the larval proportions.

## Cited calls are distinct from call groups {#s-1f940517c4}
The cited bompu call account describes groups of two to eight calls. Individual calls last 61–786 ms and contain 9–57 pulses at about 120 pulses/s, often with a longer first call. Mean dominant frequency is 1.53 kHz, reported range 1.16–1.77 kHz, with amplitude modulation but approximately constant frequency. These are the revision’s summary of Sarmah and colleagues’ 2025 work, not newly independent recordings or a count of frogs. Individual-call duration must not be treated as group duration, and the summaries do not apply to Medog calls reassigned to mechuka.

## Six named Arunachal localities retain individual elevations {#s-b1a00f0c76}
The restricted account lists Bompu in Eaglenest Wildlife Sanctuary at 1,940 m; Pange at 1,810 m and Tasi Biidang at 1,989 m in Tale Valley Wildlife Sanctuary; and Hari at 1,788 m, Sikhe at 1,671 m and Manipolyang at 1,911 m in the Ziro Valley area, Arunachal Pradesh, India. These locality elevations are not experimentally established tolerance bounds or evidence of occupancy at every intervening elevation.
