## Two Jurassic mammaliaforms preserve a jaw–ear mosaic {#s-112e6b5e0f}
The study compares two separate fossils: Dianoconodon holotype IVPP V4257 and Feredocodon paratype IMMNH-PV01925, from the Lufeng Formation and Daohugou Beds. Their jaw joints and postdentary bones let researchers compare how load-bearing may have changed before the middle ear fully detached from the jaw.

The authors infer reduced load-bearing function in Dianoconodon's articular-quadrate joint and interpret Feredocodon's middle-ear features as suited to an exclusively auditory function, although its postdentary bones remain attached to the dentary. These are anatomical interpretations, not measurements of bite force or hearing in either fossil. Their combined 201.4–161.5 Ma envelope spans distinct specimens and formations; it is not a continuous lineage.
