## Distephanus angulifolius {#s-b88cc88287}
## Sweet-scented lilac heads above ribbed climbing stems

Swelankomo and Manning's 2014 southern African account describes a scandent shrub or semi-climber 1–6 m tall. Young ribbed stems have close-lying hairs and glands, then lose some hairs. Leaves are usually opposite or nearly so, but are rarely alternate; their deltoid-ovate, rhombic or arrow-shaped blades have three strong veins from the base and coarse or wavy teeth, rarely smooth edges. They are gland-dotted and become darker above than below when dry.

Upper leaf axils carry weakly ascending cymose panicles of sweetly scented heads, each with about ten florets. The bell-shaped involucre is 4–5 mm wide, with 3–4 overlapping rows of thin, hairy, glandular bracts. Florets range from pale mauve, lilac or pink to creamy white. The 5-lobed corolla is narrowly funnel-shaped. Narrowly top-shaped fruits are sharply 5-ribbed, hairy and glandular; their white pappus has two rows of short scales and longer bristles. The source records dune and coastal forests, mainly along margins, paths and roadsides on sand or sandy loam.
