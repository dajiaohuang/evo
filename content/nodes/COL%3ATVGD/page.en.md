## A minimum count at Rose Atoll {#s-5da460ac3a}
Rapid surveys at Rose Atoll National Wildlife Refuge in American Samoa recorded 218 female green turtles during the 2012–2019 nesting seasons. Permanent tags confirmed at least 138 distinct nesting females. The first number is the total recorded; the second is a minimum count of individuals. Surveys averaged 7.6 days per year, so some nesters may have been missed. These observations describe free-living females at one rookery over eight seasons, not global abundance, a population trend or lifetime reproductive output (Murakawa et al., 2024: Abstract; Results §3.2, “Nesting beach surveys,” Table 2).

## What a local grazing experiment measured {#s-ba12745f76}
At a *Thalassia testudinum* meadow in Little Cayman, Cayman Islands, a 2016 experiment measured five clipped plots and five reference plots weekly for 12 weeks. Clipping simulated green-turtle grazing. Mean net ecosystem production (NEP) was 79% lower in clipped plots than in references (24.7 versus 119.5 mmol C m−2 d−1), although the 12-week mean remained positive. On overcast sampling days in weeks one and nine, lower gross primary production left clipped plots slightly heterotrophic (NEP < 0). Nearby areas continuously grazed by juvenile green turtles for at least a year had mean NEP 92% lower than adjacent ungrazed areas. The production-to-respiration ratio did not differ significantly among treatments (ANOVA, p = 0.16). These are measurements from one meadow: clipping was not a turtle-population experiment, and neither comparison directly measured sediment carbon stocks or establishes a species-wide effect (Johnson et al., 2017: Methods; Results, “Effect of Grazing on Seagrass Ecosystem Metabolism,” Table 1 and Fig. 2).

## Read the counts and metabolism separately {#s-94cec49c60}
The Rose Atoll study counted nesting females; the Little Cayman study measured meadow metabolism under simulated and natural grazing. These observations have different subjects and cannot be combined into a population assessment. The trait compilations below are another evidence layer, with their own historical scope and limits.
