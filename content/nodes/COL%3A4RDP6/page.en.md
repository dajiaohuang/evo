## A cold-acclimated captive cohort, not a wild tolerance limit {#s-c7ec14b535}
Frogs were collected near Arkhara in September2022 and gradually cooled from 14–15°C through 8°C and 4°C to 2–3°C. Twenty days after collection, twelve Rana dybowskii were split into six controls and six experimental animals. Controls remained at 5–7mg/L dissolved oxygen. The animals were not fed during captivity; the authors explain this by their seasonal wild feeding pattern, not by a new year-round diet study. These conditions delimit a cold-acclimated sample and do not establish tolerance across all temperatures, seasons or populations.

## Species experienced different containers and hypoxia endpoints {#s-6f74ea5d1d}
Each experimental dybowskii occupied a sealed0.63L container. Severe behavioural depression appeared after about1.5days; tissues were collected four hours after that state, with measured oxygen0.9–1.1mg/L. The amurensis group instead shared one10.3L tank and spent ten days near0.1mg/L after oxygen depletion. This is a distress-defined terminal sampling endpoint, not a measured lethal concentration or an estimate of survival time for every dybowskii. Exposure duration, oxygen level and container arrangement differ between species.

## Tissue concentrations are not metabolic flux measurements {#s-dac174bae6}
The study extracts metabolites for proton NMR and quantifies peaks against an internal standard. New measurements cover dybowskii liver, heart and brain and amurensis brain; amurensis heart and liver comparisons reuse an earlier study. The NMR instrument measured extracts at25°C, distinct from frogs held at2–3°C. Forty-four metabolites were detected in amurensis brain; smaller brain samples had higher detection limits. Failure to detect a substance need not mean complete biological absence. Volcano plots use fold-change>1.5 and FDR-corrected p<0.1, whereas selected figures use Mann–Whitney tests and SEM bars.

## Accumulation contrasts depend on tissue and exposure {#s-4dc824299d}
Within dybowskii, hypoxic liver lactate increased28-fold and heart lactate24-fold relative to controls; alanine increased9-fold and7-fold, respectively. These within-species changes differ from the cross-species concentration comparisons. Glycerol was present and increased, even though hypoxic amurensis had substantially higher amounts. Dybowskii brain had the same detected metabolite set as amurensis except2,3-butanediol, whose nondetection is not an independently certified universal absence. Larger lactate accumulation does not by itself prove the sole cause of distress.

## The pathway diagram is a mechanistic proposal {#s-453b15c613}
The authors propose diversion of glycolytic products toward alanine, glycerol and2,3-butanediol, potentially changing lactate production and redox balance. They also discuss amino-acid transport as an alternative explanation and mark some intermediates or reactions unknown in the pathway diagram. Concentration snapshots do not measure reaction flux, enzyme activity or causal protection, and yeast, bacterial or fish pathways are functional analogies rather than experimentally demonstrated frog reactions. The conclusion’s stronger redirection wording must be read with these discussion limits.
