## Hebeclinium macrophyllum {#s-797fa03336}
## Large leaves and a many-headed crown

The Flora of Panama description allows the plant to be either an erect herb or a small shrub, reaching 2.5 m. Its stems are cylindrical, faintly lined, and densely covered in fine woolly hairs. Opposite leaves are thin and papery, broadly ovate, and may reach 15 by 20 cm. Their bases are usually squared or heart-shaped; the edges are shallowly rounded-toothed, and the tips taper to a point. Three main veins start at the leaf base. The upper surface is sparsely hairy to nearly smooth, while the lower surface is densely woolly; leaf stalks reach 10 cm.

Loose, many-headed panicles have corymb-like branching. Each head is about 4.5 mm high and contains roughly 50–80 florets. About 30 overlapping bracts form four or five unequal rows around each head; the outer faces have four or five lengthwise ridges and dense short hairs. The hemispherical receptacle is densely bristly. White or pink, narrow-tubed corollas are about 3.5 mm long; their tiny lobes are about 0.2 mm and hairy outside. The dry fruits (achenes) are about 1.5 mm, mostly smooth with some glands near the top. Their pappus has about 30–35 slender, rough bristles with pointed tips. These are measurements and traits in that source description; no habitat or species-wide variation is established here.
