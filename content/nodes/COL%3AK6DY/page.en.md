## Flowers and season {#s-41b5780c6d}
The 2013 regional account describes a nearly stemless, corm-bearing geophyte 80–140 mm tall, with narrow, sword-shaped, velvety leaves that rise above the flowers. Two to five blue, two-lipped flowers with white nectar guides form a short, low-lying spike and have a spicy scent. The perianth tube is 18–20 mm long, and the upper tepal is 45–55 mm. The ovary is smooth; the green, velvety inner bracts are forked at the tip. Flowering is recorded from late July to September.

These traits, measurements, and flowering period come from a regional South African account published in 2013; the excerpt gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency.

## Comparisons with related species {#s-bdd71dea94}
A 2020 account places *B. grandiflora* in section Teretifolieae, whose described features include inner bracts forked at the tip, a flowering stem often more or less underground, and a smooth ovary. The account highlights the large flowers: the perianth tube is 18–20 mm long and the upper tepal 45–55 mm. Their scent combines violets with a spicy, acrid note. Although the flowers are large, the stamens are short, with filaments about 10 mm long; the anthers sit inside the floral cup and are mostly hidden within the funnel-shaped perianth.

The account suggests *B. nana*, a coastal Western Cape species, may be its closest relative. It distinguishes *B. grandiflora* by its narrow, upright leaves with short, velvety hairs and mostly unbranched, ground-level spikes bearing 2–5 flowers. It also notes past confusion with the widespread *B. ambigua* of deep sands at coastal and inland Western Cape sites. *B. ambigua* has inner bracts divided to the base, a shorter upper tepal (28–40 mm), and longer filaments (13–16 mm).

These diagnostic comparisons come from a regional South African account published in 2020; the excerpt gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency.
