## Strumaria gemmata: flower form and leaf variation {#s-68f4f17590}
A 2012 regional account describes a bulbous geophyte reaching 40 cm. Its two leaves are dry at flowering, spreading to prostrate and usually hairy. The star-shaped flowers are recorded as (2–)6–14, pale lemon and rarely cream-coloured. Their tepals are crisped and have a translucent swelling at the base.

A 1994 systematic account identifies *Strumaria gemmata* as the only species in the genus with pale-lemon flowers and distinctly crisped tepals. It describes a solid, shining, translucent swelling on the upper surface of each tepal, beneath the filament attachment, and suggests that it appears to mimic nectar. The leaves are described as indistinguishable from those of other hairy-leaved species in section Gemmaria, but with more variable hair covering: collections from the far west often have only rough leaf margins, while leaves elsewhere in the stated range are more or less hairy.
