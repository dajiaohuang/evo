## Name and this data account {#s-3928621f6e}
COL26.8 accepts Trimeresurus rubeus in Viperidae. Its ReptTraits v1-2 entry follows the Reptile Database 2023 name framework and retains citations for the recorded traits. The species row represents compiled sources, not a condition established for every population.

## Where and how it is active {#s-6611f1742e}
The entry records both arboreal and terrestrial microhabitats, a carnivorous diet, and nocturnal activity. These categories do not identify prey or list every habitat and activity period. The compiled row does not show whether these patterns are shared across populations, seasons, or life stages.

## How to read reproductive records {#s-3f43917c3f}
The source labels reproductive mode as viviparous. That classification does not specify fetal nutrition or establish that reproduction is uniform across populations. No supported clutch or litter count is included here.

## Evidence limits {#s-621099f0b9}
This supplemental account uses limited trait material and does not count as complete morphology or ecology. This account paraphrases the same-name ReptTraits v1-2 entry, retaining its 2023 taxonomic framework and sources for individual traits. One row per species compresses variation in sex, age, locality and populations; matching name and family do not ensure equivalent later concepts. Reported maximum length is not an absolute species limit, and reproductive means are not fixed counts in every clutch. Many mass entries are estimated from length equations and are not presented here as measured mass. Juvenile-length headers and definitions differ and are also omitted from reader conclusions. Conservation, venom, current distribution and population trends are not updated here; unrecorded fields remain gaps. Dataset citation labels preserve the source chain and do not imply that this project independently reread every original work.

## Traits not interpreted here {#s-b4b2edcfac}
The supported records cover microhabitat, diet, activity time, and reproductive mode. They do not provide a supported snout–vent or total length, sex-specific length, clutch or litter count, egg dimensions, foraging mode, or sex-determination mechanism. Blank fields and records for relatives cannot fill those gaps.
