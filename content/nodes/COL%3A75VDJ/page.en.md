## Form and flowers {#s-400c873bf3}
The regional flora describes this corm-bearing geophyte as 10–30 cm tall, with 2–8 spreading leaves that are V-shaped in cross-section and usually toothed. It bears one star-shaped flower, yellow or white and occasionally pinkish. The flower centre is often iridescent; spots may sometimes lack that sheen, and the tepals are striped beneath. The filaments are short, and the single bract is leaf-like and sheathing.

These measurements and traits come from a regional South African account published in 2012; the excerpt gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency.

## Distinguishing Pauridia canaliculata {#s-b315e4256d}
A 2014 revision compares *P. capensis* with *P. canaliculata*. Large-flowered *P. capensis* specimens with unmarked tepals are readily distinguished from *P. canaliculata*, whose flowers have a contrasting central eye. When *P. capensis* has dark blotches at the tepal base, separation may depend on how well specimens were pressed and annotated. In *P. canaliculata*, the blotches are uniform in size and glaucous grey against orange or deep-yellow tepals; spots in *P. capensis* are never glaucous and vary greatly in size, shape, and colour. Very narrow leaves can also make it difficult to tell whether they are keel-shaped (carinate) or channelled (canaliculate). The seeds provide a clear distinction: ellipsoid in *P. capensis* and J-shaped in *P. canaliculata*.

This diagnostic comparison comes from a 2014 taxonomic revision of *Pauridia* in southern Africa; the excerpt gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency.
