## Branchiostoma floridae: a genome comparison beyond the backbone {#intro}
How can an animal without a vertebral column help explain vertebrate genomes? The Florida lancelet *Branchiostoma floridae* supplied the genome sequenced by Putnam and colleagues in 2008. Its value here is as a living comparison with tunicates and vertebrates, rather than as an identified ancestor of either group.

## One male supplied the genomic DNA {#gonad-sample}
The study prepared genomic DNA from the gonads—the reproductive organs—of one gravid male collected in Tampa Bay, Florida, in July 2003. Thus the two haplotypes, the alternative chromosome copies inherited by that individual, were sampled from one animal. The reported sequence variation cannot be read as a survey of all Florida lancelets.

## Gene neighborhoods connect the comparison {#gene-order}
Comparing the Florida lancelet draft genome with other animal genomes, the researchers reconstructed 17 ancestral chordate linkage groups: sets of genes inferred to have been carried together on ancestral chromosomes. Their repeated counterparts in vertebrate genomes supported two rounds of genome duplication in the vertebrate lineage. This is a reconstruction from conserved gene neighborhoods, not a directly observed ancestral chromosome set.

## A draft keeps the assembly limits visible {#draft-genome}
Assembly version 2 covered 522 megabases, where one megabase is one million DNA bases. At each locus this version selected one haplotype; version 1 instead represented both separately. The authors could not reconstruct complete chromosomes without physical or genetic maps, so the long assembled segments supported comparisons of gene order while remaining a draft.
