## A historical AVONET record {#s-dfe75924f0}
COL26.8 accepts Otus moheliensis in Strigidae. AVONET v7 lists 3 measured individuals under the HBW–BirdLife 2020 name, all 3 with a complete measurement set. A usable sample may still differ by trait.

## Beak measurements at defined landmarks {#s-1f2cb35ed0}
Mean beak length is 11.5 mm from the front edge of the nostril to the tip; mean width is 6.2 mm and depth is 11 mm at the nostril edge. These are separate measurements at defined landmarks, not field-identification cutoffs.

## Wing, tarsus and a separate mass source {#s-e983327193}
Mean unflattened wing length, from the carpal joint to the longest primary, is 166 mm; mean tarsus length is 40.9 mm. The compiled mean mass is 104.97 g and carries source category Dunning. Mass comes from separate species-average information, so it does not show that every measured bird was weighed.

## Broad habitat and feeding categories {#s-2df3fe8e14}
AVONET assigns Forest as the habitat, Invertivore as the trophic niche and Insessorial as the primary lifestyle. Insessorial describes a bird that spends much time perched on vegetation or other substrates. These are broad comparison categories, not claims that the owl uses only forest or eats only invertebrates.

## Migration label and historical range map {#s-f2dc296c4b}
AVONET labels the record sedentary. Historical breeding and resident maps span latitude endpoints -12.39° to -12.25°. They do not give a complete account of non-breeding movements or show current positions or global occupancy. The geography and measurements come from separate compilation steps.

## Relative use of four feeding positions {#s-dc3f7b5b9d}
Species-level foraging weights are 60% for the ground, 20% for understory, edges or shrubs below 2 m, 10% for trees or tall bushes from 2 m upward but below the canopy, and 10% in or just above the canopy. These estimate relative use of feeding positions, not nest sites, all habitat or a full-day time budget. Surface-water, understory, mid/high, canopy and aerial categories have separate source definitions; they are not elevation bands and cannot be converted into flight heights or habitat area.
