## The European record begins with an incomplete skull {#s-8369f34e24}
The 2012 description names Europejara olcadesorum from MCCM-LH 9413 at Las Hoyas, Spain, reporting the first European tapejarid then known. It extends the named record to Iberia without establishing presence at every Early Cretaceous European site. Claims of the oldest known toothless pterosaur and Eurasian origin retain the study’s dated sampling context, without a comprehensive check of later discoveries.

## Part and counterpart preserve cranial material rather than a whole body {#s-d0e2b7cc2f}
The holotype comprises jaws and postorbital skull elements on part and counterpart slabs. The upper jaw and skull are incomplete, crushed and dorsoventrally flattened, while the mandible lies in lateral view. Postorbital bones are displaced and some small elements were removed during acid preparation. No skin or keratinous beak is preserved; a site-level soft-tissue label does not apply automatically to this specimen.

## A backward-curving mandibular crest informs diagnosis {#s-c1a3353c84}
Diagnosis combines a backward-curving, dorsoventrally deep dentary crest, a crest-height/mandibular-ramus-height ratio near four, and a medial dorsal bulge with shallow depressions. An estimated crest height near 90 mm and a 22 mm ramus height describe this material. Crest function remains uncertain; size alone does not establish sex, courtship or a species-wide growth stage.

## Toothless jaws and the preserved palate have separate limits {#s-3d49d506ec}
The mandible preserves about 230 mm, with total length estimated near 255 mm. Its symphyseal dorsal edge is step-like and slightly sigmoid, with toothless cutting edges. Dorsal palatal exposure shows two narrow choanae and a thin vomer, while other sutures are poorly visible. Slender hyoid rods are preserved rather than tongue soft tissue. Bone names retain the 2012 interpretation without implying a later CT-based reassessment.

## Two-metre wingspan and an upper crest are different reconstructions {#s-352e39a232}
A roughly two-metre wingspan is scaled from other tapejarines; the holotype lacks complete wing elements for direct span measurement. A high premaxillary crest and possibly backward-curving outline are inferred from the mandibular crest and relatives, with parts of the head reconstruction based on Tapejara. These are not an intact preserved upper crest or measured complete wing, nor maximum adult size, established mass or flight speed.

## Las Hoyas context does not define the species’ entire life history {#s-44f3e3acdf}
The holotype comes from upper Barremian laminated limestone of the La Huérguina Formation. The authors describe subtropical wet, forested and aquatic settings over low-relief karst, with abundant aquatic organisms and rare pterosaur material. This is depositional and assemblage context, without establishing aquatic habits, exclusive forest residence or one local plant as its sole food; the database age bracket is not an individual lifespan.

## An early phylogenetic result does not establish geographic origin {#s-dbd8ba0e70}
The 2012 analysis uses equal-weight, unordered characters and obtains 135 equally parsimonious trees. Strict consensus supports tapejarine affinity but leaves relationships within that group poorly resolved. Eurasian origin and later expansion to Brazil are proposals combining stratigraphic succession, distribution and phylogeny; uneven sampling can also produce apparent absences. The earliest preserved locality does not directly establish true origin.

## Fruit feeding and seed dispersal lack direct dietary evidence {#s-8205e9ca13}
Toothless jaw form and analogies motivate discussion of fruit, seed or mixed feeding and possible early-angiosperm seed dispersal. The paper explicitly lacks direct evidence such as gut contents; temporal and geographic congruence does not establish interaction or coevolution. No definitive menu, particular fruit association or demonstrated dispersal behavior is assigned here, and toothlessness alone does not prove herbivory.

## Compare the European mandible with Chinese material {#s-5793045d9d}
Read the revised jaw identification in [Eopteranodon lii](../../Eopteranodon/Eopteranodon_lii/page.en.md), then growth, crests and taxonomy in [Sinopterus dongi](../../Neoazhdarchia/Tapejaroidea/Tapejaridae/Sinopterus/Sinopterus_dongi/page.en.md). The 2012 European description and 2023 Chinese reassessment remain separate studies, not an established ancestor chain. Their evidence does not provide a complete growth series or direct dietary evidence.

## The named collection and age {#s-a1b8742136}
PBDB records the Las Hoyas (MCCM collection) in Castilla-La Mancha, Spain (region code ES), in the La Huérguina Formation. Its database age bracket is 125.77–121.4 Ma, assigned to the Late Barremian. This selected record does not give a complete species distribution.

## What the age envelope means {#s-a8f031bf57}
The selected PBDB record has an outer age envelope of 125.77–121.4 Ma. This combines the database's oldest and youngest bounds; gaps may occur, and the interval does not date the species' origin, extinction or an individual's lifespan.

## Collection setting and preservation {#s-9057e7ddd9}
The PBDB collection fields label Las Hoyas as lacustrine - large and list body, adpression, trace and soft parts as preservation modes. These collection-level labels do not establish the species' diet or habitat or describe every fossil individually.

## The reference listed for the collection {#s-1bf7c46a98}
PBDB abbreviates the collection's citation as Vullo et al. (2012), ref:42229. A separate reference entry preserves the full seven-author citation; the occurrence row and the full paper citation remain distinct records.

## Evidence limits {#s-5dbe75ff74}
This account follows the 2012 description and a dated PBDB record for one collection. The incomplete skull and jaws do not preserve a full wing or upper crest, so wingspan and crest shape are reconstructions. Toothlessness and jaw form motivate dietary hypotheses but do not directly establish a menu or seed-dispersal behavior; the earliest known locality also does not determine the species' geographic origin.
