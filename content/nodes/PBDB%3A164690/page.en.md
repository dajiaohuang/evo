## Similar filtration, different oral structures {#s-b68d9e6004}
The 2017 Liaodactylus description uses a skull, mandibles and first two cervical vertebrae; 152 closely spaced teeth shorten toward the rear and support the authors’ filtering interpretation. The 2023 Balaenognathus case instead has a near-complete skeleton, at least 480 teeth, crown hooklets and a spatulate toothless front edge, with specific water-flow models. Both frame a question about dental filtration, while specimen completeness, tooth geometry and inferred mechanisms retain different limits. Pumping proposed for the latter is not known behavior in the former.

## Different structures can support dental filtration {#s-0844e712e1}
The 2023 Balaenognathus description compares fine-toothed pterosaurs including Ctenochasma, Gnathosaurus and Pterodaustro. Their shared functional question is how water enters and passes a dental filter, while teeth and snouts differ. This Balaenognathus has crown-tip expansions limiting closure and a toothless spatulate front opening; the Pterodaustro comparison emphasizes exceptionally slender lower teeth and small, more rounded upper teeth. These comparisons follow the paper’s discussion without a fresh audit of every compared specimen. Its buccal/gular pumping model is not assigned as fixed behavior to all descendant groups.
