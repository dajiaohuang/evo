## A relatively frequent pond record in the sampled sites {#s-685d25ebef}
Visual encounter surveys in the Tena microbasin recorded 36 Dendropsophus bifurcus specimens in ponds, making it one of the two most numerous pond species reported for that effort. The entire visual sample comprised 248 specimens, including 195 from ponds and 53 from river transects. These counts concern the six wet-season visits per site in 2020 and 2021; the study does not establish a species-wide density or an annual population trend.

## Specimen identification and water sequencing have separate evidence {#s-168e1f9c31}
Survey specimens were photographed, described in life and identified using keys, local collection comparisons or Bioweb Ecuador; preserved vouchers were placed in the Ikiam–INABIO collection. The study also analysed short 16S amplicons from water. A visual specimen count cannot be replaced by the number of sequencing reads, and this article does not newly establish the developmental biology or breeding schedule of Dendropsophus bifurcus. Individual vouchers and supplementary detection rows were not independently examined here.

## Historical local survey records are distinct from a current provincial census {#s-8f8837e4c0}

Counts are cumulative records, not an independently certified number of distinct marked animals, population density or a current abundance estimate. Surveys combine daytime/night-time visual and photographic encounters, ten 8 × 8 m quadrats, four 100 × 20 m transects and fifty pitfall traps across six habitats. The site description’s 900–1,100 m gradient and later 2025 weather-summary temperature are not this species’ measured elevation limits or thermal tolerance. Province-wide occurrence and all source identifications have not been independently certified.
