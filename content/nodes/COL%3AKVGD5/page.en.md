## Two species, separate field marks {#brief}
A 2013 comparison of geographically separated populations described Hollandichthys taramandahy in southern Brazilian coastal basins and distinguished it from H. multifasciatus by a combination of fin-ray counts and pigment marks. A separate study then measured life-history variation in H. multifasciatus across ten blackwater streams.

## Marks that separate the described species {#diagnosis}
Bertaco and Malabarba’s description distinguishes H. taramandahy from H. multifasciatus by a small black mark at the base of the median caudal-fin rays, fewer dorsal and ventral procurrent caudal-fin rays, no black mark on the anterior half of the adipose fin, and—among specimens over 60 mm standard length—no humeral mark. These are diagnostic comparisons in the 2013 species description, not a statement that every fish in either species was measured alike.

## What ten blackwater streams showed {#blackwater-life}
Cardoso and Esteves examined 185 H. multifasciatus from ten streams during the low-precipitation period, June–September, sampling a 100 m reach at each stream. Most measured traits were similar among sampled populations; differences were reported for mean length of immature fish, gonadosomatic index of mature males, condition factor of immatures and mature adults, and the length–weight slope for immatures. The authors attributed the pattern to food availability and/or other local factors, so this study does not establish a single life-history profile for the genus.
