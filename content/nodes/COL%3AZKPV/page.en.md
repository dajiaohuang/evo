## Form and flowering structure {#s-12092014fe}
One archived SANBI description calls this a geophyte, up to 1 m tall, growing alone or in small clumps, with pleated leaf blades. It describes a horizontal flower spike that is straight to slightly zigzagging, and hooded, funnel-shaped, red-orange perianths 70–90 mm long. Flowering is recorded for January and February.

## Diagnostic comparison {#s-ca299d8ed9}
A separate SANBI diagnostic account describes a slender stem seldom reaching 1 m, often unbranched or with at most one or two short branches. It contrasts this with the strongly branched *C. paniculata*, which may be confused with *C. pearsei*. The account describes the spikes of both species as strongly flexuose, with flowers in two opposed rows facing away from one another. It gives *C. pearsei* flowers as about 60–70 mm long, with a perianth tube 40–45 mm long and a dorsal tepal about 20 mm long; typical *C. paniculata* flowers have a 33–40 mm perianth tube and a 14–18 mm dorsal tepal. The account also contrasts their habitats: *C. paniculata* is described from wet sites, deep rich soils, and moderate elevations, whereas *C. pearsei* is associated with high-altitude, well-drained basalt slopes and cliffs. These are separate source measurements: the first description gives perianth length, while the diagnostic account gives flower length and other floral measurements.
