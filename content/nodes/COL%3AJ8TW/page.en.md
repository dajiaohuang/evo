## Larval guild and adult ecomorph are separate source categories {#s-6ad9d3501a}
The study codes Atelognathus reverberii as larval LE (lentic) and adult TER (terrestrial). It keeps these life stages separate; the adult code does not automatically apply to tadpoles, or vice versa. Lentic, lotic and torrential labels describe broad habitat associations, not measured flow tolerance. Saxicolous or terrestrial codes do not define a full geographic range. Endotrophic tadpoles are distinct from direct development and still have a larval stage. The authors compiled these functional codes from species accounts rather than experimentally measuring each species’ feeding, hydrodynamics, locomotion or habitat choice.

## Paired-stage comparisons do not establish complete developmental independence {#s-450dbaad8f}
Across sampled species, larval–adult shape dissimilarities have a weak but significant Mantel association (r 0.16, p 0.003); disparity correlations vary among families. The authors read the overall pattern as support for adaptive decoupling. It does not show zero correlation, genetic independence, an experimentally selected trait or this species’ developmental trajectory. Phylogenetic analyses include 74 of 83 sampled species. Ancestral shape and timing patterns are model reconstructions from living data, not newly measured fossil morphology. Adult K is 0.44 with p 0.001, indicating less close-relative similarity than the reference expectation; the paper’s phrase “not phylogenetically structured” does not mean the test was nonsignificant. Supplement S3 repeats family N values that sum to 112 for both stages. Without raw matrices, adult individual denominators and imputed cells remain uncertified.
