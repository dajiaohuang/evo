## atlas-profile / overview {#s-103cc12dd8}
The Burgess Shale fossils of Tokummia katalepsis preserve paired jaws and several kinds of limb appendages. A morphology analysis places it among stem mandibulates, close to but outside the living lineages represented by insects, myriapods and crustaceans.

## atlas-profile / evidenceSummary {#s-677b1a0493}
ROM 63823 and paratypes preserve the anatomy used in the published analysis. Its position is a tree-based interpretation, not evidence that Tokummia was a direct ancestor.

## Jaws and limbs preserved together {#s-ade76a797e}
The holotype ROM 63823 and paratypes preserve paired mandibles, maxillipeds and subdivided basipods—the basal parts of the limbs. These appendages provide the evidence for comparing Tokummia with other early arthropods.

## A position on the mandibulate stem {#s-7b9c2b5892}
A morphology matrix places Tokummia on the stem leading toward mandibulates, including insects, myriapods and crustaceans. This is a branching relationship, not direct ancestry. Diet and movement are inferred from the jaws and limbs, not directly observed.
