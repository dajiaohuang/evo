## Stem surface {#s-c5113cdf03}
The Brazilian Flora 2020 record calls the stem glabrous, or hairless, but also lists simple hairs and describes their direction as spreading outward or recurved, bending back. It gives no specimen-level context to reconcile these fields. Capitate glandular hairs, whose tips form small heads, are present; branched and unbranched bristles and short lateral shoots (brachyblasts) are absent. Prickles are sparse and point backward.

## Leaves and inflorescences {#s-13f232c398}
Stipules, small structures at leaf bases, are caducous and fall early. Extrafloral nectaries (nectar-secreting glands outside flowers) and projections between pairs of pinnae (the leaf’s primary side divisions) are absent. The inflorescence, or flower cluster, is globose; its floral bracts (small leaf-like structures) are shorter than the flowers before they open (anthesis).

## Flowers {#s-83e5debcb8}
The perianth (calyx and corolla together) is recorded as having four parts. The membranous calyx, the outer sepal whorl, has triangular lobes. Corolla (petal-whorl) lobes have one to three veins and are listed as hairless or hairy. The androecium (the stamens collectively) is diplostemonous, with two stamen whorls; the filaments are pink.

## Habitat category {#s-4bbc039349}
The regional dataset labels the habitat “Terrícola,” Portuguese for terrestrial. This category does not specify a collection locality or establish the species’ full range.
