## What four flippers do in a water channel {#s-eaf2b58ef8}
In a recirculating water channel, researchers moved rigid models of paired fore- and hind flippers through prescribed strokes. They measured thrust, torque and efficiency while varying the timing between the flipper pairs. Some phase offsets let vortices shed by one pair interact with the other pair and improved measured performance.

The experiment tests whether those hydrodynamic effects are physically plausible. Its reconstructed flippers and controlled motions do not reproduce a living plesiosaur, and the results do not establish one universal stroke for every taxon, speed or manoeuvre. The measurements constrain a swimming model; they are not observations of an extinct animal’s gait.
