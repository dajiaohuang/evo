## How PBDB organizes fossil occurrences {#s-00171a96b0}
PBDB groups the selected fossil-occurrence records for *Pakicetidae* under its recorded classification path and links them to collections, strata, age brackets, and references. This hierarchy organizes database records; it remains distinct from COL taxonomy and is not a phylogenetic hypothesis.

## Named pakicetid records and collections {#s-479b0625a2}
The occurrence route includes names such as *Nalacetus ratimitus*, *Pakicetus calcis*, *P. chittas*, *P. attocki* and *P. inachus*. Each record links its collection, age bracket and source reference. Compare those details record by record; occurrences and collections do not measure ancient population size.

## How to interpret the displayed age brackets {#s-0c7b396039}
These PBDB age brackets bound the registered occurrences shown here. They do not by themselves establish a taxon’s global first or last appearance or a clade’s origin or extinction.

## Early whale material is incomplete {#s-64bfec7d67}
Pakicetidae is reviewed as a separate early Eocene cetacean family represented by incomplete and composite fossils. Comparing these remains helps test how early whales relate to other cetaceans, but the specimens do not form a demonstrated straight line of descent.

## Read the interval as a review sample {#s-4548175ff5}
The displayed 50–47 million-year span summarizes the pakicetid material included in the synthesis. It is a sampled family interval, not proof that all later whales descended directly from pakicetids.
