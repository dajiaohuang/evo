## Aloe buhrii: H-shaped leaf spots {#s-34f438752d}
A South African regional account describes a stemless succulent perennial that sometimes forms dense colonies by suckering. The leaves are yellow-green to glaucous green with a reddish tinge, marked by clear striations and irregular white H-shaped spots. They are lanceolate-deltoid and up to 40 cm long; their margins are reddish, smooth or minutely toothed. Loose, near-head-shaped racemes with spreading branches reach 60 cm. Flowers are orange-red, rarely yellow, green-tipped in bud and 25–27 mm long; the stamens and stigma project beyond the flower.

A comparative account says the leaf spots are more conspicuous than in other species in its section. The leaves are firmer, fleshier and narrower than those of *A. reynoldsii*. Its dense racemes resemble those of *A. striata* subsp. *striata* and subsp. *komaggasensis*, but differ from those of subsp. *karasbergensis* and *A. reynoldsii*. The flower colour is described as intermediate between subsp. *striata* and the yellow flowers of *A. reynoldsii* and subsp. *komaggasensis*.

A further account describes *Aloe buhrii* as related to *A. striata* Haw., which it locates in the eastern Cape and parts of the central west Cape. It distinguishes *A. buhrii* by its arched, upright or steeply ascending leaves with toothed margins, and by a branched, paniculate inflorescence rather than a corymb.
