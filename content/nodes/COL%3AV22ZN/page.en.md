## Accepted name and placement {#s-033d7007f4}
COL26.8 accepts Centrolene marcoreyesi Franco-Mena, Székely, Culebras, Batallas-Revelo, Reyes-Puig & Guayasamin, 2024 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Centrolenidae (family) → Centrolene (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## Male measurements do not supply the unknown female range {#s-62a3ac19c9}
The diagnosis gives six adult males at 24.5–27.0 mm snout–vent length and explicitly leaves female size unknown. Dorsal skin is finely roughened, usually with low whitish spots; the anterior two-thirds of the belly are pale and the posterior third translucent. Adult males have humeral spines, vocal sacs and slits, while ulnar and tarsal folds are conspicuous. Live photographs of MUTPL 271 show green dorsal surfaces, pale spots, green bones and a white iris with a lavender tinge and fine brown reticulation. These specimen and sex-specific features are not measurements of females or a certified diagnostic state for every population.

## Streamside perches use several reference surfaces {#s-8d3495ebcc}
Males were recorded at night on leaves and herbaceous vegetation by streams, in primary and secondary forest and pasture edges. Abra de Zamora callers were about one metre above water; Podocarpus callers were about two metres above fast streams. Guarumales records included 150–350 cm above ground, with some individuals lower or on ferns, and streams two to eight metres wide. Ground and water height are not interchangeable, and these perch observations do not identify all breeding substrates or measure habitat preference. The sympatric species lists do not establish competition, predation or equivalent ecological niches.

## A male calling near one nineteen-egg clutch {#s-79c99de89b}
At Guarumales, male CJ 12631 was observed calling near a clutch containing nineteen eggs, one reported dead. This is one clutch and a nearby calling male, not a mean clutch size, a population hatching rate or proof of continuous parental care. The dead egg cannot be used to infer the eventual survival of the other eighteen. Neither parentage nor duration of attendance was established in the text. Egg evidence from elisae, including its three counts and Gosner stage, belongs to that separate species and is not transferred to marcoreyesi.

## One-note calls and closely spaced consecutive calls {#s-9a0ad7b247}
The pooled description used three 1975 recordings at 11 °C and two 2017 recordings at 14.5 °C from Abra de Zamora. Regular calls contained one pulsed note, with mean duration 0.085 s, mean dominant frequency 3.3 kHz and mean interval 34.1 s. Two modern males also emitted consecutive calls separated by 0.8–1.7 s; those were still consecutive calls, not the two-note structure described for elisae. The authors proposed an effect of nearby competing males, whose distance was below half a metre, but did not experimentally isolate that cause. Repeated calls do not become additional independent animals.

## Historical differences are confounded by recording conditions {#s-242daa3125}
The 1975 calls lasted 0.098–0.131 s, with pulse rate 58.8–93.4 per second, whereas 2017 calls lasted 0.053–0.081 s with pulse rate 103.3–137.5 per second. The later sample was recorded at a higher temperature, and the authors considered weather and male behaviour as other explanations. These comparisons do not demonstrate evolutionary change over forty-two years. The old tape states elevation 2,850 m, which the authors considered probably incorrect; it is not added as a verified upper distribution record. Historical buckleyi identifications of those recordings require the revision’s specific reassignment, not transfer of every older buckleyi recording.

## Four southern Andean localities and threats within protected areas {#s-cdda33e13a}
The distribution account names San Francisco Scientific Station, Abra de Zamora, Podocarpus and Guarumales on the eastern southern Ecuadorian Andes, at 1,840–2,190 m. The authors estimated extent of occurrence below 100 square kilometres and proposed Endangered. Identified concerns included cattle-driven habitat change, introduced species, fires and legal or illegal mining; protected-area occurrence did not remove those concerns. These are the 2024 paper’s records and assessment proposal, not a fresh audit of each site or confirmation of a current Red List category. Extent of occurrence is not occupied area or population size, and no percentage decline caused by each threat was quantified.

## Lineage support and sister-node support differ {#s-d9b3bd066e}
Marcoreyesi was inferred monophyletic with posterior probability 0.99 and bootstrap 98. Its sister relationship to sabini had posterior probability 0.82 and bootstrap 94, which the authors characterized as moderate rather than equivalent to support for marcoreyesi itself. Analyses used mitochondrial 12S and 16S. These support values are not dates, geographic dispersal probabilities or experimental evidence of reproductive isolation. The relationship does not permit importing sabini morphology, eggs or calls into marcoreyesi, nor does it certify absence of gene flow.
