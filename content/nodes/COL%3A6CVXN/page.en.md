## Leaflets, flowers and pods {#s-fe648e5ac8}
The historical Flora of Panama account describes this vine in open areas and forest edges, with stems covered in grayish, rust-coloured hairs. Its leaflets are broadly lance-shaped to oval or narrowly obovate, 7–10 × 4–6 cm; tips are acute, bases blunt, and the upper surface is usually nearly hairless while the lower surface has variable hairs. Side leaflets are oblique. Triangular stipules are about 3 mm long, and the slender petioles are 4–6 cm. Flower clusters are slender and can reach about 70 cm, with flowers along roughly half their length; individual flower stalks are 5–15 mm. Flowers are about 2.5 cm long. The calyx tube is about 8 mm, with its upper lobes fused; the inner surface is velvety. Each flower has ten stamens, and the ovary contains about eleven ovules. The flat, oblong pod reaches about 11 × 2 × 0.4 cm and splits open; it carries stiff rust-coloured hairs and narrow wings along its upper seam. A pod contains 7–12 flat, hard, reddish-brown seeds, often mottled, about 10 × 6 × 2 mm. This historical regional account gives no sampling frame or observation date and is not a current or global description.

## A historical range statement {#s-c8abc4efda}
The same Flora of Panama source describes the species as widespread in tropical South America, extending through Central America into Mexico. This is a historical regional statement, not a current range or complete global inventory; the source does not classify the records as native, introduced, cultivated, escaped or wild.
