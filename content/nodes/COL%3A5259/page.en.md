## Comparative shape results span many species {#s-9e266d5c0b}
Across the study’s full comparative sample of 83 species, a Mantel test comparing larval and adult shape-distance patterns found a weak but statistically significant association (r = 0.16, p = 0.003); shape-disparity patterns also differed among families. The authors interpret this as adaptive decoupling, but it does not demonstrate complete genetic independence or a causal developmental mechanism. Phylogenetic analyses retained 74 of the 83 sampled species. Reconstructions along PC1 (the first principal component) and geological dates are model estimates, not newly observed fossils. The study also reports a lower adult K statistic (p = 0.001), while family sample sizes and statistics remain inconsistent in the source. These broad comparisons do not establish size or convergence as universal diagnostic traits of Hylorina sylvatica.
