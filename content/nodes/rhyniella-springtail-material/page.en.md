## Rhyniella specimens and the springtail furcula {#s-0d3a142b4a}
Four head capsules and about ten later Rhyniella body specimens from the Rhynie chert include a reported intact abdomen and a furcula resembling the springtail jumping organ. The sample supports collembolan affinity, not a global origin date or ecological portrait.

## More than isolated head capsules {#s-1453e0b2e9}
The early material consists of four head capsules. Roughly ten additional body specimens were described later, including the first reported intact abdomen and a furcula—the forked springtail structure used in jumping—comparable to that of living Collembola.

## A fossil sample with a limited reach {#s-34a472caa5}
The body and furcula support placing Rhyniella among springtails, or Collembola. This is a claim about the sampled Rhynie-chert fossils. It does not establish when Hexapoda first became terrestrial worldwide, where the group originated, or how ecologically dominant it was.
