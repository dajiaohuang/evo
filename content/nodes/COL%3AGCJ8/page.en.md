## Sample behind the measurements {#s-0ba4d356a7}
COL26.8 places *Ardea purpurea* in Ardeidae. The AVONET v7 record uses its HBW–BirdLife 2020 name and lists nine measured individuals; four have a complete morphological set. Sample sizes can vary by trait, so the measurements below are averages from the compilation, not a description of every bird.

## Beak measurements {#s-bc015e27c1}
Mean beak length from the front edge of the nostril to the tip is 102.5 mm. At that landmark, mean width is 13.6 mm and depth is 19.6 mm. These dimensions use standardized points and should not be treated as field-identification thresholds.

## Wing and lower-leg measurements {#s-6e0c2d66fb}
Mean unflattened wing length, from the carpal joint to the longest primary feather, is 349.7 mm; mean tarsus length, the lower-leg segment measured here, is 117.5 mm. AVONET gives a separate compiled mean mass of 1,064.48 g. That value does not inherit the nine-bird morphological sample, and it does not imply that every measured bird was weighed.

## Wetland feeding categories {#s-6535b0d4b7}
The dataset classifies the habitat as wetland, the main feeding niche as aquatic predator, and the primary lifestyle as terrestrial. These are broad comparison categories from the source. They do not imply exclusive use of wetlands or give a complete account of local feeding behaviour.

## Migration and mapped latitude {#s-4be17e3d02}
The migration field is classified as migratory. Historical breeding and resident maps span latitude endpoints from −34.84° to 53.18°. These mapped limits do not trace the full non-breeding movements or establish current occupancy. The geography and morphology come from separate compilation steps.

## Linking the life-history record {#s-407c2dbd50}
A separate 2015 life-history database contains a historical *A. purpurea* record. Seven selected fields have cited species-level rows carrying the same name and family. This supports linking the records, while leaving open the possibility that the older compilation inherited data among historical species or subspecies.

## Mass at two life stages {#s-3b7f239b43}
The life-history summary gives adult mass as 903.88 g and mass at birth or hatching as 35 g. Adult values may combine males, females and birds of unspecified sex; the smaller value concerns a different life stage. The summaries are not repeated measurements of the same birds and do not describe the full range of individual masses.

## Timing of reproduction and development {#s-26a02a19a6}
The database reports female age at first conception as 546.73 days, offspring per litter or eggs per clutch as 4.2, time from laying to hatching as 26 days, and age at flight capability as 47.5 days. Some source records define the last event as leaving the nest. These are compiled medians, or means of the middle pair for an even number of records; the decimal clutch value is a summary, not a fractional brood. The time fields describe distinct events rather than a single reproductive cycle.

## Reading the longevity value {#s-2765d96b99}
The maximum-longevity field is 25.42 years, the largest value found in the collected life-history material under its method. It is not a typical lifespan, a mean wild lifespan or a lifespan ceiling for every individual. The compilation does not consistently identify wild versus captive conditions.

## References behind the life-history fields {#s-e102c7e141}
The source labels attached to life-history rows include Dunning (1992), Szekely, Lislevand and Figuerola (2007), de Magalhaes and Costa (2009), and Bennett (1986). The archive retains the cited row and value for each field. A row may point to a paper, book or database, and several rows or repeated citations do not represent the same number of measured birds or independent experiments.

## Food and feeding position {#s-076fdd71ad}
EltonTraits 1.0 has an older same-name, same-family record for *A. purpurea*. Its taxonomy predates COL26.8, so the name match provides a comparison link rather than a resolution of later splits or synonymy.

## Relative food weights {#s-608efcebc1}
The food record assigns relative weights of 30% to invertebrates, 30% to reptiles and amphibians, 20% to mammals and birds, and 20% to fish. EltonTraits code A signals high compiler confidence in the source and diet information. The percentages standardize verbal descriptions; they are not measured intake, and an unlisted category would not prove that the bird never eats it.

## Use of the water surface and ground {#s-033caeb87b}
The source marks its feeding-position estimate as species-level: 70% on or just below the water surface and 30% on the ground. These weights describe relative use of feeding positions, not nest sites, all habitat or a full-day time budget. They are not elevation bands and cannot establish flight height or habitat area.

## The cited source for these estimates {#s-0f18ebad71}
The food and feeding-position fields are linked to volume 1 of *Handbook of the Birds of the World*. That book has not been independently checked for this account; the percentages should be read as EltonTraits’ standardized literature summaries, not as new observations.

## Limits of these historical data {#s-e600db2594}
These pages summarize historical datasets rather than a current species monograph. AVONET’s morphological averages come from a small sample with trait-specific counts; the separate mass value and the historical breeding/resident map limits have different sources and scopes. The 2015 life-history database reports medians or means of the middle pair, except that longevity is the largest collected record. It may inherit values across historical species and subspecies, and wild or captive conditions are not consistently recorded. EltonTraits converts literature descriptions into relative food and feeding-position weights; the underlying works were not individually reread here. Matching names do not prove that these taxonomic concepts are identical to COL26.8. Present-day movements, population, conservation and local variation need other evidence.
