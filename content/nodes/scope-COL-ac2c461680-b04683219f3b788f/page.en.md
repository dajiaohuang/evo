## Taxonomic record and scope {#s-afffac0ac9}
COL26.8 usage 4C92G is the accepted species record. The biological examples here come from separate studies; habitat-use evidence is bounded to one wild community at Bossou.

## Feeding evidence {#s-176f7d3d16}
The Bossou study records feeding locations, not a species-wide diet.

## Habitat {#s-522f568d8b}
Mature forest was the most selected habitat overall in the sampled Bossou community. This is a site-level result.

## Movement {#s-d67fd69739}
The selected habitat-use study did not assess locomotion.

## Body size {#s-f2f374b58e}
The selected habitat-use study did not assess body size.

## Ecological role {#s-e9cf9decb5}
A one-site habitat-use study does not establish a species-wide ecological guild.

## Selected evidence {#s-0730bdccf6}
Researchers followed one wild community at Bossou daily for a year, including 10 adult focal individuals. These observations do not establish species-wide habitat use or a current range-wide synthesis.

## Different studies answer different questions {#s-c90f785a3d}
Female life-history records at Gombe, feeding observations at Bossou and body-mass comparisons across care settings examine different aspects of chimpanzee lives. Each has its own population and sample; together they do not define one fixed pattern for every chimpanzee.

## A range account with a date {#s-b32771f84c}
The 2016 IUCN assessment described a discontinuous range of more than 2.6 million km², from southern Senegal across the forest belt north of the Congo River to western Tanzania and western Uganda. It described four subspecies ranges. This dated account is not a current range map.

## What one forest-edge study found {#s-ed80714dc8}
At Bossou, Guinea, researchers followed one wild western chimpanzee community for up to six hours a day from April 2012 to March 2013; the focal sample included 10 adults. Mature forest was the most selected habitat overall. Among feeding events in uncultivated habitat, records more than 200 m from fields exceeded chance expectations, while the 0–200 m bands fell below them. The authors suggested field-associated risks may shape feeding locations. This is a local result, not a species-wide habitat rule.

## Stick use in one foraging task {#s-3ccf926c57}
At Taï National Park in Côte d’Ivoire, a cross-sectional video study recorded 1,460 stick-use events from 70 wild western chimpanzees in three neighboring communities. In a model of grips used to insert sticks, the multi-digit grip became predominant at about 5.2 years, with a 98% predicted probability of use by age 15. Only three females from one community contributed observations above 45 years. This pattern describes the task and sample studied; it is not a universal developmental curve or proof of a cognitive cause.

## Maturity and first births at Gombe {#s-6d98c0bebc}
Long-term records at Gombe National Park in western Tanzania covered 36 known-age wild females. Their mean age at sexual maturity was 11.5 years and mean age at first birth was 14.9 years. First-birth estimates came from a smaller, censored subset in which non-dispersing females were overrepresented, so they may be biased low. These are estimates for one population, not fixed values for every chimpanzee.

## Body mass across care settings {#s-2ad03ac32f}
A study compared 298 chimpanzees in African sanctuaries, 1,030 in zoos and 442 in research facilities. After age adjustment, adults in zoos and research facilities had greater body mass than sanctuary animals in both sexes; estimated growth was slower in the sanctuary cohort. The groups included mixed or unknown subspecies and different care settings, so the comparison cannot stand in for wild morphology or a subspecies comparison.

## A model of population history {#s-908246ed65}
A whole-genome site-pattern study sampled four chimpanzee lineages—central, eastern, Nigeria–Cameroon and western—alongside bonobos. Its best-fitting model estimated gene flow from western into eastern chimpanzees and placed the shared ancestor of the four sampled chimpanzee lineages at about 987,000 years ago. These are model-based population-history estimates; the authors discuss competing histories, and the estimates are not fossil dates.

## A regional conservation estimate {#s-ddcf30b485}
A spatial model estimated 128,760 weaned central chimpanzees in Western Equatorial Africa in 2013 (95% CI 114,208–317,039); it estimated that 80.7% of this subspecies in the modeled region was outside the protected-area network. The result concerns one subspecies and a historical model period. The cited IUCN assessment is version 2016-2, with errata published in 2018; its Endangered category is not presented here as a verified current status.

## Evidence boundaries {#s-16f42060e6}
These are selected studies, not a complete species review. They do not provide a current range or conservation assessment, or a broad synthesis of wild diet, locomotion and morphology.
