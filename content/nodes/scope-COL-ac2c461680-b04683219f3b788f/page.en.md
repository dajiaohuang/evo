## Chimpanzee evidence at distinct scales {#s-afffac0ac9}
COL26.8 usage 4C92G is the accepted species record. Selected evidence ranges from one Bossou wild community and task-specific stick use to Gombe life-history records, care-setting comparisons and population-genomic models. The results do not form one species-wide pattern.

## Feeding evidence {#s-176f7d3d16}
The Bossou study records feeding locations, not a species-wide diet.

## Habitat {#s-522f568d8b}
Mature forest was the most selected habitat overall in the sampled Bossou community. This is a site-level result.

## Movement {#s-d67fd69739}
The selected habitat-use study did not assess locomotion.

## Body size {#s-f2f374b58e}
The selected habitat-use study did not assess body size.

## Ecological role {#s-e9cf9decb5}
A one-site habitat-use study does not establish a species-wide ecological guild.

## A year of observation at one Bossou community {#s-0730bdccf6}
Researchers followed one wild community at Bossou daily for a year, including 10 adult focal individuals. These observations address local habitat and feeding use; they do not establish species-wide habitat use or a current range-wide synthesis.

## Different samples answer different questions {#s-c90f785a3d}
Gombe female life-history records, Bossou habitat and feeding observations, Taï stick-use videos, care-setting body-mass comparisons and genomic models each address different questions. Their populations and samples should remain separate.

## A dated account of chimpanzee range {#s-b32771f84c}
The 2016 IUCN assessment described a discontinuous range exceeding 2.6 million km², from southern Senegal across the forest belt north of the Congo River to western Tanzania and western Uganda, with four subspecies ranges. This is a dated account, not a current range map.

## Bossou feeding locations near fields {#s-ed80714dc8}
At Bossou, Guinea, researchers followed one wild western chimpanzee community for up to six hours daily from April 2012 to March 2013; the focal sample included 10 adults. Mature forest was most selected overall. In uncultivated habitat, feeding records more than 200 m from fields exceeded chance expectations, while records in 0–200 m bands fell below them. This is a local result, not a species-wide habitat rule.

## Stick grips in one Taï foraging task {#s-3ccf926c57}
A video study at Taï recorded 1,460 stick-use events from 70 wild western chimpanzees across three neighboring communities. In the model for grips used to insert sticks, the multi-digit grip became predominant at about 5.2 years, with a 98% predicted probability by age 15. Only three females from one community contributed observations above 45 years. This is task- and sample-specific, not a universal developmental curve.

## Gombe estimates of maturity and first birth {#s-6d98c0bebc}
Long-term records at Gombe covered 36 known-age wild females. Mean age at sexual maturity was 11.5 years and mean age at first birth was 14.9 years. First-birth estimates came from a smaller, censored subset overrepresenting non-dispersing females and may be biased low. These are estimates for one population.

## Body mass across three care settings {#s-2ad03ac32f}
A study compared 298 chimpanzees in African sanctuaries, 1,030 in zoos and 442 in research facilities. After age adjustment, adults in zoos and research facilities had greater body mass than sanctuary animals in both sexes; estimated growth was slower in the sanctuary cohort. Mixed or unknown subspecies and care settings limit interpretation.

## A model of chimpanzee population history {#s-908246ed65}
A whole-genome site-pattern study sampled central, eastern, Nigeria–Cameroon and western chimpanzee lineages alongside bonobos. Its best-fitting model estimated gene flow from western into eastern chimpanzees and placed the shared ancestor of the four sampled chimpanzee lineages at about 987,000 years ago. These are model-based estimates, not fossil dates.

## A 2013 regional estimate for central chimpanzees {#s-ddcf30b485}
A spatial model estimated 128,760 weaned central chimpanzees in Western Equatorial Africa in 2013 (95% CI 114,208–317,039), with 80.7% of this subspecies in the modeled region outside the protected-area network. This is a historical regional model. The cited IUCN assessment is version 2016-2, with 2018 errata; its category is not stated as verified current status.

## Limits of the selected chimpanzee studies {#s-16f42060e6}
These examples do not provide a current range or conservation assessment, or a broad synthesis of wild diet, locomotion and morphology. Each result remains tied to its study population, task, model and period.
