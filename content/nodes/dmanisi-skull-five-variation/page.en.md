## Dmanisi Skull 5 and early Homo variation {#s-ac7777c306}
Cranium D4500 and mandible D2600 belong to one adult from the roughly 1.8-million-year-old Dmanisi sample. CT imaging estimates its endocranial volume at 546 ± 5 cm³; the specimen adds to observed variation but does not by itself merge named Homo taxa into one lineage.

## Two pieces, one associated skull {#s-bd53becd23}
The study treats cranium D4500 and mandible D2600 as Skull 5, an unusually complete adult specimen from Layer B1y, Block 2 at Dmanisi. CT data give an endocranial-volume estimate of 546 ± 5 cm³. Figure 2 compares it with the other four Dmanisi crania, placing the specimen within a real but small local sample.

## Variation does not settle species boundaries {#s-ea7fd62ca5}
Skull 5 expands the craniofacial variation documented among the Dmanisi individuals. The authors interpret that sample as compatible with a single evolving early-Homo lineage, but this is a taxonomic reading of a small fossil sample. One skull cannot establish a universally accepted ancestry claim or erase every distinction among named early Homo species.
