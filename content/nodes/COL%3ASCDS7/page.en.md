## Accepted name and placement {#s-34f96da219}
COL26.8 accepts Pristimantis giorgii Oliveira, Alves da Silva, Guimarães, Penhacek, Martínez, Rodrigues, Santana & Hernández-Ruz, 2020 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Strabomantidae (family) → Pristimantis (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## Diagnostic characters retain individual variation {#s-2b3d0cf650}
The 2020 description associated giorgii with a shagreen dorsum, basal toe webbing and digital fringes, a discoidal fold, and a belly usually smooth centrally but granular laterally. The variation account also recorded completely smooth bellies in named vouchers and absent dorsal tubercles in BLM 1089. These exceptions prevent treating every listed diagnostic state as invariant. Lower-Tocantins individuals were described as strongly barred, while middle-Xingu material had fewer bars. The reported female and male size averages were very close, so the wording that females are larger is not proof of nonoverlapping sex ranges or a sufficient sex-identification rule.

## Forest fragments and seasonal calling positions {#s-cd5709d08e}
The account recorded giorgii in Pará at Altamira, Portel, Marabá and Melgaço, in preserved forest and some pasture-surrounded fragments. During the rainy season males were described calling from vegetation at about two metres height; in the dry season frogs were found in leaf litter. These are dated locality and seasonal observations, not a complete current range, a measured optimum calling height or proof that disturbed fragments provide equivalent survival to intact forest. Calling activity does not independently document egg substrates, clutch size or the full reproductive calendar.

## Three to four notes in the recorded advertisement calls {#s-73109752e4}
The study’s giorgii advertisement calls consisted of three to four pulsed notes, with recordings from seven Portel males. Calls were analysed at 44.1 kHz and 16-bit resolution, and archived as MAP-V 306–312. The note-count range overlaps with the other described species’ ranges, including pictus and moa, so it is not a stand-alone identification criterion or proof of mate recognition. The short duration range in the diagnosis is not relabelled here as whole-call duration without the corresponding acoustic definitions. Recordings from a few males do not establish every population’s call repertoire.

## Sympatry and mitochondrial evidence have separate implications {#s-1de21b5fc5}
The authors reported giorgii and latro in sympatry and described giorgii’s mitochondrial relationship with the other Tocantins-basin new lineage, moa. Their delimitation combined morphology, calls and 16S/COI evidence. Lack of shared sampled mitochondrial haplotypes and differences between calls support the study’s taxonomic argument, but do not directly measure hybridization, nuclear gene flow or mating discrimination. Proposed secondary contact and acoustic radiation remain historical explanations, rather than observed dispersal routes or experimentally demonstrated reproductive barriers.
