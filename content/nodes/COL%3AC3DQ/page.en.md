## Aloe arenicola: spotted leaves and coastal growth {#s-d35e6f3bdb}
A South African regional account describes a succulent shrublet with creeping stems up to 1 m long, simple or branched, and persistent dry leaves. The blue-green leaves have many irregularly scattered, oblong white spots on both surfaces. They are lanceolate and taper toward the tip, up to 20 cm long, with whitish margins and tiny teeth; the spots can be faint. Dense racemes form head-like or nearly corymb-shaped clusters, usually simple and occasionally branched, up to 50 cm long. The flowers are red, paler toward the opening, about 40 mm long, with stamens and stigma projecting beyond the flower.

A comparative account says *Aloe arenicola* has leaves similar in size to those of *A. meyeri* but differs in several other characters. It describes *A. arenicola* as lying along coastal sand, with leaves covering its elongated stem; *A. meyeri* is described as hanging from inland rocks and bearing leaves in a distinct rosette. The inflorescences of *A. arenicola* are longer, with larger flowers on longer pedicels. The source says differences from *A. perfoliata* are discussed under that species but gives no details here.

A second comparison says *A. mitriformis* and *A. distans* have sprawling stems with leaves along 1 m or more, whereas *A. arenicola* has leaves only on the terminal 20 cm. Its stems lie along the ground, with the leafy part rising to an ascending or nearly erect position, whether supported by bushes or growing exposed. The account describes its leaf and rosette characters as fairly constant, while flower shape and size vary considerably.
