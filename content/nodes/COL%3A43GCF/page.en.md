## Stem surface {#s-640efaabfa}
The Brazilian Flora 2020 record preserves several alternatives rather than selecting one condition. The stem is described as hairless, pubescent, or hairy; hair direction is unreported or described as spreading, curving inward, or bending back. Simple hairs and capitate glandular hairs, whose tips form small heads, are listed as present. Branched and unbranched bristles are absent; prickles are absent or sparse and backward-pointing; short lateral shoots (brachyblasts) are absent. The field does not show whether the alternatives reflect variation or combined reports.

## Leaves and inflorescences {#s-7b4c324631}
Stipules, small structures at leaf bases, are listed as either caducous (falling early) or persistent. Extrafloral nectaries (nectar-secreting glands outside flowers) are absent; projections between pairs of pinnae (the leaf’s primary side divisions) may be absent or present. The inflorescence, or flower cluster, is globose, and its floral bracts (small leaf-like structures) are shorter than the flowers before they open (anthesis). The record does not assign the alternative states to particular specimens.

## Flowers {#s-0fb7fa23cd}
The perianth (calyx and corolla together) is recorded as having four or five parts. The calyx, the outer sepal whorl, is membranous; its lobes are listed as absent, triangular, or lance-shaped. Corolla (petal-whorl) lobes have one to three veins and are described as hairless or pubescent, with short soft hairs. The androecium (the stamens collectively) is diplostemonous, with two stamen whorls; the filaments are pink.
