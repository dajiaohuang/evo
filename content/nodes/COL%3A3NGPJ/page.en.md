## Seven reported Ecuadorian localities {#s-7f6d95bfa9}
The 2025 account reported seven localities in northern to central eastern Ecuador, at 389–1391 m elevation. These records belong to the taxonomic concept used in that account and describe observed geography rather than complete occupancy or physiological altitude limits.

## Calling sites and resting sites {#s-c333466ae8}
The account describes nocturnal frogs associated with streams and ravines. Males called beneath rocks or in sheltered sites such as crevices, caves and leaf litter. Some individuals perched on vegetation up to 2.5 m high without being observed calling there. Perching and calling observations therefore have separate scopes in this study.

## An absent reference sequence limits molecular identification {#s-a827bc4c6e}
The authors included Hyloscirtus albopunctulatus in the 62-taxon list considered potentially detectable in the Tena microbasin, but could not compile its 16S reference sequence for their study database. References were available for 59 of those source taxa. This is a documented reference gap at the time of database construction, not a new field detection, proof of local absence or a claim that no sequence exists today. It limits interpretation of an unassigned water signal; a short sequence-match workflow cannot confirm a taxon missing from its references.
