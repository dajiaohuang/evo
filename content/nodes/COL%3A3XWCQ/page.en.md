## A distinct lateral stripe accompanies overlapping adult sizes {#s-bf8bc75ba9}
Adult males are reported at 33.3–39 mm and females at 38.2–42 mm snout–vent length. The diagnosis emphasizes a sharply defined white lateral stripe extending from the snout along the head and body to the inguinal region, and larger size than ambony. Male and female ranges overlap; size does not supply a strict sex cutoff. The stripe is used within the revision’s comparison of species, not as evidence that every similarly striped Madagascan frog belongs to ambreensis.

## Streamside perches differ between day and night encounters {#s-39fcc382b1}
The semi-aquatic forest account associates ambreensis with flowing streams or rivers and rocks, with encounters from 09:00–23:00 and mainly nocturnal activity. Females at night were on leaves rather than branches; daytime records sometimes were on river banks and rarely on ground. Males were on banks by day and leaves or occasionally rocks at night. Reported male heights are 10–200 cm and female heights 100–200 cm. A river association and irrigation-canal occurrence at Station les Roussettes are observations, not experimental preferences or demonstrated dependence on artificial channels.

## One rock-edge clutch and a cited March call record are distinct evidence {#s-d1fed87d36}
At Vohibola, Tsaratanana, an April 2006 clutch of about 100–120 eggs was found on a rock edge beside calm shallow water. Egg diameter is given approximately as 2–3 mm, and eggs hatched after a couple of days in a plastic bag. The holding context means this is not a controlled natural incubation-duration estimate or a universal clutch range. The same paragraph cites an earlier March afternoon record of calling males on the ground by a forest brook. A call occurrence gives no measured duration or frequency and does not extend this single clutch observation to all months or populations.

## The revision extends the concept beyond Montagne d’Ambre {#s-646e93bb8c}
The distribution account gives northwestern and northern Madagascan forests at 200–1,150 m, including Montagne d’Ambre, Manongarivo, Tsaratanana, Andramanalana and Sorata, extending to Irony River and Ambohibola Forest at the northern High Plateau limit. This is a source record summary, not full survey coverage or a proof of continuous occupancy. These ambreensis records are not interchangeable with morphologically attributed ambony records, despite similar stripes and overlapping regions.
