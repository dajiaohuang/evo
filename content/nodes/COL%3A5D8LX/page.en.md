## A 2015 compilation linked by name {#s-080a70ddf2}
COL26.8 accepts Zosterops capensis in Zosteropidae. The August 2015 life-history archive has a same-name entry with cited species-level rows for six fields. That supports reading the values together, while historical taxonomic inheritance may remain in the summaries.

## Adult mass in the compilation {#s-8a1aafbf76}
The reported adult body-mass summary is 11.08 g. The source may combine males, females and adults whose sex was not recorded, so this is not a range of individual masses. No birth or hatching mass is included in this entry.

## Reproduction and development use different clocks {#s-b64bc145fb}
The archive reports female maturity at 196.95 d, defined by the source as age at first conception; 2.8 offspring per litter or eggs per clutch; 11 d from laying to hatching; and 12.5 d to flight capability, which some source material records as leaving the nest. Here d means days. The litter/clutch summary combines counts of young or eggs, so 2.8 is not a fractional animal in one event. These timings describe different milestones, not one reproductive cycle.

## Ten years is the longest collected record {#s-7997861e00}
The archive reports 10 years for maximum longevity, using the largest record found under its method. This is neither typical lifespan nor mean wild lifespan, and it is not a survival ceiling for every individual or a claim that the record can be repeated today.

## Following the field-specific citations {#s-239fb03e95}
The same-name rows cite Dunning, 1992; Szekely, Lislevand, and Figuerola, 2007; BC Birds - Meyers et al., 2009; Bennett, 1986; and Jetz, Sekercioglu, and Bohning-Gaese, 2008. The archive preserves each field’s source rows, values and labels alongside the reference explanations. A label may point to a paper, book or database. Row counts are not counts of measured birds, and repeated citations are not independent replication.

## How to read these life-history values {#s-8425c0f74a}
The August 2015 archive reports compilation medians (or the mean of the two middle values); maximum longevity instead uses the largest collected record. These summaries are not individual measurements, variation ranges, wild-population averages or current surveys. Historical species and subspecies records can share inherited data, and a matching name, family and source row do not prove identity with the COL26.8 concept or remove every inherited component. Wild versus captive conditions are not consistently established, and the underlying publications have not been individually reviewed. Diet, behaviour, current distribution, conservation and other unlisted fields require separate sources.
