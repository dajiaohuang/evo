## What flower-stalk traits are recorded for this shrublet? {#s-39310efc7b}
A regional account describes a shrublet up to 30 cm tall. Its flower stalk is about 4 mm long, usually shorter than the petiole, and usually carries one bright yellow flower. The epicalyx bracts are linear and awl-shaped.

## How does the revision distinguish it from Hermannia rehmannii? {#s-e510438f28}
A 1981 revision explains that the two species can look alike, and that one specimen used in an older description of M. griquensis was a mixed collection: four parts had the linear, awl-shaped epicalyx bracts of M. virescens, while a fifth had the ovate bracts of H. rehmannii. Their reported ranges overlap in the northern Cape and parts of the Transvaal. In M. virescens the flower stalk is short, usually shorter than the petiole, and upright; any individual flower stalks are also short and upright. In H. rehmannii these stalks are longer, often as long as or longer than the petiole, and the flower stalks are longer, thinner and drooping. The revision reports that M. virescens extends farther west into what it calls South West Africa. Collector notes described it as restricted to lime-rich soil, while H. rehmannii was less restricted and more widespread. The revision reassigned other specimens added to the old M. griquensis description to H. rehmannii.

## What soil is reported? {#s-837410baa8}
The regional flora record lists lime-rich soil. It gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency.
