## Which trait records are available for the genus? {#s-65ec88bef0}
Pinned COL26.8 places Homopus in Testudinidae. The ReptTraits v1-2 material linked here contains two species records with trait-specific citations: Homopus areolatus and Homopus femoralis. The workbook retains its 2023 name framework. These records offer a reading route into the genus, not a complete list of its accepted species.

## How should the two shell-length records be compared? {#s-55e743fefb}
Among these size records, Homopus femoralis has a reported maximum straight carapace length (SCL) of 100 mm and Homopus areolatus 110 mm. SCL is measured in a straight line along the carapace. The values are compiled maxima for different species, not a within-species range or the actual extremes for the whole genus. Comparing turtle SCL directly with snout–vent length in another group would mix different measurements.

## What do the life-history records cover? {#s-0c3ccaf417}
The available life-history route has 1 historical record, for Homopus areolatus. Each of these fields appears once: adult body mass, offspring per litter or eggs per clutch, time from egg laying to hatching, and the compilation's longest-lifespan record. These counts show which source fields are represented, not how many species the genus contains. Before comparing values, check each field's event, unit and aggregation method.

## What these records can and cannot show {#s-fce2eea0f1}
The two routes use different inclusion rules, and neither is a complete Homopus species list. The ReptTraits route includes entries with at least four sourced focal traits plus a size or reproductive value, matched by name and family in COL; the life-history route includes historical same-name, same-family entries with at least three sourced focal fields. Compiled maxima are not averages or identification thresholds. Medians, longest-lifespan records and historical taxonomic inheritance retain the definitions of their source fields; they do not settle later taxonomic revisions or population differences. A genus-level route does not imply that every member shares one reproductive mode or life history. These records do not provide current conservation or population status, so none is inferred here.
