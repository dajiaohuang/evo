## Recorded classification and direct parent {#s-574517cd65}
COL26.8 records Mirza Gray, 1870 at genus rank, with Cheirogaleidae Gray, 1873 as its direct parent.

COL26.8 recognizes Mirza as a genus with two linked species pages here: *M. coquereli* and *M. zaza*. The studies summarized across those pages use different places and methods, so a result for one species or site should not be read as a trait of the whole genus.

## Environmental associations in a genus-level analysis {#s-2b2a4fccbd}
A 2021 literature-based analysis modeled Mirza density across Madagascar. It reported positive associations with temperature and precipitation, and no reported association with the measured vegetation-quality, human-footprint or elevation variables. These are model associations at genus level, not causal effects or species-specific results. The linked species studies address separate questions: population genetics and a Zombitse canopy survey for *M. coquereli*, and nest sharing and local density in Sahamalaza for *M. zaza*.

## Read life-history values with their event and unit {#s-91179029db}
One historical life-history entry is linked to *M. coquereli*. It provides one record each for adult body mass, female age at first conception, offspring per litter or eggs per clutch, and the interval from conception or fertilization to birth. These are field counts in this batch, not a denominator for every accepted Mirza species. Before comparing values, check what event the field measures, its unit and its source.

## Food, foraging positions and activity are compiled categories {#s-c71fdbec7b}
The same historical *M. coquereli* entry has food-category weights, a foraging-stratum record and mammalian activity-period fields. The weights summarize descriptions, while the other fields use categories or binary codes. Their meanings and source limits differ, so these entries do not establish a single ecology shared by Mirza. Counts describe the data in this batch only.

## What these records can establish {#s-8bc6336240}
This guide brings together a genus-level literature model, local species studies and historical trait compilations. Each has a different scale and evidence type: the model reports associations, local studies concern their sampled places or animals, and compiled fields retain the definitions and taxonomic limits of their sources. The linked pages do not form a complete natural-history account of Mirza. A shared genus name or a historical name match also does not establish that every record represents the same present-day species concept.
