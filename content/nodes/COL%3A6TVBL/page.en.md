## Form and flowers {#s-d29128c0b5}
The regional flora describes a corm-bearing geophyte up to 15 cm tall, with a small, non-fibrous corm. It has 2–4 thin-textured, strap-shaped leaves that are curved and keeled, and 2 star-shaped yellow flowers with green undersides. The cylindrical ovary is at least twice as long as the tepals, triangular in cross-section, and single-chambered. Two broad bracts spread beside the flowers, and the capsule splits open lengthwise.

These traits come from a regional South African account published in 2012; the excerpt gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency.

## Diagnostic features and comparisons {#s-1d43e15b7a}
A 2014 taxonomic revision highlights the strongly compressed, two-edged flower stalk and the narrow, elongated, sharply three-cornered ovary, whose staminal filaments are joined to the style. The ovary has one chamber throughout, with ovules attached to its inner wall, and the mature fruit reaches 30 mm, longer than in other *Pauridia* species. Unlike the capsule opening around its middle, usual in the genus, this species' capsule splits along the partitions, with long slits running down the narrow fruit. When flowers and fruits are absent, curved pale-green leaves, up to five, can help identify the plant; they are unusually brittle to the touch.

The revision notes that *P. pusilla*, from the Gifberg and Matsikamma Mountains north of Clanwilliam, is the only other species with all six staminal filaments joined to the style. It is smaller in every feature and has a maroon stigma rather than a plain yellow one. *P. alba*, *P. aquatica*, *P. nana*, and *P. umbraticola* also have elongated, narrow ovaries up to five times or more longer than wide, but they are mostly three-chambered. In *P. alba* and *P. aquatica*, the partitions may be incompletely fused at the tip, more noticeably after flowering. The revision considers *P. scullyi* from Namaqualand a probable close relative, sharing the single-chambered ovary, thin leaves, and broad flower-cluster bracts. *P. scullyi* differs in having 2 to as many as 4 or 5 flowers per cluster, a shorter ovary (3.5–10 mm), and a capsule that opens around its middle.

These diagnostic comparisons come from a 2014 taxonomic revision of *Pauridia* in southern Africa; the excerpt gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency.
