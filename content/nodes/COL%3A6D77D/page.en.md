## Plant and flower description {#s-1ec228292a}
The SANBI account gives plant height as 0.70–1.25 m; stems grow singly or a few together in small tufts. The corm is about 15 mm across, and the lowest peduncle is 45–110 mm long. There are a few foliage leaves, 550–850 × 3–5(–9) mm, and about four reduced sheathing leaves. Spikes are pendulous: the terminal spike bears 6–12 flowers, and there are 2–5 lateral spikes, sometimes compounded, each with 4–10 flowers.

Bracts are elliptic-lanceolate, tapering to a point, and measure 15–18(–23) × 5–8 mm. Their length is reported as less than or up to two internodes. They are membranous, pale reddish brown, and heavily flecked in the lower part and along the midline; the margins and shoulders are white. About five lateral veins occur on each side of the main vein. Flowers are bell-shaped, with tepals upright or scarcely flaring at the tip, creamy white to pale yellow and rarely pale pink. The perianth tube is 6–8 mm long, tepals 9–12 × 4–6(–7) mm, and anthers 5–7 mm. Stigmas are held 3–4(–9) mm below the top of the perianth.

## Diagnostic comparisons {#s-2c88a7b516}
A separate SANBI account describes solitary stems or small tufts with brown-flecked bracts and small to moderately sized, rather closed flowers. They are creamy white to pale yellow, rarely pale pink, with a perianth 16–19(–25) mm long. *Dierama pallidum* from south-central KwaZulu-Natal, between Pietermaritzburg and Durban, is described as having similar flowers but pure-white bracts. *Dierama argyreum* also has white bracts, but its flowers are larger, bell-shaped, white or ivory, with an 18–35 mm perianth and flaring tepals.
