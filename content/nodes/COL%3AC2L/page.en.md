## atlas-profile / overview {#s-83643af121}
What can fossil wings and living genomes each tell us about beetles? Hardened forewings, or elytra, preserve a record of changing form: Zhao and colleagues compare Permian and Triassic elytra across a major vegetation transition. A separate genomic tree samples 373 living beetle species, spanning about 67% of families. The fossil and living studies illuminate different parts of the order’s history; neither supplies a complete account.

## atlas-profile / ecology / diet {#s-6e86e16fdc}
The fossil study discusses plant associations and herbivory in its sampled lineages; it does not assign one diet to all beetles.

## atlas-profile / evidenceSummary {#s-18d0f4366a}
Zhao et al. connect changes in fossil elytral form with end-Permian vegetation turnover; McKenna et al. infer relationships from a broad but incomplete living sample. The two studies do not settle beetle ecology, the full order-wide tree or its complete range.

## atlas-profile / readerLimitations {#s-e8ca08f166}
The fossil study discusses plant associations only in its sampled Permian–Triassic lineages; the living genomic tree is a partial sample. Together, these studies do not define an order-wide ecology, flight performance, or a comparable body-size and distribution record.
