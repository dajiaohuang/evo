## Accepted name and placement {#s-635ad0c558}
COL26.8 accepts Centrolene camposi Cisneros-Heredia, Yánez-Muñoz, Sánchez-Nivicela & Ron, 2023 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Centrolenidae (family) → Centrolene (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## A very small male type sample {#s-72a3c1609e}
The description rests on male holotype DHMECN 11407 and male paratype DHMECN 11408, collected at La Enramada on 31 March 2015. Their snout–vent lengths are 29.1 and 31.2 mm; females are unknown in this account. The diagnosis combines absent dentigerous vomerine processes, a sloping lateral snout, dorsal warts and spicules, a male humeral spine, and enamelled limb folds. These two males do not establish population extrema, female morphology or a complete identification key.

## Living and ethanol colours remain distinct {#s-6ca2c95320}
The live holotype has a bright green back with lighter warts, a thick pale lip stripe, yellow-green flanks and green bones. In ethanol its back is lavender and underside cream. The paratype has lighter dorsal warts and some dark flecks. The iris background is called white in the definition and grey in the live-holotype account; both descriptions are retained rather than certified as one invariant colour.

## Heard calls were not recorded or described {#s-a03c4c51f5}
Both new species vocalized during March 2015 sampling, but no recordings were taken. The diagnoses place calling males on the upper surface of leaves while explicitly leaving advertisement-call properties, fighting, eggs, parental care and tadpoles unknown. Humeral spines and nuptial structures do not fill those missing observations. Neither one month of calling nor the absence of recordings establishes a complete breeding season.

## A single stream site and later nondetections {#s-6bd613279f}
The type locality is La Enramada in Azuay, southwestern Ecuador, with the type account at 2950 m and the forest/fieldwork account at 2900 m. These source statements remain separate, not a certified continuous elevation range. The two species occurred in the same steep creek in montane evergreen forest. Only the March 2015 expedition collected them; April 2019 and December 2022 surveys did not. Those nondetections do not prove extinction, absence in other seasons or a complete range boundary.

## Different relatives despite a shared site {#s-8824478062}
The study recovers camposi sister to condor. Results report 12S distance 1.04% with standard error 0.338%, and ND1 distance 6.1–6.5% with separately reported standard errors; discussion cites average 12S distance 1.08%. Those printed summaries remain distinct. The proposed recent trans-Andean history is an interpretation, not a molecular-clock date or directly measured dispersal event.

## The 2023 Data Deficient proposal {#s-a67bdb5a7a}
The authors propose Data Deficient for both new species after limited records and later survey nondetections. They discuss regional forest loss, degradation and fragmentation as concerns. This is a dated author proposal, not a newly verified official IUCN category or measured decline in these species. Three type specimens across two species do not constitute a population census or sufficient monitoring of trends.
