## Aquatic larvae and metamorphosed offspring vary among studied lineages {#s-d2b013d624}
The paper’s northern Iberian background distinguishes larviparity, birth of aquatic larvae, from pueriparity, birth of metamorphosed young. Bernardezi is described as generally pueriparous; fastuosa mostly larviparous but with pueriparous populations; gallaica and bejarae generally larviparous, with insular pueriparous gallaica exceptions. The Discussion reports mixed-mode females in contact areas, drawing partly on earlier and unpublished observations rather than a new controlled breeding cohort. Striped, round-snouted and smaller versus blotched, pointed-snouted and larger morphotypes overlap in admixed areas. These regional descriptions do not establish one species-wide reproductive mode, a standardized body-size range, gestation duration or litter size.

## Three rainy-night contact-zone samples have different denominators {#s-bae49b2875}
From November 2013 to May 2015, mostly adults and a few larvae were sampled on rainy nights, usually one to three animals per locality, then released. Northern Galicia yielded 129 animals at 86 localities; the Cantabrian Mountains 73 at 38; the Asturias–Cantabria border 123 at 58. After missing-data filtering, paired mitochondrial–nuclear comparisons used 127, 68 and 107 animals respectively, totalling 302. These collection and analytical denominators must remain distinct. The quoted landscape elevation extents describe study-area pixels, not every sampled animal’s elevation or species-wide altitude tolerance. The survey delimits examined contact zones, not the entire Salamandra salamandra range.

## Two-cluster assignment and hybrid classes were model choices {#s-6592cfa5f5}
Mitochondrial cytochrome-b-region sequences were compared with a microsatellite panel. STRUCTURE was deliberately run at K = 2 per contact zone to assign animals between two focal subspecies, not to discover the total number of species-wide clusters. Potential admixture was defined using membership coefficients near 0.1–0.9; NEWHYBRIDS then assigned parental, F1, F2 or backcross categories with posterior probability at least 0.7. These are statistical ancestry and generation-class assignments, not known pedigrees or direct observations of matings. An admixed STRUCTURE label can differ from the subsequent NEWHYBRIDS label.

## Broad admixture in two zones contrasts with the mountain sample {#s-a9ca0406b7}
In northern Galicia, 35 of 127 analysed animals were potential hybrids in STRUCTURE; 29 were assigned F2 by NEWHYBRIDS. At the Asturias–Cantabria border, 50 of 107 were potential hybrids; 38 were assigned F2, while four were subsequently assigned to parental categories. In the Cantabrian sample, only three of 68 initially appeared admixed and all three were then assigned pure bejarae by NEWHYBRIDS. Reported mitochondrial–nuclear discordance was 19%, 16% and 6% respectively. These comparisons support geographically variable introgression, not a uniform hybrid frequency throughout the species or complete reproductive isolation in the mountains. Some individuals remain outside the named high-confidence generation assignments.

## Mitochondrial history is not the nuclear subspecies tree {#s-c2408d7b2f}
Quality filtering and trimming produced 302 mitochondrial sequences of about 350 bases, shorter than the approximately 1,100-base amplified region. The authors explicitly warn that the mitochondrial genealogy does not represent the full subspecies relationships: fastuosa groups differently in earlier nuclear phylogenomic evidence. Clock-based estimates used substitution-rate and external divergence priors; they are conditional lineage dates rather than directly observed species origins. Mitochondrial capture, expansion and sex-biased dispersal are possible explanations of discordance, but competing histories remained unresolved. Neither discordance nor an inferred F2 category determines the exact parental route of a particular animal.

## Landscape resistance is an association at the analysed scale {#s-6f85a20436}
The models compared geographic distance, environmental difference and landscape resistance, using climate summaries, elevation and seasonal vegetation-index information. Geographic isolation was the strongest selected single predictor in Galicia and Asturias–Cantabria. The Cantabrian zone also supported climate and habitat resistance, with some disagreement between two genetic-distance metrics. NDVI is a vegetation/habitat surrogate, not a directly measured prey supply or breeding substrate. The analysis accounted for shared individuals in pairwise distances, but observational model selection does not experimentally establish a barrier mechanism, individual crossing rate or a physiological climate limit. Topographic complexity alone did not explain the mountain pattern.

## Proposed corridors and adaptation require further evidence {#s-31e2476e72}
The Discussion suggests occasional valley crossings in the mountains and semi-permeable river barriers in the eastern contact zone, but says the data cannot robustly compare crossing rates by reproductive mode. Historical land conversion and fragmentation may affect movement; observed admixture does not show that agricultural or urban habitat is harmless. No support for the tested isolation-by-environment scenario does not exclude latent adaptive divergence. Alternative histories of contact-zone age and range expansion remain possible. These findings guide future research without measuring intervention success, current population trend or a newly verified conservation category.
