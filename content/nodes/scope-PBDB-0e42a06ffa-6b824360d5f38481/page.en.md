## What survives in the partial skull {#s-43782c0b36}
Saadanius is represented by the partial adult cranium SGS-UM 2009-002 from the middle Shumaysi Formation of western Saudi Arabia. The face, palate, teeth and ear region survive, while most of the skeleton does not. This Oligocene specimen therefore offers a rare but anatomically limited view of a catarrhine-grade primate from a region with few comparable fossils.

## How the character combination is placed {#s-6b68890916}
The authors compared the cranium with living and fossil catarrhines in a 36-character matrix. Saadanius has catarrhine-associated features, including a tubular ectotympanic, a bone of the ear region, but lacks the diagnostic specializations used for crown apes or Old World monkeys. The analysis places it on the stem side of the catarrhine split; that result depends on the coded characters and comparison set.

## The fossil age does not date the split {#s-3710f6136e}
The paper assigns the fossil an age of roughly 29–28 million years from the formation’s biochronology. That interval helps frame when catarrhine evolution may have been under way, but it is not a direct measurement of the ape–Old World monkey split. Its stem-group placement also does not identify this individual or species as their direct ancestor.

## atlas-profile / readerLimitations {#s-59a06a908d}
The account rests on one partial cranium and one published character analysis. Biochronological age is not a direct date for a divergence, and the proposed stem-catarrhine position does not establish direct ancestry.
