## One cranium, a 36-character analysis and biochronological age {#s-fbd8cb85bc}

## What survives in holotype SGS-UM 2009-002 {#s-43782c0b36}
Saadanius is known from one partial adult cranium from the middle Shumaysi Formation of western Saudi Arabia. The face, palate, teeth and ear region survive, while most of the skeleton is unknown. This Oligocene fossil offers a limited cranial sample from a region with few comparable finds.

## A stem-catarrhine result from coded characters {#s-6b68890916}
The authors compared the cranium with living and fossil catarrhines using a 36-character matrix of 19 taxa. Saadanius has catarrhine-associated features, including a tubular ectotympanic, but lacks specializations diagnostic of crown apes or Old World monkeys. Its stem placement depends on the coded characters and comparison set.

## Biochronology does not measure the split {#s-3710f6136e}
The paper assigns the fossil an age of roughly 29–28 Ma (about 29–28 million years ago) from formation biochronology. This frames a possible interval for catarrhine evolution; it is not a direct measurement of the ape–Old World monkey divergence. Stem placement does not identify Saadanius as a direct ancestor.

## Limits of one partial cranium {#s-59a06a908d}
The account rests on one partial cranium and one published character analysis. Biochronological age is not a direct divergence date, and the proposed stem-catarrhine position does not establish direct ancestry.
