Archived PBDB field scm (source-language text; not translated or interpreted):
The Dolnice locality belongs to the upper part of the so called "coal seam beds" and is located immediately above the main lignite seam with its grey and brown mudstones (so called "Kapuzin-Tone"). The "coal seam beds" are overlain by the muddy to clayey Cypris beds which are characterized by ostracode bearing freshwater limestone lenses. The upper "coal seam beds" are subdivided into 3 fossiliferous horizons, named Dolnice 1-3 of which Dolnice 1 is the stratigraphically deepest (Fejfar, 1974).

Archived PBDB field ggc (source-language text; not translated or interpreted):
The name Dolnice (old German name: Dölitz or Delitz) refers to a homestead immediately north of the city of Cheb, western Czech Republic. The locality actually comprises several sublocalities (see Fejfar, 1974, Palaeontographica A 146) but in the paleontological literature there is almost always (if at all) stratigraphical differentiation only. The locality name "Dolnice 1" as used in Fejfar et al. (1998) apparently refers to the stratigraphic level "Dolnice 1" rather than to a sublocality within the Dolnice area.
Coordinates, formerly based on the town of Cheb (50°4'47"N, 12°22'1"E), have been refined to the position of the Dolnice homestead(s).
