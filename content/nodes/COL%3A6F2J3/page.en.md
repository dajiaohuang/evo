## Which parts around its flower cluster turn red? {#s-fbae7e3d59}
A 2012 regional account describes *Elegia fucata* as a neatly tufted, dioecious (male and female flowers occur on separate plants) perennial reaching 40 cm, with leaf sheaths that fall away. Its compact inflorescences are up to 5 cm long. The surrounding spathes and spathellae, flower-cluster bracts (leaf-like structures around flowers), bend somewhat backward and turn red; each female flower is 1.2–1.8 mm long. The account gives no dated sampling frame or frequency for these traits.
