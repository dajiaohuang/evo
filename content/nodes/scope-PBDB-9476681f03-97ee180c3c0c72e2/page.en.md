## atlas-profile / overview {#s-1e886fad2c}
Two separate fossils, shoulder specimen MUZM 60 from Moroto I and femur MUZM 80 from Moroto II, anchor this Morotopithecus bishopi account. They are not one associated skeleton. The study compares each bone to investigate early Miocene hominoid movement.

## atlas-profile / evidenceSummary {#s-2d53929e70}
MacLatchy and colleagues compare MUZM 60 and MUZM 80 on pages 159–183, Figures 1–12 and Tables 1–10. Locality attribution, movement reconstruction and evolutionary placement are separate inferences; the bones do not show observed behavior or direct ancestry.

## Shoulder and thigh from separate finds {#s-6f279e3269}
The paper discusses two finds: shoulder material MUZM 60 from Moroto I and femur MUZM 80 from Moroto II in Uganda. They were not found together as one skeleton. Each bone is compared with other primates, while the locality records help frame the analysis. The species name Morotopithecus bishopi therefore should not be mistaken for one associated individual.

## What the bones suggest about movement {#s-592da12ab5}
The study compares the shoulder and femur to test possible movement. Together, their form supports a varied arboreal repertoire rather than a single label based on one bone. This is a functional reconstruction from fossil anatomy and comparison with living and extinct primates. No one observed Morotopithecus moving, and the bones do not preserve its everyday behavior.

## Movement and ancestry are different questions {#s-e08040cf40}
A locomotor model does not by itself determine a place on the primate family tree. These two postcranial bones do not form a complete skeleton, establish diet or habitat, or show that Morotopithecus directly gave rise to living apes. The paper’s conclusion is a movement model for a limited sample; missing anatomy remains unknown.

## atlas-profile / readerLimitations {#s-582a2e1fde}
This account follows a functional study of two separate postcranial fossils. It does not provide a complete skeleton, direct behavioral observations, a species-wide ecological profile or proof of direct ancestry.
