## Morotopithecus: two separate postcranial fossils {#s-1e886fad2c}
Shoulder specimen MUZM 60 from Moroto I and femur MUZM 80 from Moroto II anchor this Morotopithecus bishopi account. They are not one associated skeleton. The study compares each bone to reconstruct possible early Miocene hominoid movement.

## Two specimens and a functional comparison {#s-2d53929e70}
MacLatchy and colleagues compare MUZM 60 and MUZM 80 on pages 159–183, Figures 1–12 and Tables 1–10. Locality attribution, movement reconstruction and evolutionary placement are separate inferences; the bones do not record observed behavior or direct ancestry.

## Shoulder and femur from different localities {#s-6f279e3269}
The paper discusses shoulder material MUZM 60 from Moroto I and femur MUZM 80 from Moroto II, Uganda. They were not found together as one skeleton. Each is compared with other primates; the locality records frame each specimen separately.

## A locomotor model from two bones {#s-592da12ab5}
The study compares the shoulder and femur to assess possible movement. Together, their form supports a varied arboreal repertoire rather than a single label based on one bone. This is a functional reconstruction from fossil anatomy and comparison, not observed behavior.

## Movement does not establish ancestry {#s-e08040cf40}
A locomotor model does not determine a place on the primate family tree. These two postcranial fossils do not form a complete skeleton, establish diet or habitat, or show that Morotopithecus directly gave rise to living apes.

## Limits of a two-specimen functional study {#s-582a2e1fde}
This account follows a functional study of two separate postcranial fossils. It does not provide a complete skeleton, direct behavioral observations, a species-wide ecological profile or proof of direct ancestry.
