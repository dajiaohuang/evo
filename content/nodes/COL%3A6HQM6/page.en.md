## Form and flowers {#s-aba2f7e403}
The regional flora describes a cormous geophyte 200–500 mm tall, branching from the upper leaf axils; the branches turn clockwise. The sword-shaped leaves have overlapping sheaths, grow in a spiral arranged in two ranks, and arch outward. Flowers are yellow with brown spots and margins. The account describes their scent as ranging from sweet to putrid. Broad tepal claws form a wide cup; nectaries are prominent, anther lobes parallel, and the capsule beaked.

## Diagnostic details and comparison {#s-ff65e907b8}
A separate SANBI account says the flowers sometimes have a pleasant, sweet scent reminiscent of violets, with a slightly sour undertone, unlike those of the related *Ferraria crispa* and *F. foliosa*. The flowers otherwise resemble those species: their tepals spread widely, and the broad claws form a shallow cup about 8 mm deep and about 12 mm wide at the rim. The claws bear large, heart-shaped nectaries. The tepal limbs are creamy pale yellow with small dark-brown spots, matching the crisped margins.

Vegetatively, the account says *F. schaeferi* most closely resembles *F. foliosa*, which also has leaves and branches in a two-ranked spiral and thick, leathery leaf blades with a weakly defined central vein. The ovary and capsule of *F. schaeferi* have a pronounced beak up to 8 mm long, distinguishing it from *F. foliosa*. In sandy habitat, the plant also spreads by long runners from the stem base; they may extend a metre or more underground from the parent, each ending in a corm.
