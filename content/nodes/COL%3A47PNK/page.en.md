## The name and this data account {#s-7833d1ca1c}
COL26.8 accepts Nonnula brunnea in Bucconidae. AVONET v7 links the name to a historical HBW–BirdLife taxonomy entry from December 2020. That row includes 5 measured individuals, 4 with a complete measurement set; the usable count can differ by trait. The row is marked Inference=NO.

## Measured beak dimensions {#s-067c7d769f}
AVONET reports a mean beak length of 15.8 mm from the anterior edge of the nostrils to the tip. Width is 5.2 mm and depth 5.6 mm, both measured at that nostril landmark. These are separate standardized dimensions, not field-identification cutoffs.

## Wing, tarsus and mass {#s-9d43bec637}
Mean unflattened wing length, from the carpal joint to the tip of the longest primary, is 63.2 mm; mean tarsus length is 12.4 mm. AVONET lists mean mass as 25 g with source category Updated_literature and a separate reference to Dunning (2021). The morphology sample counts are not assigned to that mass estimate.

## Habitat, feeding and lifestyle {#s-947602c9d4}
AVONET assigns Forest habitat, an Invertivore trophic niche and an Insessorial primary lifestyle. In its definitions, Forest means tall tree-dominated vegetation with a more or less closed canopy; Invertivore means at least 60% of food comes from terrestrial invertebrates; and Insessorial describes spending much time perched above ground on vegetation or other raised surfaces. These literature-derived classes summarize a pattern, not exclusive habitat or feeding.

## Movement and geographical summary {#s-c4bf350bdd}
Migration code 1 means sedentary in AVONET. The historical breeding/resident geography has latitude endpoints -6.9° and 3.02°; these do not describe non-breeding movements, a current location or a complete current range. Geography and morphology come from separate compilation steps.

## Foraging strata differ from whole-day activity {#s-06d2a81cbc}
The archive marks the foraging-stratum estimates as species-level: 50% ground and 50% below 2 m in forest understory, forest edges, bushes or shrubs. These are estimated shares of foraging use across categories, not nest sites, all habitat or whole-day time budgets. They do not describe elevation, flight height or habitat area.

## Evidence limits {#s-ecf0f70cad}
AVONET's measurements are compiled species means, not identification diagnoses or ranges of individual variation; the usable sample count can vary by trait. The mass estimate has its own updated-literature category and reference, so the morphology counts do not apply to it. Habitat, trophic niche, lifestyle and migration are categorical summaries, while the latitude endpoints describe historical breeding/resident geography only. The name match and historical crosswalk do not prove that the 2020 taxonomy and COL26.8 represent identical species concepts. Reproduction, current range, population and conservation status need other sources.

The EltonTraits account covers only the supported foraging-stratum field group, not a full species monograph or the remaining diet and activity fields. Its percentages are estimated category use from literature, not measurements of nest sites, habitat area or whole-day time budgets. The source label and species-level flag do not replace reading the underlying book. The diet group is withheld: the archive marks it D1 because of uncertainty/interpolation, unresolved source labels or unusable category values, so this page draws no diet conclusion.
