## Accepted name and placement {#s-4a5d496f88}
COL26.8 accepts Hyalinobatrachium viridissimum (Taylor, 1942) as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Centrolenidae (family) → Hyalinobatrachium (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## A local cloud-forest occurrence and its elevation {#s-0805837aa0}
The 2021 Santa Cruz Tepetotutla checklist reports Hyalinobatrachium viridissimum at 1250 m above sea level during its 2018–2019 survey. Table 1 compares this with an earlier reported elevation of 20–1275 (Mendoza-Henao et al. 2020) and lists photographic catalogue number IBH-RF 605. The locality is in the Chinantla region of Oaxaca, Mexico. This is a reported local occurrence, not a surveyed global range, independent verification of the archived photograph, or evidence of occupancy at every intermediate elevation. Historical elevations are retained as attributed comparisons rather than merged automatically with the new observation.

## Interpret the record within the survey design {#s-d696b174ac}
Four observers surveyed pristine and managed cloud-forest sites on eleven five-day trips in 2018–2019, in wet and dry seasons. Three daily windows were 09:00–13:00, 15:00–19:00 and 21:00–01:00; the paper reports 2,640 effective field hours for the whole herpetofaunal inventory. Animals were photographed and released, and GPS locations had a reported 5 m error range. The checklist's Hyalinobatrachium viridissimum row establishes presence in that sampling context. Total effort and the nineteen amphibian species are not species-specific detection probabilities, population estimates or trends. The accumulation curve had not reached an asymptote, so a missing record cannot establish local absence.

## Dated conservation labels in the checklist {#s-59475f6f27}
Table 1 prints IUCN category LC, EVS entry M (10), Mexican NOM-059 entry Not included and endemism label Not endemic for Hyalinobatrachium viridissimum. These are the source's historical labels, not a newly checked current assessment or legal classification. The IUCN and Mexican columns use different frameworks; a “Not included” entry is not evidence of low risk or unrestricted activity. EVS is retained as the printed index entry rather than a measured extinction probability. The study argues that community-conserved forest supports threatened species, but its presence inventory does not isolate the causal effect of management or demonstrate a population increase.
