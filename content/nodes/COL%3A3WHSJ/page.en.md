## Name and historical record {#s-3c2d332a72}
Pinned COL26.8 accepts Lybius rubrifacies in Lybiidae. This section links the same-name, same-family historical AVONET v7 entry. The name and two one-to-one crosswalks do not establish that species concepts remained identical after later splits or lumps.

## Habitat, food and perching position {#s-19c14583f4}
The recorded habitat category covers woodland, forest edges and other relatively open tree habitats; the food niche is fruit-centred. The lifestyle field describes frequent perching above ground on vegetation, rocks or other substrates. The source uses a 60% threshold for these dominant food-resource categories. They are coarse labels, not independently measured diet proportions, exclusive feeding claims or local microhabitat observations.

## What sedentary means here {#s-ca7881e86e}
The entry is classed as sedentary. That is the source category; it does not separately list regional seasonal movements, routes, schedules or distances for every population.

## Latitude on the historical maps {#s-aa13966103}
Historical breeding and resident maps give latitude endpoints -3.72° and 0.16°. The worksheet associates these fields with BirdLife/HBW 2019.1 maps accessed on 27 March 2020. Endpoints summarize the mapped range: they are not a locality list, proof of occupancy at every point, a complete non-breeding movement account or a current distribution estimate.

## Evidence limits {#s-516e156a6e}
This is limited historical ecology and geography, not a complete species monograph. AVONET1_BirdLife uses HBW–BirdLife 5.0 names from December 2020. Same-name, same-family links and one-to-one BirdLife/eBird/BirdTree crosswalks support association but do not prove equivalence to current COL26.8 concepts. Ecological categories derive from literature, which was not individually reread. Original morphology may be inferred from relatives and mass may use genus averages or models, so this account withholds those interpretations rather than presenting them as direct species measurements. Historical maps cover breeding and resident ranges, not current populations, occupancy or conservation.
