## How the 2024 assessment ranked the species {#s-6b858024bb}
Table 4 of a 2024 Chilean assessment prints a conservation-priority index of 1.13 for Alsodes verrucosus and places it in the low category. The assessment covered 58 selected species using literature, citizen-science records and government plans; it did not newly census every population. The index combines distribution, threat, population-trend, taxonomic-uniqueness and body-size scores with a protected-area term. It is a management ranking under that model, not an extinction probability, current IUCN assessment or direct population-size measurement. “Low” or “non-priority” does not establish that the species is safe or has no conservation needs.

## The adult record is figure-based and labelled male {#s-b8f5d011af}
The adult-source field reads “ImageJ, figure,” meaning measurements taken with ImageJ from published figures; the voucher field is “-” and the sex field is “M” (male). These labels describe the compiled adult record, not the species' sex ratio or proven sexual dimorphism. The study selected ten linear measurements, mostly from male dorsal or lateral images and earlier descriptions, and used principal-component analysis (PCA) to fill some missing values. Table S1 does not flag every imputed cell. For log-shape ratios, the study scaled linear measurements by their geometric mean, except for snout–vent length. These are image- or literature-derived external proportions, not live performance, internal anatomy or a new sex-specific size range.

## The larval and adult habitat codes differ {#s-ecd0b662d4}
The study's row codes the larva LO (lotic, or flowing-water associated) and the adult TER (terrestrial). It keeps the stages separate, so the adult code cannot be assumed to describe tadpoles, or the reverse. Still-water, flowing-water and torrent labels summarize broad habitat associations, not measured flow tolerance; rock-dwelling or terrestrial labels do not define a full geographic range. An endotrophic tadpole category is distinct from direct development and still includes a larval stage. The authors compiled these functional assignments from species accounts rather than experimentally measuring each species' feeding, water-flow response, movement or habitat choice.

## The shape comparison shows a weak association across species {#s-0f1c2860ce}
Across the sampled species, a Mantel test found a weak but statistically significant association between larval and adult shape dissimilarities (r = 0.16, p = 0.003); correlations in shape disparity varied among families. The authors interpret the overall pattern as support for adaptive decoupling. It does not show zero association, genetic independence, experimental selection of a trait or the developmental trajectory of Alsodes verrucosus. Phylogenetic analyses included 74 of the 83 sampled species. Reconstructions of ancestral shape and timing use models fitted to living-species data; they are not new measurements of fossil morphology. For adults, K = 0.44 with p = 0.001 indicates less similarity among close relatives than the reference expectation. Thus, the paper's phrase “not phylogenetically structured” does not mean its test was nonsignificant. Supplement S3 repeats family sample counts that sum to 112 for both stages; without the raw matrices, adult individual denominators and imputed cells cannot be confirmed.
