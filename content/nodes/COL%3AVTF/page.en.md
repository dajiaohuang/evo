## Six Zhangjiajie sites represent different management histories {#s-eed289acf6}
Between 2020 and 2023 the study collected sixty free-living Chinese giant salamander samples within the Hunan Zhangjiajie reserve: Wumuyu eight, Yuanzi eleven, Jinbianxi ten, Xixiping eight, Xiangshi twelve and Liangshuikou eleven. Most DNA sampling used skin swabs; some large animals supplied small caudal-fin clips, and animals were released on-site. Wumuyu and Yuanzi were historically natural cave sites with minimal translocation involvement; Jinbianxi had long-term reinforcement; the other three had recent reintroductions. Free-living collection does not establish indigenous ancestry. The discussion suggests davidianus/sligoi lineages but does not certify each animal against contemporary accepted species, so these data remain on the Andrias genus page.

## A farm-bred genome sample supported marker discovery {#s-505d3ac8be}
Muscle from one farm-bred individual, voucher JWS2021322, supplied the genomic sequencing sample; it is distinct from the sixty-site-sample diversity panel. Approximately 410 gigabases of raw reads produced a shallow, highly fragmented assembly, reported as about eightfold genome coverage. The assembled scaffold total was about 2.89 gigabases, with scaffold N50 of 744 bases. The authors used this draft to discover microsatellite markers rather than present a complete chromosome-level genome. Assembled sequence length is not a validated total genome size, and genomic results cannot be assigned independently to every sampled lineage.

## Nineteen selected markers reflect a staged screening procedure {#s-a755ee689e}
Of two hundred randomly selected primer pairs, 116 amplified bands and seventy-one produced specific single bands in two initial animals. Testing ten animals then identified twenty-six polymorphic pairs; seven low-polymorphism pairs were discarded, leaving nineteen for the sixty-animal comparison. Selecting polymorphic loci conditions the resulting diversity estimates; this is not random genome-wide variation. The validation section says retained loci had PIC above 0.25, but the later sixty-animal results report Y152 below 0.25. These statements concern different stages and samples and are retained rather than forced into a single invariant threshold.

## Heterozygosity and differentiation describe sampled loci {#s-1c46968fac}
Across the nineteen markers the study reports 107 alleles, with mean expected heterozygosity 0.565 and observed heterozygosity 0.773. These are marker statistics, not counts of animals or measures of survival. Observed exceeded expected heterozygosity in all six site summaries, and most loci departed from Hardy–Weinberg expectations. Yuanzi versus Wumuyu had the largest reported genetic distance, 0.144, and Fst, 0.065; Xixiping versus Xiangshi had the smallest, 0.004 and 0.013. Those comparisons do not measure migration rates or prove a taxonomic boundary. The authors’ suggested shortage of breeders is a possible explanation rather than a directly counted breeding population.
