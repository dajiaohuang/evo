## Accepted name in the catalogue {#s-ab944400af}
The 20 August 2026 release of Catalogue of Life (COL26.8) accepts Eleutherodactylus coqui Thomas, 1966 as a species. Its recorded path is Anura (order), Eleutherodactylidae (family), then Eleutherodactylus (genus). This is the name and placement in that release; another checklist or a fossil database needs its own identity evidence.

## What the assembly metadata can show {#s-493059b639}

## Repeat annotations are computational results {#s-d0af85232b}
The study built species-specific repeat libraries with RepeatModeler2, classified unknown elements with RFSB, added vertebrate repeats and ran RepeatMasker. The paper calls its hits insertions, but a hit is not by itself proof of a complete, expressed or mobile element. Superfamily labels may combine distinct families, while element counts, total occupied length and genome proportions measure different things; overlapping hierarchy categories should not be added as independent fractions. No new repeat annotation or expression experiment was performed for E. coqui here.

## Correlations across 61 assemblies {#s-1b5a8cb985}
The study compared 61 assemblies using phylogenetically adjusted models, an earlier pruned tree, contig-N50 covariates and multiple-comparison correction. In that sample, sequences associated with recently active Ty3/DIRS1 elements correlate with genome size. “Recently active” is inferred from sequence divergence, not a direct observation of transposition in E. coqui; length and open-reading-frame similarity filters do not establish protein expression. The authors’ developmental-constraint explanation is a comparative hypothesis, not evidence that transposable elements cause this species’ larval duration or clutch size.
