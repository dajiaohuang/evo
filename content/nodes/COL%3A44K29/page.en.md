## The name and this data account {#s-a59033f2ec}
COL26.8 accepts *Mulleripicus pulverulentus* in Picidae. AVONET v7 records the species under its HBW–BirdLife 2020 name, with 5 measured individuals and a complete morphological set for 4. Individual traits may use smaller samples.

## Measured beak dimensions {#s-e713bef97a}
At the source's standardized landmarks, mean beak length from the front edge of the nostril to the tip is 47.5 mm; width and depth at that edge average 14.6 mm and 14.3 mm. These dimensions are not field-identification cutoffs.

## Wing, tarsus and mass {#s-81df047b4f}
Mean unflattened wing length from the carpal joint to the longest primary feather is 238.6 mm; mean tarsus length is 38.9 mm. A separate compilation reports mean mass of 450.19 g under source category Dunning. That species-average value does not mean the morphological specimens were all weighed.

## Habitat, feeding and lifestyle {#s-e7f7063276}
The dataset's dominant categories are Forest habitat, Invertivore trophic niche and Insessorial lifestyle. They support cross-species comparison; they do not mean that forest is the only habitat or invertebrates the only food.

## Movement and geographical summary {#s-50f8c98918}
The migration field classifies the record as sedentary. Historical breeding and resident maps extend between latitude endpoints −7.55° and 32.49°. They do not describe all non-breeding movements and are not a current-position or global-occupancy estimate. The map summary and measurements were compiled separately.

## Read this historical record through food and foraging position {#s-ecd6bb0a94}
An older *Mulleripicus pulverulentus* record in EltonTraits 1.0 supplies two ecological fields for comparison with the accepted COL26.8 entry. It shares the name and family but follows an earlier bird or MSW3 mammal classification; the match does not settle later splits or synonymies.

## Relative weights of food categories {#s-ae3209a9ab}
Invertebrates carry the only nonzero food weight in the archive: 100%. Code A indicates high compiler confidence in the source and diet information. The weight standardizes written descriptions rather than measures the diet directly; zero does not prove that a food is never eaten. Nect, Seed and PlantO are broader source categories than their everyday names and do not imply exclusive use of nectar, seeds or plants.

## Foraging strata differ from whole-day activity {#s-9f61e065bc}
Species-level feeding-position weights are 50% in trees or tall bushes from 2 m upward but below the canopy, 40% within or just above the canopy, and 10% in understory, edges or shrubs below 2 m. These estimate relative use while feeding, not nest sites, all habitat or a day-long time budget. Surface-water, understory, mid/high, canopy and aerial categories have distinct source definitions; they are not elevation ranges and cannot yield flight heights or habitat area.

## Evidence limits {#s-43c47d2fac}
AVONET v7 offers historical comparisons rather than a complete species account. Morphological measurements are averages for the listed samples, not identification diagnoses or individual-variation ranges. Mass comes from separate cited literature or weighing sources; the morphological sample count cannot be transferred to mass. Habitat, diet and migration are broad labels, and the mapped geography covers historical breeding and resident ranges only. A one-to-one historical crosswalk plus the catalogue name supports this association but does not prove that species concepts remained equivalent after splits or lumps. Reproduction, current populations and conservation status require other sources.

The EltonTraits section interprets a historical same-name, same-family record, not a full monograph. The match does not establish equivalence with COL26.8. Food and bird feeding-position percentages are coarse relative weights standardized from literature descriptions, not direct measurements of stomach contents, intake, energy use or observation time. Bird diet codes A/B retain source and proportional uncertainty. Mammal ABC diet or activity codes may also draw on an ecologically similar congener, so this page does not claim that every field is a direct observation of *M. pulverulentus*. Genus- or family-level typical values, bird strata lacking a species-level designation and split records awaiting individual review do not establish definite conclusions. The underlying books and papers have not been individually reread. Other sources are needed for individual, seasonal and local variation, current range, population and conservation.
