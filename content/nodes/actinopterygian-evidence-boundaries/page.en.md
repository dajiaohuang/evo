## Seven evidence boundaries in ray-finned fish history {#s-a6d3d84366}
Seven case studies show why a fossil's occurrence, a modelled event date, a living-species tree and a database's name coverage answer different questions.

## Cheirolepis anatomy from named specimens {#s-beaaae625e}
CT scans of named Cheirolepis specimens show an imperforate propterygium, a shaft of the hyomandibular bone and scales with peg-and-socket joints. These features describe the sampled fish; its late Eifelian occurrence is neither the global first ray-finned fish nor a direct ancestor.

## Dating a genome duplication with molecules {#s-6da8401366}
Thirty duplicated-gene pairs place the teleost third-round genome duplication (3R) at 286.18–267.20 Ma in the study's models, before its crown-Teleostei interval. These are inferred time windows, not a fossil first appearance or an instantaneous observation.

## A tree placement changes fossil calibrations {#s-30ab332311}
The 2017 analysis places Fukangichthys and other scanilepiforms on the polypterid stem, changing which fossils can calibrate living actinopterygians. This depends on the tree topology and differs from the published 2014 placement.

## Several early forms at one Triassic quarry {#s-c741c444de}
Named Rüdersdorf holotypes and referred specimens preserve several early teleosteomorph body forms at one quarry, near 244 Ma. Their stem placement and single-locality sample establish neither the crown-Teleostei first appearance nor a worldwide radiation.

## Living genomes group gars and bowfin {#s-a5e8e39365}
The spotted-gar genome, bowfin transcriptome and comparative synteny support gars and bowfin together as Holostei, sister to teleosts. These living-species data do not directly date fossils or show that anatomy stayed unchanged.

## A tail feature spans the gar–teleost split {#s-a0ca2b5475}
Developmental series show a hypural–diastema complex in both gar and teleost tails, while bowfin and other comparisons reveal a mosaic pattern. Inheritance followed by losses and latent parallelism remain alternative explanations.

## Independent markers support a deep split {#s-e008ec90cd}
Gene trees and chromosome-adjacency markers support Elopomorpha plus Osteoglossomorpha as sister to Clupeocephala in the sampled living genomes. This is neither a complete species tree nor a direct observation of an ancient chromosome fusion.
