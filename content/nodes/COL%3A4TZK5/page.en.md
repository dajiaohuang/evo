## Feeding research {#s-b33012d863}
A two-year study during 2012–2014 followed phenology in 205 tree species and observed unhabituated groups along nine 2-km transects in Central Amazon floodplain forest. Fruit availability was higher in high water, when monkeys travelled less and fed more; in low water, when fruit was scarcer, feeding choices shifted toward fruit. The results are limited to this floodplain study area and its seasonal samples.

## Monitoring records {#s-f64bdc3050}
Nine 2-km trails in the southeastern Mamirauá Sustainable Development Reserve were repeatedly surveyed from 2009–2013; the five-year effort covered 410 km and recorded 346 social units. Distance sampling estimated an overall density of 359 individuals/km² (95% interval 293–439), with no significant decline among years. This is a reserve-trail estimate, not a direct census of the entire species population.
