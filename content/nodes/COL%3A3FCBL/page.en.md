## Form and flowers {#s-7cdbcd86be}
The 2012 regional flora account describes this stemless succulent as reaching 1 m. Its leaves form a spiral or two-ranked rosette; the account describes the leaves, margins, and keels as smooth. Flowers hang from an inclined, simple or branched raceme and are reddish and swollen below.

A 1994 account gives a more detailed diagnosis: erect to spreading bright reddish-pink racemes with a yellowish throat, and a stemless rosette of short, triangular to triangular-lanceolate, strongly keeled leaves. Plants may grow alone or form small groups, and the inflorescence may be simple or have ascending side branches. This account describes the plant's vegetative form as environmentally variable. With frequent veld fires, plants may stay small and juvenile; their strap-shaped, densely tuberculate leaves can lie flat against the ground and be hard to see. Where annual fires are absent, leaves may develop into a rosette, although two-ranked forms also occur and grade continuously into the typical form. These fire-related descriptions and the 2012 account's description of smooth leaves come from different regional treatments.

The 1994 treatment also compares *G. nitida* with *G. ellaphieae*, *G. carinata*, and *G. vlokii*. It reports that the first three species other than *G. carinata* flower in December and January, while *G. carinata* flowers in spring; the four species are described as allopatric. It also notes that *G. nitida*, *G. ellaphieae*, and *G. vlokii* share bright red-pink inflorescences. The account records resemblance to the juvenile leaves of *G. pulchra* and *G. excelsa*; it distinguishes *G. excelsa* by sharply serrulate leaf margins and large panicles with light-pink flowers, and reports natural hybrids between *G. pulchra* and *G. excelsa* where they grow close together.

These are descriptions and comparisons in regional South African flora accounts. They do not establish global representativeness or population frequencies; the cited excerpts provide no dated sampling frame.

## Habitat {#s-ca22f9aa7a}
The cited regional account records *G. nitida* in grassy fynbos and renosterveld, often on sandstone soils.

This is a regional South African flora account; it does not establish habitat use across the species' full range, seasonality, or population frequency.
