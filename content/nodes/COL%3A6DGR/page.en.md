## Six species, distinct research questions {#s-3b68df7ec7}
The pinned COL26.8 roster connects six Papio species: olive, yellow, Hamadryas, Kinda, Guinea, and chacma baboons. The studies here ask separate questions. Altmann examined yearling-female diet nutrients in yellow baboons at Amboseli; Lewis and O’Riain followed one naturally foraging chacma group on the Cape Peninsula for 13 months, recording activity and ranging. Weyher et al. analyzed nine years of female–male social-bond data from one Kinda population at Kasanka. Patzelt et al. made fixed-point observations for three months in Niokolo Koba, including Guinea-baboon parties splitting and fusing while crossing an open area. Mir et al. used movement records for collared adult males at selected sites in southwestern Saudi Arabia. Jordan et al. compared Alu-insertion polymorphisms in two individuals from each of six living Papio species. These cases differ in sample, place, method, and question; they do not combine into one social or ecological model for the genus.

## Fields in the Amniote life-history archive {#s-a6d1ba5952}
The August 2015 Amniote archive contributes five historical Papio accounts on this route. All five include adult mass, the source-defined female-maturity field—age at first conception—offspring per litter or eggs per clutch, and conception or fertilization-to-birth duration. Examples span P. anubis, P. cynocephalus, P. hamadryas, P. papio, and P. ursinus. These are historical entries under a pinned catalogue route, not a complete current species review.

## Food and activity fields in EltonTraits {#s-2927c64716}
EltonTraits contains five Papio entries on this route, one for each name in the fixed roster. Food-category weights, foraging strata, and mammalian activity periods are available in all five. Food weights translate literature descriptions into coarse categories; strata and activity use categorical or binary codes. These fields are not direct measurements of every animal’s diet or daily time budget.
