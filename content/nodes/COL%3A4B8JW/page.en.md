## Measured beak dimensions {#s-07b951a06b}
Mean beak length is 11.1 mm from the front edge of the nostril to the tip; mean width is 5.1 mm and depth is 10 mm at the nostril edge. These are separate measurements at defined landmarks, not field-identification cutoffs.

## Wing, tarsus and mass {#s-d372f3e112}
Mean unflattened wing length, from the carpal joint to the longest primary, is 135 mm; mean tarsus length is 22.9 mm. The compiled mean mass is 95 g and carries source category Dunning. Mass comes from separate species-average information, not from weighing this one AVONET specimen.

## Habitat, feeding and lifestyle {#s-403b88144c}
The dataset codes habitat as Forest, trophic niche as Invertivore and lifestyle as Insessorial, meaning mainly perched on vegetation or another support. These broad comparison categories do not mean the owl uses only forest or eats only invertebrates.

## Foraging strata differ from whole-day activity {#s-c49f41db93}
Species-level foraging weights are ground 40%, understory, edges or shrubs below 2 m 20%, trees or high bushes from 2 m upward but below the canopy 20%, and in or just above the canopy 20%. These are relative weights for feeding positions, not nest sites, all habitat or a daily time budget. The archive's strata have distinct definitions; they are not elevation bands and do not show flight heights or habitat area.
