## A new species described from two adult types {#s-f94e00f621}
The 2025 description names the species for its red bands against a green back and proposes the English common name Red-barred Green Pitviper. Its type series comprises a male holotype, RIM-00078, and a female paratype, RIM-00079. Both were collected on 7 October 2024 near Tham Khao Siwa Cave in Sa Kaeo, Thailand, at about 250 m elevation.

## Measurements and colour belong to the sampled individuals {#s-cbbeaf8702}
Table 2 reports a snout–vent length of 425 mm and tail length of 110 mm for the male, and 545 mm and 105 mm for the female. These measurements describe the two types; they do not establish the species’ size range or a general difference between sexes. The male has a white stripe below the eye that is absent in the female. Both have golden irises and grass-green backs, while the female’s reddish-brown bands are less distinct.

## What the mitochondrial tree supports {#s-d8fdea5c3d}
The analysis compares three mitochondrial DNA fragments—cytochrome b (cyt b), ND4, and 16S rRNA—with 2,296 aligned bases, meaning sequence positions matched for comparison. It places T. erythrochloris as sister to T. venustus, with moderate support: ultrafast maximum-likelihood bootstrap 94 and Bayesian posterior probability 0.87. This mitochondrial result includes no nuclear-genome test and provides no divergence date.

## Rainy-night observations around limestone and forest {#s-9a43e399b2}
The survey covered lowland mixed semi-deciduous monsoon tropical forest at about 200–300 m. Collected snakes were on low bushes after rain, between 20:00 and 01:00. Uncollected sightings were on limestone rocks, saplings, and fallen branches; some were as far as 400 m from rock outcrops. A downward-facing posture was interpreted as possible ambush behavior, but the observations did not identify prey that had been eaten.

## Recorded locality and unconfirmed range {#s-2e21460000}
The description confirms records from the Khlong Hat limestone hills. Presence farther along the karst chain, including in Cambodia, is a prediction that still needs confirmation. The species’ full distribution, population size, and trends therefore remain unresolved. The authors’ tentative suggestion of Data Deficient is not evidence of a current formal IUCN assessment.

## An unresolved paratype-number inconsistency {#s-c0f516e9bd}
The description contains conflicting numbers for the female paratype: the variation paragraph says RIM-00078, whereas the type section, Table 2, and Figure 4 say RIM-00079. The latter number is used in the specimen account because those three locations agree; the conflict remains unresolved here, with no museum check or erratum claimed.

## Evidence limits {#s-ffec625f79}
The account rests on the 2025 description and its 2024 survey. The two adult types and additional uncollected sightings do not establish diet, reproduction, venom, lifespan, or current population trends.
