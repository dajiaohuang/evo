## Name and classification {#s-c58bc2376d}

## A local puddle-associated reproductive record {#s-14b3d202da}
The Ha Nam study encountered specimens on the ground beside a small puddle between 20:30 and 21:00 in mixed secondary forest. Males called there, and tadpoles were detected in temporary puddles. These collection observations support the stated local association, without supplying egg counts, development duration or evidence that every population uses the same sites.

## Local sexual size summaries and a molecular comparison {#s-5e57f901b3}
Kim Bang mukhlesuri males had SVL 16.2–25.2 mm (mean ± SD 20.69 ± 2.22; n = 31), and females 23.2–28.9 mm (26.08 ± 1.90; n = 11). Ranges overlap and do not define every population. Voucher HUS 2026 A.01 had a rounded snout, no vomerine teeth, unwebbed fingers, basal toe webbing and a dark X-shaped dorsal mark. Its 556-bp mitochondrial sequence was 97.66% similar to a mukhlesuri sequence from Raozan, Bangladesh. This is the paper’s identification evidence, not a universal sequence threshold, an estimate of migration from Bangladesh or certification that every older geographic assignment uses the same taxonomic concept.

## Local food composition and the largest recovered stomach count {#s-257a2e2ecf}
Forty-two mukhlesuri yielded 878 stomach items, with a mean ± SD of 20.9 ± 22.58 items and range 3–125. The sampled diet included five insect orders and spiders. Isoptera had the highest reported IRI, 65.13%, followed by Hymenoptera and Coleoptera; standardized niche breadth was 0.06. A stomach containing 125 recovered items is not a daily intake rate or normal meal size for every animal. The April and July 2025 flushing sample does not measure prey availability or demonstrate an obligatory ant-and-termite diet. Reported prey volume was modelled from prey length and width, not weighed consumption energy.

## Encounter settings 0.5–4.5 m from the water edge {#s-7b61dc96bd}
Mukhlesuri were recorded on the ground (29), lawn or fallen-leaf carpet (8), stream bank (2), and puddles (3), at 0.5–4.5 m from the water edge in the surveyed secondary-forest stream setting. Sampling occurred between 20:30 and 23:00 during two visits in April and July 2025. Counts describe where sampled frogs were detected, not standardized abundance, annual microhabitat preference or evidence that all reproduction occurred in those puddles. The proposed link between terrestrial foraging and the ant-and-termite-rich stomach sample is a local ecological interpretation rather than an experimental test of habitat-driven food selection.

## A high overlap with heymonsi and lower overlap with lingnanica {#s-dee4551c2c}
The sample’s Pianka overlap index was 97% for mukhlesuri versus heymonsi and 47% for mukhlesuri versus lingnanica. Abundance and volume ANOSIM comparisons were not significant for the two Microhyla but significant for mukhlesuri versus lingnanica. This describes the sampled trophic axis, not identical whole-species niches or direct evidence of competition. Nonsignificance does not prove biological equivalence, and the lower overlap does not show that competition is impossible. The study did not manipulate food resources or measure changes in survival caused by the other frog species.

## An effort-bounded Gunung Belumut survey record {#s-9491b1bcdd}
Table 1 records 7 Microhyla mukhlesuri encounters at Gunung Belumut Amenity Forest in Johor, Peninsular Malaysia, or 4.1% of the 172 reported amphibian records. Sampling combined drift-fenced pitfall traps with searches along streams and forest trails in parts of 2022 and February 2023. Most captured animals were released and selected vouchers retained; the article does not document marking that would certify every encounter as a different individual. This is a local survey count, not population density, an elevational limit or complete distribution. The forest’s 1,010 m summit elevation is not assigned as the elevation of every frog record.
