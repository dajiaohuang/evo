## Twelve mammal dossiers, not an ancestor ladder {#s-83d46d4d98}
These twelve cases compare fossils, a genome draft, bone-density measurements, and evolutionary trees. Each records a different sample or analysis; together they do not form a single sequence of direct ancestors.

## Three molars in a Steropodon jaw {#s-08eefa631c}
AM F66763 is a fragment of the right lower jaw with three molars. Their shapes were compared with monotreme and tribosphenic dental patterns; the fragment supplies a focused dental sample, not a complete skull or skeleton.

## A draft genome for the platypus {#s-66572d4da0}
The genome project assembled about 26.9 million reads into a 1.84-gigabase draft at roughly sixfold coverage. It ordered 437 megabases on 20 chromosomes and predicted 18,527 protein-coding genes. These are properties of the draft assembly, not a complete sequence.

## Eomaia: skeleton and tree placement {#s-e45bfc6914}
CAGS 01-IG-1a,b preserves the skull, teeth, and much of the postcranial skeleton. In the original study, the character matrix and limb comparisons placed Eomaia near the base of Eutheria and supported a scansorial, or climbing, interpretation.

## Juramaia and a conditional Jurassic signal {#s-2419aa6bc8}
BMNH PM1343B preserves skull, teeth, and postcranial bones. The original study associated it with strata about 160 million years old and recovered it near the base of Eutheria; both the age signal and placement belong to that study's evidence and analysis.

## Ear and hyoid bones in Ambolestes {#s-435ce06022}
STM33-5 preserves the ectotympanic, malleus, and hyoid bones. The published character matrix placed Ambolestes and Sinodelphys within Eutheria, a result tied to that matrix and its sampled characters.

## A placental matrix paired with genes {#s-17044c7afa}
One study scored 4,541 phenomic characters for 86 living and fossil species, using more than 12,000 annotated images, and combined the matrix with 27 nuclear genes. The result depends on this combined morphological and genetic sample.

## Four clades in a placental gene tree {#s-6903553ca4}
A 16,397-base-pair dataset combined 19 nuclear genes with three mitochondrial genes. Bayesian and maximum-likelihood analyses recovered four major placental clades in the sampled tree.

## Eritherium: a growing anatomical sample {#s-b775b11394}
The Eritherium holotype, MNHN PM69, preserves a rostrum and upper-jaw teeth. Fourteen additional specimens extend the sampled upper and lower dentition and skull anatomy beyond the holotype.

## Bone compactness across Thalassocnus {#s-a6c87e1b23}
CT slices and thin sections show increasing bone compactness across ribs and limb bones in the sampled Thalassocnus series. The comparison includes named T. littoralis specimens MNHN.F.SAS 2 and MNHN.F.SAS 53; it tracks skeletal density, not behavior directly.

## Mimolagus teeth and ankle bones {#s-b7223286d5}
The right third upper molar of holotype IVPP V20115, together with referred teeth, an astragalus, calcanei, and a cuboid, defines the anatomical sample and locality used in the study. These elements come from more than one referred specimen.

## Wing and cochlea comparisons in Onychonycteris {#s-177e4361c4}
ROM 55351A preserves elongated wings and a relatively small cochlea. Researchers compared limb proportions and cochlear size with living and fossil bats; these anatomical proxies do not directly record how an individual flew or sensed sound.

## A seven-gene bat topology {#s-600b8d214e}
Four nuclear and three mitochondrial genes contributed 8,230 aligned base pairs. In the study's analyses, sampled rhinolophoids grouped with megabats rather than with the other sampled microbats; this is a result of those datasets and methods.
