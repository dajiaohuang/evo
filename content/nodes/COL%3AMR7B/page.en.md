## Regional gradients and survey gains do not certify recovery {#s-7ff0597753}
The reserve description spans 1,150–3,830 m, the sampling Methods report 800–2,600 m, and statistical Methods describe observed amphibians at 800–2,500 m. These intervals describe the reserve and survey methods, not a measured range for this species. Richness peaks in two regional bands but its linear elevation association is nonsignificant (reported R² 0.114, p 0.412). Historical recorded-species increase is also nonsignificant (Mann–Kendall p 0.148). Neither result establishes unchanged populations, increased abundance or a causal benefit of management. The paper’s discussion makes broader claims about fragmentation, conservation success and large populations, but the reported tests do not establish them. The article does not compare the underlying records or historical survey effort in enough detail to resolve those claims.

## A new reserve record is distinct from a newly evolved species {#s-5f7837aa78}
Results name Boulenophrys minor among three newly documented reserve species. This is a local inventory addition, not a new species description, proof of recent colonization or a dated range expansion. Changes in survey coverage, detectability and historical identification must be evaluated before interpreting the addition as population recovery. The nonsignificant historical trend cannot establish conservation causality.
