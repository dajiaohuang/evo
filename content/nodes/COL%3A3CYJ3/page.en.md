## What the cross-stage comparison found {#s-a6fb9fc116}
Across sampled species, larval–adult shape differences show a weak but significant Mantel association (r = 0.16, p = 0.003), while disparity correlations vary among families. The authors interpret the pattern as support for adaptive decoupling; it does not show zero correlation, genetic independence, an experimentally selected trait or this species’ own developmental path. Phylogenetic analyses include 74 of 83 sampled species. Ancestral shape and timing are model reconstructions from living data, not new fossil measurements. Adult K = 0.44 (p = 0.001) describes weaker resemblance among close relatives than the reference expectation; the paper’s phrase “not phylogenetically structured” does not mean the test was nonsignificant. Supplement S3 repeats family N values summing to 112 for both stages, so the adult individual denominator and imputed cells remain uncertain without the raw matrices.
