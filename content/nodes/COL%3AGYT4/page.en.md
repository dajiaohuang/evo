## The accepted catalogue name {#s-89faa62b6d}
COL26.8 records Arthroleptis wahlbergii Smith, 1849 as an accepted species under Anura, Arthroleptidae and Arthroleptis. The study described below addresses how this frog was named in an isiZulu-language compilation; it does not revise the catalogue’s scientific name or establish new biological traits.

## A local naming study, not a species survey {#s-5c5601b8c0}
This page follows a bounded study of isiZulu names for frogs in Zululand. Its sample and naming process can explain how a vernacular label was assembled, but they do not measure this species’ habitat, range, appearance or present-day recognition in the community.

## The published isiZulu name {#s-66176e1258}
The 2019 study’s Table 1 pairs Arthroleptis wahlbergii with the isiZulu name “Umanswininiza wasehlathini”. The table describes this form as a modification of names in Tarrant (2016), prepared with field-guide author Bongani Mkhize. It is a published vernacular naming proposal, not proof that workshop participants already used this exact species-specific phrase and not a zoological synonym. The spelling follows the table; its superscript source marker is kept separate from the name.

## How the workshop produced its vocabulary {#s-ff99959968}
The workshop used an opportunity sample of 13 volunteer native isiZulu speakers, aged 18–55, from five Zululand areas. Ten took part on 28 November and three on 1 December 2016 at Tembe Elephant Park; they viewed frog photographs and discussed names as a group, settling disagreements through discussion. The six broad names elicited there did not individually distinguish all 58 species. Species-level names were assigned afterwards with Bongani Mkhize, combining workshop vocabulary with earlier published names and naming guidelines. Group discussion gives no independent per-person frequencies, dialect-wide agreement or test of recognition accuracy for Arthroleptis wahlbergii.

## What the naming categories do—and do not—show {#s-11e625445e}
The study separates broad local naming categories from the scientific species labels in its compilation. Some categories span several genera or families, and their correspondence was assessed only within this regional set—not across African amphibians as a whole. Words referring to habitat, appearance, locality or calls are part of the naming exercise; their wording is not a measurement of this frog’s habitat preference, colour, calls or geographic limits. The 2019 table is neither a current occupancy survey nor a complete present-day Zululand checklist, and no independent linguistic validation or current community-use survey is reported.
