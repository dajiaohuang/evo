## A dated description resolves a former satelles label {#s-73cb6ec83b}
The 2024 paper describes Stefania imawari from Angasima-tepui, formerly included under satelles. Earlier multilocus work referred to it as Stefania sp.3; the present description uses external morphology, preserved material and microCT comparisons. It does not newly sequence all populations or certify every historical specimen under the old name. The authors emphasize that closely related summit frogs can look very similar, so colour or one anatomical character alone is insufficient for identifying all records.

## The sex and maturity of the descriptive sample matter {#s-3f03405387}
The type series consists of a48.5mm-SVL adult female holotype, five adult male paratypes, two other adult females and one immature female. The preserved adult ranges are44.1–48.9mm in five males and48.5–55.4mm in three females; the immature female is not part of that adult denominator. Rounded calliper measurements describe the examined material, not complete population limits, a juvenile growth curve or a statistically established universal sexual size difference.

## Live and preserved colours have different evidence {#s-9e0cc0091e}
Examined imawari varied from golden or chestnut brown to dark greenish brown, with different markings. The adult iris was faintly two-coloured, sometimes with a light-dependent bluish-grey tint. The paper reports no apparent sex-linked colour pattern in this small series, not a population-wide proof of independence. Ethanol preservation fades colours and removes some visible canthal stripes; descriptions of live and preserved material must not be pooled as one fixed colour state.

## Known summit records and candidate extensions stay separate {#s-dab22d772e}
Stefania imawari is reported from the summit of Angasima-tepui at 2,122–2,154m in Bolívar State, Venezuela. This is the examined locality scope, not a continuous elevational tolerance curve or an independently updated complete range. Katuran-tepui is proposed as a possible additional occurrence, but the authors require molecular confirmation because the summits are isolated. Exact collection coordinates are not repeated in this reader.

## A short observation period does not establish absence of brooding {#s-83d8a03116}
During11–16May2011, specimens were active at night on rocks, low vegetation and Brocchinia hechtioides; three hid under rocks during the day. No egg- or juvenile-carrying females were observed, but IRSNB4220 retained a glutinous back patch. That patch is not a directly observed clutch or measured brood success. The short rainy-season visit does not define a full breeding calendar, absence of parental care or exclusive nocturnality.

## The 2024 CR proposal has range and threat assumptions {#s-3adea325c1}
The authors propose Critically Endangered under criteriaB1 andB2a,b, using very small known occurrence, a single location and projected decline from fires and climate change. They give threshold conditions EOO<100km² andAOO<10km² and describe summit occurrence smaller than3km². These quantities are not interchangeable measured areas; the proposal is not a verified current official Red List entry. Population size and the number needed to maintain fitness remain unknown, so microendemism is not itself a measured decline rate.
