## Living amphibian data: genome, experiment and tree {#s-12ff45d293}
A frog genome, receptor-knockout experiments, and a 7,238-species timetree answer different questions; none is a fossil record of ancestry or complete amphibian coverage.

## One frog genome, with a defined sample {#s-c7c283e426}
The Xenopus tropicalis draft assembly is a valuable genomic reference tied to a traceable accession. It represents one living species and a bounded sample, not the genomes of all amphibians.

## Receptor knockouts affect organs differently {#s-8270b16fb4}
In the experiment, loss of TRβ delayed tail regression, while loss of TRα advanced hindlimb development. The results reveal receptor- and tissue-specific effects in this laboratory model, not a universal rule for amphibians.

## Molecular data and taxonomy shape the timetree {#s-583665c684}
The posterior timetree includes 7,238 species: molecular data cover 4,061, while the rest enter through soft taxonomic inference. Its node ages are model estimates, not fossil dates or directly observed events.
