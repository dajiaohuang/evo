## A downstream molecular signal is not a local range extension {#s-90fbd7fc9b}
The Tena study assigned water-derived molecular units to Pristimantis eriphus without observing the frog at its six monitoring sites. It places this taxon among mountain frogs associated with the higher Colonso-Chalupas exploration area, reported at 1,518 m elevation. The authors interpret the downstream detections as possible signals from outside the sampled sites and call them false positives for site-level presence. They are not new live-animal records establishing a lower elevational range. The paper itself gives inconsistent elevation descriptions for the monitoring area—600–710 m in Study site versus below 600 m in the Discussion—so an exact lower elevation is not certified here.

## Transport and contamination remain different explanations {#s-11418bc4f5}
Downstream transport of genetic material is the authors’ preferred explanation for the Pristimantis eriphus signal. Rainfall, drainage and slope provide context, but the study did not directly trace DNA from an upstream individual to a downstream sample. Reported negative field, amplification and sequencing controls reduce detected contamination concerns; they do not logically exclude every cross-contamination event, index misassignment or sequencing error. Species assignments used a short 16S match threshold above 97%, an operational rule rather than a universal species-delimitation test.
