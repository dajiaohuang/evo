## Can Nectocaris be a cephalopod without a shell? {#s-aeabdabc0d}
Ninety newly prepared Burgess Shale specimens of Nectocaris preserve paired eyes, two tentacles, side fins and a ventral funnel-like structure. These soft parts prompted a cephalopod interpretation, but the fossils do not preserve several structures that would diagnose that placement: a chambered shell, its connecting siphuncle, a beak or a radula. The proposed relationship therefore remains contested rather than a secure record of crown cephalopods.
