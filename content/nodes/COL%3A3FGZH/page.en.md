## Form and flowers {#s-971b67056b}
The regional account describes a cormous geophyte 4–12 cm tall, with woody, concentric corm tunics. Leaves range from sword-shaped to oblong, are leathery, and often lie close to the ground. Each spike bears a few small flowers, creamy white to yellow.

## Diagnostic details and comparisons {#s-a50838186b}
A separate SANBI account describes three or four short leaves, inclined or lying close to the ground, mostly up to 20 mm long but occasionally up to 45 mm. Flowers are creamy white to pale yellow, with a 3–4 mm perianth tube and tepals 6–8 mm long. Populations north of the Hex River Mountains can often be distinguished by having four leaves; southern populations usually have three, though the account notes exceptions.

Some northern populations may be difficult to distinguish from the related *Geissorhiza ovalifolia*. The account describes *G. ovalifolia* as having white flowers, softer-textured leaves, and usually a cormlet in one or more lower leaf axils. Hybrids between *G. parva* and the taller, larger-flowered *G. ovata* have been recorded near Caledon; the reported hybrids are intermediate between the two species.
