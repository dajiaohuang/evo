## Four fossil records on the chondrichthyan stem {#s-a528e6c186}
Follow the evidence from isolated tooth sets to articulated skeletons. Each specimen shows a different body part or character combination; none by itself settles crown placement or a global first appearance.

## Tooth sets without a known body {#s-426886aa25}
About 23 Qianodus tooth whorls—repeating sets of teeth—date to roughly 439 Ma and directly document jawed-vertebrate dentition. No associated jaw or body is known. Scales and spines found nearby cannot be assigned to Qianodus by proximity alone.

## A shoulder assembled from separate pieces {#s-8e5dd2f367}
Fanjingshania’s disarticulated, skin-derived skeletal elements are also roughly 439 Ma. They preserve a spine-and-plate complex that grew from several centres and records cyclical tissue resorption. Because the pieces are not articulated, they do not reveal a complete body or associated teeth.

## Armour in an articulated body {#s-ac73809796}
At about 436 Ma, the Shenacanthus holotype combines minute scales and a chondrichthyan-like rear gill region with large thoracic armour plates. It is a single toothless specimen, and its placement changes with analytical method; the armour does not make it a crown shark.

## A later stem form with a mixed anatomy {#s-aa99e639af}
Around 385 Ma, UMZC 2000.32 preserves jaws, gill arches, cartilage, scales and teeth in an unusual character mix. Compression and a missing tail limit reconstruction; its proposed feeding ecology is inferred rather than directly observed.
