## A historical record under this name {#s-8ce345cad4}
COL26.8 lists Megascops trichopsis as an accepted species in Strigidae. AVONET v7 reports measurements and ecology under the historical HBW–BirdLife 2020 name. Its record includes 4 measured individuals, all 4 with a complete measurement set; the usable sample may still differ by trait.

## Three beak measurements {#s-6507cf7982}
Mean beak length is 9.8 mm from the front edge of the nostril to the tip; mean width is 5.1 mm and depth is 10.1 mm at the nostril edge. These are separate measurements taken at defined landmarks, not cutoffs for identifying a bird in the field.

## Wing, tarsus and body mass {#s-64ed89413a}
Mean unflattened wing length, measured from the carpal joint to the longest primary, is 148.8 mm. Mean tarsus length is 24.9 mm. The compiled mean mass is 88.26 g and is labelled with source category Dunning. Mass comes from a separate species-average source, so these values do not show that the measured birds were weighed.

## Habitat and broad ecological categories {#s-2fc94d95c5}
AVONET assigns the categories Forest for habitat, Invertivore for trophic niche and Insessorial for lifestyle. They are dominant labels used in cross-species comparisons, not claims that the owl uses only forest or eats only invertebrates.

## Migration label and historical range map {#s-411d13c195}
The migration field labels the record sedentary. Historical breeding and resident maps extend between latitude endpoints 12.76° and 31.89°. They do not document all non-breeding movements, current positions or global occupancy. The map and the measurements come from separate parts of the compilation.

## A separate historical source for diet and foraging {#s-ae0b5b7ebd}
EltonTraits 1.0 contains a same-name, same-family record for Megascops trichopsis with 2 ecological field groups. Its historical taxonomy may differ from COL26.8; matching name and family do not resolve later species splits or synonym disputes.

## Relative weights for food categories {#s-3a35d4b048}
The archive assigns 100% to invertebrates among the nonzero food-category weights. Source code A means the compilers recorded high confidence in the source and diet information. The percentages standardize verbal descriptions; they are not direct measurements of diet, and a zero value would not prove that an item is never eaten. Labels such as Nect, Seed and PlantO cover broader categories than their everyday wording and do not mean the owl eats only nectar, seeds or plants.

## Where the record places foraging {#s-6d41495120}
The species-level foraging weights are 50% for trees or tall bushes from 2 m upward but below the canopy; 20% for understory, edges or shrubs below 2 m; 10% for the ground; 10% in or just above the canopy; and 10% in the air well above vegetation or other structures. These estimate relative use of feeding positions, not nest sites, all habitat or time spent over a whole day. The source defines surface-water, understory, mid/high, canopy and aerial categories separately; they are not elevation ranges and cannot be converted into flight heights or habitat area.

## Follow the source labels {#s-99daef746d}
Both EltonTraits field groups carry the label Ref_5 in the bird-source table. Diet, foraging and activity may draw on different material, and the same Ref number in bird and mammal tables need not point to the same citation. The archive retains each file, row, field group and reference-table label for tracing. The cited books and papers were not individually read here; a reference label is not a separate observation.

## Evidence limits {#s-da325e4f61}
AVONET v7 reports species averages for its listed samples, not identification diagnoses or ranges of individual variation. The 88.26 g mass comes from a separate source and cannot be assigned the morphological sample size. Habitat, feeding and migration are broad categories, and the map covers historical breeding and resident ranges only. The historical crosswalk and matching name support this record association but do not prove that older and COL26.8 species concepts remained equivalent after taxonomic changes. Reproduction, current populations and conservation status require other evidence.
EltonTraits 1.0 is a separate historical, same-name and same-family record, not a complete species monograph. Its relative diet and foraging percentages are standardized from literature descriptions, not direct measurements of stomach contents, intake, energy or observation time. Code A records compiler confidence in the source and diet information; it does not turn the categories into direct measurements. Elsewhere in the archive, bird A/B diet and mammal ABC diet or activity codes have their own source and proportional uncertainties; mammal ABC entries may also use a congener judged ecologically similar. Genus- or family-level values, non-species-level strata and unresolved split records do not establish traits for this species. The underlying books and papers were not individually read, and individual, seasonal and local variation, current range, population and conservation status need other sources.
