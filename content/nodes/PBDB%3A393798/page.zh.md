Limited information on the Mavro Mouri cave complex is given by Kuss (1970).
Two vertical vertical rock faces, which run apart from each other at an angle of up to 10 m, include layered red sediment in the apex. The ceiling of this former karst cave has collapsed. On the eastern wall between + 7-8 m a groove from the Milazzien, z. T. still covered with remains of the former cave filling. The layers of red sediment fall with 20 to 30 degrees to NE. Change of red, mostly mixed with many stones and homogeneous yellowish layers. The latter almost fossil. Most of the mammal remains from this site are from a sediment tribe on the east wall between 4-6 m above sea level. In one layer numerous elephant bones, perhaps from the same individual. - Above the gap in +17 m a grotto. - Mawro Muri IV can only be reached from Mawro Muri V via a rocky outcrop.
So far, chelonian remains are known from the cave Mavro Mouri IV and are included in the present thesis. Kuss (1970) reports mixed sediments in the cave, of mainly yellowish sand with coarser and finer parts and horizontal development. Besides the chelonian specimens, the cervid material has received great interest by Dutch researchers, producing numerous publications (see van der Geer et al. 2014 and references therein).
