## What do the fossil records count? {#s-668d4076ef}
Rhamphorhynchus muensteri appears under the database parent Rhamphorhynchus in the selected PBDB pterosaur records. The set contains 14 occurrence records, drawn from 11 collections and linked to 4 reference identifiers. These counts describe database records, collection groups and citations; they do not measure the number of fossils or animals.

## What does the age interval mean? {#s-1bc4955a40}
The selected records have an outer age interval of 152.21–145.06 Ma; Ma means millions of years ago. This span combines the oldest early bound with the youngest late bound among the chosen records. It does not show continuous presence through every intervening time or directly date the species origin, extinction or an individual lifespan. Each collection keeps its own bracket; this summary does not narrow it to a specimen age or revise the source dating interpretation.

## What can the setting labels tell us? {#s-f2b6ee0079}
The collection records carry these setting and preservation tags:
- Solnhofen (Uppsala Collection): lagoon or restricted shallow-subtidal setting (lagoonal/restricted shallow subtidal); body fossil, compression impression (body,adpression).
- Eichstätt, Solnhofen (MfN collection): lagoon or restricted shallow-subtidal setting (lagoonal/restricted shallow subtidal); mold or impression, compression impression, soft tissues (mold/impression,adpression,soft parts).
- Compsognathus type, Kapfelberg (BSP): lagoon or restricted shallow-subtidal setting (lagoonal/restricted shallow subtidal); body fossil, mold or impression, compression impression, calcite replacement (body,mold/impression,adpression,replaced with calcite).
The setting is the depositional context, not proof of the animal’s living habitat or diet. Preservation tags describe a collection and need not apply to every fossil within it.

## What this record set cannot tell us {#s-daffe0cda7}
This page summarizes PBDB occurrence records dated 2026-10-02 for one name in a fixed set of 276 pterosaur entries. Included rows have a matching current name, a recorded parent path to Pterosauria and a species-level occurrence without an uncertain-identification qualifier. Those rules organize the database; they do not settle the identification of an individual fossil or every taxonomic dispute, and a same-name COL entry is not merged as the same taxonomic concept. Collection site, age, depositional setting and preservation are separate kinds of information. Together they do not give a complete range, exact origin or extinction, species-wide habitat, population size or the preservation state of every specimen. This is a guide to selected records, not a full account of the species biology.
