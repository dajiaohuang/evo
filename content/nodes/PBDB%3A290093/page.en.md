## Three fossils define the species {#s-bd1b66adab}
One female and two male specimens from the Yixian Formation were described as a stem stick insect. The female preserves an abdominal operculum and the forewings a shoulder pad; dark longitudinal veins prompted a separate, tentative leaf-mimicry hypothesis.

## Feeding remains unobserved {#s-8f2fafac9b}
The proposed plant association comes from comparing wing pattern with a plant organ; the fossils do not preserve feeding.

## A named formation, not a full habitat range {#s-3a1400dc72}
All three described specimens come from the Yixian Formation of the Jehol biota in Inner Mongolia, China. This records their deposit, not the species' complete habitat range.

## What the forewings suggest {#s-a447b98be7}
Long forewings support describing a winged insect; flight capability is inferred from anatomy rather than observed.

## Measurements of the described specimens {#s-27b9e167e6}
The described female is about 56 mm long; the two males are about 47–51 mm.

## Leaf mimicry is a hypothesis {#s-2173169090}
Dark wing veins were proposed as possible leaf mimicry. A plant-associated crypsis guild is not directly observed.

## Age and sample limits {#s-1e51d85f5f}
The study dates the Yixian Formation to about 126 ± 4 Ma; the displayed 130–122 Ma window applies to these three specimens, not a global species range. The insect diagnosis uses preserved structures, while leaf mimicry remains a functional interpretation.

## A diagnosis from three specimens {#s-da0aedecd5}
The female and two male fossils come from the Yixian Formation in the Jehol biota of Inner Mongolia. The female abdominal operculum and forewing shoulder pad support the published stem-Phasmatodea diagnosis. Their formation is dated to about 126 ± 4 Ma; the 130–122 Ma display converts that deposit age and applies only to this sample.

## A wing pattern and a mimicry hypothesis {#s-9a434473c5}
The described wings have dark longitudinal veins. The study compared this pattern with a contemporaneous plant organ and proposed possible leaf mimicry. That is a functional interpretation: the fossils do not show camouflage in action or directly record diet, and three specimens cannot establish a species-wide ecological guild.
