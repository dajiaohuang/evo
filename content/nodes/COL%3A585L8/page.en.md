## Trianthema portulacastrum {#s-0619966eca}
## Unequal paired leaves and flowers tucked beside the sheaths

The historical regional description in the 2013 Flora of Panama treats this plant as an annual herb that may grow erect or prostrate. The two leaves at a node are strikingly unequal: the larger is at least twice the smaller, and the blades are obovate to rounded-spatulate, 1–3 cm long and 0.5–2 cm wide. Their tips may be acute, blunt or notched, sometimes ending in a small point; the bases are wedge-shaped and both surfaces hairless. Petioles join at the stem into a sheath, while the small stipules between them may have sparse teeth or entire edges.

Flowers are solitary and stalkless, with a bell-shaped perianth tube closely associated with the leaf sheath. The tube lobes are lanceolate, about 1.5 by 1 mm, and have a horn-like, barbed appendage. Each flower has ten stamens and a style about 1 mm long. The capsule sits nearly enclosed in the petiole sheath, is about 4.5 mm long, and splits around its middle. It contains two to five wrinkled, kidney-shaped seeds, reddish-brown to black, about 1.5–1.75 mm across.

These details belong to that historical Panama-region flora entry; they are not presented as a current or global account.
