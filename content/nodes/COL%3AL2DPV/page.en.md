## Habit and stems {#s-5f78f9d7c3}
The Flora of Panama describes the plant as herbaceous to partly woody. Its small branches are cylindrical in cross-section and pubescent or nearly hairless. They may bear a few or many thorns with broad bases and black tips.

## Leaves {#s-1bdb74e79c}
The leaves are described as moderate to moderately large and sensitive. They are bipinnate: each leaf divides into pinnae, which then carry leaflets. Three to five pinna pairs, usually four, cluster near the petiole tip in an almost finger-like arrangement. There are usually about 30 or more leaflet pairs. The petiole reaches 8 cm and is usually bristly but lacks glands; the leaf axis is very short (under 3 mm), while pinnae reach 12 cm. A bristly pulvinus, or swollen joint, sits at the base of each pinna. The linear leaflets are 4–9 mm long and usually 1–2 mm wide, usually pointed at the tip and unevenly rounded at the base. Their midrib and widely spaced lateral veins are slightly raised. The leaflets are hairless except for bristles along the margins and sometimes on the lower surface. The linear-lanceolate stipules (small leaf-like appendages at leaf bases) are sickle-shaped, bristly, and about 5 mm long.

## Inflorescences and flowers {#s-4ffee73ee4}
Flower clusters arise from leaf axils as stalked heads or very short spikes. Their short stalks are usually just over 1 cm; each dense head bears many flowers. The narrow, nearly linear bractlets have bristly, finely cut edges. Flowers are pinkish or purplish and lack stalks. The calyx is minute; the glabrous, four-lobed corolla is funnel-shaped and about 2 mm long. Four free stamens extend several millimetres. The ovary is glabrous and described by the source as bilateral; the style is as long as the stamens.

## Fruits {#s-7b77f97928}
The legumes (pods) usually form dense clusters. Each is typically 10–15 mm long and about 4 mm wide, flat, hairless, and divided into segments, with a constriction between the jointed segments. Each segment contains one seed. The pod margins bear many bristles and prickles and remain after the segments disperse.

## Historical distribution statement {#s-2797f84195}
The Flora of Panama distribution field places the reported range from Panama to Brazil. This is a historical regional statement, not a current or global range inventory; the excerpt does not establish whether the locations are native, introduced, cultivated, or wild.
