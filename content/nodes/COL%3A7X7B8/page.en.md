## Foraging strata differ from whole-day activity {#s-a5e4faf845}
The archive marks these species-level foraging-stratum weights: understory, edges or shrubs below 2 m 50%; trees or high bushes from 2 m upward but below the canopy 50%. They estimate relative use of feeding positions, not nest sites, all habitat or a full-day time budget. The source defines surface-water, understory, mid/high, canopy and aerial categories separately. They are not elevation bands and cannot establish flight height or habitat area.

## Evidence limits {#s-83d4d7a42d}
Only one supported ecological field group is interpreted here; the other diet, activity and stratum fields remain unfilled. The EltonTraits 1.0 values here are historical foraging records matched by name and family; they do not make a complete species monograph. Matching names and classification do not show that historical species concepts are equivalent to COL26.8 concepts. Food and bird-foraging-stratum percentages are coarse relative weights that the authors standardized from literature descriptions; they are not direct measures of stomach contents, intake, energy or observation time. Avian A/B diet codes still carry source and proportional uncertainty. Mammalian ABC diet or activity codes may also refer to a congener the compilers judged ecologically similar; not every field is claimed as a direct observation of this species. Genus/family typical values, non-species-level avian strata and split records needing individual review do not fill definite conclusions. The underlying books and papers are not individually summarized here. Other sources are needed for individual, seasonal and local variation, current range, population and conservation.
