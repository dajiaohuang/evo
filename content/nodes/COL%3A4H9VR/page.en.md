## What does Phyllopodium multifolium look like? {#s-4dda5ad3b9}
This glandular-hairy annual can reach 37 cm. Its leaves are egg-shaped to inversely lance-shaped and may appear crowded near the base. Dense flower heads lengthen into spikes as they develop. The flowers are blue or mauve with a yellow patch at the base of the upper lobes.

## How does it differ from related plants? {#s-fd272d81e3}
At Potteberg and Silver River, the source contrasts it with P. elegans: P. multifolium has mauve rather than white flowers, broader basal leaves, more glandular hairs, and a somewhat larger corolla. Both can be mistaken for Selago, but Phyllopodium has many ovules per ovary chamber and produces many-seeded capsules; Selago has two ovules and two one-seeded fruit segments.
