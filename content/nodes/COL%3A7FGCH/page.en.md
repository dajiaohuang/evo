## Accepted name and compiled traits {#s-b677459b0e}
COL26.8 accepts Varanus albigularis in Varanidae. The ReptTraits v1-2 profile retains the workbook’s Reptile Database 2023 name framework and cites individual traits. A separate 2015 life-history archive also contains same-name rows; those records are introduced below as a distinct historical source.

## Length measured before the tail {#s-bc661a98b9}
The reported maximum snout–vent length is 850 mm, and the female maximum is 518.3 mm. Snout–vent length ends at the body opening rather than the tail tip. These compiled maxima may come from different studies or localities; they are not typical sizes, growth limits, or enough on their own to measure population-wide sexual dimorphism.

## A habitat list and broad activity labels {#s-d6b2a77fa9}
The profile classifies microhabitat as arboreal, saxicolous (associated with rock), and terrestrial. It also records a carnivorous diet, diurnal activity, and foraging mode ACT (active foraging). These are broad, potentially overlapping categories; they do not specify a prey list, a particular site, or a fixed daily routine.

## Egg laying and the profile’s reproductive summaries {#s-fadd733031}
The reproductive mode is oviparous, or egg-laying. The sex-determination entry is GSD (ZW/ZZ), a genetic sex-determination category. The consolidated litter or clutch value is 32, with reported mean endpoints of 2 and 51; the annual summary is one clutch. These are aggregated records, not guaranteed counts in each clutch. The endpoints describe reported means, not necessarily the smallest and largest observed clutches.

## A second, older archive under the same name {#s-9e16f6e755}
COL26.8 places the accepted name in Varanidae. Separately, this page uses five focal fields from a 2015 life-history archive with same-name, same-family sparse rows. That archive can retain historical taxonomic inheritance, so the name match supports a cautious comparison but does not make its historical concept identical to the pinned one.

## Adult and hatchling mass refer to different stages {#s-8de609d77e}
The archive summarizes adult body mass as 8,000 g and birth or hatching mass as 21 g. Adult mass can combine records for males, females, and adults of unspecified sex. The two values describe different life stages and are not a measured growth trajectory or a range for individual body masses.

## The archive’s clutch and incubation summaries {#s-a9fa041e50}
In the 2015 archive, litter or clutch size is summarized as 24 offspring or eggs, and oviposition-to-hatching time as 138.5 days. The first field combines offspring and egg counts; it should not be merged with the separate ReptTraits value of 32 as though both were a single measurement. Incubation time refers to a different event from maturity or clutch production.

## A maximum longevity record is not a typical lifespan {#s-72b07385e0}
The archive reports maximum longevity of 16.98 years, using the largest record found under its method. This is not a mean wild lifespan, a survival limit for every animal, or a current record shown to be repeatedly attainable.

## Following the source trail {#s-c1914d3077}
The same-name historical rows cite sources including de Magalhaes and Costa (2009), Meiri (2010), Meiri, Brown, and Sibly (2011), and BC Reptiles (2013), among others. The archive retains field-specific raw row numbers, values, and labels. A row count is not a count of measured animals, and repeated citations are not independent experiments.

## Evidence limits {#s-2687518cec}
The ReptTraits page retains a 2023 taxonomy framework; the life-history archive is from 2015 and may carry inherited records across historical species or subspecies. Matching name and family do not establish equivalence with the COL26.8 concept. Archive values are compiled medians or averages of the two middle values, except maximum longevity, which uses the largest record found. They are not direct measurements of one individual, population ranges, or current surveys; wild or captive conditions are not uniformly confirmed. Underlying papers were not individually reviewed here. Current distribution, conservation, venom, and traits absent from these sources remain unaddressed.
