## A bushy shrub with lightly divided leaves {#s-a0729fb53a}
*Polemannia simplicior* is described as a bushy shrub up to 3 m high, with yellow flowers. Its leaflets are usually undivided, only rarely divided, and have moderately pointed tips.

## Separating it from *Polemannia montana* {#s-52fac2c870}
The two species have subtle formal differences, yet the flora account says they are easy to tell apart in the field across a broad area. It argues that their broad, parallel occurrence and field distinction weigh against treating them as subspecies. *P. simplicior* is bushy and favors open mountain slopes, though it also grows beside rocky streams at high elevations, including Ben McDhui. *P. montana* is usually a slender shrub with cane-like branches, occasionally a small tree, associated with rocky stream banks and river boulder beds. Its leaflets are coarsely toothed or more deeply divided and sharply pointed, unlike the less divided, moderately pointed leaflets of *P. simplicior*. Both can resprout from the base, and the account says the leaf differences persist on these shoots.

At Sani Pass the distinction becomes less clear: collections there span a wide range of leaf forms, with intermediates. The account suggests *P. montana* probably grows along rivers and *P. simplicior* on steep, unstable valley slopes; it considers hybridization likely and cites collections Killick & Vahrmeijer 3770 (PRE) and Hilliard & Burtt 15646 (E, NU) as examples. Sterile twigs have also been confused with *Heteromorpha*. In *Polemannia*, the vein network is coarser and includes a distinctive vein just inside the leaf margin, which *Heteromorpha* lacks.
