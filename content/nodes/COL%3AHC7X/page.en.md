## What happens to its growth in a laboratory study? {#s-871e1d41d1}
In one experiment, laboratory-grown *Aspergillus nidulans* exposed to dichlobenil showed changes in hyphal form and growth, alongside changes in the expression of cell-wall-related genes. Hyphae are the threadlike filaments that make up a fungal body. These findings apply to the tested chemical exposure and conditions, not to the fungus’s untreated natural form.

## Hülle cells formed spore-producing colonies {#s-901b8a3e83}
A separate laboratory study found Hülle cells at several positions along hyphae. When these specialized cells germinated, they grew into hyphae and colonies that produced conidiospores, or asexual spores. In the reported experiment, colonies started from Hülle cells produced conidiospores sooner than colonies started from spores. This is a specific developmental observation, not a complete life-cycle account.
