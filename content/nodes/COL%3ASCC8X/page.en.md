## Accepted name and placement {#s-002dd4641b}
COL26.8 accepts Pristimantis tamia Ortega, Brito-M. & Ron, 2022 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Strabomantidae (family) → Pristimantis (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## Larger sex samples with an absent membrane but present annulus {#s-bfcd99a1be}
Tamia females had reported SVL 21.27–29.88 mm, average 25.96, from twenty-one adults; fifteen males were 16.04–19.81 mm, average 18.28. The diagnosis included a present tympanic annulus but absent membrane, no basal toe webbing and no vocal slits or nuptial pads. Heel tubercles were absent in some individuals, and the described iris colours ranged from violet to light greenish blue. These are diagnostic observations and variation, not an independently measured communication mechanism or universal colour criterion. The external membrane state is distinct from internal ear anatomy.

## Primary-forest perches from half a metre to five metres {#s-4890fc7f0a}
Tamia was recorded around Zarentza in Llanganates at 1,221–1,419 m in Eastern Montane and Eastern Foothill Forest. All specimens were collected at night on leaves or branches 50–500 cm above ground in primary forest, sometimes near creeks. The five-metre observation is not a maximum climbing limit or a quantified preference for high perches. Nearby water does not establish stream breeding, and primary-forest collection alone does not prove inability to use secondary habitat. The short account does not give egg, tadpole or complete breeding-season observations.

## A locally common collection sample is not a complete population estimate {#s-8439967229}
The authors called tamia one of the most common anurans in the type-locality collections, with seventy-four individuals recorded during 14–27 February 2015, yet assigned Data Deficient because overall population size and range were unknown. The count is a collection-period observation, not standardized density or a current census. Their 2018 map estimated 11.55% deforestation within five kilometres of known localities, which is a land-cover statistic rather than proportional population loss. Local commonness and buffered mapping do not independently establish low extinction risk or current demographic stability.
