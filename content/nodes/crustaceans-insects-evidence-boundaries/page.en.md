## Thirteen dossiers from Cambrian mandibles to insect genomes {#s-3db7c9fd30}
Follow thirteen fossil, phylogenomic, developmental, and dating studies without turning Pancrustacea, terrestrialization, or flight into one ancestor ladder.

## Two Tokummia specimens carry matrix characters {#s-aad05382b7}
Holotype ROM 63823 and paratype ROM 63824 preserve appendage combinations scored in the published character matrix. Their placement is a phylogenetic result: neither specimen shows Tokummia to be a direct ancestor or a crown crustacean, insect, or myriapod.

## A larger Waptia sample reveals more mandibles {#s-bac979de0a}
ROMIP 56432 illustrates mandibles and maxillules; ROMIP 64294 preserves disarticulated, opposing mandibles within a larger specimen series. Those structures inform anatomical and phylogenetic comparisons, but active swimming predation is an interpretation, not stomach contents or directly observed behavior.

## Phosphatization preserves Yicaris growth stages {#s-9d55545961}
The Yicaris topotype series includes several developmental stages. Phosphatized soft-part anatomy supports reconstruction of one stage with eight thoracic segments (thoracomeres). This sample shows growth variation, but does not fix the crown crustacean origin or settle whether similar appendages are homologous epipodites.

## Nine transcriptomes test an undersampled branch {#s-172073a875}
Nine new 454 transcriptomes were combined with morphology, fossils, expressed sequence tags, genomes, mitochondrial proteins, and ribosomal data. Deep placements, including ostracod monophyly, change with fast-evolving genes, outgroups, and data partitions; the resulting branches are inferred relationships, not fossils.

## Remipedes repeatedly group with hexapods {#s-35f367e9ef}
With expanded remipede sampling, analyses repeatedly recover Remipedia and Hexapoda as sister groups while supporting several major pancrustacean clades. Other deep relationships remain sensitive to sampling and method, so this is a tested tree hypothesis rather than a settled ancestor ladder.

## Seven added taxa reshape the pancrustacean matrix {#s-d8f16dbf50}
The final matrix samples 105 taxa—90 transcriptomes, 15 genomes, and 576 protein-coding genes—and is compared with a 98-taxon matrix assembled through the same pipeline. Recovered clades vary with dataset and model; divergence dates are clock estimates informed by vetted calibrations, not observed fossil events.

## A Devonian springtail-like fossil preserves a furcula {#s-ef4130cc24}
The Rhyniella sample includes four head capsules and about ten later body specimens. It contains the first reported intact abdomen and a furcula, a forked springing structure comparable to that of living springtails. This occurrence supports collembolan affinity but does not date the global origin of hexapods or their move onto land.

## One head fragment yields competing identities {#s-279bed9ac6}
NHMUK PI IN 38234 is an isolated head fragment. A 2004 study interpreted its mandibles as dicondylic—having two jaw hinges—and possibly pterygote-like; a 2017 three-dimensional reanalysis found a better fit with a myriapod, perhaps a scutigeromorph centipede. The conflicting readings leave insect and flight calibrations uncertain.

## Genes and fossils model routes onto land {#s-54c96e367f}
The core dataset combines 57 taxa and 246 genes; fossil calibrations and a 30-species panarthropod subset test alternative trees. The favored marine route and its dates depend on topology, calibrations, and ancestral-state coding. They are model outputs, not observed migrations.

## One Carboniferous wing fragment records venation {#s-cb35051ffb}
An approximately 10 mm crushed forewing from lowermost Namurian strata preserves cross-veins and branches of ScP, R, and other main veins. Archaeorthopteran affinity is inferred from incomplete venation; one fragment cannot date powered flight or the first global occurrence of Pterygota.

## Gene perturbations test a wing-homology model {#s-d868a8807b}
CRISPR perturbations and expression domains in Parhyale are compared with proximal leg segments, body-wall structures, and wings in crickets and other insects. These developmental correspondences test a homology model; they do not identify the first insect wing or a unique fossil precursor.

## A 1,478-gene matrix estimates insect branching times {#s-1349c7048e}
The study samples 144 species and 1,478 protein-coding genes, including archived transcriptomes from BioProject PRJNA183205. Tree topology and node ages depend on taxon sampling, orthology, sequence models, fossil assignments, and clock priors; the ages are estimates, not observed fossil first appearances.

## Five Pennsylvanian fossils broaden the sample {#s-1dec9014b1}
The described Pennsylvanian sample includes a stem coleopterid, a holometabolous larva, a stem hymenopterid, an early hemipteran, and an early psocodean. These fossils broaden the record but do not show synchronous origins, exact first appearances of crown orders, or one climatic cause for later diversification.
