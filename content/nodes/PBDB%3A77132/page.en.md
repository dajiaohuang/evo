## Overview {#s-52fa93b363}
Frasnian Tiktaalik roseae is documented by NUFV 108 and other Fram Formation material from southern Ellesmere Island. Its preserved body combines scales and fin rays with a mobile neck, robust ribs and wrist-like joints within a pectoral fin. The combination informs comparisons of fins and limbs; it does not identify a direct ancestor.

## Diet {#s-c3888dab09}
An aquatic predatory role is inferred from anatomy; the cited sample does not preserve specific prey.

## Habitat {#s-9783a3ff96}
The fossils come from a shallow marginal-water setting in the Fram Formation, not a complete map of the genus’ distribution.

## Locomotion {#s-10331f6c59}

## A fin with a distinctive combination {#s-b648f075ae}
The named specimens retain scales and fin rays while also preserving wrist-like joints, a mobile neck and robust ribs. These structures occur together in one body, letting researchers compare anatomical combinations across early tetrapodomorphs.

## From preserved bones to movement {#s-4f72cfe23c}
A joint or rib constrains what movements may have been possible, but it does not record a daily action. Load-bearing use of the fin and routine walking on land remain interpretations rather than direct observations.

## The sample has a geographic boundary {#s-770b1319da}
The material discussed here comes from the Fram Formation on southern Ellesmere Island. That named fossil sample supports a local occurrence; it does not map the full range of Tiktaalik.

## Anatomical affinity is not ancestry {#s-80cde4ace7}
The studied character combination informs a phylogenetic comparison. It does not make Tiktaalik a demonstrated direct ancestor of later tetrapods.
