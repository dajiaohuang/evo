## atlas-profile / overview {#s-9fe659629d}
Grass history comes from evidence at different scales: phytoliths in latest-Cretaceous coprolites from India, later phytolith assemblages in Great Plains soils, and a sampled plastid family tree. A coprolite can preserve what passed through an animal; soil assemblages track vegetation at a regional scale; a tree models relationships among sampled lineages. None alone supplies one grass-origin date, a universal C4 history or a global grassland narrative.

## atlas-profile / ecology / diet {#s-c359c52826}
Grasses are photosynthetic primary producers. The fossil samples do not establish one ecological role for every member of the family.

## atlas-profile / ecology / habitat {#s-2800761220}
The Great Plains palaeosols record heterogeneous mixtures of C3 and C4 vegetation in the sampled region, not a single global grass habitat.

## atlas-profile / ecology / locomotion {#s-c4176c678d}
Grass plants grow in place; this biological description does not identify the vegetation structure represented by any one phytolith sample.

## atlas-profile / ecology / bodySize {#s-fd493ee4b8}
Phytolith assemblages do not yield a family-wide body-size range.

## atlas-profile / ecology / guild {#s-73f86b702c}
The evidence supports the primary-producer role of grasses. C4 biomass in a sample does not by itself show that open grassland dominated the landscape.

## atlas-profile / traits {#s-16cfe48620}
The Lameta coprolites contain phytolith shapes that the cited study identifies as diagnostic of grasses; this microscopic evidence is tied to those specimens and their locality.

## atlas-profile / traits {#s-9ebbd0115d}
Great Plains samples include chloridoid and other short-cell phytoliths considered potentially C4 PACMAD grasses; the assignment describes the sampled assemblages.

## atlas-profile / traits {#s-22a8220f4b}
On the sampled plastid tree, C4 photosynthesis is reconstructed as arising more than once rather than as a single family-wide innovation.

## atlas-profile / evidenceSummary {#s-71183bc14d}
Prasad and colleagues describe a disputed phytolith assemblage from a particular Lameta locality; Strömberg and McInerney quantify a later regional change in Great Plains soils; GPWG II reconstructs multiple C4 origins on a sampled plastid tree. Together, these studies do not establish a body-fossil first appearance, a crown age or global vegetation cover.

## atlas-profile / readerLimitations {#s-ac209e0473}
The profile joins three evidence scales: a disputed phytolith assemblage in one Lameta coprolite sample, regional Great Plains soils and a sampled plastid tree. Together they do not establish a body-fossil first appearance, a single C4 origin or global grassland dominance.
