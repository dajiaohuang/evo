## Beak, wing and compiled mass have different denominators {#s-a6142c2312}
Morphology covers 8 birds (4 female and 4 male), with 4 complete measurements. Mean nostril-to-tip beak length is 47.9 mm, beak width at the nostrils 18.6 mm, unflattened wing 226.4 mm and hand-wing index 58. These are species averages; the index is defined by Kipp’s distance/wing length ×100, not flight speed. Mass 612.56 g comes separately from Dunning’s literature compilation and cannot inherit the morphology sample size. Inference=NO retains the workbook’s non-inference flag without supplying individual bounds from unread raw material.

## Read feeding niche and trophic level separately {#s-c882bae0ce}
Habitat is Wetland and density code 3 means open habitats. Trophic.Niche=Aquatic predator denotes an aquatic-animal-oriented niche; Trophic.Level=Omnivore denotes a category with more balanced use across trophic levels. The columns have different definitions. Original labels are retained without rewriting either from the other or deriving seasonal diet percentages. Aquatic lifestyle means substantial time on water and feeding while afloat or diving; it does not specify dive depth, frequency or the species’ particular feeding action.

## Movement category and the observations still needed {#s-6d30f454ec}
Migration=3 classifies the species by long-distance migration in the majority of its population. The row contains no individual tracks, departure month or full route, and does not require every bird to move identically. Hand-wing index and habitat labels cannot replace behavioural observations. Locality, timing and explicit sources are needed to connect the category to particular populations’ life histories.
