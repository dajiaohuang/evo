## Context-dependent hoo calls {#s-dd9d96618b}
At Khao Yai National Park, Thailand, a close-range study analyzed 462 hoo calls from 14 males and 11 females observed over 117 days (about 600 hours). The authors compared 9 social and behavioral contexts. Acoustic parameters differed among contexts with enough calls; for example, raptor-context calls had lower peak and low frequencies than duet calls. Alert, separation, and snake-response contexts were too sparse for comparable inference. The study shows context-related variation in this sample; it did not test how listeners respond or represent the full repertoire.

## Responses to duet and predator songs {#s-f84f9769db}
At the same park, 12 playback trials involved 6 habituated groups. After duet-song playback, 5 groups counter-sang; predator-song playback elicited antipredator responses but no duet singing. These are reactions to selected recordings in a limited experiment, not a demonstration of every duet function or of the full call repertoire in the wild.
