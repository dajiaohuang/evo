## Age at first birth in two mountain populations {#s-7bc7a8ac15}
Estimated age at first birth was similar for the mountain gorilla females studied at Virunga and Bwindi: 10.1 ± 1.7 years for 56 females at Virunga and 10.5 ± 1.3 years for 10 at Bwindi. The comparison was not statistically significant (p = 0.55), which does not prove the populations are the same. The sample also excludes Grauer's gorillas and does not cover every Eastern gorilla population.

## Evolutionary evidence {#s-acd132a5e0}
In a mitochondrial-genome comparison focused on Grauer's gorillas, historical samples had 20 haplotypes and modern samples had 11; the authors reported lower haplotype and nucleotide diversity in the modern sample. The comparison included 68 historical and 29 modern individuals. It uses mitochondrial markers, not nuclear genomes; the authors attributed the change to peripheral-population loss, an inference bounded to their samples and model.

## Field sites and observation effort {#robbins16-field-sites}
Robbins et al. compared habituated wild mountain-gorilla groups at Karisoke in Volcanoes National Park, Rwanda, and Bwindi Impenetrable National Park, Uganda. Three Karisoke groups were followed regularly in 2006–2008, with six additional groups observed after fissions and new formations through 2014; one Bwindi group was observed for 13 years, with no calendar endpoints reported for that duration. The nearest points of the two populations were about 30 km apart and the study groups about 45 km apart, with cultivated and developed land between them. These are two field sites and their observation histories, not a complete range assessment or a species-wide behavioral-frequency estimate.
