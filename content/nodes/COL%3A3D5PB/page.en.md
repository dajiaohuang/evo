## Euryops montanus {#s-6d80413620}
## A compact green shrub with constricted leaf bases

Nordenstam describes a dense, rounded dwarf shrub 20–60 cm tall, erect or ascending and hairless except in the leaf axils. Young branches are leafy at the tips; lower stems lose their leaves and retain overlapping leaf-bases and scars. Leaves are crowded, linear and 5–20 mm long, either entire or divided into two to four lobes. They are leathery and bright green, with a clear midrib; each leaf narrows above its widened, partly stem-clasping base. The base persists after leaves wither as a triangular scale.

Flower heads are solitary and terminal, sessile or nearly so. The involucre is a narrow bell or almost cylindrical cup, 5–9 mm high, with 8–12 bracts in one row. Heads have 6–10 yellow ray florets and about 8–15 disc florets. The white pappus bristles are usually 1.5–2 mm long. Hairless, shiny brown to yellow-brown dry fruits are 3–4 mm long and have 7–10 low ribs.

The constriction above the widened leaf-base and the pure green colour distinguish *E. montanus* from the closely allied, duller blue-green *E. decumbens*. A historical locality note mentions streambanks on the Drakensberg summit, but Nordenstam says the species also grows in varied rocky or stony places and often in alpine Erica–Helichrysum heath, sometimes alongside *E. decumbens*.
