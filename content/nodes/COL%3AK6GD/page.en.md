## Leaves, flowers, and season {#s-2baf291dbd}
The 2013 regional account describes a corm-bearing geophyte 80–150 mm tall, with an inclined, smooth, occasionally branched stem. The leaf sheaths are cobwebby; the lance-shaped blades are sword-shaped, wavy-edged, twisted near the tip, and have a prominent midrib. They are smooth or have ciliate margins. The account's flowering phrase is “Flowers 76–127 mm a horizontal secund spike”; the 76–127 mm measure is not clearly attached to a structure. It describes the spike as horizontal and one-sided, with bilabiate flowers that are pale yellow or flushed with mauve and have yellow-green nectar guides. The perianth tube is about 7 mm long, the upper tepal 20–25 mm, the ovary and bracts smooth, and the inner bracts forked in their upper third. Flowering is recorded from June to early August.

These traits, measurements, and flowering period come from a regional South African account published in 2013; the excerpt gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency.

## Distinguishing Babiana sinuata {#s-a7571d6e9e}
A 2020 account identifies *B. striata* by its unusual inclined leaves, with wavy, tightly crisped margins, a smooth surface or at most ciliate edges, and a loosely twisted blade. Its fairly small flowers sit on an inclined to horizontal spike and have pale-yellow tepals flushed light purple on the outside. The cataphylls and leaf bases are cobwebby, as in most species of section Antholyzoides.

The account says *B. striata* is often confused with blue-flowered *B. sinuata*, which has similarly wavy, twisted leaves but otherwise differs. In *B. sinuata*, long hairs line the leaf-sheath margins and upper blade margins. Its larger flowers have an upper tepal about 28 mm long, versus 20–25 mm in *B. striata*; they are pale blue, with pale-yellow lower tepals tipped pale blue and a conspicuous dark-violet mark on the lower lateral tepals. *B. sinuata* also has filaments about 20 mm long and joined anthers, a feature that remains visible in pressed specimens. In *B. striata*, the filaments are about 15 mm long and the anthers are separate. The flowers appeared unscented in live plants examined near Kotzesrus; the account does not generalize this observation beyond those plants.

These diagnostic comparisons come from a regional South African account published in 2020; the excerpt gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency.
