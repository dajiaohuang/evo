## Several leaf forms and vein-count alternatives {#s-32609f1329}
The Brazilian Flora 2020 v393.147 field lists star-shaped or tree-like hairs on young branches. Leaves may be elliptic, ovate, or lance-shaped, with acuminate tips and rounded, wedge-shaped, or blunt bases. Their margins are ciliate (fringed with hairs) or finely toothed. Veins arise at the base. The field lists either five lengthwise veins, three plus two faint veins just inside the margin, or five plus two faint intramarginal veins. The underside has sparse or medium-density hairs, described as sessile (without stalks) and star-shaped. The alternatives are not assigned frequencies.

## Four petals and a persistent calyx {#s-dbba73fa6e}
Flowers grow in dichasial clusters, a branching arrangement. The flower cup (hypanthium) is bell-shaped. The calyx persists, has irregular lobes, and is closed in bud. Each flower has four petals. The connective, the central tissue in an anther, is not extended; its appendage has one upper (dorsal) tooth. Anthers are yellow with one small pollen opening, and the ovary bears hairs.

## A terrestrial label, not a distribution account {#s-1477d4976c}
The habitat field records “Terrícola,” meaning terrestrial. It gives no locality, survey date, or range coverage and does not identify wild, cultivated, managed, or fossil material. The habitat category supplies no geographic range.
