## Shared traits among living rhinos {#s-b1672e1912}
Living rhinoceroses have robust limbs, normally with three digits on each foot. Their horns are made of keratin. Feeding ranges from browsing to grazing; one species does not represent the ecology of the entire family.

## Where to compare life-history fields {#s-5d454a3c9d}
Five historical life-history accounts are represented here: *Ceratotherium simum*, *Dicerorhinus sumatrensis*, *Diceros bicornis*, *Rhinoceros sondaicus* and *Rhinoceros unicornis*. Each account reports adult body mass, female age at first conception, offspring per litter or eggs per clutch, and time from conception or fertilization to birth. These counts describe the entries available here, not the full accepted-species roster. Check each field’s event, unit and source conditions before comparing values.

## Do these accounts show a shared ecology? {#s-77627b0dba}
The same five accounts report food-category weights, foraging strata and mammalian activity periods. Food weights are coarse estimates, while strata are categories and activity fields are binary codes. These records can be compared only with their source and measurement limits in view; they do not show that the entire family shares one ecology.

## How to read reproductive timing and lifespan {#s-c33b8585b5}
All five accounts report gestation, female age at first reproduction and maximum lifespan. First reproduction is distinct from first birth and sexual maturity; maximum lifespan is distinct from average lifespan. Source conditions can differ among traits. Missing fields need other sources, and a matching name does not resolve historical changes in species concepts.

## Limits of the historical life-history records {#s-bffa101141}
These five historical life-history accounts do not represent every accepted descendant or the family’s complete biology. They use matching names and families plus a one-to-one MSW93/MSW05 same-name table; equivalence to current species concepts remains unproven. Except for maximum lifespan, relevant fields generally average literature sources with equal weighting. Inputs can include published averages, individual values or range midpoints when other data are unavailable. Sample sizes do not determine the weights, and source studies may overlap. Maximum lifespan is the longest available record, not typical lifespan. Adult mass is not interpreted in these entries, and relatives do not fill missing fields. Original reference numbers remain on each page; the cited species literature and all wild, captive and locality conditions have not been individually established here.
