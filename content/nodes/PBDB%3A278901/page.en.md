## The holotype and its preserved parts {#s-1d501f930c}
The 2025 Araripe review describes holotype MN 4726-V as a nearly complete skull and lower jaw, accompanied by a scapula, coracoid, sternum, humerus, part of the right first phalanx of wing digit IV and rib fragments. This is not a complete skeleton. The review retains the original authors’ adult interpretation but gives no exact age, sex or growth series.

## Features used to identify the species {#s-80fde844ed}
The diagnosis combines a front-to-back premaxillary crest, a downturned snout and a slightly concave lower skull margin. The front of the jaws meets without a gap. The jugal’s lacrimal process points backward, and the quadrate lies at about 150° to the lower skull margin. These features describe the specimen; the crest does not demonstrate sex, display or temperature control.

## How palate openings are defined {#s-2261c13a93}
A 2024 study used CT and laminography of other pterosaurs to redraw the boundaries among the maxilla, palatine, pterygoid and ectopterygoid. Reassigning bones changes the names applied to some openings, and earlier Caupedactylus illustrations enter that comparison. The older label slit-like postpalatinal fenestra must be read using that paper’s bone boundaries; matching names do not guarantee that studies mean the same opening.

## Comparison without a new scan {#s-d09deabe61}
Table 1 of the 2024 paper lists MN 4726-V but does not mark it as newly scanned; the Caupedactylus palate illustration is credited as modified from earlier work. The authors suggest a possible Y-shaped palatine, larger pterygoid openings and a more rearward margin of the choanae. These are comparisons and interpretations, not structures newly revealed by a CT scan of this holotype.

## Other referred material remains uncertain {#s-8541eb0701}
A complete skull sold at auction in 2009 has an unknown present location. Palatal fragment MPSC R 859 resembles the holotype in its flat palate before the choanae, but its breakage prevents a firm referral. The review also compares SMNK PAL 6410 and discusses KPMNH DL 84. These accounts do not confirm all material as Caupedactylus, establish an individual count or extend the species range.

## A placement proposed in later analyses {#s-596da5b04f}
The original study treated the species as an early-diverging tapejarid. Later analyses summarized in the 2025 review group it with Aymberedactylus cearensis in Caupedactylia, citing features such as no step-like dorsal ridge on the mandibular symphysis and a flat dentary-fossa surface. The directory’s Tapejarinae parent records database classification, not every published tree.

## A feeding interpretation from modeling {#s-8640d5526f}
The 2025 review summarizes a 2021 bite-force study that interpreted Caupedactylus ybaka as a possible ground-feeding generalist. Jaw features and lower muscular mechanical advantage support a different functional model from the hard-plant diet proposed for Tapejara. This is an interpretation reported through the review, not gut contents, an observed meal or a demonstrated division of ecological roles.

## The formation record remains unresolved {#s-71b3d94b4d}
The 2025 review assigns the holotype to the Romualdo Formation and says its exact locality is unknown; the 2013 original paper’s title also names Romualdo. The PBDB collection row instead lists Crato and 119.57–113.2 Ma. Keep the discrepancy visible: a database row does not fill in the holotype locality or directly date the specimen.

## The PBDB name and record counts {#s-20a90b01a5}
PBDB accepts Caupedactylus ybaka under Caupedactylus. The selected data contain 1 species-level occurrence from 1 collection and 1 reference identifier; these are database counts, not specimens or animals.

## The collection recorded in PBDB {#s-f628de6201}
The database collection is Between Nova Olinda and Santana do Cariri (Ceará), region code BR, stratum Crato, with age bracket 119.57–113.2 Ma and early interval Late Aptian. This describes the database collection row; the review lists the holotype locality as unknown.

## The age range of this record {#s-737805616c}
Across the selected record, the outer age envelope is 119.57–113.2 Ma (Ma means millions of years ago). It is not a continuous record or a measured origin, extinction or lifespan. The broad database bracket is not a direct date for the holotype.

## Collection setting and preservation {#s-b46ad73c80}
The database labels the collection environment terrestrial indet. and preservation mode body, original phosphate. These are collection-level descriptions; they do not establish the animal’s living habitat or the condition of every fossil.

## Reference linked to the collection {#s-aaefbdcf30}
PBDB links the collection to Kellner (2013), reference ref:48270, DOI 10.1017/S1755691013000327. The paper title names Romualdo, while the database row says Crato; the 2025 review says the holotype locality is unknown. The three records preserve a real discrepancy rather than one resolved locality.

## Scope of the evidence {#s-8610a2c433}
The holotype’s locality remains unknown in the review, and its formation differs between that account and the database collection row. Additional skull material is referred only conditionally, and feeding and family placement depend on later comparisons and models. The record does not supply a growth series or complete distribution.
