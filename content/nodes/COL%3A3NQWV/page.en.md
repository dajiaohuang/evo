## Name and classification in COL26.8 {#s-15a5fea5a4}
COL26.8 (Catalogue of Life dataset 316115, released 20 August 2026) records *Hyperolius olivaceus* as an accepted species. Its recorded hierarchy is Anura (order) → Hyperoliidae (family) → Hyperolius (genus). This is the classification in that release; the name and placement alone do not describe the organism’s biology.

## What this record establishes {#s-36af1d552e}
Alongside the catalogue placement, this record summarizes observations from the named studies and datasets below. Each result belongs to the source taxon concept, sample, place, date, and method stated with it; a shared name alone does not establish that the source concept matches the current classification. These records do not cover every member or every part of the taxon’s biology.

## Sampled records preserve source localities and names {#s-54ed7e2cf3}
The narrative includes eight LMR frogs, twelve from other Gabon sites and five Congo frogs originally called cinnamomeoventris. These records carry the source label Hyperolius olivaceus. Museum references outside the road corridor are not automatically LMR observations, and a bounded survey non-detection is not a complete range or absence finding. Original vouchers and present occurrence have not been independently inspected here.

## Working identification and reference conflicts remain explicit {#s-7b20631ad8}
The authors explicitly reassign these25 samples to olivaceus following a cited revision. They occupy three geographically structured COI BINs but one ABGD group and one16S clade. This uses the article’s final name, not an automatic synonymization of all cinnamomeoventris references; remaining reference mixtures are flagged for revision.

## Two mitochondrial markers and uneven references limit inference {#s-e810b31eb7}
For Hyperolius olivaceus, the study’s identification workflow combines COI BIN/ABGD clustering,16S reference comparison and morphology/type-locality context. COI and16S are both mitochondrial and do not constitute independent nuclear confirmation or reproductive-isolation tests. The authors use neighbor joining to cluster similar sequences, explicitly limiting its use for evolutionary-history inference. Most per-species material came from only one or two localities, and not all GenBank vouchers could be morphologically examined. Reference labels and missing sequences therefore remain evidence limits. Fieldwork took place in May–June2012 and March–April2013; GenBank/BOLD comparison queries refer to the2016 source-library snapshot, not a refreshed present-day database audit.
