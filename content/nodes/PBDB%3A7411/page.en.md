## From isolated plates to a body-plan model {#s-4909d4e981}
Micrina is represented by separated hard plates, not an articulated animal. Sellate plates preserve paired muscle scars; projections on mitral plates are interpreted as muscle platforms. Together they support a testable reconstruction of a sessile, two-valved body, but the proposed articulation and valve homology are not preserved.

## Feeding is not preserved {#s-ddb2c0ebe1}
The isolated plates do not record diet or feeding mode.

## Habitat in the reconstruction {#s-9b5d87cb5e}
The reconstructed animal is sessile in a marine carbonate setting; attachment and habitat are interpretations, not direct observations of a living animal.

## Movement in the reconstruction {#s-401060ccee}
The proposed body plan is sessile. The fossils do not preserve observed movement.

## Whole-body size is unknown {#s-fa2cb71b09}
The preserved phosphatic plates are millimetre-scale; the reconstructed animal's full size is not established.

## No ecological guild is assigned {#s-6cdf4383c7}
An attached seafloor life is inferred from the reconstruction, not directly observed.

## Sample and age limits {#s-9708b1864a}
The study covers four localities in three broadly synchronous late Atdabanian carbonate units, including the Wilkawillina Limestone of South Australia. The 521–514.5 Ma interval is a conservative envelope for that sample, not a global genus range or a brachiopod origin date. Muscle platforms, whole-body assembly, valve homology and stem-brachiopod placement remain interpretations.

## How the plates support a reconstruction {#s-55bfcfea60}
The fossil record contains separate mitral and sellate sclerites. Paired muscle scars are visible on the sellate plates; the mitral projections are interpreted as muscle platforms. Researchers assembled the pieces into a near-bilateral, sessile animal with two valves. That model is testable, but no fossil preserves the proposed valves joined together, and their homology or a position on the brachiopod stem remains a comparative hypothesis.

## Four localities bound this study sample {#s-3428cda47d}
The material comes from four localities in three broadly synchronous late Atdabanian carbonate units. One named locality is the Wilkawillina Limestone in the Arrowie Basin, South Australia. The conservative 521–514.5 Ma envelope is tied to these studied units; it is not the genus's full duration, a place of origin, or a sequence of direct ancestors.
