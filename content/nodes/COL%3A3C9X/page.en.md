## A source concept and its comparisons {#s-064fda750b}
A 2025 paper describes a source concept called “lulai” and compares it with 20 species assigned to the pernix group, using skin texture, sex-specific snout–vent length, colour in life and preservative, external digits, and advertisement calls. These comparisons do not cover every accepted Brachycephalus species. “Lulai” is absent from the pinned accepted-species list, so it remains a separate source concept; its measurements are not reassigned to B. auroguttatus or B. quiririensis. A four-locus analysis supports a clade containing those three names but does not resolve their relationships. Similar colour or close placement alone does not establish synonymy.

## Call and morphology sample limits {#s-7c69bdec97}
Table 2 reports an N for each character; for calls, N counts animals with at least one recorded and analysed call. Sample sizes differ among sex, skin, colour, and acoustic traits. This study treats a sequence of notes as a call, whereas some earlier descriptions use one note as a call. A call sequence, note, note group, pulse, and attenuated note are different units and should not be combined into one duration or count. Mariaeterezae has no call data in this comparison. Small samples, non-standard colour descriptions, and the observed-only call maxima limit diagnosis; these maxima are not genus-wide constants.

## A proposed refuge and assessment limits {#s-519b184f34}
The paper reassesses 20 comparison species using spatial criteria and proposes a 6,600-hectare Serra do Quiriri refuge for lulai, auroguttatus, quiririensis, Melanophryniscus biancae, and their habitat. This is a proposal, not an established reserve or a completed conservation action. Record-based and potential-habitat AOO, EOO, and threat-defined locations measure different things. The authors' 2025 categories and Green Status scenarios are dated assessments, not current official listings or population counts. Table 8 contains numerical inconsistencies. The authors heard quiririensis in disturbed vegetation in 2004 but could not revisit after 2010 to confirm persistence; neither continued presence nor extinction is established.

## SWS2 findings in other frog groups {#s-29e0bd4940}
The study recovered SWS2 sequence material in non-dendrobatid focal groups, including Brachycephalus, unlike the result of its bait-capture work in dendrobatids. Many assemblies had low coverage downstream, so positions after amino acid 129 were excluded. This difference supports the authors' comparative-loss hypothesis, but they note uncertainty about complete SWS2 function and the green-rod cell type in these other diurnal lineages.

## Fluorescent bones are cited, signalling remains proposed {#s-689bd49bc8}
Ephippium and pitanga dermal-bone fluorescence comes from cited earlier work. The opsin paper proposes a possible signalling role without testing visibility to receivers, colour-choice behaviour or causal effects of selected residues. Foot-waving and aposematism discussions likewise have separate evidence scopes; they do not establish one function for every Brachycephalus species.
