## Mountain wetlands and nighttime calling {#s-ad41b87bec}
The account described mountain swamps, ponds, aquatic grassland and nearby dense weeds. Frogs were difficult to detect while hidden in the vegetation, and males called at night. Detectability and calling observations do not quantify abundance or establish egg and larval habitats.

## One Guizhou sample adds mitochondrial attribution {#s-44a8b6f8b2}
The study collected six adults, three males and three females, and five tadpoles at montane ponds in Zheng’an County, Guizhou, at 1,326 m, on 7 July 2024 and 11 April 2025. These dates and life stages are not eleven adults or independent populations. Mitochondrial 16S and COI comparisons cluster the new material with named type sequences, with reported p-distances 0.000–0.005 and 0.000–0.004 respectively. The threshold cited from previous Nidirana studies is a contextual comparison, not a universal delimitation rule. Two mitochondrial fragments do not independently establish nuclear-genomic identity, range-wide variation or equivalence of every historical record.

## The new males have internal vocal sacs {#s-54731afa7f}
Three Guizhou males are reported at 41.1–43.4 mm snout–vent length, with smooth dorsal skin lacking horny spines, dorsolateral folds interrupted posteriorly, and breeding-season protruding suprabrachial glands and one nuptial pad on the dorsal base of finger I. The new account reports internal vocal sacs and explicitly contrasts this with the original type-locality description, proposing an earlier descriptive inaccuracy. The difference remains a source-level revision rather than a demonstrated geographic change or experimental explanation. Tibiotarsal reach varies from eye region to nostril; neither size nor limb reach supplies a fixed sex or identification cutoff.

## Female size ranges overlap the measured males {#s-6cf2965951}
The discussion and revised diagnosis give females at 42.2–47.0 mm snout–vent length; the individually described female MT ZA20240707005 is 45.15 mm. Female and male ranges overlap despite the source describing females as slightly larger. This reference female has head length/width 1.07, tympanum/eye diameter 0.89, tibia length 47.6% of SVL and tibiotarsal reach to the anterior eye corner. Live colour includes a brown spotted back, a light middorsal stripe without dark edges, barred limbs and denser reddish-brown throat, chest and ventral-limb spots than males. These individual proportions and sex-associated descriptions are not immutable population-wide patterns.

## Stage-thirty larvae have their own measurement definitions {#s-2a6bc1f4d0}
Larvae were staged using Gosner and measured from scaled photographs with ImageJ. The revised diagnosis lists stage-30 total length at 47.1–63.7 mm, body length at 14.5–21.5 mm and tail length at 63.5–67.1% of total length. Reference larva MT ZA20250411010 is separately reported with total length 63.66 mm, SVL 21.48 mm and tail length 41.73 mm. Total length, body length and SVL retain the authors’ distinct labels rather than becoming interchangeable adult SVL. The reference has a flattened elliptical body, rounded tail tip and spotted yellow-brown live body with translucent speckled fins. These stage-specific data are not maximum larval size, growth rate or metamorphosis timing.

## The reference larva has a left spiracle and funnel-like oral disc {#s-882900fea3}
The stage-30 reference has a single left-side spiracle low on the body, with a short tube free at the tip, and a median anal tube attached to the ventral fin. The oral disc is described as terminal and funnel-like; the upper jaw sheath has a median notch and the lower sheath is thin, sickle-shaped and serrated. The labial tooth-row formula is recorded as I:1+1/1+1:II, preserving the source notation. Two upper and three lower submarginal papilla rows are described. These are specimen/stage characters, not an experimentally established diet or universal diagnosis of every ranid larva. The text calls nostrils closer to the eye but prints eye-to-nostril/snout-to-nostril ratio 1.19; that wording/numeric discrepancy is not silently corrected.

## Two coordinates do not support an unchecked northwest extension {#s-4623ba07da}
The source lists the type locality at Gaolu Village, Mala Town, Qianjiang, Chongqing (29.24203° N, 108.902202° E; 1,419 m) and the new ponds at Xinzhou Town, Zheng’an, Guizhou (28.77761° N, 107.26797° E; 1,326 m). Its discussion calls the expansion approximately 300 km northwest. The printed new latitude is lower and longitude farther west, so the direction wording does not agree with those coordinates; the distance is not independently certified here. Both locality records and the original claim remain distinguishable. They do not prove continuous occupancy between provinces, an expanded area of occupancy or absence from unsurveyed intervening habitat.

## Circular nests are observed without a complete breeding sequence {#s-3865d0072e}
The new locality consists of montane swamps, ponds and aquatic grassland with dense weeds. Adults hide in weed patches and males call at night; circular nesting structures are reported for this species, while larvae occupy stagnant pools within ponds. The paragraph names sympatric Polypedates megacephalus, Zhangixalus chenfui and Hylarana guentheri, without establishing interactions, competition or predation. Circular nesting observations do not provide clutch counts, guarding duration, reproductive success or a measured construction sequence. Night calling is an occurrence description, not quantified acoustic parameters or a complete daily activity census.

## The paper proposes habitat protection without a current assessment {#s-2b5a938d17}
The discussion identifies potential agricultural pollution and fragmentation threats and recommends protecting pond and farmland ecosystems in Chongqing and Guizhou. It describes the Red List status as unassessed while citing IUCN 2022. That is a dated source statement, not a live verification of the species’ current status or a newly assigned threat category. Potential threats are not measured pollutant exposure, experimentally demonstrated reproductive harm or a quantified population decline in these vouchers. Two known localities provide a reason for further surveys rather than a complete range or certified extinction-risk calculation.
