## Sex-specific spotting accompanies the diagnostic combination {#s-3ba422c87f}
Males measure 39.7–48.2 mm and females 53–66 mm snout–vent length. The diagnosis gives terminal disc width relative to its base at least 1.70, a dimensionless ratio. Females have one large white spot below the eye, whereas males have numerous white spots. The tibiotarsal articulation reaches the nostril; a continuous pale head/flank line and pale inguinal patch are absent. Spotting and limb reach belong to a combined diagnosis, rather than a colour-only identification rule or a millimetre measurement of the disc ratio.

## Encounter positions change between daytime and night records {#s-14a590fcc3}
Frogs were encountered from 16:00 to 21:30 around small rocky streams less than one metre deep. Daytime records were on the ground 5–10 m from banks; night records were 3–5 m from rivers, including leaf perches 50–100 cm high. UADBA 7125 was found on a rock in fast water. The authors interpreted that occurrence as a possible misfortune, which is not proof of an accident or inability to use flowing water. Distances and heights describe reported encounters, without a controlled habitat preference or complete activity census.

## Eastern-slope forests are described without numeric elevation limits {#s-63f23fbfdd}
The source places poissoni in forests on Madagascar’s eastern slope at middle and high elevations. This paragraph supplies no numerical limits, and no elevation range is reconstructed from the qualitative wording. Forest occurrence does not establish continuous occupancy along the eastern slope or abundance within each locality. ## Twelve examined specimens include one genetic reference {#s-7518687f8a}
The resurrection account reports twelve examined poissoni specimens, while the species comments identify AMNH A174653, field number RAX 9367, as the sole genetic reference. The morphological sample count therefore is not a count of independent sequenced individuals. The revision uses type comparison and mitochondrial evidence to reinstate the species; one genetic reference does not establish variation throughout its range, multilocus population structure or current equivalence of every historic record.
