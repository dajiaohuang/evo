## Stem surface {#s-2f2fd2b108}
The Brazilian Flora 2020 record describes the stem surface as glabrous or strigose—that is, hairless or bearing stiff hairs pressed close to the surface—and separately records hairs as backward-pointing and appressed. It does not say whether those alternatives refer to different specimens or were combined from reports. Simple hairs are marked absent, while unbranched bristles are thread-like. Branched and glandular hairs, prickles, and short lateral shoots (brachyblasts) are absent in the record.

## Leaves and inflorescences {#s-6406af06b3}
Stipules, small structures at leaf bases, are persistent. The record marks extrafloral nectaries (nectar-secreting glands outside flowers) and projections between pairs of pinnae (the leaf’s primary side divisions) as absent. The inflorescence, or flower cluster, is globose; floral bracts (small leaf-like structures) are smaller than the flowers before they open (anthesis).

## Flowers {#s-6a985af097}
The perianth (calyx and corolla together) is recorded as having four parts. The membranous calyx, the outer sepal whorl, is marked as lacking lobes. Corolla (petal-whorl) lobes have one to three veins and are recorded as hairless or pubescent (covered with short soft hairs). The androecium (the stamens collectively) is isostemonous, with stamens equal in number to the petals; the filaments are pink.

## Habitat category {#s-f857cd105c}
The regional dataset labels the habitat “Terrícola,” Portuguese for terrestrial. This category does not specify a collection locality or the species’ full range.
