## What does Jamesbrittenia fruticosa look and smell like? {#s-3bb5d40fae}
The source describes a strongly branched, unpleasant-smelling shrublet up to about 1.3 m tall. Its leaves spread outward, and the tubular flowers may be white, blue, purple, or mauve, with dark bars at the base of each lobe. The plant often turns black as it dries. Flowering is reported mainly from August to September, with an extended range of July to January.

## Which details help separate it from its relatives? {#s-271016b2fe}
A second account emphasizes the stem leaves: usually elliptic, narrowing at both ends, and spreading almost at right angles to the stem. The hairs can be shorter than in related species, so the source recommends examining leaf margins. It also notes short flower stalks, narrow calyx lobes, and the long corolla tube (usually 20–26 mm) with only a light beard of short hairs inside.
