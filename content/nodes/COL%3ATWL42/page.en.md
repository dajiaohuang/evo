## Accepted name and placement {#s-6758fc1922}
COL26.8 accepts Zhangixalus melanoleucus Brakels, Nguyen, Pawangkhanant, Idiiatullina, Lorphengsy, Suwannapoom & Poyarkov, 2023 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Rhacophoridae (family) → Zhangixalus (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## Four male types and a single larger female {#s-837fbd665e}
Male holotype BEI 01010 and three male and one female paratypes were collected on Phou Samsoum Mountain, northeastern Laos, on 16 July 2020 at 2066 m. The holotype measured 35.0 mm snout–vent length; the male type interval was 34.4–36.3 mm and the single female measured 53.7 mm. The male comparison included nuptial pads, an external subgular vocal sac and greyish throat. A single female is not a female size range, and these types do not establish universal size limits or a statistically tested population dimorphism.

## Printed proportions and descriptive wording disagree {#s-0913344df6}
The holotype account calls the head much longer than wide but gives HW/HL = 0.98. It also calls the tibia slightly over half the snout–vent length but gives TL/SVL = 0.42. These printed ratios and qualitative statements are kept together as unresolved source inconsistencies; no corrected ratio is invented. Other holotype observations include finger discs with circumferential grooves and moderate finger and toe webbing, which are anatomy rather than measured climbing or swimming performance.

## Green live colours turn blue-grey in ethanol {#s-659c6d8016}
The live account describes a grass-green dorsum, white-cream flank and hidden-limb regions with irregular black markings, an unspotted cream chest and belly and a reddish-orange iris. After two years in ethanol the green became blue-grey while contrasting black flank markings remained visible. The single female was darker bottle-green, and the authors observed lighter dorsal colours at night than by day. These states retain preservation, individual and observation context; they are not invariant colours or experimentally demonstrated signalling, camouflage or toxicity.

## Stage-35 larvae with one sequenced specimen {#s-80e2393492}
Three tadpoles were listed as referred material under ZMMU A-7783. The larval paragraph assigns Gosner stage-35 tadpoles using a partial 16S sequence obtained from one specimen and gives the diagnostic keratodont-row formula as 1:5+5/1+1:2. Only that molecular denominator is certified by the read paragraph; the three tadpoles are not all claimed to have been sequenced. Detailed larval measurements and anatomy were delegated to unread supplements and are not reconstructed here or interpreted as an adult trait.

## Six call recordings from two identified males {#s-f3ced7b7be}
The call description used six calls from holotype BEI 01010 and paratype ZMMU A-7781. Fifty measured notes lasted 0.24–0.30 s and contained 2–3 pulses; 40 inter-note intervals lasted 0.48–0.85 s. Dominant frequency ranged from 3040–3280 Hz, summarized as about 3.14 kHz. The authors reported shorter first pulses. Calls, notes, intervals and males remain different sampling units, and mean-plus-error summaries are not labelled as standard deviation without reading the supplement’s definitions. The frequency summary is not treated as an identical frequency in every note.

## Rainy-night leaves and temporary-puddle breeding observations {#s-ed413d0f39}
At 2000–2200 m, specimens were found during rain between 19:00 and 22:00, mostly on leaves 1.5–3.0 m above ground in undisturbed montane evergreen forest with dense canopy and undergrowth. The authors inferred forest specialization. They reported breeding observations from April to July; in July males called from grass or at and within temporary puddles, where tadpoles were also recorded. These observations do not demonstrate absolute exclusion from disturbed forest, a complete annual season or an egg-site observation for every puddle.

## A dated single-mountain range and Data Deficient proposal {#s-536771f06d}
In 2023 the authors knew only the Phou Samsoum type locality and anticipated possible records on other Lao mountains and adjacent Vietnam. Those anticipated sites are not confirmed occurrences. They described the locality as within the newly designated Yod Nam Mo–Phou Samsoum protected area, 98873 ha, and proposed Data Deficient while calling for distribution, population and trend research. The protected-area size is not frog occupancy, and the dated designation and assessment proposal are not independently checked current official statuses.

## Mitochondrial genealogy with separate bootstrap and posterior support {#s-813ef51db8}
The study combined partial 16S and COI mitochondrial sequences to infer a maternal genealogy using maximum likelihood and Bayesian approaches. It recovered the Phou Samsoum lineage as sister to Z. nigropunctatus with reported support 100 ultrafast bootstrap and 1.0 posterior probability. Uncorrected 16S divergence from compared congeners was 3.4–8.8%, with the lowest to nigropunctatus. Those support types and distance comparisons are distinct; they are not a nuclear-genome species tree, proof that a universal 3% cutoff defines species or a diagnosis of reproductive isolation.
