## Boulders beside torrential river sections {#s-e4a38d70a5}
Within the Sekayu lowland-forest study, the authors reported this frog along torrential sections of the Peres and Bubu rivers. Frogs perched on stacked boulder surfaces beside streams and occasionally on nearby low vegetation. The account’s wording is limited here to those study rivers, rather than proof that no other population uses different settings.

## Tadpoles attached below the waterline {#s-0cc4b99898}
The same account reported A. gerutu tadpoles clinging to boulders below the waterline. This documents a local larval attachment substrate, without supplying measured flow limits, diet, developmental duration or an experimentally tested attachment mechanism.

## Characters in the dated group comparison {#s-b3042e6a0d}
The 2023 description of *A. ailao* compares *Amolops gerutu* in its larutensis species-group account and lists vomerine teeth (teeth on the vomer bones in the roof of the mouth), a groove on the disc at the tip of the first finger, and a vocal sac (a throat pouch involved in calling) among the compared characters. This summary comes from earlier descriptions reviewed by the authors; *Amolops gerutu* was not newly measured for this comparison. A historical group assignment does not establish that current taxonomic concepts match or that every individual has the listed anatomy.
