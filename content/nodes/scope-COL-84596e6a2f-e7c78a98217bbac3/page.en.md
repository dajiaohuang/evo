## A Devonian liverwort in the fossil record {#s-bbc188178e}
Metzgeriothallus sharonae is the central fossil in this account: a Middle Devonian liverwort preserved as a body fossil. Older Ordovician spore masses may be related, but they do not establish a crown-liverwort assignment.

## Food and growth {#s-11cc1ed8c7}
Like other small terrestrial primary producers, the profiled plant made its own food by photosynthesis.

## A likely moist, terrestrial setting {#s-e6c313e551}
The fossil is associated with moist terrestrial substrates, but the exact surface and moisture conditions were not directly observed.

## Stationary growth {#s-ef4861234b}
The profiled plant grew in place; this account treats it as sessile rather than mobile.

## A small thalloid plant {#s-5a3c671d24}
The fossil is interpreted as a small thalloid gametophyte. It does not establish a body-size range for the whole phylum.

## A small primary producer {#s-73667e9d74}
The sample represents a small land-based primary producer; it cannot define every ecological role among living liverworts.

## A thalloid gametophyte {#s-2b4a38ace1}
The fossil preserves a thalloid gametophyte, the plant form described in this account.

## A costa and branching form {#s-66823225c1}
Metzgeriothallus has a medial costa, or central ridge, and a bifurcating thallus.

## An associated capsule {#s-f0f00c4240}
The fossil sample also includes a sporophyte capsule associated with the plant material.

## Where the secure fossil evidence begins {#s-aac3823665}
Givetian specimens support a liverwort body-fossil record and its anatomy. Older cryptospores and sporangia remain uncertain in affinity, so they do not securely move the first established crown-liverwort evidence earlier.
