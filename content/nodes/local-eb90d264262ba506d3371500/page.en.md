## The deposited caecilian record {#s-bf354106c1}
The fourth version of CaecilianTraits lists Sylvacaecilia grandisonae in the genus Sylvacaecilia and family Grandisoniidae. It keeps individual, population and species records separate. Whether this source concept matches a current Catalogue of Life (COL26.8) concept has not been established.

## Measurement material {#s-eaa441cf66}
The compilation lists one measurement record for this source concept, at individual level; it lists none at population or species level. One row is not a population estimate or proof that the record represents a unique specimen. The row retains its specimen identifier, sex code and reference. Body-size comparisons also need to distinguish fresh, preserved and summary measurements.

## Compiled habitat categories {#s-5dacdf5390}
The compilation labels the broad habitat “Terrestrial” and “Freshwater (inland waters),” and the microhabitat “Fossorial.” These are source categories: they do not show how often each setting is used or describe the species’ complete geographic range.

## Compiled reproduction category {#s-94ec5d6af6}
The compilation classifies the species as oviparous, meaning that it lays eggs. This category does not tell us about parental care, when reproduction occurs or how it varies among populations.

## Compiled anatomical counts {#s-6a56d8315d}
At species level, the compilation gives a primary-fold count of 84 and a secondary-fold count of 69. These values may summarize records rather than describe one animal; they do not establish that every individual has the same diagnostic counts.

## Water-dependent larval development {#s-788d9c5427}
The compilation marks a water-dependent larval stage as present. This is the dataset category described in its accompanying data paper; it does not mean adults always live in water. The reproductive metadata and original reference are needed to understand the observation’s scope and qualifications.

## Compiled clutch counts {#s-9c966105e8}
The source species record lists a maximum clutch size of 25. This count comes from cited reproductive accounts and describes the compiled source material; it does not establish a universal reproductive limit or annual output. The associated reproductive metadata remains part of its interpretation.
