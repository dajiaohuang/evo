## What basic traits are listed? {#s-804ff4a655}
A 2012 regional flora summary describes a shrublet up to 1 m tall. Its leaves are small, compressed on the upper side and 1.5–2.5 mm long. The bracts are nearly round and keeled on the upper side. Flowers form dense spikes; the floral tube is about 2 mm long and lacks a neck. The fruits are dry.

## How does the diagnostic account separate it from similar species? {#s-319f9e6e03}
A 2003 diagnostic account describes P. paleacea as less robust than the coastal-sand species P. rigida, reaching at most 1.5 m, with abundant nearly head-shaped flower clusters and dry fruit; the separate 2012 summary gives a lower height, up to 1 m. The account says its coastal, maritime habit and flower clusters once led to confusion with P. truncata (also called P. glomerata), but describes the two as morphologically and geographically distinct: P. truncata is reported inland from Vanrhynsdorp through the Cederberg and several named towns to Seven Weeks Poort. It also says past confusion with P. ericoides likely reflected their overlapping occurrence, although their morphology differs. By contrast, P. rigida has nodding branches bearing many hanging branchlets, extended spikes and fleshy yellow berries.

## What habitat does the regional flora record? {#s-06d061388c}
The 2012 regional flora summary lists coastal sands. It does not specify survey dates or how completely the habitat was sampled.
