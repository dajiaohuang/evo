## Leaves and inflorescence {#s-db09971393}
The field lists solitary or clustered, long aerial stems. The leaf blade is irregularly pinnate, with its base meeting the axis at an acute angle; veins stand out in cross-section. The inflorescence may sit between or below the leaves and branch at the first, second, or third order. Its stalk bract is narrow, elongated, and tubular. The prophyll, a bract near the inflorescence base, is described with elongated unbranched veins, or a flat densely hairy surface, or an irregular fringed margin. Smaller branches are not threadlike. Floral pits are arranged spirally, in opposite pairs, or in groups of three; each has a distal lip and no hairs on its inner surface.

## Flowers and fruit {#s-ca1b6f828d}
Male flowers fall after flowering; the pollen sacs (thecae) diverge when the flower opens. Unfertilized female flowers also fall. The fruit apex is recorded as a prominent asymmetric projection, conical, or rounded. The fruit is black or purple, with a regular, hairless outer surface.
