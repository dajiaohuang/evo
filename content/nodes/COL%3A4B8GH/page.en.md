## Two historical sources, one matching name {#s-35bab630ad}
COL26.8 accepts Otus beccarii in Strigidae. EltonTraits 1.0 and AVONET v7 also contain historical records under this name, but their taxonomies predate the pinned catalogue concept. Matching names connect the records for comparison; they do not settle later splits or synonym disputes.

## A standardized food-category estimate {#s-a81a490d20}
EltonTraits assigns 100% of its nonzero food-category weight to invertebrates. Code A records high compiler confidence in the source and diet information. The percentages standardize verbal descriptions rather than measure food intake; a zero would not prove that the owl never eats an item. Labels such as Nect, Seed and PlantO cover broader categories than their everyday wording and do not mean exclusive feeding on nectar, seeds or plants.

## Relative use of four foraging positions {#s-fa39bf0581}
The species-level foraging weights are 40% for the ground, 20% for understory, edges or shrubs below 2 m, 20% for trees or tall bushes from 2 m upward but below the canopy, and 20% in or just above the canopy. They estimate relative use of feeding positions, not nest sites, all habitat or a whole-day time budget. The source defines surface-water, understory, mid/high, canopy and aerial categories separately; they are not elevation bands and cannot be used to calculate flight heights or habitat area.

## Follow the citation labels {#s-59e3324c01}
The EltonTraits diet and foraging fields carry Ref_5 in the bird-source table, which identifies Handbook of the Birds of the World, volume 5 (1992–2011). Diet, foraging and activity may cite different material, and a matching Ref number in bird and mammal tables need not mean the same reference. The archive retains file, row, field group and table labels for tracing. The cited volume was not read here, and a citation label is not another observation.

## Life-history evidence limits {#s-588caaa7b6}
EltonTraits is a historical same-name, same-family record, not a complete species account or proof of equivalence with COL26.8. Its diet and foraging percentages standardize literature descriptions; they are not direct measures of stomach contents, intake, energy or observation time. Code A reflects compiler confidence in the source and diet information, not direct measurement. Elsewhere in EltonTraits, avian A/B diet codes retain source and proportional uncertainty, while mammalian ABC diet or activity codes may borrow from a congener considered ecologically similar. Genus- or family-level values, non-species-level strata and unresolved split records do not establish traits for this species. The underlying books and papers were not individually reviewed. Individual, seasonal and local variation, current range, population and conservation status need other sources.

## A historical AVONET name match {#s-3a2fbc5313}
AVONET v7 records Otus beccarii under the same name and family used by COL26.8. Its one-to-one BirdLife/eBird and BirdTree crosswalks support this association, but do not resolve species concepts after later splits or lumps.

## Habitat and perching categories {#s-25bc19ec49}
The habitat category describes tall-tree vegetation with a relatively closed canopy. The primary-lifestyle category, Insessorial, describes time spent perched above ground on vegetation, rocks or other substrates. These broad labels do not replace food lists, behavior observations or descriptions of a local microhabitat.

## What “sedentary” records {#s-5cf15b4235}
AVONET labels the migration category sedentary. The source does not separately list regional seasonal movements, current routes, schedules or movement distances for each population.

## Latitude endpoints on historical maps {#s-1aa23b64d5}
The historical breeding and resident maps run from latitude -1.22° to -0.63°. The source worksheet links these fields to BirdLife/HBW 2019.1 maps accessed on 27 March 2020. The endpoints summarize the mapped range; they are not a locality list or proof of occupancy throughout it, and they do not cover all non-breeding movements. They establish neither current distribution nor survival or conservation status.

## Fields withheld from interpretation {#s-431f58b919}
Three of the four ecological categories have readable records; trophic niche is not interpreted. Historical latitude endpoints should be read only within their map scope. The source flags inference from relatives for some biometric fields and lists updated literature as the mass source, so this page does not interpret morphology or mass. Reproduction, growth and conservation also lack complete accounts here. A missing field does not show that the species lacks the trait.

## Limits of these historical data {#s-67655c4413}
AVONET1_BirdLife uses HBW–BirdLife 5.0 names from December 2020. Matching names and families, along with the one-to-one BirdLife/eBird/BirdTree crosswalks, connect this record to the catalogue but do not establish equivalence with current COL26.8 species concepts. Its ecological fields are broad categories drawn from literature; the underlying species literature was not individually read. The source flags possible inference from relatives for morphology and lists updated literature for mass. Those fields are therefore withheld from interpretation as direct measurements of this species. Historical maps cover breeding and resident ranges only; current population, occupancy and conservation require other sources.
