## A local comparison with lojana {#s-2be211cf6e}
The revision separates elicioi from lojana by distinct dark canthal stripes, lightly rather than heavily mottled groin and thigh surfaces, and shorter, thinner, less defined dark limb bars when present. It emphasizes that morphology alone can be difficult to use. These are cited comparative distinctions, not an assertion that every individual has an identical pattern. The authors did not encounter lojana despite intensive searches; this is not proof of its absence throughout Ecuador.

## Short notes precede the longer pulsed notes {#s-ff841d2906}
The identification account highlights an advertisement call with long pulsed notes after short notes, contrasting the usual order reported for other southern Ecuadorian Gastrotheca. Males were heard frequently, especially on rainy days. This is a qualitative account cited by the revision, not one of its five newly analyzed call datasets, and no recording-derived numerical frequency or temperature-adjusted rate is assigned here.

## Western-slope forest and nearby Loja {#s-7f21982dfc}
The authors report elicioi as common in Abra de Zamora and near Loja, on the western slope in evergreen upper montane forest below2600m. Camouflage made individuals difficult to find even when calls were detectable. This illustrates different visual and auditory detectability, not a calculated detection model. The description is a local2020 record and does not establish the complete contemporary distribution.
