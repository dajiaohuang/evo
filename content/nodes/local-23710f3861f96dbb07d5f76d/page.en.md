## The deposited caecilian record {#s-e37121c55a}
CaecilianTraits version 4 lists Ichthyophis acuminatus in the genus Ichthyophis and family Ichthyophiidae. It keeps individual, population and species records separate. This source concept has not been reconciled with a current Catalogue of Life (COL26.8) species concept.

## Measurement material {#s-2ca3777698}
The compilation lists 13 measurement records for this source concept, all at individual level and none at population or species level. These row counts are not estimates of population size or proof of 13 distinct specimens. Each record retains its specimen identifier, sex code and reference. Body-size comparisons also need to distinguish fresh, preserved and summary measurements.

## Compiled habitat categories {#s-cf4012651a}
The compilation labels the broad habitat “Terrestrial” and “Freshwater (inland waters),” and leaves the microhabitat unspecified. These are source categories: they do not show how often each setting is used or describe the species’ complete geographic range.

## Compiled reproduction category {#s-b8f0fc937d}
The compilation classifies the species as oviparous, meaning that it lays eggs. The source note presents this as presumed, based on related species whose reproductive strategies are known; it also proposes terrestrial eggs and stream-dwelling larvae. These are qualified source statements, not direct observations confirmed by this compilation.

## Compiled anatomical counts {#s-8e880b5ed6}
The species-level record gives 334.555555555556 for dorsal annuli (body rings), 327.555555555556 for ventral annuli, 4 annuli interrupted by the vent, and 109.75 vertebrae. These compiled values may summarize records rather than describe one animal; they do not establish that every individual has the same diagnostic counts.

## Water-dependent larval development {#s-cad696eb20}
The compilation marks a water-dependent larval stage as present. This is the dataset category described in its accompanying data paper; it does not mean adults always live in water. The reproductive metadata and original reference are needed to understand the observation’s scope and qualifications.
