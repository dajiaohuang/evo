## Characters used to distinguish the species {#s-a2b2ce8c13}
The species account distinguishes *A. multicingulata* from *A. liquii* by its longer striations (fine lines) at the cap margin, differences in gill color and drying response, and smaller basidiospores (microscopic reproductive spores). The paper also describes microscopic characters of the type specimen (the name-bearing reference specimen).

## Fruiting in subtropical forests {#s-59c473c79e}
The authors report fruiting bodies growing alone or scattered on soil in subtropical broad-leaved forests dominated by Fagaceae, sometimes mixed with pines (*Pinus*). These are the reported study settings; the account does not establish interaction mechanisms, resource dependence, seasonality, or population-level ecology.

## Its placement in the study phylogeny {#s-f6740fb2c7}
The paper's combined multilocus phylogeny places *A. multicingulata* as sister to *A. liquii* among the taxa included in that analysis. A sister relationship means their branches share the closest branch point in that sampled tree. This result is limited to the study's sampling and markers; broader phylogenomic evidence and divergence times were not assessed.

## Where the paper reports it {#s-71d5d79863}
The authors report the species as known from eastern China and list the type and additional collections from Huangshan, Anhui, in July 2018; the type was collected on 2018-07-13. These records do not come from an exhaustive global search, and native or introduced status was not assessed.
