## Name and placement in COL26.8 {#s-b0ad21a40a}
COL26.8 lists Ansonia longidigita Inger, 1960 as an accepted species. In the release dated 20 August 2026, its recorded path runs through Anura (order), Bufonidae (family) and Ansonia (genus). This placement belongs to that checklist; the same name in another source needs its own identity evidence.

## Evidence on this page comes from separate sources {#s-fd83169260}
This page combines trait compilations with a 2016 comparative study. The AmphiBIO record follows a Frost 2011 taxon concept; the TadpoleTraits stages and ratios come from their own deposited rows; the 2016 paper reports museum material and mitochondrial sequences. These sources use different samples and evidence types, and a shared name does not establish that every record represents the COL26.8 concept.

## Foraging categories in AmphiBIO {#s-ec5c9ffd82}
AmphiBIO's Frost 2011 historical classification flags terrestrial and aquatic foraging categories. They are broad labels, not a detailed account of seasonal or life-stage changes. A later TetrapodTraits revision reports correcting some amphibian body-mass and microhabitat entries carried over from earlier compilations. The available record does not show whether these two categories were changed, so it supports neither a replacement value nor a claim that each original label is wrong. Equivalence between this historical concept and COL26.8 is unconfirmed.

## Maximum adult body length in the compilation {#s-3a01dda1c5}
Under AmphiBIO's historical Frost 2011 classification, maximum adult body length is recorded as 70 mm. For Anura, body length means snout-to-vent length; for Caudata and Gymnophiona it means total length. This is a literature-compiled maximum, not a measurement of every individual. Its source concept has not been confirmed as equivalent to COL26.8.

## A compiled breeding-frequency value {#s-ee0f7c62ab}
Under AmphiBIO's historical Frost 2011 classification, the maximum recorded reproductive frequency is one event per year. This is a literature-compiled maximum, not a calendar observed across every population. The row does not tie the value to sex, population, date or wild-versus-captive conditions. In this compilation, offspring-size fields may refer to offspring or eggs; no such size value is reported in this entry. Equivalence with the current checklist concept is unresolved.

## A larval-stage flag {#s-6f06a171f1}
The AmphiBIO record flags a larval stage under its historical Frost 2011 taxon concept. That category alone does not describe parental care, breeding place or seasonal timing, and the concept has not been shown to match the one in COL26.8.

TadpoleTraits v4 contains six literature rows and one image row under its Ansonia longidigita source concept. Stage labels supplied across the records are 28, 29, 27, 31, 33 and 36; the last is marked “Judging based on the picture.” That image-based stage is an estimate from appearance, not a directly documented developmental observation. The row count is not a count of unique animals, and sample sizes from separate studies are not added together. Equivalence with the current COL26.8 concept is unresolved.

## Proportions measured from one selected image {#s-175eb60e5d}
TadpoleTraits image row 120, labelled stage 36 “Judging based on the picture,” reports these ratios: body length/total length 0.382428; tail length/total length 0.614453; maximum body height/total length 0.170396; maximum tail height/total length 0.155906; and tail muscle height/total length 0.0898753. These are dimensionless proportions from one selected drawing or photograph, not direct measurements of a representative wild population. Fields inconsistent with the numerator and total length in that row are excluded. The image-based stage and source taxon concept are not independently verified as equivalent to COL26.8.

## What the comparative study examined {#s-66b47bb3b0}
The study combines field observations, preserved adults and larvae, skeletal examinations of selected specimens, earlier literature and mitochondrial sequence comparisons. Only specimens explicitly marked as cleared-and-stained or radiographed carry those examination labels; an unannotated voucher is not assumed to have received either treatment. Comparisons across the genus are not measurements of every Ansonia longidigita. Historical genus size, range and trait summaries belong to the paper's 2016 context. Including the species does not amount to a current population survey, a complete species diagnosis or a new conservation assessment.

## Mitochondrial sampling and tree limits {#s-b41db3abc0}
The 2016 comparison joins partial mitochondrial 12S and 16S sequences and estimates evolutionary trees with maximum-likelihood and Bayesian methods, two model-based approaches. The focal sequences are reported as 417 base pairs and 551 base pairs; alignment lengths and raw data have not been independently checked. Table 3 includes 43 species across 21 genera, while the broad analysis uses 36 species plus selected Asian subtrees. In the broad tree, Bufoides is placed nearest the focal sequences, but support for that placement is relatively low; the result must be read in light of which taxa were sampled. Two mitochondrial regions are not independent nuclear evidence of ancestry or reproductive isolation. The paper calls its distances “uncorrected k2p”; that wording is ambiguous and is not silently converted to an uncorrected p-distance or a universal threshold for defining genera.
