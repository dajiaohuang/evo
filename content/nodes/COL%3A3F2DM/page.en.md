## Ecological evidence {#s-4db293cd48}
A field study covered three sites, recording activity budgets at Kwakuchinja in Tanzania and group-size data at Fongoli in Senegal and Lolldaiga Hills Ranch in Kenya. Of 180 focal follows at Kwakuchinja, 140 met the duration threshold for activity analysis, totaling 31.4 hours. Activity at that site varied through the night: foraging was more prevalent in the evening, while inactivity was higher before dawn.

## Ecological evidence {#s-5b356411ea}
Group-size records differed by site: 55% of 76 encounters at Kwakuchinja involved two or more individuals, whereas 79% of 185 Fongoli records and 80% of 142 Lolldaiga records were solitary. Overall calling rates were higher before dawn, while contact calls made up a relatively larger share in the evening. The authors note that Kwakuchinja used focal follows whereas the other sites relied mainly on transects; method differences may affect detection of nearby animals, so these contrasts do not define a fixed social system for the species.
