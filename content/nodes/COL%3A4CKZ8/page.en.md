## Social behavior and organization {#s-c64edba727}
Using nine years of data from a population in Kasanka National Park, Zambia, Weyher et al. analyzed grooming, proximity, aggression and whether female–male affiliations persisted over time. Females tended to have a strong affiliation with one male, while males could maintain strong affiliations with several females; males were largely responsible for initiating grooming and proximity, and aggression was low. These relationships persisted across female reproductive states and often across years. The study adds a Kinda baboon case to a literature focused on other species, but it describes one park population rather than a fixed species-wide pattern.

## Evidence limits {#s-3b90f26ef6}
