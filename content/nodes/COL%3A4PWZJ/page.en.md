## From leaf surfaces to anther pores {#s-99003dcc7e}
A Brazilian Flora 2020 v393.147 entry records a linear to lance-shaped leaf blade with an acute base and tip. It distinguishes the two surfaces: the upper face lacks scale-like hairs, while the lower face bears glandular hairs. The inflorescence is terminal; its flowers are arranged in a two-branched cyme, a cluster whose branches divide in pairs, or a reduced form. Each flower is four-parted. The hypanthium, or floral cup, has glandular hairs and appendages described as confined to the distal region or to the area between sepals. The sepals are narrowly triangular and the petals purple. The record counts eight stamens in two similar whorls. In the stamens opposite the sepals, the connective (tissue between pollen sacs) extends slightly below the thecae (the pollen sacs); the anthers open through pores at their tips. The ovary has four chambers and glandular hairs at its tip. This is one regional morphology field, not a measure of variation or a complete identification guide.

## A single habitat label {#s-fd099cccbb}
The same dataset classifies the recorded habitat as “terrícola,” meaning terrestrial. It gives no collection locality, survey date or range coverage, and does not specify whether the record concerns wild, cultivated, managed or fossil material. This one category is not a complete ecological account.
