## The accepted name and catalogue route {#s-182573df8b}
COL26.8 records Arthroleptis sylvaticus (Laurent, 1954) as an accepted species under Anura, Arthroleptidae and Arthroleptis. The study below used a working identification for specimens in a 2017 barcode library; the original vouchers and equivalence with the current catalogue concept have not been independently confirmed.

## Read the study as a bounded identification {#s-f61619626a}
The study reports sampled specimens, sequence clusters and library-wide counts. Those results help trace how the authors grouped the sampled material, but they do not establish a complete range or a definitive identification of every specimen as the currently accepted species.

## The sampled specimens and their localities {#s-9af3e5dfa8}
The retained sylvaticus clade contains two LMR specimens, USNM 558514–15, and ten specimens from the Republic of Congo. LMR is the Loubomo–Mougagara Road corridor in Nyanga, Gabon; it is distinct from the Republic of Congo and from the Democratic Republic of Congo. Museum reference specimens are not automatically LMR observations. Any survey non-detection is limited by its effort and seasons, so it cannot establish regional absence or a complete species range. The original vouchers, present-day occurrence and taxonomic identity have not been independently checked here.

## Barcode clusters retain provisional boundaries {#s-b7c91c3a37}
The twelve specimens were assigned one BIN and one ABGD group—two sequence-cluster labels—and grouped with selected 16S references labelled sylvaticus. The authors retained two additional source clades as Arthroleptis sp. A and sp. B, reporting 15% and 17% 16S divergence and some sympatry (overlap in sampled localities). Those unnamed clades are not assigned to the accepted sylvaticus page, and sequence divergence alone does not name a new species.

## Two mitochondrial markers and uneven reference coverage {#s-ecd64b9f9c}
The identification workflow combined COI barcode clusters (BIN and ABGD), comparisons with 16S references, and morphology or type-locality context. COI and 16S are both mitochondrial DNA regions, so they are not independent nuclear confirmation or tests of reproductive isolation. The authors used neighbor joining to cluster similar sequences but explicitly limited its use for evolutionary-history inference. Most species in the library were represented from only one or two localities, and not all GenBank reference specimens could be checked morphologically; reference labels and missing sequences remain limits. Fieldwork was in May–June 2012 and March–April 2013. GenBank/BOLD comparisons used the 2016 source-library snapshot, not a current database audit.

## Library totals are not species or population estimates {#s-38622526c6}
Across the multispecies library, the study reports 540 sequenced specimens, 524 COI sequences and 528 16S sequences, grouped into 94 BINs and 85 ABGD clusters. These totals do not describe Arthroleptis sylvaticus alone. For LMR, the methods describe 80 first-season and 97 second-season specimens, while Table 1 lists 79 and 97 sequenced or retained entries. The methods also describe 275 Congo reference specimens, while the table’s locality rows total 274. These collection and analysis counts differ; no exclusion reason is supplied here. Library cluster totals are not a census of currently accepted species, and raw traces, BOLD assignments and GenBank records were not independently reanalysed.
