## Delosperma deilanthoides {#s-bfa507aaa5}
## A deep tuber supports a compact, papillate plant

The 1997 description records a compact plant up to 2 cm tall and 6 cm across, with a dark-brown, turnip-shaped tuber up to 10 cm long. Six to eight very succulent leaves crowd each branch, reaching 20 mm long and 18 mm wide; they are metallic blue-green and covered with shiny, prominent papillae.

Each branch bears a single, scentless summer flower. Its pink petals shade to white at the base and reach 20 mm long. The fragile five-chambered capsule is only 4–5 mm across; the lustrous reddish-brown seeds have small rounded tubercles. The account says seedlings form tubers within three months and flower in their first summer.

The authors considered D. carolinense var. compacta the closest affinity, noting that this variety has upright stems and longer, narrower, less succulent leaves. D. dyeri was only a possible affinity if its tuberous root were confirmed; the authors had no living material and had twice failed to find it near Alicedale.
