## Two North Carolina vouchers underlie the pooled assembly {#s-ffe1515584}
On 20 May 2023, two specimens assigned to the fuscus B3 lineage were collected within approximately three to four metres of each other beneath stream-bank rocks and logs at Bell Branch, Wilkes County, North Carolina. They were a subadult of approximately 32 mm snout–vent length and an adult male of approximately 43 mm, deposited as AMNH A-194068 and A-194069. Blood and liver supplied genomic material, with additional RNA samples. These individual sizes and encounters are not a full adult range, maturity threshold or quantified habitat preference. The assembly pools two animals from one population; it is not one fully phased individual genome or species-wide variation.

## The 16.1-gigabase result is a purged pooled primary assembly {#s-9274d02202}
Individual long-read coverage was approximately twelve-fold, insufficient for well-resolved phased haplotypes. Pooling reads increased coverage to approximately twenty-four-fold. An initial 24.0-gigabase pooled assembly included suspected duplicated haplotigs; two rounds of overlap purging reduced it to 16.1 gigabases in 19,632 contigs, with reported contig N50 1.74 megabases and longest contig 27.9 megabases. The primary assembly was labelled aDesFus1-2.1. These are assembly-quality outputs, not a directly measured invariant genome size. The authors allow that unresolved repeats may inflate size and discuss approximately 14–15 gigabases as a possible true size, requiring further work.

## BUSCO recovery includes fragmented loci {#s-710db25c5c}
The merged assembly recovered 4,941 of 5,310 Tetrapoda reference loci, reported as 93.1%: 4,243 single-copy, 370 duplicated and 328 fragmented. Thus the 93.1% identified fraction is not 93.1% complete single-copy loci, and duplicated or fragmented hits remain distinct categories. Transcript assembly counts are not counts of independently validated functional genes. Separate mitochondrial assemblies were reported similar to the earlier reference in length, gene order and composition, with small differences between the two vouchers. These are source-reported comparisons; the underlying sequence files were not independently reanalysed here.

## A separate nest sample supported partial chromosome scaffolding {#s-de5170f573}
A nest-guarding female and embryos were collected in August 2021 at Leatherwood Creek, Menifee County, Kentucky, from the B1/B2 lineage, distinct from the North Carolina B3 genomic source. Collection, RNA sequencing and linkage analysis report different embryo counts; they are retained as different or unresolved processing stages rather than one certified clutch size. The linkage analysis describes nineteen offspring, and parentage is presumed. It anchored 4,286 contigs into fourteen primary linkage groups plus two smaller scaffolds spanning 7.5 gigabases, approximately 47% of the assembly and 60% of annotated mRNAs. These are partial chromosome portions, not a fully scaffolded genome. Chromosome preparations separately supported diploid 2n = 28.

## Repeat abundance depends on annotation and denominators {#s-8f978354e3}
The primary assembly was estimated as approximately 75% repetitive and 25% single-copy sequence. About 36% of the genome represented LTR elements and 15% LINEs. LTR/Ty3 accounted for approximately 3.8 gigabases, 31% of repeats by sequence coverage and 12% by element count: those denominators are not interchangeable. Around 17% of transposable elements were unclassified, potentially reflecting assembly and annotation limits or novel elements. Relative-divergence landscapes suggest differing historical expansion patterns, but do not independently date activity or identify why this lineage has a smaller genome than some salamanders.

## Protein-coding annotation remains a lower-bound estimate {#s-24c667aca1}
The annotation identified 12,408 unique entries with a common name in at least one reference database, which the authors treat as a lower bound on protein-coding genes. It is not a complete experimentally verified gene count. RNA-derived evidence came from a restricted set of tissues and animals; additional pipelines were suggested but not evaluated. The printed total isoform count and component mRNA/tRNA counts do not reconcile exactly, and summary gene/exon lengths mix categories affected by small tRNAs. Those inconsistent aggregate counts and length summaries are not converted into definitive species traits here.

## Genome–life-history explanations require broader evidence {#s-f9bca99807}
Comparisons to older Desmognathus genome-size estimates are complicated by changed species boundaries, unreported localities and different measurement methods. The authors discuss possible associations among aquatic versus terrestrial development, repeat dynamics and genome size, without directly demonstrating selection or a causal life-history transition in these specimens. Apparent chromosome rearrangements relative to Ambystoma could reflect real differences, scaffold placement error or unplaced conserved blocks. Expanded taxon sampling and more complete chromosome assemblies are proposed ways to distinguish these explanations; the present resource does not resolve them.

## Adult tapeworm associations in a historical North American checklist {#s-5ca0bbd597}
The 2023 review includes Desmognathus fuscus among amphibian hosts with records of adult intestinal cestodes. Its named parasite associations are Bothriocephalus rarus, Cylindrotaenia americana, Ophiotaenia amphiumae, Ophiotaenia cryptobranchi. These describe compiled host–parasite records, not parasites measured in every member of the species. The scope is adult worms in wild continental North American herpetofauna; larval cestode records are excluded. An intestinal association alone does not establish disease severity, host mortality, zoonotic transmission or the species’ role in completing every parasite life cycle. Prevalence, intensity, examined-host denominators and experimental susceptibility are not supplied by this host checklist.

## How to interpret the compiled parasite evidence {#s-c797752af1}
The authors assembled literature records into both parasite-first and host-first lists and supplied an identification key using earlier taxonomic works. The host-first list intentionally omits worms identified only to genus; a shorter list there is not evidence that genus-level records do not exist. The article reports 88 named amphibian hosts overall, alongside unresolved and hybrid labels in its host list; that source total is not the number of pinned COL26.8 pages linked here. This page uses only an exact source-name match to the frozen accepted name, without certifying every historical specimen’s identity.
