## Ruschia vanderbergiae {#s-fc34055bea}
## Dotted, keeled leaves and three rows of purple-pink petals

Bolus’s 1935 Latin description portrays an erect, hairless, diffusely branched plant about 20 cm tall. Branches are commonly 15–20 cm long and up to 3 mm thick, with internodes usually 1–1.5 cm long. Current-season flowering branchlets carry four to six leaves and measure 2–3.5 cm.

The compressed, keeled leaves are glaucous green with fairly large, slightly raised dots. They are 5–9 mm long excluding the sheath and 2–3 mm wide. The keel tip, and more rarely the margins, bears one or two minute teeth. Flowers have five sepals and three rows of purple-pink petals up to 7 mm long; the innermost petals are pale and may grade into staminodes, the thread-like sterile stamens. There are five slender stigmas.

A separate 2019 Eastern Cape flora entry records the habitat as gravelly plains and hills on Karoo sediments.
