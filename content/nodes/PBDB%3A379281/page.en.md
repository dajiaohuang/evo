## Filtering clues preserved in a skull {#s-3fe2f841ef}
The 2017 original description establishes Liaodactylus primus from holotype PMOL-AP00031: a nearly complete skull, articulated mandibles and atlas-axis complex from the Tiaojishan Formation about 500 m west of Daxishan village, Jianchang, Liaoning, China. Its skull is 133 mm long, with a slightly concave dorsal profile in lateral view and a rostrum about 49.1% of skull length. This specimen supplies no complete wing or body skeleton. Its skull measurement is not converted into a measured wingspan, body mass or species-wide adult size range.

## A comb of teeth shortening toward the rear {#s-8a3379daa5}
The authors record 152 marginal teeth across both sides of the upper and lower jaws. Anterior teeth are long, needle-like and inclined laterally; they shorten posteriorly until the last teeth are peg-like. Close spacing produces a comb-like dentition. The upper row extends beneath the anterior part of the nasoantorbital opening rather than being confined to the tip. This arrangement explains the filtering interpretation more specifically than a high tooth count alone. The 152 teeth are the specimen count in the description, not population abundance or a constant count established for every individual.

## How proportions and palate features aid recognition {#s-6d27709de2}
The diagnostic combination also includes a nasoantorbital opening about 31% of skull length, a mandibular symphysis about 30.5% of jaw length, and a lateral pterygoid process subdividing the subtemporal opening. Confluence of the nostril and antorbital opening links it to the broader Monofenestrata in the authors’ framework; that shared feature alone does not identify this species. Diagnosis compares a suite of preserved structures. The specimen and palate were not independently examined or redrawn here, and these few proportions are not a field-identification formula established across age, deformation or material differences.

## A filtering interpretation is not an observed diet {#s-ae20d034ab}
Dense, slender, laterally inclined teeth lead the authors to interpret Liaodactylus as a filter feeder and compare it with Ctenochasma, Gnathosaurus and Pterodaustro. Possible crustaceans, small molluscs, insect larvae or plankton are discussed for those comparative taxa, but no identifiable gut contents are reported for the Liaodactylus holotype. Its teeth support an interpretation of filtering small food items without transferring a complete comparative food list, a particular water-intake mechanism or modern flamingo behavior to this species.

## The authors’ interpretation at about 160 million years {#s-8385a0b6ad}
The paper summarizes Tiaojishan volcanic-rock dates as 161.8 ± 0.4 to 159.5 ± 0.6 Ma and uses them to interpret this approximately 160 Ma pterosaur. These are stratigraphic dates cited by the authors, not a fresh dating of the specimen here. The PBDB 161.5–154.8 Ma record below retains its separate precision. Their 2017 claim of the earliest known ctenochasmatid filtering record is a dated interpretation, not a continuously updated earliest-record ruling. Their phylogenetic analysis places Liaodactylus as sister to Ctenochasma, without establishing the latter’s direct descent from this animal or species.

## The sample limits of morphospace comparison {#s-1fa99ae922}
The authors add Liaodactylus to an existing phylogenetic dataset of 113 species and 224 characters, producing three most-parsimonious trees. A separate feeding-morphology comparison uses 89 species and 34 characters, assigns inferred feeding adaptations to ten categories, then compares morphospace with multidimensional scaling (MDS) and permutational multivariate analysis of variance (PERMANOVA). This comparison tests agreement between morphology and earlier dietary interpretations; it does not observe feeding in 89 species or sample 89 Liaodactylus individuals. Its evolutionary patterns remain bounded by the sample, coding and methods.
