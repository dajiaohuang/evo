## What does Selago acocksii look like? {#s-041fb5fcd6}
The source describes a densely leafy shrublet up to 30 cm tall. Its leaves are oblong and minutely glandular; white flowers grow in oblong spikes, with tiny glands and sharp hairs on the bracts.

## Why might herbarium specimens appear under another name? {#s-b37d024ea8}
The account says specimens may be filed under several names, including S. tenuis, which resembles S. acocksii in overall appearance. It distinguishes S. acocksii by glandular leaves and bracts, additional sharp hairs on the bracts, and a usually shorter corolla tube. The source notes that S. tenuis is known only from Namaqualand.
