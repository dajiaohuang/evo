## How do the regional records describe the underground stem, leaves and flowers? {#s-b7ae806441}
The Flora do Brasil 2020 field records a xylopodium, a woody underground storage organ, and no Malpighian-type hairs on the stem. Leaves are opposite or whorled, oval, with heart-shaped or shallowly heart-shaped bases and thickened or slightly rolled-under margins. The inflorescence is leafy. Flower stalks are 7–20 mm long and bracteoles are present. The 3–10 mm floral tube is hairy, with long or soft hairs; its blunt spur points horizontally or downward. Petals are purple or pink and absent by the fruit stage; stamens attach in the upper third of the tube. A vesicle is present, and the nectar gland at the ovary base may point downward or horizontally. The fruit is tubular and the seed margin blunt. These listed alternatives do not describe one specimen.
