## Noise-related call adjustment is a cited example {#s-585bdec0f6}
The Discussion lists Crossodactylus werneri when citing earlier work on short-term adjustments of dominant call frequency in noisy environments. That is contextual evidence from the cited studies, not a new playback experiment or recording series in this 735-species synthesis. Dominant frequency means the frequency of greatest acoustic energy, not maximum call frequency or a hearing threshold. The proposed connection between stream noise and evolutionary allometry is a hypothesis; a behavioral response within individuals does not itself prove a long-term lineage-level shift.

## Larval guild and adult ecomorph are separate source categories {#s-3cd7f98580}
The row codes Crossodactylus werneri as larval LE (lentic) and adult TOR (torrential). The two stages are retained separately; the adult code cannot be assigned automatically to tadpoles, or the reverse. Lentic/lotic and torrential labels summarize broad habitat associations rather than quantified flow tolerance, while saxicolous or terrestrial codes do not define an entire geographic range. An endotrophic tadpole category is distinct from direct development and does not remove the larval stage. The authors compiled functional assignments from species accounts rather than experimentally measuring every species’ feeding, hydrodynamics, locomotion or habitat choice.

## Paired-stage comparisons do not establish complete developmental independence {#s-f367b23420}
Across the sampled species, larval–adult shape dissimilarities show a weak but significant Mantel association (r 0.16, p 0.003), while disparity correlations differ among families. The authors interpret the overall pattern as support for adaptive decoupling; it does not demonstrate zero correlation, genetic independence, an experimentally selected trait or this species’ developmental trajectory. Phylogenetic analyses use 74 of the 83 sampled species, and ancestral shape/time patterns are model reconstructions from extant data rather than newly measured fossil morphology. Adult K 0.44 with p 0.001 describes less close-relative similarity than the reference expectation; the paper’s “not phylogenetically structured” wording cannot mean that the test is nonsignificant.
