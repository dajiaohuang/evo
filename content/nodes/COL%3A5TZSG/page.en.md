## EltonTraits record and taxonomic context {#s-e5af37f2c1}
The EltonTraits 1.0 record associated with Amazona farinosa has two ecological field groups: food weights and foraging strata. Its taxonomy follows an earlier bird classification; a name match does not resolve later splits or synonym disputes.

## Relative weights of food categories {#s-a732245e38}
The archive’s nonzero food weights are fruit 50%; seeds, nuts and spores included in this category 30%; other plant material (the source category also includes fungi) 20%. Source code A indicates high compiler confidence in the source and diet information. These percentages standardize verbal descriptions rather than measure diet composition; zero does not prove an item is never eaten. Categories such as Nect, Seed and PlantO are broader than their everyday names and cannot imply exclusively nectar, seeds or plants.

## Foraging strata differ from whole-day activity {#s-df0bdd4451}
Foraging-stratum weights flagged as species-level in the archive are trees or high bushes from 2 m upward but below the canopy 60%; in or just above the canopy 40%. They estimate relative use of feeding positions, not nest sites, all habitat or whole-day time budgets. Surface-water, understory, mid/high, canopy and aerial categories have different source definitions; they are not elevational ranges and cannot yield flight heights or habitat area.

## Trace each field group to its source labels {#s-f29bb5cf1c}
Both groups carry the label Ref_4. Diet, strata and activity can come from different material; the same reference number in bird and mammal tables does not identify the same source. Original source strings are retained with their file, row, field group and reference table. Underlying books and papers have not been individually read, and source labels do not count as new independent observations.

## Evidence limits {#s-5be6f07a5e}
This page summarizes the historical EltonTraits 1.0 food and foraging-stratum fields, not a complete species monograph. Matching names and classification do not establish equivalence between historical species concepts and COL26.8. The food weights and stratum percentages are coarse relative estimates standardized from literature descriptions, not direct measurements of intake or feeding time. Source code A indicates high compiler confidence in the source and diet information; it does not turn percentages into direct observations. Underlying books and papers have not been individually reread. Individual, seasonal and local variation, current range, population and conservation require other sources.
