## ## Winged leaves and cup-shaped nectaries {#s-479c37b294}
A morphology row in Brazilian Flora 2020 v393.147 describes *Inga santaremnensis* as having cylindrical, softly hairy branches with lenticels—small openings in the bark. Its leaves have deciduous stipules, small appendages at the leaf base that fall early, recorded as lance-shaped or egg-shaped. Both the leaf stalk (petiole) and central leaf axis (rachis) are winged. The field lists two or three pairs of leaflets and sessile (stalkless), cup-shaped nectaries (nectar-producing glands). It also records spike-like flower clusters, a tube-shaped calyx (the outer floral cup) and flowers without individual stalks. The row marks the fruit-wall texture, fruit shape and fruit hairs unknown. A separate habitat field says terrestrial but gives no locality or survey frame. These regional dataset fields do not establish the species’ full range or variation.
