## One database occurrence for this species {#s-e343f8b3a2}
The fixed Paleobiology Database (PBDB) pterosaur roster accepts Jeholopterus ningchengensis beneath the database parent Jeholopterus. This guide uses one eligible species-level occurrence, linked to one collection and one reference identifier. These count database records and citations, not bones, specimens or animals.

## The named collection and its rock layer {#s-8a4ed9e787}
The selected collection is Daohugou (IVPP collection), recorded in Nei Mongol under region code CN. Its stratum is Tiaojishan and its database age bracket is 165.3–161.5 Ma, with the early bound placed in the Early Callovian. These names follow the database record. One collection cannot map the species’ full range or show that it was absent elsewhere.

## A bracket around the recorded occurrence {#s-93bb87f235}
The selected occurrence has a database age bracket of 165.3–161.5 Ma (Ma means million years ago). This interval bounds the record; it does not show that Jeholopterus lived continuously through every part of that span, or date the species’ origin, extinction or an individual’s lifespan. The collection’s broad interval is not a precise date for the fossil.

## Collection labels are not a habitat description {#s-13a2bf15cb}
The Daohugou record carries the environment label “lacustrine - large” and preservation-mode labels “body,” “mold/impression,” “adpression,” “soft parts” and “original phosphate.” These database labels describe the collection and its fossil-preservation categories. They do not establish Jeholopterus’s living habitat or diet, and a collection-level label need not describe every fossil individually.

## The citation attached to the collection {#s-eb86c12bed}
The database attributes the Daohugou collection to Wang et al. (2002), reference 30959. This is the citation attached to the collection record; the abbreviated attribution alone does not establish that it is the original description of this species.

## Evidence limits {#s-4735cf5dfd}
This guide uses one eligible species-level occurrence from the fixed 276-name PBDB pterosaur roster. Database acceptance and parentage organize the record; they do not settle specimen identifications or every taxonomic dispute, and are kept separate from same-name Catalogue of Life concepts. A locality, age bracket, depositional setting and collection-level preservation label answer different questions. None alone establishes the species’ complete range, exact origin or extinction, abundance, habitat, diet or the condition of every specimen. The selected record does not provide a full account of the species’ anatomy or behavior.
