## Gnaphalium austroafricanum {#s-d4a6fbaf83}
## Runnering stems and heads held above the leaves

Hilliard's *Flora of Southern Africa* account describes a stoloniferous perennial herb with many stems from the crown. Stems lie or trail along the ground, branch loosely above, and may root where their lower parts touch the ground; branches are mostly 150–450 mm long. Their upper portions are thinly covered in gray-white wool. Narrow, spreading leaves measure about 10–25 by 2–4 mm, with broad bases that partly clasp the stem and fine wool on both surfaces.

Small bell-shaped heads grow in corymb-like clusters on woolly stalks held above the foliage. The inner bracts extend beyond the flowers with opaque white tips. Heads contain 83–145 florets. The 0.5 mm dry fruits have paired mucilage-producing hairs. Female florets lack a pappus; bisexual florets have short-feathered bristles. The account generally places the plant in muddy places and distinguishes it from *G. declinatum* and *G. capense* by its looser habit and elevated heads.
