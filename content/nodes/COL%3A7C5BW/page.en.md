## How many silvery female spikelets does it bear? {#s-bcce579574}
A 2012 regional account describes *Thamnochortus amoena* as a tufted, dioecious (male and female flowers occur on separate plants) perennial reaching 1 m. Its smooth culms (stems) carry dense clusters of sterile branches at their nodes, and the leaf sheaths are ragged. It bears three or four female spikelets (small flower clusters), 40–60 mm long, with membranous, silvery bracts (leaf-like structures around flowers). The account gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency.
