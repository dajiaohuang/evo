## A distinctive repeated call and an untested detection explanation {#s-9fcdea0eb9}
The 2016 account described a loud crunch-like advertisement call repeated two or three times. The authors suspected that past surveys had missed frogs because they perched higher and called less often under drier conditions. This is a proposed detection explanation, not a measured perch-height distribution or a causal experiment on humidity. The distinctive-call description supplies no numerical dominant frequency, call duration or breeding-success estimate.

## Lower-elevation records informed a dated proposal {#s-46aae84ffa}
The authors reported a broader known range, lower-elevation records beyond the formerly assumed 800–2000-m restriction and encounters in disturbed agroforest. They proposed Near Threatened instead of the cited Vulnerable category. Multiple-site screening in 2010–2012 detected no chytrid infection, which remains a sample-limited negative result. These dated findings do not establish universal agroforest suitability, infection resistance or a verified current conservation category.
