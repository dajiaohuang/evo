## Accepted name and placement {#s-586d2b64b6}
COL26.8 accepts Amolops shihaitaoi Wang, Li, Du, Hou & Yu, 2022 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Ranidae (family) → Amolops (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## Four Ha Giang vouchers and condition-dependent nuptial spines {#s-1b839f011b}
The Ha Giang comparison examined two males with SVL 33.2–33.6 mm and two females at 42.2–43.8 mm. The males were smaller than the original diagnostic male range, showing why a description’s sample bounds should not be treated as universal limits. The local vouchers had expanded digital discs, unwebbed fingers, fully webbed toes, vomerine teeth and no dorsolateral folds. Male nuptial-pad spines were less distinct than in the Chinese type series; the authors suggested different sampling months, August versus June, as a possible explanation rather than a demonstrated seasonal mechanism. Live olive-brown dorsal colours were recorded separately from ethanol-preserved material.

## A dated addition in Ha Giang rather than a complete range {#s-524eb4f622}
The May 2023 paper reported the species’ first Ha Giang provincial record from an August 2022 survey in Lung Vai Village, Phuong Do Commune, Tay Con Linh Nature Reserve. It cited earlier Vietnamese records in Lao Cai, Cao Bang and Vinh Phuc and southern Chinese records. This establishes the study’s named locality addition, not continuous occupation between provinces, every present locality or a current range boundary. “First record” concerns documentation in the paper rather than a demonstrated arrival in 2022 or range expansion measured through repeated occupancy surveys.

## Diet-category totals and diversity formula require caution {#s-2e1489d151}
One results paragraph reports sixteen male and nineteen female prey categories, while the next gives twenty-four and twenty-six respectively. The account supplies no reconciled category hierarchy, so these are not merged into an invented species-specific total. It reports greater than 65% dietary overlap between sexes, which is an index rather than the proportion of identical meals. The printed Simpson calculation includes a leading negative sign while reporting positive reciprocal diversity; no reconstructed corrected index is presented here. These numerical issues leave the observed prey records useful but restrict quantitative comparisons beyond the original sample.

## The original diagnostic sample {#s-19283efa9e}
The 2022 description reported male snout–vent lengths of 35.5–37.3 mm and female lengths of 39.2–45.7 mm in its diagnostic sample. Dorsolateral folds were absent; vomerine teeth were present, and expanded finger discs had circumferential grooves. These are observations from the description, not universal size limits or a stand-alone identification key.

## Sex and reproductive condition matter {#s-689a35102a}
The authors observed spiny nuptial pads in males and noted differences between males in breeding condition and earlier development of these structures. This connects external appearance to sex and reproductive condition in the examined material; it does not establish mating behaviour, egg deposition or the calendar length of the breeding season.

## Diet observed in one August survey {#s-b95a5638c3}
In August 2022, researchers used stomach flushing on adults from Tay Con Linh Nature Reserve, Ha Giang, Vietnam. They identified 529 prey items, including 515 animal items and 14 unidentified items. Ants, acridid grasshoppers, mantids, spiders and other Lepidoptera were prominent categories. This local sample documents consumed prey, not a species-wide fixed diet or feeding preference independent of prey availability.

## Field habitat and sample limitations {#s-17e410474d}
Frogs were observed on waterfall cliffs and large stream rocks between 20:00 and 23:00, surrounded by evergreen forest and shrubs. The paper reports 40 flushed adults and three empty stomachs, while its abstract and sex-specific analysis refer to 36 individuals (17 males, 19 females); the unexplained difference is retained. Neither these sample counts nor a local first record estimates abundance across the species range.
