## Five Mexican Pacific specimens {#summary}
A 2017 study described five *Branchiostoma californiense* specimens from sandy bottoms in Jalisco and Sinaloa. Their rostral process, oral cirri and myotome counts provide a close look at the recorded material, while the authors leave open whether geographic differences mark one variable species or a species complex.

## A rostral process and counted body segments {#form}
In the five Mexican specimens, the notochord projected forward beyond the oral hood as a distinct rostral process, and the mouth region had up to 40 fine cirri. The authors counted 39–46 myotomes before the atriopore, 16–19 between atriopore and anus, and 9–10 after the anus, for 66–72 in total (mean 69). Dorsal fin-ray chambers numbered 343–395 (mean 364), and preanal chambers 56–61 (mean 58). These are measurements of the examined specimens, not fixed limits for every individual in the species.

## Shallow sandy sites in two Mexican regions {#sample-habitat}
Galván-Villa and colleagues examined four specimens from Bahía Chamela, Jalisco, collected in October 2013 at 5–8 m, and one from Isla Melendres, Sinaloa, collected in August 2015 at 1 m. The paper describes both as coming from sandy bottoms and records collection with a biological dredge. All five were fixed in formalin, preserved in ethanol and deposited in the LEMA invertebrate collection at the University of Guadalajara. These collection sites are study records, not a complete habitat map.

## Regional measurements raise a question, not a phylogeny {#regional-questions}
The authors compared their counts with earlier reports and noted that Bahía Chamela specimens had fewer preatriopore myotomes than Gulf of California specimens, while fin-chamber counts tended to rise from northern to southern Mexican records. They called for additional sampling and a phylogenetic study to test whether the named populations are one species or a complex. Their paper does not resolve that question; geographic differences alone are not evidence of separate lineages.
