## Asymmetron inferum beneath whale bones {#intro}
What does a lancelet found beneath a decomposing whale reveal about an unusual habitat? *Asymmetron inferum* was sampled from sand under sperm-whale bones off southwestern Japan. A mitochondrial study linked those specimens to other lancelets, while leaving the animal’s feeding mechanism unresolved.

## The observation is sand beneath a whale fall {#whale-sand}
In July 2003, the Hyper-Dolphin/Natsushima cruise collected 26 specimens from sand beneath decomposing sperm-whale bones off Cape Nomamisaki, Japan, at 229 m depth. A whale fall is a whale carcass on the seabed; decomposition can create a sulfide-rich environment. The collection documents occurrence in that setting, not direct consumption of bone or a measured mechanism of sulfide tolerance.

## A mitochondrial tree separates it from the lucayanum complex {#mitochondrial-tree}
The study compared eight lancelets and three outgroup animals using mitochondrial genomes, DNA in the cell’s energy-producing organelles. Its analyses placed *A. inferum* as sister to the *A. lucayanum* species complex: a separate branch sharing their closest sampled common ancestor. The result concerns this mitochondrial sample and does not turn the complex’s unnamed lineages into additional recorded species.

## One specimen yielded a rearranged mitochondrial genome {#one-genome}
The whole body of one specimen per newly sequenced species was used for DNA extraction. For *A. inferum*, the resulting sequence AP009352 was 15,084 base pairs long. Comparison of gene order identified a reversed and relocated glutamine transfer-RNA gene, trnQ; transfer RNAs help decode protein instructions. This is a molecular difference, not evidence that the rearrangement causes life at whale falls.
