## A tomentose underside and opposite flower clusters {#s-9ff244c47f}
The Brazilian Flora 2020 v393.147 field records monopodial growth and simple hairs on the stem. Leaves are lance-shaped or ovate; the upper midrib has a continuous groove, and the underside is tomentose (covered in dense, matted hairs). Bracts or cataphylls below leaf nodes are absent. Small flowering branches are compressed, with rounded bracteoles beneath the flowers. The field lists one or two axillary inflorescences per shoot and describes the clusters as pyramidal and opposite. It also places them axillary, subterminal, or in mixed terminal positions. The source does not quantify these alternatives.

## A silky flower cup and a calyx that grows with fruit {#s-2a0f55c98b}
The two pollen sacs in each anther are equal, and hairs cover the floral disc. The flower cup is top-shaped and silky-haired. Sepals are separate; the ovary has two chambers, and the calyx opens partly by tearing. On the fruit, the calyx lobes continue to enlarge. Fruit shape is listed as ellipsoid or cylindrical.

## A terrestrial habitat category {#s-4309966558}
The habitat field records “Terrícola,” meaning terrestrial. It provides no locality, survey date, range coverage, or wild, cultivated, managed, or fossil status.
