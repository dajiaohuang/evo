## Growth and leaves {#s-7297f5b1fd}
Klopper and Smith's 2012 regional account describes a stout, succulent tree 3–5 m tall, branching dichotomously and without persistent dried leaves. Its leaves are arranged in two ranks, dull green to glaucous green, unspotted, broadly linear to strap-shaped, with rounded tips and about 30 cm long. The margins are minutely toothed in the upper third or nearly entire. The loose, cylindrical racemes are simple and reach 50 cm long. Flowers are scarlet and up to 55 mm long, with the stamens and stigma extending beyond the flower.

This description comes from a regional species account published in 2012; it gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency.

## Branching pattern and a related species {#s-25fc006153}
Reynolds's 1974 account, which uses the name *A. plicatilis*, describes many clusters of two-ranked, strap-shaped leaves, each like an open fan. It calls *A. haemanthifolia* a close leaf-relative but says it cannot be confused with this plant: *A. haemanthifolia* is very small and stemless, grows in groups, and has a dense, head-shaped raceme. Reynolds reports large *A. plicatilis* specimens reaching 4.6 m [as “15 ft.”]. The account describes their repeated dichotomous branching as arising when a leaf cluster divides into two at the summit and each new cluster divides again. Each leaf cluster produces one simple, unbranched inflorescence. Flowers reach 55 mm, are thick and fleshy, and are described cautiously as apparently always scarlet.

These observations come from a regional species account published in 1974; the excerpt gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency.
