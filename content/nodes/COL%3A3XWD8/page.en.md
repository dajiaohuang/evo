## Accepted name and classification {#s-f7d19ff22e}
The COL26.8 release of 20 August 2026 accepts Mantidactylus catalai Angel, 1935 as a species and places it along Anura (order) → Mantellidae (family) → Mantidactylus (genus). This is the name and classification recorded in that version; a similarly named entry in another checklist or fossil database needs its own identity evidence before the records can be linked.

## A combination of head, toe, and stripe characters {#s-29328f116f}
In the revision's examined material, snout–vent length (SVL, measured from the tip of the snout to the vent) is 41–45 mm in males and 51–62 mm in females. The head is broad and flattened, with a pointed snout projecting 1.75–3.45 mm. The toes are webbed, but 1–1.5 phalanges of toe IV remain free of webbing; the diagnosis also notes no superior lip stripe or obvious inguinal streak. The characters are used together, so size alone is not an independent identification. These sex-specific ranges are not total body lengths or measurements of every population.

## Observations beside water and among rocks {#s-1d65344707}
Observations in rainforest and bamboo habitat ran from 09:00 to 00:15. Frogs were found in holes and gaps between rocks near still water or rivers; fast streams were reported rarely. During daylight, observations were mainly on rocks and the ground. No leaf or branch perches were observed, although specimen UADBA 4521 was collected on a root. The account describes semi-aquatic and burrowing behaviour. These encounters do not establish obligatory underground nesting or prove that frogs never use vegetation elsewhere; observation times are not an experimentally measured daily activity budget.

## Confirmed records in southeastern Madagascar {#s-ec303fefde}
The revision assigns confirmed Mantidactylus catalai records to low- and middle-elevation rainforest in southeastern Madagascar. This account supplies no numerical elevation limits, so “low” and “middle” remain qualitative source categories. The locality summary is not a mapped area of occupancy or a complete survey of southeastern Madagascar, and it does not show that suitable unsampled forest is occupied. Records assigned to other Ochthomantis species are kept separate.

## Restoration from synonymy and one voucher reassignment {#s-359ff7e6b8}
The revision restores catalai from synonymy with femoralis after examining types, morphology, and mitochondrial 16S sequence groupings; it reports 33 examined specimens. One group includes sequence AY324821, tied to voucher FGMV 2002.173 and formerly labelled cf. mocquardi and candidate species 47, alongside material associated with Ambatolahy and Ranomafana. This is a specific voucher reassignment, not grounds to relabel every mocquardi record. The revision reports pairwise 16S distances greater than 3%; these support its combined argument but are not a universal species threshold or whole-genome proof of reproductive isolation.
