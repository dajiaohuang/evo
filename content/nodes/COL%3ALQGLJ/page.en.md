## Accepted name and placement {#s-2e60c85e5f}
COL26.8 accepts Andrias sligoi (Boulenger, 1924) as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Caudata (order) → Cryptobranchidae (family) → Andrias (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## Reading route {#s-e18315c28e}
Begin with the accepted record and its parent genus, then use the family and order pages to locate related catalogue entries. This page supplies a taxonomic orientation. Species-specific morphology, ecology, life history, distribution, evolutionary evidence and conservation information still require reviewed biological sources.

## Four introduced individuals identified through converging evidence {#s-a53f8f61c0}
The study places four Japan-held individuals in mitochondrial lineageD, assigned to sligoi. It combines that result with fourteen-locus microsatellite comparisons, external morphology and historical import information. The authors interpret them as genetically pure sligoi, but nuclear PCA showed substantial overlap among Chinese lineages. This is a study-specific assignment, not whole-genome certification of every possible hybrid ancestor or a complete revision of Chinese Andrias. Native Chinese origins of the four individuals remain unknown.

## Mitochondrial distances and nuclear overlap have different scopes {#s-0946ab0bf0}
The Bayesian cytb tree uses590–1141bp sequences; distances were calculated by pairwise deletion using samples longer than800bp. Results report davidianus-lineageB versus sligoi-lineageD distances of2.6–3.2%, while Discussion gives2.0–3.2%. These differing bounds remain source-specific rather than silently merged. Microsatellite PCA separates japonicus strongly from the Chinese group, but does not sharply separate all Chinese lineages. Neither a small mitochondrial distance nor overlap in a few PCA axes alone determines accepted species rank.

## Two live individuals were genetically sexed as male {#s-490519e23a}
Four W-associated markers produced female-specific bands in two gonad-sexed davidianus controls and no bands in two male controls. The two live sligoi lacked these bands and were assigned male. This is a marker-based inference in this sample, not direct gonad examination of the living sligoi or evidence of a male-only natural population. The other two sligoi were dead; one is preserved as KUHE41444 and described as an adult female. No successful sligoi breeding event was reported here.

## A preserved female and photographs supply different measurements {#s-19e3a89d72}
KUHE41444 has total length1115mm, snout–vent length698mm and tail417mm; these measurements describe one preserved adult female, not a species maximum or a male range. Live individuals were photographed and total length measured, with detailed examination avoided to reduce physical harm. The description reports broad flattened heads, small tubercles and developed lateral folds, but similar traits occur in other Chinese Andrias. A measured hindlimb value and printed percentage do not agree arithmetically, so that ratio is not independently certified here.

## Colour differences do not provide a complete diagnostic key {#s-91f8bc7bb6}
The examined individuals included strongly contrasted dark/pale-brown colours and a deceased individual described as entirely black. The authors question whether a historical illustration correctly depicts sligoi coloration and request further specimen examination. This is an interpretation of illustrations and a few photographed or preserved individuals, not a measured colour-frequency distribution or proof that all-black animals belong to one species. Small head tubercles support their comparison but are not alone sufficient for identification.

## Japanese holdings are introduced records with uncertain Chinese origins {#s-112ebaaa39}
The four individuals were held in Japan, including aquaria, a zoo, a private holding and a museum voucher. Their table entries give unknown Chinese origins; they cannot establish native occurrence at the Japanese holding sites. The authors discuss a Hong Kong ditch discovery behind the original description as possibly an escaped captive, with no known native giant salamanders there. Southern China is a presumed origin region, but the paper explicitly leaves the precise natural range uncertain because human transport has obscured provenance.

## A dated captive growth series is not a lifespan experiment {#s-0236482ccc}
One male’s historical measurements were305mm/113.8g in1986,890mm/4.6kg in1996 and1220mm/12.4kg in2008; in December2021 it measured1375mm and23.4kg. Another male measured approximately1250mm in March2020. These are particular captive individuals, not a species growth curve, wild maximum or age known from hatching. The paper’s roughly60-year lifespan limit is attributed to unpublished data, so it is not independently established by these measurements.

## A proposed ex-situ rescue is not a demonstrated recovery {#s-a01e3c5a21}
At the study’s reporting time two identified male sligoi remained alive in captivity; this is not a verified current inventory. The authors propose storing sperm and germ cells and finding additional individuals, especially females, for future reproduction. Those plans are not reported successful cryopreservation, breeding or reintroduction outcomes. Statements about imminent disappearance and absence of new wild F1 sightings are conservation interpretations with incomplete detection and provenance, not proof of extinction in China or Japan.

## Reference-marker role in a Japanese ancient-DNA comparison {#s-7368bb0674}
The 2025 Ehime study uses previously published Andrias sligoi sequence accession entries MH051435; LC650454 in its short cytb comparison. Table1 repeats KIZYPX2513 for two sequence entries, one with Xinglong, Chongqing and one with unknown Chinese locality; these are not automatically two independent animals. The prose Methods species list omits sligoi despite its table inclusion. These table entries are reference material, not newly excavated remains of this species or a complete population sample. The fossil assignment to japonicus does not date the origin or expansion of Andrias sligoi, and a93 bp marker does not certify modern nuclear ancestry, hybridization history or all species boundaries. Original sequence records and specimen identities have not been independently rechecked.
