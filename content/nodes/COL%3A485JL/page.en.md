## Did brachial-gland licking occur during grooming or hunting? {#s-ab4a8c2724}
At Cikananga Wildlife Center in West Java, Indonesia, researchers presented prey in 75 trials to 22 rescued adult greater slow lorises that had been captured from the wild. They recorded brachial-gland licking—the gland is on the upper arm—five times, four during grooming. In the only non-grooming observation, a male approached a caterpillar, withdrew to a perch and licked the gland without trying to catch it. This captive rescue-center experiment does not show how wild lorises use venom during prey capture.
