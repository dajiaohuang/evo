## atlas-profile / overview {#s-7ce7735b2e}
More than 35 fossils from the White Sea region preserve a bilateral, soft-bodied animal with a broad foot-like area and a non-mineralized cover on its back. The sample lets researchers reconstruct a distinctive body plan; it does not settle whether Kimberella belongs within crown Mollusca.

## atlas-profile / ecology / diet {#s-4d34c4dd89}
A surface-feeding interpretation is inferred from the body form and associated traces. No radula is preserved, so the feeding mechanism remains unknown.

## atlas-profile / ecology / habitat {#s-5370412b6c}
The fossils come from a shallow-marine seafloor in the White Sea region. This is a sampled setting, not a map of the animal’s full geographic range.

## atlas-profile / ecology / locomotion {#s-427ff4113e}
The broad foot-like region is consistent with crawling across the seafloor, but the movement itself is inferred from preserved anatomy.

## atlas-profile / ecology / bodySize {#s-5f9b01c096}
The fossils represent a centimetre-scale soft-bodied animal; the available description does not define a broader size range.

## atlas-profile / ecology / guild {#s-fd04a65273}
A mobile benthic feeder is a reconstruction based on the body plan and associated traces, not a preserved behavior or direct gut-content record.

## atlas-profile / traits {#s-83e735064d}
The fossil bodies are bilaterally organized, with a recognizable front-to-back axis.

## atlas-profile / traits {#s-0a91518977}
A broad, foot-like ventral region is central to the proposed crawling reconstruction.

## atlas-profile / traits {#s-837f6224b2}
The dorsal cover was non-mineralized, so the fossil preserves its outline and impressions rather than a shell of hard plates.

## atlas-profile / evidenceSummary {#s-70a82f66be}
The White Sea sample supports a reconstruction of body form and likely seafloor activity. It preserves no radula, does not establish membership in crown Mollusca or direct ancestry, and cannot stand for a complete geographic range.

## atlas-profile / readerLimitations {#s-e692bb6d77}
The White Sea sample preserves body form, but no radula. Crawling and surface feeding are reconstructions from anatomy and associated traces; the fossils do not establish crown-Mollusca membership, direct ancestry or the full species range.
