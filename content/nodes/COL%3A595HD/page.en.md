## What does the historical account describe about the leaves and spiny fruit? {#s-ffa47d781a}
The historical Flora of Panama account describes a shrub usually 1–2 m tall, sometimes a small tree; it prints the tree height as “4 in.”, so that unit is unclear. Young branches and 2–7 cm petioles bear short star-shaped hairs and longer simple bristles. Broadly elliptic leaves are usually three-lobed, sometimes only faintly so, and measure 7–14 by 5–11 cm. Their base is rounded to nearly heart-shaped, the tip tapers, and the edges are irregularly, bluntly toothed. The upper surface has long hairs; the lower has scattered fine star-shaped hairs and some spreading simple hairs. Flower clusters grow in leaf axils or opposite leaves, with two or three small clusters per cyme. The flowers are bisexual, 8–13 mm long, with narrow pointed sepals and five narrow obovate petals, yellow or sometimes orange. The ovary is densely covered with tiny hooked spines; the style is 6–7 mm long. The spherical fruit body is about 4 mm across and bears roughly 75 backward-pointing spines, each 3–4 mm long. Each of its three or four chambers contains two seeds; the lens-shaped seeds are 2–3 mm long. This is a historical regional account without a stated population sample or observation date.

## What range and setting does the historical flora report? {#s-70d69dbbb1}
The historical account calls it a weedy species widely distributed through tropical America and reports it growing in open sunlight on hillsides. This is the source’s historical summary, not a current range survey.
