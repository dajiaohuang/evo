## The name and this data account {#s-b090fea985}
Brachygalba lugubris is accepted in COL26.8 within Galbulidae. AVONET v7 links the record to its HBW–BirdLife 2020 name and reports 5 measured individuals, 4 with complete morphological sets. Trait-level samples may differ, and the historical link does not prove modern taxonomic equivalence.

## Measured beak dimensions {#s-6c67779817}
Mean beak length is 40 mm from the anterior nostril edge to the tip; width and depth at that edge average 4.9 mm and 5.3 mm. These are separate standardized dimensions rather than identification cutoffs.

## Wing, tarsus and mass {#s-d361b5256f}
Mean unflattened wing length is 68.4 mm from the carpal joint to the longest primary; mean tarsus length is 9.1 mm. The separately compiled mass mean is 15.9 g, labelled Dunning. It is not a weight measurement assigned to each morphology specimen.

## Habitat, feeding and lifestyle {#s-846f3e8984}
AVONET labels habitat Forest, trophic niche Invertivore and lifestyle Insessorial. These are broad categories for comparison, not a complete account of habitat use or feeding.

## Movement and geographical summary {#s-ed00bd6d57}
The movement field is Sedentary. Historical breeding and resident maps have latitude endpoints −21.42° and 8.17°. They do not capture all non-breeding movement or current occupancy; geography and morphology were compiled separately.

## Relative weights of food categories {#s-38af8859ae}
Invertebrates account for all nonzero food weight (100%). Code A signals high compiler confidence in the source and diet information. This standardized weight reflects literature descriptions, not a direct measurement of prey intake.

## Foraging strata differ from whole-day activity {#s-04dfa519cc}
Species-level foraging weights are 60% in or just above the canopy, 20% in understory, edges or shrubs below 2 m, and 20% in trees or high bushes from 2 m upward but below the canopy. These are relative source categories, not exact heights, nest sites or an all-day time budget.
