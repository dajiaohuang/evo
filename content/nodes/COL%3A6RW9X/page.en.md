## A named species in COL26.8 {#s-4d84477e9d}
COL26.8 lists *Neogobius melanostomus* (Pallas, 1814) as an accepted species. The evidence discussed here comes from selected European river populations and does not form a complete account of the species.

## A Lower Rhine catch result {#s-184f385eb5}
Annual July angling surveys at a groin field near Rees on the Lower Rhine, Germany, collected 2,905 gobiids from 2010 through 2020. Of those caught fish, 93.5% were *N. melanostomus*. This is the species' share of gobiids caught at one site with one survey method over those years; it is not an estimate of the species' total population or range. The study does not assess how other seasons, habitats or collection gear would change the result.

## Mitochondrial variants and a proposed route into the Rhine {#s-2f2f4f6fd5}
The study compared mitochondrial control-region sequences from 286 fish sampled at four European sites: Rees on the Lower Rhine, Basel on the Upper Rhine, Deggendorf and Passau on the Danube, and the Trave near the Baltic Sea. It reported three sequence variants, or haplotypes. Rhine samples contained all three; sampled Danube fish had haplotype A, while sampled Trave fish had B and C. The authors interpreted this pattern as support for two invasion routes into the Rhine. This is a historical inference from a limited set of populations and one mitochondrial region, not a species-wide phylogeny. The study also states that its data do not settle how many invasion waves occurred or when; the collection dates for the comparative genetic samples are not specified in its main-text methods.
