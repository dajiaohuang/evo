## A bonebed preserves several growth stages {#s-fd9a79792a}
Caiuajara dobruskii comes from Cruzeiro do Oeste, Paraná, Brazil. The 2014 original description designates partial skeleton CP.V 1449 as holotype and compares abundant cranial and postcranial material from the locality across different sizes. This provides growth evidence beyond one isolated skull, although most bones are mixed and cannot be allocated to particular individuals. A 2022 study adds skull CP.V 8175 and other material to examine cranial preservation and variation.

## The accumulation includes repeated burial and transport {#s-17398fd873}
The 2014 paper reports hundreds of bones and a minimum of 47 individuals based on premaxillae, a different unit from one database occurrence row. Material occurs at several nearby levels: some remains are articulated, most are disassociated and mixed, and some bones broke before burial. The authors also identify evidence of hydraulic sorting. The deposit cannot simply be reconstructed as one intact group dying simultaneously, and its minimum individual count is not total population size. The number is quoted from the main text without independently recounting the supplementary list.

## A toothless rostrum and diagnostic cranial features {#s-07daefd5f4}
The toothless rostrum bends downward, with a tall premaxillary crest and a bony expansion projecting into the nasoantorbital fenestra. A rounded depression on the mandibular occlusal surface also forms part of the original diagnosis. Premaxillary foramina lead the authors to infer a birdlike horny covering, rather than directly documenting a complete preserved sheath. The bony expansion has no established function, and diagnostic morphology alone does not determine the share of fruit or other foods in the diet.

## Crests change with growth and retain individual variation {#s-554b283a15}
In the 2014 sample, small individuals have smaller, backward-inclined premaxillary crests; larger individuals have taller, steeper crests and more developed dentary crests. The authors mainly interpret this sequence as development. Comparing similarly sized individuals in 2022, researchers also find differences in the thickness and shape of the posterodorsal crest margin. They discuss possible sexual dimorphism while warning that immature individuals may resemble adults of another sex. Crest presence, size or shape therefore cannot directly label sex, and size is not an exact age.

## Body proportions and fusion record different aspects of growth {#s-7877be7496}
Humeri and most postcranial elements retain broadly similar shapes across sizes in the original description, while ossification, sternum, prepubis and coracoid still vary with development. The scapula and coracoid fuse in adults but remain separate in juveniles; no notarium is reported. Early establishment of body proportions leads the authors to suggest flight at a young age, without observations of juvenile launch or an age at first flight. Similar proportions do not establish that every tissue is already mature.

## Social behavior and mortality retain different uncertainties {#s-08d4ab8cc2}
The 2014 authors interpret dense, repeated accumulations as gregarious pterosaurs around an inland desert lake. They also consider migratory visits, preferring longer residence. Storm transport, drought and storm mortality are discussed, but the actual causes of death remain unknown. The 2022 introduction records other vertebrates at the site, including Keresdrakon, so the whole locality is not a single-species assemblage. Aggregation does not directly document parental care, and an interdunal depositional setting does not establish a complete life history or diet.

## Later cranial work adds a symphyseal diagnosis {#s-5cdfbb7689}
CP.V 8175 in the 2022 study preserves a partly articulated skull, lower jaw and manual material. Three-dimensional preservation coexists with weathering, mandibular cracking and separation around the orbit. Using comparisons including CP.V 5703, the authors identify dentaries meeting at three positions, forming a symphyseal shelf with internal openings, and add this to the diagnosis. New material helps reinterpret older fossils without making every feature an observation from one undamaged skull. This guide covers selected material, diagnosis and discussion passages, not the full anatomical description.
