## Name and classification {#s-005b33e6aa}
The COL26.8 release of 20 August 2026 lists Craugastor gollmeri (Peters, 1863) as a species in Anura, family Craugastoridae, genus Craugastor. This identifies the name used in that catalogue; it does not by itself show that every historical record under the same name belongs to the same biological species.

## Two kinds of evidence {#s-3ec824f56b}
The page brings together two distinct records: historical traits compiled in AmphiBIO and host entries in a 2025 regional parasite checklist. Each has its own source scope. Neither provides a complete account of the species, and the older name has not been matched conclusively to the current catalogue concept.

## A parasite host entry in a 2025 review {#s-bb4c8bc146}
In its Molineidae checklist, a 2025 review lists Craugastor gollmeri as a host associated with Oswaldocruzia costaricensis. The association is compiled from earlier reports; the checklist does not describe a newly examined frog sample. The host name also appears among the additional hosts in the parasite account. The review gives no denominator for examined frogs, prevalence, infection intensity, pathology, or mortality, and the older reports and specimens have not been reidentified for this account.

## Locality code in the host entry {#s-bae7d21a89}
The host entry associates Oswaldocruzia costaricensis with the code CR. The locality list inspected for the review did not define CR, so its place remains unresolved. This is a host-specific literature record, not the parasite's full distribution. The historical observation does not establish present-day occupancy, the frog's complete range, native or introduced status, or absence of the parasite elsewhere; the name match also does not verify every older host identification under the current species concept.

## How the review searched {#s-082a3b4c31}
The review searched BioOne, ISI, JSTOR, PubMed, SciELO, Scopus, Web of Science, and monographs for regional Molineidae literature published from 1819 to 2025. It excluded incomplete records, unpublished theses and meeting data, and parasite records not identified to species; it also sought species-level molecular records in publications and GenBank. Its inspected methods do not give search strings or database-specific search dates, so absence from the checklist is not a reproducible negative result. The review uses defined Caribbean, Panamanian, and Neotropical regions; it is not a global parasite inventory. Supplementary Table S1, deposited sequences, and original reports were not independently examined for this account.
