## Accepted name and catalogue position {#s-6d3e58bd96}
COL26.8, released on 20 August 2026, records Engystomops montubio (Ron, Cannatella & Coloma, 2004) as a species in the genus Engystomops, family Leptodactylidae, and order Anura. This is the classification in that release; a matching name in another checklist or fossil database needs separate evidence before the records can be treated as the same biological concept.

## What the classification can tell us {#s-e37154fd6f}
The accepted record places this species within Engystomops, Leptodactylidae and Anura. That classification gives a route to related catalogue entries, but it does not by itself establish the species' full morphology, life history, distribution, evolutionary history or conservation status.

## Three field encounters with dates and vouchers {#s-bd39431b64}
At Pedro Franco Dávila Protected Forest, a female encountered on 12 March 2016 at 72 m had a snout–vent length (SVL) of 22.71 mm (MZUA-An.1664) and was foraging in leaf litter beside a trail. At Mocache on 12 January 2014, two males at 57 m measured 18.78 and 18.86 mm (MZUA-An.0919 and 0920); they were in partly submerged litter in a small puddle near a wetland. These three encounters do not document egg-laying, tadpole habitat, seasonal breeding or preference measured against available habitat.

## Identification features and reported range {#s-bbc4b034f9}
The identification account describes dorsal tubercles and flank or parotoid glands, and reports no tarsal tubercle or vomerine odontophore, a tooth-bearing structure on the roof of the mouth. It separately cites mean male and female SVLs of 20.6 and 18.52 mm; these means are not the later individual measurements, including the 22.71 mm female above. The Los Ríos records extend the range known to that article eastward; they do not demonstrate recent colonization. Its country-endemic label is tied to the source date and does not establish that the present-day range is complete.

## Survey and measurement limits {#s-2c36b4cfaa}
The article describes unrestricted visual searches and manual captures, not detection sampling with equal effort. Its methods give expedition dates from January 2014 through December 2023, while the species accounts also include a 2008 alytolylax record and a February 2024 muricatus record. GPS elevations refer to encounters, not camps. The authors say small-animal SVLs were rounded to 0.1 mm, although many printed values have two decimal places; those values are retained as reported without implying greater precision. Tissue collection and voucher deposition are statements in the article; the specimens were not independently inspected here, and these statements do not add a genetic identification.
