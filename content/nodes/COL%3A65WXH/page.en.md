## Localities beyond Carajás National Forest {#s-aa547132ab}
The study recorded one male’s call at São Felix do Xingu, reporting agreement with the published description, and sequenced specimens from São Geraldo do Araguaia. Their 16S sequences differed by 0.5–1.1% from a cited paratopotype sequence. The authors interpreted these as the first confirmed localities outside Carajás National Forest. Acoustic and mitochondrial comparisons support the reported specimen assignments; they do not establish new species boundaries or all intervening occupied areas.

## An explicitly disputed Belo Monte assignment {#s-dc1e8792cb}
The authors questioned a prior assignment from UHE Belo Monte at Vitória do Xingu, citing a 16S sequence 2.0–2.2% divergent from their other A. carajas comparisons, including the type series. That locality remains a disputed identification in this account. Sequence divergence alone is not converted into a new species diagnosis or a resolved correction of the older voucher.

## A cited sister relationship and distinct diagnostic evidence {#s-c875291799}
The account described the frog as diurnal and ground-dwelling in preserved Serra dos Carajás rainforest and cited earlier molecular work placing it as sister to the morphologically similar A. crombiei. It cited 5% uncorrected 16S divergence between those species, alongside finger-IV morphology and call note counts and intervals as diagnostic evidence. These are attributed prior comparisons, separate from the study’s own sampled males and sequences; the percentage is not a universal species threshold or a new comprehensive phylogenetic analysis.
