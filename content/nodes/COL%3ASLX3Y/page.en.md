## Accepted name and placement {#s-82ce90dce1}
COL26.8 accepts Raorchestes drutaahu Garg, Suyesh, Das, Bee & Biju, 2021 as a species. Its recorded classification places it along Anura (order) → Rhacophoridae (family) → Raorchestes (genus). The name and placement refer to the release issued on 20 August 2026; names in another checklist or fossil database require their own identity evidence.

## A variable brown frog identified by a character combination {#s-0a6d6fdea5}
The diagnosis gives male snout–vent length of 20–23 mm and a distinct tympanum about half the eye diameter. Brown to straw-coloured dorsal skin carries horny spinules and ridges, paired concave bands, and a pair of irregular black spots near the groin on the posterior back. Flanks and groin lack contrasting colour blotches; the brown iris has a golden tinge and lighter upper versus darker lower halves. Foot webbing is limited. The five measured specimens showed differences in dorsal colour and markings, including faint versus prominent bands. These sample-based features must be combined with comparisons against related species; a brown dorsum alone does not identify drutaahu, and the measured male range is not a complete female or population size distribution.

## Two recorded localities on opposite sides of the Palghat gap {#s-f9d26ebc81}
The 2021 description reported drutaahu from Kadalar in Idukki district south of the Palghat gap and Siruvani in Palakkad district north of it, at 1,000–1,450 m above sea level. Frogs occurred in forest at grassland–shola edges or fragmented forest patches near plantations and were located on leaves of short shrubs 0.5–1.5 m high. These are recorded localities and perch observations, not a complete range boundary, evidence of continuous occupancy across the gap or an experimental preference for plantation edges. Leaf perches do not establish the egg-laying substrate, clutch size or parental care. The dated description does not independently verify present occupancy or conservation status.

## Short calls with closely packed pulses {#s-e991720aae}
The article described one male call type, delivered singly rather than in call groups, with closely packed pulses. Its typical-call summary gives duration 50.6 ms, six pulses, pulse rate 134.5 per second and a mean dominant frequency of 3.6 kHz. Duration, pulse count and pulse rate are distinct measured acoustic properties and should not be recomputed as though every interval were uniform. The authors warn that their summary values do not account for temperature and social-context effects or all within-individual and among-individual variation. This is a described acoustic sample, not proof that additional call types never occur or that every male has an invariant frequency.

## A supported relationship to coonoorensis {#s-b1998aa470}
The study placed drutaahu in the charius species group and recovered a well-supported sister relationship with coonoorensis. Reported mitochondrial 16S divergence from coonoorensis was 4.3–5.2%, with different ranges for other group members. The broader phylogenetic analysis combined mitochondrial and nuclear markers, with tRNA-VAL excluded from the final matrix because many downloaded sequences lacked it. Pairwise 16S distances are not the whole multilocus tree and do not supply a universal species threshold, a divergence date or proof of reproductive isolation. The paper used morphology and acoustics alongside sequence evidence for its integrative description.
