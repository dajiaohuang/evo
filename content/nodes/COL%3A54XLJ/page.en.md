## An accepted name and a compact profile {#s-aba642ac0c}
The pinned Catalogue of Life release COL26.8 accepts Tarentola neglecta in Phyllodactylidae, the leaf-toed gecko family. The profile below paraphrases the species row in ReptTraits v1-2, which follows the workbook’s Reptile Database 2023 name framework and cites sources trait by trait. One row condenses reports and cannot show that every population shares the same traits.

## Length measured to the vent {#s-c345916f84}
The largest reported snout–vent length is 73.2 mm; the largest female value is 61.2 mm. Snout–vent length (SVL) measures from the snout to the vent and excludes the tail. These are maxima in the cited material, not typical sizes or growth limits. If the sex-specific maxima come from different places or studies, their difference alone cannot measure size differences within a population.

## A broad description of setting and activity {#s-d0fc927e8c}
The record combines arboreal and terrestrial settings, labels the diet carnivorous and activity nocturnal, and codes foraging mode as ACT, or active. The habitat categories can coexist; they are not an exhaustive account of where the gecko lives. The diet label does not identify prey, and this compact record may not capture differences among populations, seasons or life stages.

## What one egg or young means in this summary {#s-e82eb94fe6}
The source labels this species oviparous, or egg-laying. The consolidated field is 1 offspring or egg per litter or clutch, and the lowest and highest reported clutch or litter means are each 1. The summary may use midpoints of reported means, or midpoint values from reported extremes when means are unavailable. These fields describe summaries, not a guarantee that every clutch contains one egg; the endpoints are not necessarily the smallest and largest clutches directly observed. The labels do not establish embryo nutrition or uniformity among populations.

## Evidence limits {#s-d67561416a}
This profile summarizes a single ReptTraits species row, retaining the workbook’s 2023 name framework and cited sources. Such a row compresses variation in sex, age, locality and population; a shared name and family do not prove identity with later taxonomic concepts. The reported maximum lengths are not species-wide biological limits, and the clutch summaries are not fixed counts at every nest. Mass entries are often estimated from length equations, so no mass estimate is presented here as a direct weighing. Conservation, venom, current distribution and population trends are not updated; blank fields remain gaps. Citation labels preserve the source trail but do not mean the project independently reviewed every original work.
