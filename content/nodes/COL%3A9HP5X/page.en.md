## A name fixed to COL26.8 {#s-8d85afde1a}
Catalogue of Life release COL26.8 records Spatula versicolor (Vieillot, 1816) as the accepted species name (published 2026-08-20). This card preserves that version's identity; the name entry alone does not describe anatomy or ecology.

## Beak, wing and compiled mass have different denominators {#s-949f1a4fdb}
Morphology covers 5 birds (2 female and 3 male), with 4 complete measurements. Mean nostril-to-tip beak length is 31.4 mm, beak width at the nostrils 15.4 mm, unflattened wing 185 mm and hand-wing index 49.9. These are species averages; the index is defined by Kipp’s distance/wing length ×100, not flight speed. Mass 595.21 g comes separately from Dunning’s literature compilation and cannot inherit the morphology sample size. Inference=NO retains the workbook’s non-inference flag without supplying individual bounds from unread raw material. The workbook gives the same mass number, 595.21 g, for Spatula puna. Dunning’s book and individual records were not read to explain this equality, which does not establish identical individual masses in the two species.

## Read feeding niche and trophic level separately {#s-cb2c8d1c69}
Habitat is Wetland and density code 3 means open habitats. Trophic.Niche=Herbivore aquatic denotes an aquatic-plant-oriented niche; Trophic.Level=Herbivore denotes a plant-oriented trophic level. The columns have different definitions. Original labels are retained without rewriting either from the other or deriving seasonal diet percentages. Aquatic lifestyle means substantial time on water and feeding while afloat or diving; it does not specify dive depth, frequency or the species’ particular feeding action.

## Movement category and the observations still needed {#s-0983166ccc}
Migration=2 is partially migratory, covering minority long-distance migration or widespread short-distance, nomadic or distinct altitudinal movements. The code alone does not identify specific routes or movements of each local population. Hand-wing index and habitat labels cannot replace behavioural observations. Locality, timing and explicit sources are needed to connect the category to particular populations’ life histories.
