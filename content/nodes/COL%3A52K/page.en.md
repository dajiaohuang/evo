## Accepted name and placement {#s-45560c50de}
In COL26.8, Dendrobatoidea is classified as a superfamily within the order Anura. The release's complete accepted-species query returns 349 species below this path. This is a dated catalogue count: it describes the names and concepts in that classification, not a count of sampled animals or a biological survey.

## Study and catalogue concepts {#s-d16b3bd27f}
The 2006 study uses Dendrobatoidea for a dart-poison-frog clade and proposes two families. In the separate COL26.8 hierarchy, Aromobatidae and Dendrobatidae are listed below this superfamily; open either child page to read material about that taxon and its own sources. The catalogue records classification and navigation, but by itself does not establish that all descendants share one biology or evolutionary history, or that the study and catalogue use identical taxon concepts.

## A phylogenetic proposal {#s-e1f13ef9dd}
Grant and colleagues’ 2006 total-evidence study combined morphological, behavioural and defensive-chemical characters with mitochondrial and nuclear DNA to test relationships among dart-poison frogs and their relatives. They elevated the dendrobatid clade to Dendrobatoidea and proposed a revised arrangement of families, subfamilies and genera; this is the classification hypothesis of that study, not a trait shared automatically by every frog called a poison frog.

## Two families and a sister lineage {#s-c242df0308}
In the authors’ arrangement, Dendrobatoidea contains Aromobatidae and Dendrobatidae as sister families. Aromobatidae is divided into Anomaloglossinae, Aromobatinae and Allobatinae; the paper places Colostethinae, Hyloxalinae and Dendrobatinae within Dendrobatidae. Hylodidae is treated as the sister family to Dendrobatoidea in this analysis. These names describe the 2006 topology and rank proposal; later checklists may use other placements.

## What morphology could diagnose {#s-bfc306ea42}
The authors did not find a phenotypic character state that unambiguously diagnoses Aromobatidae at its node, and they reported its support through diagnostic DNA transformations. Their character analysis nevertheless maps specific features elsewhere in the tree, including a loss of reproductive amplexus on the Dendrobatoidea branch. Such mapped states are evolutionary inferences from the sampled matrix, not direct observations that every living descendant behaves identically.

## A broad Neotropical range {#s-eeb5b8bdb7}
The 2006 treatment describes dendrobatoid frogs across Central and South America and the Lesser Antilles, with many species on the eastern Andean slopes, across Amazonia and in Brazil’s Atlantic Forest. This is a group-level range summary in that publication; the paper does not make every locality a record for every member species.

## Short introduction {#s-02f6835077}
A 2006 combined-evidence analysis proposed Dendrobatoidea as a two-family clade, mapped character changes onto its tree, and summarized a broad Neotropical range.
