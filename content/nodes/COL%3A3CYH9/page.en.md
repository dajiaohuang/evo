## Paired-stage comparisons do not establish complete developmental independence {#s-b84f432619}
Across the sampled species, larval–adult shape dissimilarities have a weak but significant Mantel association (r 0.16, p 0.003)—a test of correspondence between distance matrices—while disparity correlations vary among families. The authors interpret the overall pattern as support for adaptive decoupling, but it does not show zero correlation, genetic independence, an experimentally selected trait or Eupsophus calcaratus’s own developmental trajectory. Phylogenetic analyses use 74 of the 83 sampled species. Reconstructed ancestral shape and timing are model results from living-species data, not new measurements of fossil morphology. For adults, K 0.44 with p 0.001 indicates less similarity among close relatives than the reference expectation. The paper’s phrase “not phylogenetically structured” therefore cannot be read to mean that the test is nonsignificant. Supplement S3 repeats family N totals of 112 for both stages; without the raw matrices, the adult individual denominator and imputed cells remain uncertified.
