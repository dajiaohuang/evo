## Accepted name and record source {#s-f2949bb7a4}
COL26.8 accepts Crocodilurus amazonicus in the family Teiidae. This account draws on its ReptTraits v1-2 profile, whose taxonomy follows the workbook’s Reptile Database 2023 framework and whose traits retain their cited sources. Because the dataset gives one row per species, the values summarize this record rather than every population.

## Length measured without the tail {#s-216d37e855}
The listed maximum snout–vent length is 320 mm; the female maximum is 211.7 mm. Snout–vent length runs from the snout to the body opening and leaves out the tail, so it is not total length. These are compiled maxima, not typical sizes or biological growth limits. The sex-specific figures may come from different studies or localities and cannot by themselves measure population-wide size dimorphism.

## Four categories, not a full behaviour account {#s-a9d84bb9e6}
The profile classifies microhabitat as semi-aquatic, diet as carnivorous, active time as diurnal, and foraging mode as ACT. These labels describe broad recorded categories: they do not specify a particular habitat, prey, or daily routine. Habitat categories can overlap, and diets or activity may vary by place, season, and life stage.

## Reproductive numbers are summaries {#s-c3fe753de2}
The reproductive mode is listed as oviparous, or egg-laying. The consolidated mean is 5.59 offspring or eggs per litter or clutch; the lowest and highest reported litter or clutch means are 3 and 6, and the yearly summary is 1.5 clutches. The dataset may use midpoints of reported means, or extrema when means are missing, so these figures are not necessarily averages of observed clutches. A fractional value is a summary, not part of a single clutch; the endpoint fields do not guarantee the smallest or largest actual clutch.

## Evidence limits {#s-f4562f40c3}
This page paraphrases the same-name ReptTraits v1-2 entry and retains its 2023 taxonomy framework and trait sources; a matching name and family do not establish equivalence with later taxonomic concepts. One row per species compresses variation in sex, age, locality, and populations. A reported maximum is not an absolute limit, and reproductive summaries do not fix the count in every clutch. Most mass values are estimated from length equations and are not presented here as measured body mass; juvenile-length fields also use inconsistent headings and definitions, so they do not support a conclusion here. Conservation, venom, current distribution, and population trends are not updated. Missing fields remain gaps. Source labels preserve the dataset’s citation chain but do not mean that this project independently checked every original paper.
