## Name and source records {#s-dbfaf40399}
Vipera eriwanensis is an accepted species in pinned COL26.8. This page brings together a ReptTraits v1-2 trait record, using the workbook’s Reptile Database 2023 name framework, and a same-name entry in the 2015 life-history archive. These are separate records with different methods; the name match does not establish that their taxonomic concepts or measurements are interchangeable.

## One reported length measurement {#s-a23718440e}
ReptTraits reports a maximum total length (TL) of 510 mm. Total length includes the tail; snout–vent length (SVL) ends at the vent, so the two measures cannot be substituted for one another. The reported maximum summarizes material in the workbook. It is neither typical adult size nor a biological growth limit.

## Microhabitat in the workbook {#s-1bf44648a8}
The ReptTraits microhabitat field is coded “Terrestrial.” This is one categorical entry, not a full account of the places, seasons or conditions in which the species occurs. Its source label is Feldman (2015); the record does not provide further site-level detail here.

## Reproductive fields {#s-1ac9747dc0}
The workbook records the reproductive mode as viviparous and gives 10 in its combined offspring-per-litter or eggs-per-clutch field. The field’s definition covers both offspring and eggs across the dataset, so read the value with its source and event context; it does not mean every litter contains exactly ten young. The mode is attributed to Zimin et al. (2022); the count cites De Magalhaes and Costa (2009) and Feldman et al. (2015).

## Historical life-history entry {#s-d7819727f8}
The 2015 archive contains a Vipera eriwanensis row with three fields retained for readers here: adult body mass, litter or clutch size, and maximum longevity. It is a historical compilation linked by same name and family. That association supports presenting the archived summaries together, but does not establish equivalence with the pinned COL26.8 concept or remove inherited historical data.

## Adult body mass {#s-71bb1c3a88}
The archive reports adult body mass as 180.5 g. Its reference field derives this summary from Boback and Guyer (2003); it is not a range of individual masses or a measurement of every adult. The archive does not identify the sex and locality for each contributing value in this reader entry.

## Litter or clutch size {#s-bd47623807}
The archive’s combined litter-or-clutch field is 10. Its source record reports a median assembled from nine cited values, including IUCN (2011) and Street (1979). This historical summary should not be read as ten young in every litter or as a newly collected population estimate.

## Maximum longevity {#s-511d199883}
The archive lists maximum longevity as 19 years, using the largest record found in its source material and citing de Magalhaes and Costa (2009). This is a compiled maximum, not typical lifespan, mean wild lifespan, or a current survival record for the species.

## Following the source trail {#s-e5f2dd8fd1}
ReptTraits attaches citations by field: Feldman et al. (2016) for total length, Feldman (2015) for microhabitat, Zimin et al. (2022) for reproductive mode, and De Magalhaes and Costa (2009) plus Feldman et al. (2015) for the litter/clutch field. The 2015 archive preserves separate row locators and reference labels for each summary, including Boback and Guyer (2003), IUCN (2011), Street (1979), and de Magalhaes and Costa (2009). The archive’s source chain is not independent replication, and this page has not re-reviewed every underlying work.

## Evidence limits {#s-3aec8d6c58}
The ReptTraits row follows its 2023 name framework and compresses information to one row per species, leaving sex, age, locality and population variation unresolved. Its 510 mm value is a reported maximum, not an absolute limit. The reproductive count comes from a field that combines litter and clutch records, so its definition and citations matter. This page does not infer current range, venom, conservation status or population trends from the workbook; fields outside its cited scope remain unanswered.

The life-history values come from the August 2015 archive. Its methods report medians or the mean of two middle values, except that maximum longevity is the largest record found. The summaries are not measurements of one individual or current population surveys, and historical species or subspecies records may be shared or inherited. Matching name, family and a same-name sparse row do not prove concept equivalence. The underlying cited publications were not individually reviewed here. The archive also contains a gestation_d value of 730.48 days, but its Supplemental Table 9 scopes gestation to mammals and incubation to reptiles; the value and citation remain in the archive and are not interpreted here as a species-specific event.
