## What one leaf fragment preserves {#s-a6e3383b1f}
The Glösa assemblage in eastern Germany preserves Carboniferous moss-like remains, including a leaf whose cell pattern can be described even though the plant it came from cannot be reconstructed. That small sample offers a fossil starting point for the documented history of living mosses; it does not mark when mosses first evolved.

Late Visean specimen KS27–074, called Type I in the study, is a nearly complete leaf made of a single cell layer. A central band contains narrower cells, and small pores occur in the walls facing along the leaf surface. Those features support the study's description of moss remains. The material is fragmentary: it does not preserve complete plants or securely identify the fossils as any living moss group.

## A sampled fossil range, not an origin date {#s-467902ee59}
For mosses in the strict sense, this node's fossil range begins with the late Visean material from Glösa, rounded here to about 335 million years ago, and continues to living mosses. Older cryptospores are not counted as fossils of the moss crown group. The date is a rounded anchor for the sampled fossil occurrence; it is not an estimate of the group's evolutionary origin.
