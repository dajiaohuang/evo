## The name and this data account {#s-bab5caf86a}
Phoeniconaias minor is accepted in COL26.8 within Phoenicopteridae. AVONET v7 links the record to the HBW–BirdLife 2020 name and reports 5 measured individuals, 4 with complete morphological sets. Trait-level sample sizes can differ, and the historical link does not establish modern concept equivalence.

## Measured beak dimensions {#s-add6ecd484}
Mean beak length is 79.4 mm from the anterior nostril edge to the tip; width and depth at that edge average 27.5 mm and 35.2 mm. These are separate standardized dimensions, not field-identification thresholds.

## Wing, tarsus and mass {#s-3cbf5b551d}
Mean unflattened wing length is 318.4 mm, from carpal joint to longest primary; mean tarsus length is 190 mm. AVONET’s compiled mean mass is 1,500 g (source category Dunning). It is a separate species-average source, not a weight assigned to each morphology specimen.

## Habitat, feeding and lifestyle {#s-11d4f87c62}
AVONET labels the habitat Wetland, the trophic niche Herbivore aquatic and the lifestyle Terrestrial. These categories summarize the source record for comparison; they do not mean exclusive use of a wetland or one food type.

## Movement and geographical summary {#s-6aa3946e1a}
The movement field is Sedentary. Historical breeding and resident maps span latitude −28.89° to 24.74°. This is not a complete account of non-breeding movements or current occupancy; geography and body measurements come from separate compilation steps.

## The 2015 life-history entry and this name {#s-dea751efdf}
Seven focal fields are available from the 2015 same-name life-history entry. Its species-level rows support a historical association with this accepted name, but older taxonomic inheritance may remain in the archive.

## Different stages in the mass fields {#s-c801ba3420}
The life-history archive reports adult mass 1,760 g and birth or hatching mass 85.5 g. These describe different stages and come from a separate compilation than AVONET’s 1,500 g mass mean; they are not a longitudinal series or an individual mass range.

## Reproduction and developmental timing {#s-9b79d14d1d}
The other fields report female age at first conception of 1,090.4 days, one egg or offspring per clutch or litter, 28 days from laying to hatching, and age at flight capability of 72.75 days. The source combines egg and offspring counts in one field; fractional summary values do not describe a fractional event.

## A longest record differs from usual lifespan {#s-49ea3b45bd}
The archive’s maximum_longevity_y value is 50 years, the largest collected record under its method. It is not typical lifespan, a wild-lifespan average or a ceiling for every individual.

## How to trace these values to sources {#s-68cb336422}
Same-name rows carry labels including Dunning (1992), Szekely, Lislevand, and Figuerola (2007), Ricklefs (2010b), and Bennett (1986). Field-specific row locations and source explanations remain in the archive; a row count is not an animal count, and repeated citations are not independent replication.

The species-level foraging weight is 100% at the water surface or just below it. This is a relative feeding-position estimate, not a nest location, whole-day time budget or flight-height measurement.

## Evidence limits {#s-fa137b6a42}
AVONET v7 reports morphology means from five individuals and a separate 1,500 g mass compilation under the historical HBW–BirdLife name. The Herbivore aquatic, Wetland, Terrestrial and Sedentary labels are broad source categories; latitude endpoints describe historical breeding and resident maps, not current occupancy. A matching name does not prove equivalence to COL26.8.

The 2015 life-history archive reports a different adult mass (1,760 g), hatchling mass and other compiled fields; these sources should not be merged into a single measurement series. Historical inheritance may remain, while longevity is a maximum record. EltonTraits weights translate literature descriptions under older taxonomy. Current range, populations, conservation and individual variation need other evidence.
