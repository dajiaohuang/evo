## Leaves and form {#s-698186f6a3}
Brown's 1965 account describes a simple, stemless plant about 7 cm across, with numerous glaucous-green leaves. The leaves are oblong-lance-shaped, taper to a point, and measure 3–4 cm long and 10–15 mm wide near the base. Each ends in a simple, translucent bristle about 10 mm long. Younger leaves are more or less upright and inward-curving; older leaves spread more. The upper leaf face is somewhat convex above and becomes somewhat concave toward the base, while the back is convex. The keel is smooth, without hairs or teeth, and glaucous green. Both sides have many deep-green net-like lines from the base to mid-leaf; the translucent upper part has numerous green lines of varying length, with the middle line sometimes reaching the tip. The upper margins have irregular, spaced, slender translucent teeth 1.5–2.5 mm long. Occasionally, 1 or 2 isolated teeth occur on the keels of a few leaves.

These measurements and characters come from a regional species account published in 1965; its excerpt gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency.

## A comparison in the source {#s-1b4af8b5d6}
The same account distinguishes *H. decipiens* from *H. arachnoidea* by its shorter leaves, smooth keels, and slender, more widely spaced teeth along the margins.

This comparison is from a regional South African account published in 1965; it does not establish global representativeness or population frequencies.

## Habitat {#s-e078c64a3c}
A 2019 Eastern Cape flora lists local populations in Karroid shrubland or marginal thicket on skeletal soils. The account names the Lower Karoo, Albany Thicket, Rainshadow Valley Karoo, and Eastern Fynbos-Renosterveld.

This is a regional species account; it gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency and should not be taken as a full-range habitat survey.
