## Two EltonTraits field groups {#s-fc6d5aea45}
COL26.8 accepts Ardeola idae in Ardeidae. EltonTraits 1.0 links a historical record with the same name and family and supplies two ecological field groups: diet and foraging position. Its historical classification does not settle later splits or synonymies.

## Relative weights of food categories {#s-7c84398c25}
EltonTraits assigns relative food weights of 50% to reptiles and amphibians, 30% to fish and 20% to invertebrates. Code A indicates high compiler confidence in the source and diet information. The weights standardize verbal descriptions; they are not direct measurements of diet, and a zero category would not prove that a food is never eaten.

## Foraging strata differ from whole-day activity {#s-791451349e}
The species-level foraging entry assigns 100% of its weight to the water surface or just below it. This estimates relative use of feeding positions, not nest sites, complete habitat use or a whole-day time budget. It does not provide water depth, flight height or habitat area.

## Trace each field group to its source labels {#s-7d054bdd3d}
Both field groups carry the Ref_1 label in the EltonTraits archive. It links the food and foraging summaries to their recorded source trail; the label is not a separate observation, and the underlying work was not individually read for this page.

## Life-history evidence limits {#s-db42cecb77}
EltonTraits converts descriptions from earlier sources into broad food categories and relative weights; these are not direct measurements of intake, energy or observation time. Code A records the compiler’s high confidence in the source and diet information, not a direct dietary measurement. The historical name association does not establish equivalence with the current species concept. The record does not describe local or seasonal variation, present distribution, population trends or conservation status.

## A separate AVONET record {#s-558d444a2a}
AVONET v7 also contains a historical same-name, same-family record for Ardeola idae. Its one-to-one eBird and BirdTree crosswalks associate the lists; they do not settle species concepts after later splits or lumps.

## Wetland habitat and feeding categories {#s-4fc2d9d16f}
The AVONET habitat category is freshwater wetlands such as lakes, marshes or reedbeds; its trophic niche is aquatic animals. The primary-lifestyle category is terrestrial, with the source description placing much activity on the ground while walking, hopping or wading. AVONET defines a dominant feeding niche at a threshold of at least 60% of food resources. This threshold defines a category; it is not an independent diet measurement or evidence of exclusive feeding.

## Migration category {#s-28b93bd4e3}
The recorded migration category is sedentary. Regional seasonal movements are not listed separately, so this entry does not provide routes, schedules or distances for each population.

## Latitude in historical maps {#s-c92135e334}
Historical breeding and resident maps span latitude endpoints -25.09° to -9.34°. The source worksheet links these fields to BirdLife/HBW 2019.1 maps accessed on 27 March 2020. Endpoints summarize a mapped range; they are not a locality list or evidence of occupancy throughout it, and omit a complete account of non-breeding movements. They do not establish current distribution or conservation status.

## Biometric fields are inferred {#s-b43eb91996}
The AVONET record contains all four ecology categories: habitat, trophic niche, primary lifestyle and migration. It flags at least some biometric fields as inferred from relatives and mass as inferred from a related species. This account does not present those dimensions or mass as direct measurements. Missing fields do not show that the species lacks a trait.
