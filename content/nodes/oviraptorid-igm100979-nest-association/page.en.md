## What a dinosaur's pose above a nest can tell us {#s-c0a3bc4fef}
At Ukhaa Tolgod in Mongolia, the partial oviraptorid skeleton IGM 100/979 was preserved with its forelimbs spread over a nest of at least fifteen visible eggs and its hindlimbs folded. The arrangement and lack of evident post-mortem transport make brooding or nest attendance a strong interpretation of the association. The fossil preserves a pose and eggs, not the animal's actions, sex or habitual parenting; alternative explanations remain part of the evidence.
