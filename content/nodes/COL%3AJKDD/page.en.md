## ecology {#s-beced62b62}
The Sydney funnel-web occupies cool, humid retreats and silk-lines its burrow, extending irregular trip-lines outside the entrance. When prey such as beetles or cockroaches disturb a line, the spider rushes from the burrow. Mature males leave to search for females on warm-season nights, explaining why wandering males are occasionally encountered near urban habitat.

## distribution {#s-a956a8c811}
In the range updated by the Australian Museum using the 2025 study, this species occurs mainly from the Central Coast of New South Wales south to the Georges River and west to Baulkham Hills, with some records from the Blue Mountains and Wollongong. Its range overlaps that of the Southern Sydney funnel-web, A. montanus, so locality or general appearance alone cannot confirm an identification.
