## A narrowed name after removal of the Nanyo lineage {#s-7f7c1c1593}
The 2022 study used H. hirosei sensu stricto for samples remaining after recognition of Nanyo as H. oni, including Mt. Ishizuchi topotypes. Tsurugi and Central nuclear groups formed a clinal transition; the authors deferred whether they were separate species until further hybrid-zone analysis. The narrower name therefore is tied to this taxonomic treatment, not applied uncritically to every older hirosei record. The reported transition does not provide a quantified present-day migration rate or settle species limits independently.

## Previously published clutch samples from two mountain areas {#s-a10e34113f}
For comparison with H. oni, the paper cited mean clutch size 34.3 and range 25–59 from 81 observations on Mt. Ibuki, and mean 31.0 with range 15–52 from nine observations in the Tsurugi Mountains. These were attributed to 2004 and 2012 publications, not newly collected unified samples. Mountain, date, denominator and original uncertainty definitions remain distinct. The two summaries do not establish a species-wide fecundity constant, egg survival or a fixed number for every breeding female.

## Syntopic species and a requested conservation reassessment {#s-c7933c348e}
The discussion reported H. hirosei sensu stricto syntopic with H. kuishiensis, H. tsurugiensis and Onychodactylus kinneburi, proposing body-size differences as a possible explanation for coexistence. This interpretation does not demonstrate measured diet partitioning or exclude other mechanisms. After separating H. oni, the authors requested immediate reassessment of hirosei conservation status; the Near Threatened category they cited was from 2014, while local Red Data Book statements used the broader name. Neither is silently converted into a verified current national status for the narrowed concept.
