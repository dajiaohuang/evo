## Repeat annotations are computational observations {#s-3c91d947e0}
The authors built repeat libraries for each species with RepeatModeler2, classified unknown elements with RFSB, added vertebrate repeats and then ran RepeatMasker. The paper calls its hits insertions, but a hit alone does not show that an element is complete, expressed or mobile. A superfamily label may combine distinct families. Counts, total occupied length and share of the genome measure different things, and overlapping categories in the classification cannot be added as independent fractions. The study did not make a new repeat annotation or test repeat expression in Atelopus laetissimus.

## Across-species correlations do not prove this species mechanism {#s-c0f2fdc449}
The study compared 61 assemblies with models adjusted for phylogenetic relationships, using an earlier pruned tree, contig-N50 covariates and multiple-comparison correction. In that sample, sequences associated with recently active Ty3/DIRS1 elements correlate with genome size. “Recent activity” is inferred from sequence divergence; it is not direct observation of current transposition in this species. Length and open-reading-frame similarity filters also do not show that proteins are expressed. The proposed developmental constraints are comparative hypotheses, not evidence that transposable elements cause changes in this species' larval duration or clutch size.
