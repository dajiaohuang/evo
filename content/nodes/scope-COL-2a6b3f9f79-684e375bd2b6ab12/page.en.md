## Three slides open a window into an early plant {#s-a36bcab80c}
Horneophyton lignieri is known from Rhynie Chert material in Scotland. The study checked 24 museum sections and chose three historical thin sections for confocal imaging. Its reconstruction shows a rhizoid-bearing rhizome and branching aerial axes, but this small selected sample does not establish the plant's full size range or ecological breadth.

## Cell walls are observed; their role is inferred {#s-8c4ac0aa4b}
The imaged conducting cells have papillate, labyrinth-like wall ingrowths. The study compares them with transfer cells, which move materials between tissues, and discusses a transport role. It did not identify distinct xylem and phloem in these sections, and a resemblance does not by itself prove the cells' exact job. The plant is associated with Rhynie sinter surfaces; the sample does not measure carbon uptake or a population-wide habitat preference. The 411.5 ± 1.3 Ma zircon age constrains the deposit, not each fossil slide directly.
