## *Strychnos amazonica* {#s-7230aea9fc}
## A tendril-bearing stem and a thick fruit wall

The Brazilian Flora 2020 field records a solitary tendril (a slender climbing organ) and no stem spines. The leaf stalk is given as about 8 mm in a slash-separated source value; leaves have three veins, with no beard of hairs where the basal veins meet on the underside. Flower clusters arise in the leaf axils and are thyrses, branching clusters with a corymb-like outline. The corolla is tubular or salver-shaped, with a tube longer than its lobes. The ovary may be hairless or hairy at its tip, and the style is hairy.

Fruits are about 2 cm across, with a subwoody pericarp 1–2 mm thick; the fruit stalk is about 4 mm across. Seeds are usually 3–5, with a crust-like coat. The entry preserves these source-recorded measurements and alternatives; it does not describe a population survey.
