## Aloe ferox: a tall, single-stemmed aloe {#s-e10b156db9}
A South African regional account describes a succulent perennial tree with a single upright stem, usually 2–3 m tall but reaching 5 m, densely covered by persistent dry leaves. The dull-green leaves may be tinged red, lack spots and reach 1 m long. They are lanceolate to sword-shaped; their surfaces are smooth or bear scattered irregular spines, and their margins carry stout, triangular teeth from reddish to brownish red. Dense, branched, cylindrical racemes are about 1 m long. Flowers are orange to scarlet, occasionally yellow or white, about 33 mm long, with stamens and stigma projecting well beyond the flower.

The comparative account refers readers elsewhere for differences from *A. pluridens* and *A. africana*. It describes *A. ferox* leaves as more spreading than those of *A. marlothii*. Its erect, symmetrical racemes with red to orange flowers, rarely white, contrast with the usually near-horizontal, one-sided racemes of orange to yellow flowers in *A. marlothii*. The bracts of *A. ferox* are about twice as long. The account also says adult *A. ferox* plants are relatively light and can be carried by one strong person, whereas *A. marlothii* plants of the same size are much heavier and require several strong people to lift.

A further account describes *A. ferox* as most closely allied to *A. candelabrum*, called a Natal species, and says the two look similar at a distance. It distinguishes *A. candelabrum* by its longer, more deeply channelled recurved leaves and by a terminal raceme that rises above the others. The inner tepal tips are brown to dark brown in *A. ferox* and described as always white in *A. candelabrum*.
