## Stem surface {#s-ad1abd3b03}
The Brazilian Flora 2020 record describes a strigose stem surface, with stiff hairs lying close to the surface and pointing forward. Simple hairs are present. Unbranched bristles are thread-like or flattened from front to back; branched and glandular hairs are absent. Prickles are listed as absent or as spreading outward and scattered, with no specimen-level assignment; short lateral shoots (brachyblasts) are absent.

## Leaves and inflorescences {#s-5d62ba1d65}
Stipules, small structures at leaf bases, are persistent. The record marks extrafloral nectaries (nectar-secreting glands outside flowers) and projections between pairs of pinnae (the leaf’s primary side divisions) as absent. The inflorescence, or flower cluster, is globose; floral bracts (small leaf-like structures) are shorter than the flowers before they open (anthesis).

## Flowers {#s-1da71dbf00}
The perianth (calyx and corolla together) is recorded as having four parts. The calyx, the outer sepal whorl, is paleaceous, or thin and papery; its lobes are pappus-like, resembling a tuft of fine bristles or scales. Corolla (petal-whorl) lobes have one to three veins and are pubescent, covered with short soft hairs. The androecium (the stamens collectively) is isostemonous, with stamens equal in number to the petals; the filaments are pink.
