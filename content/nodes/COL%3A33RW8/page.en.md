## What is recorded about this root parasite? {#s-86b26eef47}
The 2012 regional account describes a dioecious root parasite—male and female flowers occur on separate plants—up to 5 cm tall, occasionally 10 cm. Its leaves are scale-like and obovate. Flowers grow in sessile clusters and range from orange to red or dark purplish; they have five or six tepals, from hairless to shaggy.

## Where does it grow and what does it parasitize? {#s-ce17f097e0}
The same regional account places it on sandy or stony flats and slopes, where it is parasitic mostly on shrubby members of the Asteraceae family. The excerpt gives no population frequency or dated sampling frame.
