## Sample and historical name {#s-01a59e809e}
COL26.8 accepts Zosterops olivaceus in Zosteropidae. AVONET v7 retains its HBW–BirdLife 2020 name and records 4 measured individuals, all with a complete morphological set. The averages below describe this sample.

## Beak dimensions {#s-d80ad5013e}
Mean beak length from the front edge of the nostril to the tip is 7.8 mm; width and depth at that edge average 2.5 mm and 2.6 mm. These are distinct measurements at standardized landmarks, not identification thresholds.

## Wing, tarsus and mass {#s-98ea9abff3}
Unflattened wing length from the carpal joint to the longest primary averages 57.2 mm; tarsus length averages 18 mm. The compiled mean mass is 9.13 g, classed as Dunning. It comes from a separate species-average source, not from weighing each bird in the morphological sample.

## Habitat and feeding categories {#s-5d31e22bc6}
AVONET classifies habitat as Forest, trophic niche as Nectarivore and primary lifestyle as Insessorial, meaning chiefly perching. These broad labels are useful for comparisons; they do not mean the bird uses only forest or feeds only on nectar.

## Migration and historical map {#s-6f8d62f0ea}
The migration category is Sedentary. Historical breeding and resident maps span latitude endpoints −21.37° to −20.86°. This map summary does not describe all non-breeding movements or establish current occupancy. Geography and morphology were compiled separately.

## Two historical feeding records {#s-a3d0791e8a}
EltonTraits 1.0 also links a same-name, same-family historical entry for Zosterops olivaceus, with diet and foraging-position groups. The source follows an earlier bird classification. Its link to the COL26.8 name helps locate the entry but does not settle later splits or synonymies.

## Relative weights of food categories {#s-b60817bdeb}
The diet record assigns 50% relative weight to nectar, pollen and plant exudates, 30% to invertebrates, and 20% to fruit. Code A records high compiler confidence in the source and diet coding; these values are still standardized estimates from verbal descriptions, not a direct measure of what the bird ate. A zero category would not prove that the food is never eaten.

## Feeding positions below the canopy {#s-f4434f60c4}
The species-level foraging entry assigns 50% to understory, edges or shrubs below 2 m and 50% to trees or tall bushes from 2 m upward but below the canopy. These weights estimate relative use of feeding positions; they are not nest sites, a complete habitat description or a whole-day time budget. The categories are not elevation bands and cannot establish flight height or habitat area.

## Scope of the historical summaries {#s-1d75cf6a92}
The AVONET measurements are averages for the recorded sample, not diagnoses or individual-variation ranges; mass has a separate source. Habitat, diet and migration are broad labels, and the map covers historical breeding and resident ranges. EltonTraits standardizes literature descriptions into relative weights, not direct measures of intake, energy or observation time. Historical name links associate the entries with COL26.8 but do not prove equivalent species concepts or establish current distribution, population or conservation status.
