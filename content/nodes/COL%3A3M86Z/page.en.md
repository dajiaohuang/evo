## Which traits distinguish *Hollandichthys taramandahy*? {#s-b6bc0d21ef}
The taxonomic treatment distinguishes *H. taramandahy* from *H. multifasciatus* by a small black spot at the base of the middle caudal-fin rays, no distinctive marks on the adipose fin, and no shoulder-area (humeral) spot in specimens over 60 mm standard length (SL). Standard length runs from the snout to the base of the tail. In the comparison sample, dorsal and ventral procurrent rays—supporting rays along the tail-fin base—numbered 8–11 (mode 10; n=20) and 7–8 (mode 8; n=20) in *H. taramandahy*, versus 10–15 (mode 12; n=72) and 9–12 (mode 10; n=72) in *H. multifasciatus*. The description also reports backward-pointing bony hooks on the anal and pelvic fins of sexually mature males. These characters and counts apply to the treatment's examined specimens and stated comparisons.

## What do the collection notes show? {#s-b632f64aed}
The treatment reports fish from lateral river puddles and very small tributaries with shallow, still black water, muddy and leaf-covered bottoms, and dense vegetation along the banks. Larger specimens (70–80 mm SL) were found alone; smaller ones (30–40 mm SL) were collected in groups of three to six. Stomach contents from three specimens in lot MCP 24621 included spiders, ants, beetles and insect parts. These are observations from the treatment's collections and stomach samples, not a species-wide habitat or diet rule.
