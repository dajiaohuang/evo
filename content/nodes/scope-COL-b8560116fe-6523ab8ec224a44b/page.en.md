## Two fossils record different shell mosaics {#s-f765c0d0e2}
Three Odontochelys specimens from southwestern China preserve a developed plastron, expanded ribs and neural plates, but no complete carapace. Pappochelys from Vellberg, Germany, preserves broadened ribs and paired gastralia. These named stem-turtle samples inform comparisons of shell evolution; they do not form a demonstrated ancestor sequence or date the origin of crown turtles.

## What the two specimens contribute {#s-793448d9e5}
Odontochelys and Pappochelys preserve different combinations of ribs and shell bones. Researchers compare those named fossils to test how the turtle shell assembled. Their positions in a comparison do not show that one was the direct ancestor of the other, and neither fossil sets the date when crown turtles first appeared.

## Depositional settings of the named fossils {#s-33fa10e32e}
Li et al. interpret the marine deposits that yielded Odontochelys as a marginal sea or river-delta setting. This describes that fossil's depositional context, not turtle habitat as a whole.

## What the shell comparison can show {#s-4d48323689}
Li et al. describe three Odontochelys specimens from southwestern China; Schoch and Sues describe Pappochelys from Vellberg. The comparison informs shell-evolution hypotheses, while each specimen and the published analyses retain their own limits.
