## What the material shows {#s-739c8f7f78}
About 280 teeth, jaws and ankle bones from the early Eocene revise the older picture of “Miacis” latouri based on only two teeth.

## Feeding {#s-308b3a2eee}
Dentition supports an inference of small-prey or insect eating; stomach contents are not preserved.

## Setting {#s-34b7e5bbac}
A warm woodland is inferred from the locality context, not directly recorded as the animal’s preferred habitat.

## Movement {#s-5f6308cc5b}
Tarsal anatomy suggests climbing ability, but movement was not observed.

## Size {#s-1f227ab943}
The sample documents a small carnivoraform; it does not provide a precise body-mass estimate.

## Ecological role {#s-f941d0f6fd}
A small, climbing-capable faunivore is an anatomical inference, not a directly observed ecological role.

## Preserved features {#s-92416cc246}
The expanded sample supports a reconstructed permanent dentition.

## Preserved features {#s-5c9381fd09}
Ankle bones include astragali and a calcaneum.

## Preserved features {#s-0a2ffa261a}
A sampled character matrix places it among basal carnivoraforms.

## How to read the evidence {#s-943831a06b}
The fossils support dental, ankle and matrix observations. They do not demonstrate membership in crown Carnivora, a direct ancestor, or the global first carnivoramorphan.
