## The holotype: skull and jaw {#s-e4a002629e}
The 2003 naming paper designates SMNK PAL 3828 as the holotype, held at the Staatliches Museum für Naturkunde Karlsruhe in Germany. It is a nearly complete skull and lower jaw on a limestone slab; many bones remain articulated and teeth sit in their sockets. This specimen does not include an associated body skeleton with complete proportions. The fossil and the database occurrence below are different units, not population counts.

## A damaged crest with an incomplete outline {#s-09b44db9b8}
The skull is laterally compressed and fractured, and the hyoid bones are rotated to the right. The base of a crest behind the skull is preserved, but the distal part was cut away at the stone yard before museum preparation. The surviving base shows that a crest was present, but not its full outline or size. It does not preserve the crest's complete length, area, soft-tissue edge, or aerodynamic function.

## Provenance and geological context {#s-25a3d55af9}
The original description says the fossil came through a commercial source, with quarries near Nova Olinda in Ceará, Brazil, reported as its origin. The authors use the limestone and a small Dastilbe fish on the slab to support assignment to the Nova Olinda Member of the Crato Formation and retain an Aptian age interpretation. The site was not documented by the authors through excavation layer by layer. The database collection name and age bracket do not supply exact coordinates or an individual fossil's age.

## Skull and teeth {#s-e5e789a1c5}
The description notes a crest behind the skull that points backward and is flattened from side to side. The upper surface of the snout is rounded and lacks a crest above the snout; larger, curved teeth occur toward the front of the jaws and smaller ones farther back. That dentition makes resemblance to Pteranodon only a partial shape comparison, not an identification as a toothless pterosaur. The authors also compare a projection of the lacrimal bone into the eye socket and other skull openings. These features alone do not establish sex, diet, flight mode, or a complete family tree.

## A leaf preserved between the jaws {#s-5f3b4bb4cd}
A long, tapering leaf with coarse parallel fibers lies between the two lower-jaw branches. Its lower end is damaged and frayed. The original authors compare it with leaves of living Cordyline, Yucca, and Agave, while noting that the fossil form had not been formally described. Similarity is not a botanical identification and does not show that Ludodactylus routinely ate leaves or reveal the full ancient plant community.

## A debated explanation for the leaf {#s-8e8081a30b}
The authors suggest the leaf may have been mistaken for prey and lodged in soft tissue or a possible throat pouch; they interpret the fraying as damage from attempts to remove it. They compare this idea with debris trapped by pelicans and discuss possible consequences such as being unable to feed, weakness, or infection. The leaf's position and damage are observations, but ingestion, a pouch, starvation, and sepsis are not directly preserved. The proposed sequence is not a confirmed cause of death.

## What the hyoid bones suggest {#s-a53091183f}
A 2018 study by Li, Zhou, and Clarke includes Ludodactylus among pterosaurs with elongated ceratobranchial bones. Its supplementary table S1 refers to the same holotype, SMNK PAL 3828, and the 2003 description by Frey and colleagues. Comparisons of bones, muscles, and function in living animals inform hypotheses about tongue movement. CT images of living birds and crocodilians are not preserved muscles in this fossil. The hyoids constrain interpretation but do not reveal full tongue length, movement range, swallowing behavior, or specific prey.

## PBDB name and record count {#s-0206d0d051}
In the fixed PBDB pterosaur roster, Ludodactylus sibbicki is accepted under the parent name Ludodactylus. This guide includes one eligible species-level occurrence record, one collection, and one reference identifier. Those are database record counts, not counts of specimens or animals.

The selected collection is Nova Olinda quarries (Ceara), region code BR, in the Crato Formation. Its database age bracket is 119.57–113.2 Ma, with the early interval labelled Late Aptian. ## Limits of this record card {#s-d0fc760151}
The skull and jaw are nearly complete, but the rest of the body is not associated and the crest tip is missing. The plant identity and the proposed throat-pouch, ingestion, and death scenario remain interpretations; hyoid anatomy does not preserve a complete tongue or behavior. The reported commercial provenance and one database collection do not establish precise find coordinates or a full species range.
