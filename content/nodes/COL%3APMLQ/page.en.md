## Ecological evidence {#s-cf529feb99}
A study used playback surveys in 45 Atlantic Forest remnants around Alfenas, Minas Gerais, Brazil. From July 2010 to January 2013, black-fronted titi monkeys were recorded in 28 fragments. The researchers compared patch area, functional isolation, visibility between remnants and surrounding land-use composition; patch area was the leading explanation for local occurrence records. They tested candidate models again in 20 fragments near Poço Fundo, but the abstract and Figure 6 caption disagree about the secondary variable in the named validated model for this species. This guide leaves that discrepancy unresolved. The results describe local occurrence in two neighboring landscapes, not abundance or habitat preference across the full range.
