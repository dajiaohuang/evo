## Eastern Arussi Plateau records and unconfirmed extension {#s-cc09625e6c}
The revision reported eastern Arussi Plateau localities in Oromia, Ethiopia, at 2380–2850 m. It suggested possible occurrence farther toward Barbari because no physical barrier was apparent. That inferred extension is kept distinct from examined locality records and does not establish continuous occupancy or a physiological elevation range.

## Night calling from grassy roadside puddles {#s-0de7be8bf2}
Males were found calling at night, often in large observed numbers, from shallow roadside puddles usually overgrown with grass. The qualitative encounter count is not a standardized abundance estimate; the calling observation does not establish egg-deposition sites or the full annual breeding calendar.

## A geographically sampled mitochondrial and nuclear dataset {#s-a13085903c}
The 2023 Ethiopian Highlands study lists 9 mitochondrial samples and 3 nuclear-SNP samples for Ptychadena nana in Table 1, with mitochondrial nucleotide diversity π=0.16%. Samples across the twelve-species study were collected in 2011–2019; these are analysed specimens, not a population census. All assembled mitochondrial genomes had the usual two rRNAs, thirteen protein-coding genes and twenty-two tRNAs, but no species had a completely resolved control region because repeats could not be confidently assembled from short reads. The phylogenetic alignment used selected genes (13,937 bp across 303 samples), rather than an alignment of complete control regions.

## A study-defined highland clade and regional distribution {#s-8e0b404319}
The paper places Ptychadena nana in its P. erlangeri clade and reports it from the eastern side of the Great Rift Valley; the discussion identifies the Ahmar mountain slopes north of the Shebelle River valley. These distributions combine the authors' geographic sampling and earlier range work; they are not independently updated complete range polygons. The twelve-species highland clade is a subset of Ptychadena. Its mitochondrial-clock analysis estimated a common ancestor around 11.7 million years ago, with a reported interval of 8.827–15.494 million years. The east-of-rift ancestral reconstruction had a model probability of 69%; neither that estimate nor inferred crossings is a directly observed migration event or a nuclear-genome divergence date for this species.
