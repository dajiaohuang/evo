## Aloe dabenorisana: hanging rosettes and cliff habitat {#s-c4a3a8a967}
A South African regional account describes pendent plants, usually in clumps up to 800 mm across. Young leaves are arranged in four or five ranks and later form rosettes up to about 450 × 450 mm. The strongly recurved leaves are narrowly lanceolate with tapering tips, 200–400 × 40–55 mm, slightly channelled, green to reddish and striate when green. The dense raceme is usually capitate to conical, 250–300 mm long and branched two to four times. Its peduncle bends backward and bears a few sterile bracts; the one-veined bracts are 6–10 × 2–3 mm. Flowers are red to yellow, 23–25 mm long, with all segments free and pedicels 20–30 mm long. Anthers and style are not or only slightly exserted. Fruit was not seen. Flowering is reported sporadically through the year, mainly from August to November.

These characters, measurements and flowering months come from a regional South African flora account cited to a 2000 publication. The excerpt gives no dated sampling frame or population frequency and does not establish variation across the full range. It does not independently classify the material as native, naturalised, cultivated or domesticated.

The habitat account places the plants on nearly inaccessible cliffs on the highest Orange River hills, in arid Namaqualand Broken Veld. It says rain is similarly unlikely in every season and suggests that mist precipitation may be important to the species’ water relations.
