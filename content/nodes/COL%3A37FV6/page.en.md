## Mantle and statolith measurements in hatchlings {#s-ef0e02c173}
At Woods Hole in June–August 2011, eggs laid in laboratory capsules by adults trawled from Vineyard Sound were reared in seawater at about 20 °C under control and elevated CO₂ conditions. The target gas partial pressures were 390 and 2,200 μatm; measured gas concentrations averaged 394 ± 6 and 2,267 ± 10 ppm, and calculated seawater CO₂ partial pressures averaged 626 μatm for controls and 2,440 μatm for the treatment. Across two trials, hatchlings (paralarvae) in the elevated-CO₂ treatment had slightly shorter mantle lengths (a body-size measurement) than controls: 1.78 ± 0.01 mm versus 1.81 ± 0.01 mm. The pooled difference was statistically significant but small. The study also found about 25% lower surface area, greater irregularity and more pores in the treatment animals' statoliths, the structures measured in this comparison. These are early-stage laboratory measurements, not demonstrated effects on adult form or field performance; later development and recovery were not followed.

## Hatching under elevated CO₂ {#s-0b3740ab43}
In both replicated laboratory trials, elevated CO₂ delayed hatching of Doryteuthis pealeii. In trial 1, the time until 90% of eggs had hatched was about 24 hours later than in controls; in trial 2, the delay was shorter than 24 hours. The eggs came from laboratory-laid capsules of trawled Vineyard Sound adults, and the newly hatched paralarvae were not reared beyond the yolk-sac stage. This is a result about hatching time under controlled conditions, not a demonstrated change in natural recruitment; later survival and growth were not measured.
