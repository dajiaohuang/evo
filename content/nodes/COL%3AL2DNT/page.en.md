## Stem surface {#s-e1ea6f9b44}
One Brazilian Flora 2020 morphology field lists several alternatives without tying them to individual observations. It describes the stem as glabrous, hirsute, or strigose (hairless, covered with coarse hairs, or bearing stiff hairs that lie close to the surface); hairs may be spreading, curved, or directed forward while appressed. Simple hairs are listed as present. Unbranched bristles are absent or thread-like; branched and glandular hairs are absent; prickles are absent or sparse and backward-pointing; short lateral shoots (brachyblasts) are absent. The field does not show whether these alternatives reflect biological variation or combined reports.

## Leaves and inflorescences {#s-6d52dbc833}
Stipules, small structures at leaf bases, are persistent, and extrafloral nectaries (nectar-secreting glands outside flowers) are absent in the record. Projections between pairs of pinnae (the leaf’s primary side divisions) may be absent or present. The inflorescence, or flower cluster, is described as globose or ellipsoid; floral bracts (small leaf-like structures) may be smaller or larger than the flowers before they open (anthesis). The field does not assign these alternatives to individual specimens or conditions.

## Flowers {#s-db8f88ae07}
The perianth (calyx and corolla together) is recorded as having four parts. The calyx, the outer sepal whorl, is membranous; its lobes are listed as absent or triangular. Corolla (petal-whorl) lobes have one to three veins and are recorded as hairless or hairy. The androecium (the stamens collectively) is isostemonous, with stamens equal in number to the petals; the filaments are pink. These alternatives are not assigned to separate specimens in the source field.
