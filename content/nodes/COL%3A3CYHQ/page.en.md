## Name and placement in COL26.8 {#s-25a4844fc7}
COL26.8 lists Eupsophus migueli Formas, 1978 as an accepted species. In the release dated 20 August 2026, its recorded path runs through Anura (order), Alsodidae (family) and Eupsophus (genus). This placement belongs to that checklist; the same name in another source needs its own identity evidence.

## What the records on this page cover {#s-31ed65d4eb}
This page brings together a conservation assessment and a literature review of nematode hosts. Both reports use the name Eupsophus migueli, but each has its own scope: the assessment scores selected Chilean species, while the review compiles parasite records from earlier studies. A name match helps locate a record; it does not make the two sources one study or establish the species' complete biology.

## A 2024 conservation ranking {#s-1384bcaa1d}
Table 4 of a 2024 Chilean assessment gives Eupsophus migueli a conservation-priority index of 2.25 and a medium category. The study assessed 58 selected species using literature, citizen-science records and government plans; it did not newly count every population. Its index combines scores for distribution, threat, population trend, taxonomic uniqueness and body size with a protected-area term. The result is a management ranking from that model, not an extinction probability, a current IUCN assessment or a direct population count.

## What the assessment's codes mean {#s-a06671cf2c}
Using the definitions in Table 2, Table 3 assigns Eupsophus migueli population-trend code 4 (recent decline) and land-protection code 1 (the whole population outside protected areas). These are the authors' category assignments, not new census counts or measurements of how many animals are protected. Threat code 3 is a grouped category and does not identify a specific official status on its own. The area-of-occupancy (AOO) and extent-of-occurrence (EOO) scores place the species against area criteria; they are not independently reconstructed maps. A protected-area score also does not show whether protection is effective.

## A published nematode host record {#s-35ddeab2f3}
A 2025 review's Molineidae checklist links the written host name Eupsophus migueli with the nematode Oswaldocruzia neghmei. This is an association compiled from earlier studies, not a new examination of frogs or evidence that every individual is infected. The host appears in additional-host entries in the parasite accounts. The checklist does not give the number of frogs examined, prevalence (the share infected), parasite numbers per host, disease effects or deaths; the cited host records are not reidentified by the review as part of a new sample.

## Where the host entry was reported {#s-27a35c4f1b}
For the host entry associated with Oswaldocruzia neghmei, the review reports Mehuín [MEH; Chile (CHL)]. The codes are retained as printed because the inspected methods do not define them. This is a host-specific locality, not the parasite's full distribution. The historical record does not establish where the frog lives today, its complete range, whether the record is native or introduced, or that the parasite is absent elsewhere. ## Scope of the literature search {#s-e43f7e1c56}
The 2025 review searched BioOne, ISI, Jstor, PubMed, Scielo, Scopus, Web of Science and monographs for regional Molineidae literature dated 1819–2025. It excluded incomplete records, unpublished theses or meeting data, and parasite records not identified to species; it also sought species-level molecular records in the literature and GenBank. The inspected methods do not give search strings or database-by-database search dates, so an omitted record is not a reproducible finding of absence. The review organizes Caribbean, Panamanian and Neotropical material into its stated regions. Its named host entries are the evidence summarized here, not a newly assembled global parasite inventory.
