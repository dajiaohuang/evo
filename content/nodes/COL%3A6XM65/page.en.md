## A local cloud-forest occurrence and its elevation {#s-cbe554fe32}
The 2021 Santa Cruz Tepetotutla checklist reports Sarcohyla celata at 2210 m above sea level during its 2018–2019 survey. Table 1 compares this with an earlier reported elevation of 2559–2890 (Caviedes-Solis pers. com.; Duellman 2001) and lists photographic catalogue number IBH-RF 622. This is a reported local occurrence, not a surveyed global range, independent verification of the archived photograph, or evidence of occupancy at every intermediate elevation. ## Interpret the record within the survey design {#s-3ba858973b}
Four observers surveyed pristine and managed cloud-forest sites on eleven five-day trips in 2018–2019, in wet and dry seasons. Three daily windows were 09:00–13:00, 15:00–19:00 and 21:00–01:00; the paper reports 2,640 effective field hours for the whole herpetofaunal inventory. Animals were photographed and released, and GPS locations had a reported 5 m error range. The checklist's Sarcohyla celata row establishes presence in that sampling context. Total effort and the nineteen amphibian species are not species-specific detection probabilities, population estimates or trends. The accumulation curve had not reached an asymptote, so a missing record cannot establish local absence.

## Dated conservation labels in the checklist {#s-ceae515fc8}
Table 1 prints IUCN category NT, EVS entry H (14), Mexican NOM-059 entry Not included and endemism label Oaxaca SMO for Sarcohyla celata. These are the source's historical labels, not a newly checked current assessment or legal classification. The IUCN and Mexican columns use different frameworks; a “Not included” entry is not evidence of low risk or unrestricted activity. EVS is retained as the printed index entry rather than a measured extinction probability. The study argues that community-conserved forest supports threatened species, but its presence inventory does not isolate the causal effect of management or demonstrate a population increase.
