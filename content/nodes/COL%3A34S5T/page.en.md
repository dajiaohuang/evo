## A neotype anchors a narrower use of leucophyllatus {#s-135287e6e9}
The revision could not examine the lost original holotype. It associated leucophyllatus with its clade B using the historical leaf-shaped sacral mark and the original Suriname locality, and designated a male neotype from Sinnamary, French Guiana. This restricted concept differed from earlier accounts that used an hourglass mark and combined material from several other Amazonian clades. Those historical leucophyllatus labels are therefore not automatically records of leucophyllatus sensu stricto. The revision’s name assignment is recorded here, while equivalence of every older specimen or dataset concept still requires specimen-level reconciliation.

## An elongated leaf-shaped sacral mark in the diagnosis {#s-6d4f283ac5}
The sampled males measured 26.8–32.3 mm snout–vent length, mean 29.7 mm, with twelve males; two females measured 36–39.8 mm, mean 37.9 mm. The diagnostic combination includes an axillary membrane extending halfway toward the elbow, basal finger webbing, toe webbing, a single palmar tubercle and moderate pectoral patches. White or live bright-yellow dorsolateral bands extend toward the snout, and an elliptical, elongated leaf-shaped mark lies over the sacrum. Some individuals show a reticulated pattern. The small female sample and variable patterns prevent treating the ranges or each mark as absolute species limits.

## Photographs distinguish live colours from preserved marks {#s-4b2cb215ce}
Live colour descriptions were derived from digital photographs. Pale areas could be white or bright yellow against brown to dark-brown dorsal surfaces. The account described limb undersides, thigh surfaces and webbing as pink at night and red by day; the vocal sac was yellow, belly creamy white and iris dull to coppery bronze. These are source observations, not a controlled test of daily physiological colour change or camouflage. The diagnosis otherwise treats colours as preserved unless explicitly stated, so preserved white marks should not be assumed to remain white in life.

## Advertisement and aggressive calls are separate accounts {#s-6f767dcb65}
The advertisement account describes a Type I trill, mean note duration 0.10 s with ten to fourteen pulses, followed by one or two shorter Type II notes. The whole advertisement call averaged 0.17 s and dominant frequency about 2,749 Hz. Calls classified as aggressive contained three to five Type II notes and averaged 0.25 s, with dominant frequency about 2,616 Hz. Note and whole-call durations are different units. The methods include newly recorded and reanalysed historical material and, where available, individual averages from several calls. A reported advertisement-duration standard deviation differs between the two paragraphs (0.02 versus 0.002 s), so it is not silently harmonized. These summaries do not establish invariant functions or a universal recognition threshold.

## Ponds, coastal swamps and forest-savanna edges {#s-3600e6e639}
Under the revision’s restricted concept, records came from northern Brazilian Amazonia, Suriname, Guyana and French Guiana, with known locality elevations from sea level to 400 m. Specimens occurred at permanent or semipermanent ponds along roads or in pristine forest, large coastal swamps and forest-savanna edges. Perches ranged from a few centimetres above water to several metres. These are locality and perch observations rather than measured breeding substrates, habitat preferences or density. Roadside records do not prove immunity to disturbance, and earlier broad leucophyllatus distributions cannot be added without reconciling identity.
