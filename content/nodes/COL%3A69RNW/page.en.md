## Linking Major C to the name retains an inferred locality {#s-9952972051}
The 2014 revision proposes Major C as major in the sense of Groves (2000), using cytb sequences from several museum specimens as group material. The Paris neotype lacks a definite original locality. Historical routes to Madagascar lead the authors to suggest the vicinity of Toamasina or the area between it and Antananarivo; this is not a recorded collecting coordinate or newly confirmed type locality.

## The major group’s mask, ear and cranial combination {#s-70199f6c9d}
The major-group account describes rounder eye rings and weaker mask connections than in the crossleyi group, sparsely haired ears and a less sharply bounded transition from reddish-grey upperparts to pale underparts, with a short, high, straight facial skeleton and narrow interorbital space. A group combination does not make one colour diagnostic for every animal. The authors also discuss ravus as either a distinct lineage or an unusual morph; this page does not adjudicate that historical dispute.

## Read the revision’s range separately from the Mandena study {#s-2b01bff6a9}
The 2014 Major C account describes a narrow east-coast range from Masoala to the Mahanoro River. Lahann’s Mandena study lies farther south and retains its own historical name and local ecological scope. A name match does not equate the two major concepts, reidentify the Mandena animals, or establish continuous Major C range to that site.

## Research observations {#s-642b5baa95}
At a 25-ha lowland forest site in Mandena, southeastern Madagascar, Lahann used capture, radio-tracking, and focal observations during rainy seasons in 2002 and 2003–2004. The study identified two presumed family groups; body mass and tail circumference increased from November to February, consistent with pre-hibernation fattening. The author also noted that the local timing and duration of hibernation remained unresolved.
