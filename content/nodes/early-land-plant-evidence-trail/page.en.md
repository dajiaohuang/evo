## How the early land-plant record takes shape {#s-834e8dabd7}
Evidence runs from a modelled crown divergence through dispersed spores, plant fragments and anatomically preserved Devonian fossils. These records answer different questions and do not fix one origin date.

## A modelled crown interval {#s-6bd3d0c761}
A fossil-calibrated relaxed molecular clock places the crown group of land plants (Embryophyta) in a broad middle Cambrian–Early Ordovician interval. This is a model result, not a fossil occurrence from that interval.

## Dispersed spores on emerged land {#s-de77529366}
An Argentine Dapingian assemblage contains five cryptospore genera—dispersed spores rather than whole plants. The propagules do not reveal a complete parent plant or establish crown-bryophyte identity.

## Spores linked to plant fragments {#s-111f5e78d3}
Late Ordovician fragments from Oman preserve dense spore masses and probable tissue around a sporangium, the spore-producing case. This strengthens the land-plant interpretation, while affinity to the liverwort crown group remains tentative.

## A three-axis rooting system {#s-7f5c7c5463}
Three-dimensional reconstruction shows Asteroxylon with leafy shoots, axes bearing roots and separate rooting axes. This is an extinct developmental arrangement.

## A liverwort body-fossil record {#s-cb02f22ea8}
Givetian Metzgeriothallus preserves flat gametophytes and an associated spore-producing capsule. The fossil adds body-plan evidence, but one occurrence does not date the origin of the whole plant group.
