## Morphology {#s-4b3c464069}
During a 2015 capture-release period on Cayo Santiago, Puerto Rico, researchers measured 125 free-ranging adult male rhesus macaques aged at least six years and 21 related females. In pedigree models combining male and female data, body mass, crown-rump length, canine length, and upper abdominal skinfold thickness met the authors' heritability threshold (h²≥0.1); the testis-volume estimate was below it. The authors emphasize that heritability intervals were wide. These are selected traits in one managed island population, not a description of morphological variation across the species range.

## Evolutionary evidence {#s-4cd0f7f46a}
The same study used average annual offspring production as a proxy for reproductive success and tested its association with male traits; it found no definitive evidence of directional, stabilizing, disruptive, or correlational selection on the measured traits. Annual offspring count is not lifetime reproductive success, and the absence of clear detected selection does not show that these traits are never selected.
