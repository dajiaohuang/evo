## Feeding research {#s-be1c7e2636}
Aristizabal et al. studied two black-howler groups in forest remnants in Tabasco, Mexico: a 0.8-ha fragment and a 2.1-ha fragment 500 m away. Across 106 observation days from June 2016 to May 2017, they measured both feeding and the spatial availability of leaves and fruits. In the dry season, leaf-and-fruit food-selection patches measured 6.9–14 m in the two analytical sites where selection was spatially aggregated; mature-fruit availability patches measured about 18–19.5 m, and helped explain that pattern. The study shows how local fruit clusters can shape feeding decisions at a fine scale, while many food-item and season combinations showed no spatial pattern. Its results concern two groups in very small fragments, not a complete diet or species-wide ranging pattern.
