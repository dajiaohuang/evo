## One compressed fossil preserves many systems {#s-56284446b8}
UMZC 2000.32, from the Upper Givetian Lower Plattenkalk at Unterthal near Bergisch Gladbach, is the single specimen in this account. CT imaging and histology reveal parts of the neurocranium, jaws, gill skeleton and pectoral girdle, along with cartilage, scales and teeth. The combination makes the slab informative, but it remains one dorsoventrally compressed individual with its tail missing.

## What the fossil can and cannot establish {#s-8209f9f54a}
The cited phylogenetic analysis places Gladbachus on the chondrichthyan stem, outside the crown group of living sharks and rays; that result depends on the character matrix. The fossil does not directly reveal diet, habitat preference or swimming performance, and the missing tail prevents a complete body reconstruction. Its roughly 385-million-year-old occurrence is a record for this specimen and locality, not a global first appearance for chondrichthyans.
