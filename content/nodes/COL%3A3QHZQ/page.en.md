COL26.8 accepts Jacamaralcyon tridactyla in Galbulidae. AVONET v7 uses the HBW–BirdLife 2020 name: 5 individuals were measured, of which 4 have a complete morphological set. Trait-specific samples may differ.

## Beak dimensions {#s-7bc488b50e}
Mean beak length from the anterior nostril edge to the tip is 36.1 mm; width is 4.7 mm and depth at that edge is 5.8 mm. These standardized measurements describe separate dimensions, not identification cutoffs.

## Wing, tarsus and mass {#s-3adf756b3b}
Mean unflattened wing length from carpal joint to longest primary is 71.6 mm; mean tarsus is 10.3 mm. Dunning is the source category for the separately compiled mean mass of 18.3 g; the morphology specimens do not supply its denominator.

## Ecological categories {#s-92905d4314}
AVONET assigns Forest habitat, Invertivore trophic niche and Insessorial lifestyle. These broad labels support comparisons; they do not imply exclusive habitat use or diet.

## Migration and mapped range {#s-b16f9ae384}
The migration category is sedentary. Historical breeding and resident maps span latitudes -25.88° to -18.51°. They omit a complete account of non-breeding movement and do not estimate current position or global occupancy; the map and morphology were compiled separately.

## Two EltonTraits field groups {#s-272f0b7f89}
The same-name, same-family EltonTraits 1.0 record is associated with Jacamaralcyon tridactyla in pinned COL26.8. Two ecological field groups have source support. Historical taxonomy means that a matching name does not resolve modern splits or synonyms.

## Food-category weights {#s-172e8bb5ca}
Invertebrates are the sole nonzero food category at 100%. Code A marks high compiler confidence in source and diet information. This is a standardized reading of descriptions, not measured diet; a zero does not prove a food is never eaten. Nect, Seed and PlantO extend beyond their ordinary meanings as source categories.

## Feeding positions {#s-f0b706ace2}
Species-level weights are understory, edges or shrubs below 2 m 50%, and trees or high bushes from 2 m upward but below the canopy 50%. They estimate relative use of feeding positions, not nesting, all habitat or time budgets. Surface-water, understory, mid/high, canopy and aerial categories have distinct definitions and are not elevation or flight-height measurements.
